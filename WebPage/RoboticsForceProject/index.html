Precise physical interaction is a bottleneck for visual imitation learning. This project studies whether a human operator should feel robot contact forces during demonstration collection, and whether that haptic loop produces safer, more useful data for force-aware policies.

Paper Code Dataset

Haptic-fused imitation learning framework: demonstrations are collected with synchronized vision, robot state, force-torque sensing, and rendered haptic feedback.

Haptic teleoperation + force-aware diffusion policy

Motivation

Contact-rich tasks such as pressing compliant objects, wiping a surface, inserting parts, or manipulating deformable materials require more than visual alignment. The robot must regulate contact force continuously. A policy can be trained with wrist force-torque readings, but in many teleoperation pipelines the human demonstrator never feels those forces while collecting data.

This project closes that missing sensory loop. A wrist-mounted force-torque sensor measures robot contact, the signal is filtered and scaled, and a Haply haptic controller renders that force back to the operator. The study asks whether this changes demonstration quality and downstream policy behavior.

System

The hardware stack combines a Universal Robots UR10e, a Robotiq parallel gripper, two Intel RealSense RGB cameras, a 6-DoF MAE SensuReal force-torque sensor, and a Haply Inverse3 controller with a VerseGrip attachment. The UR10e is controlled through RTDE for low-latency state feedback and command execution.

At each control cycle, the system acquires robot state, operator input, camera observations, and force-torque measurements. Haptic feedback is conditioned with low-pass filtering, rate limiting, contact thresholds, and smooth blending between free-space motion and contact before it is sent back to the device.

Teleoperation Loop

The UR10e streams end-effector force, torque, position, and velocity into a force-alignment module. The signal is conditioned, passed through a virtual wall model, scaled, and mapped into refined haptic feedback for the Haply Inverse3. In the other direction, the operator's position, velocity, and button states drive the robot command loop, closing the bilateral teleoperation cycle.

Experimental Conditions

The evaluation separates the effect of force observations at policy time from the effect of force feedback at demonstration time.

Condition Demonstration Collection Policy Input
Force-blind No haptic feedback to the operator Vision and proprioception
Force-aware No haptic feedback to the operator Vision, proprioception, and force
Haptic-fused Force feedback rendered to the operator Vision, proprioception, and force

The policy uses a diffusion policy backbone. Multi-view RGB observations are encoded with ResNet-18 backbones, low-dimensional proprioceptive and force inputs are concatenated with visual features, and a 1D U-Net denoises action trajectories over time.

Dataset Quality

The bottle-cap press task reveals a clear force regulation effect. Demonstrations collected with haptic feedback apply lower normal contact forces while preserving consistency across episodes.

Median peak normal force dropped from 71.3 N without haptic feedback to 25.7 N with haptic feedback, a 64.0% reduction.

Task Contrast

The whiteboard wiping task shows that haptic feedback does not simply make operators press less. In this task, effective execution requires sustained contact, so feedback helps operators maintain more stable normal force instead of backing away from the surface.

Policy Behavior

The policy trained from haptic-feedback demonstrations produces more controlled contact behavior during bottle-cap pressing. The preliminary force traces suggest that data collection modality can shape downstream force profiles, even when the final policy receives the same observation channels.

Policy inference force profile. The policy trained with haptic-enabled demonstrations produces reduced and more stable normal force during contact.

Discussion

The central lesson is task dependent. If contact state can be inferred from vision alone, haptic feedback may not create a large policy-performance gap. The advantage should be clearer in tasks where force is weakly observable visually, such as peg-in-hole insertion, cap tightening, deformable object manipulation, or tasks with hidden contact.

Future versions can also improve force integration inside the policy. Candidate directions include deeper force embedding injection, force-budget gating for action scaling, and hybrid switching into the UR10e native force-control mode when contact is detected.
