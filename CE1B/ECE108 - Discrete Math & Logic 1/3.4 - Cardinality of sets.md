> [!quote] Definition
> ## Finite and Infinite Sets

We call a set $S$ <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">finite</strong></u> if $\exists n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exist a [[3.3 - Functions#Special Relationships|bijection]] $f: S \rightarrow \mathbb{N}_n$, where $S \neq \emptyset$. Otherwise, $S$ is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">infinite</strong></u>.

> [!hint] Hint
> Intuitively Think: size of $S$ is some integer $n$

> [!example] Example
> ## Proof that $\mathbb{N}$ is Infinite

Prove $\mathbb{N}$ is infinite.

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> We prove by contradiction. Suppose $\mathbb{N}$ is finite, we let function $\exists n \in \mathbb{N}, f: \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{N}_n$ be a **bijection**, which tells us that there exists the inverse $f^{-1}$.
>
> The domain and range of $f$ is $\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{N}_n$, therefore the domain and range of $f^{-1}$ is $\mathbb{N}_n$ and $\mathbb{N}$. Let $m = max \{f^{-1}(1), f^{-1}(2), ..., f^{-1}(n)\}$, we have:
> $$
> m \in \mathbb{N} \implies m+1 \in \mathbb{N} \implies f(m+1) \in \mathbb{N}_n
> $$
> This indicates that $m+1 = \max\{f^{-1}(1), ..., f^{-1}(n)\}$ which contradicts our previous assumption.

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Countable Sets

We call a set $S$ <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">countable</strong></u> if there exists an **injection** $f: S \rightarrow \mathbb{N}$

> [!hint] Hint
> Intuitively Think: $S$ is index-able, that an element can be written as as $S_i$

> [!example] Example
> ## Proof that $\{r\in\mathbb{R} \mid 0 < r< 1\}$ is Uncountable

Prove $\{r\in\mathbb{R} \mid 0 < r< 1\}$ is uncountable.

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> We prove by contradiction. Suppose $(0, 1)$ is countable, therefore we have injection $f: (0, 1) \rightarrow \mathbb{N}$.  Thus, we should have a bijection $f^{-1}: \text{range}(f) \rightarrow (0, 1)$. Therefore, we can write $(0, 1)$ as an indexed list, and expand its digits:
> $$
> \{r_i = f^{-1}(i)=0.d_{i1}d_{i2}d_{i3}... \mid i \in \text{range}(f)\}
> $$
> We construct a new number
> $$x \in (0,1) = 0.e_1e_2e_3... \quad \text{where } e_i = \begin{cases}
> 4 & (d_{ii} \neq 4)\\
> 5 & (d_{ii} = 4)
> \end{cases}$$
> We observe that
> $$\forall i \in \text{range}(f), e_i \neq d_{ii} \implies \ r_i \neq x \implies f(x) \neq i$$
> Since f is injection. Let $n=f(x) \in \text{range}(f)$, both $f^{-1}(n) = f^{-1}(i) = x$, this contradicts our claim earlier that $f^{-1}$ is a bijection.
