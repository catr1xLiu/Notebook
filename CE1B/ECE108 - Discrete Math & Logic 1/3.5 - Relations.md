
> [!quote] Definition
> ## Binary Relations

A relation describes **which** elements in a set is **related** (connected) to which elements in another.

$R \subseteq A \times B \iff$ "R is a relation between A and B"

![[3.5.1 - Relations|80%]]

Specifically, the relation between a set $A$ and itself can be written as $R \subseteq A \times A$ or $R \subseteq A^2$.

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Symmetric, Asymmetric and Anti-symmetric

A relation $R \subseteq A^2$ is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">symmetric</strong></u> when:$$
\forall x,y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \iff (y,x) \in R
$$
A relation is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">asymmetric</strong></u> when every element is strictly not reversible: $$
\forall x, y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \implies (y,x) \notin R
$$
A relation is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">anti-symmetric</strong></u> when only equal element can be reversible:$$
\forall x, y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \ \land \ (y,x) \in R \implies x=y
$$
![[3.5.2 - Symmetric|100%]]

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Reflexive and Transitive

A relation $R \subseteq A^2$ is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">reflexive</strong></u> when every element relates to itself:$$
\forall x \in A \mid\quad (x,x) \in R
$$
A relation is <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">transitive</strong></u> when it chains:$$
\forall x,y,z \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \ \land \ (y,z) \in R \implies (x,z) \in R
$$

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Equivalence Relation

A relation $R \in A^2$ is called an <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">equivalence</strong></u> when $R$ is:
- **reflexive**: Because one should be equal to itself
- **symmetric**: Because $a = b \iff b=a$
- **transitive**: Because $a=b, b=c \implies a=c$

We can now write that two items $\forall a, b \in A$ are **equivalent** under an equivalence relation $R$ as: $$
\begin{aligned}
a \equiv_{R}b & \iff b\equiv_R a \\
\iff (a,b) \in R & \iff (b,a) \in R
\end{aligned}
$$

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Equivalent Classes

Given an equivalence $R$, the <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">equivalence class</strong></u> of $a \in A$ is the set of all elements related to $a$:$$
[a]_R = \{b \in A \mid (a,b) \in R\}
$$

---

> [!example] Example
> ## $\mod 4$  Equivalence Relation

Consider this relation, prove that it is an equivalence: $$
R_{(\mathbb{Z}, \mod4)} = \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 \mid x_{\mod4} = y_{\mod4} \}$$

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> - Prove **reflexive**: $\forall x \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x_{\mod4} = x_{\mod4}$, so $(x,x) \in R$.
> - Prove **symmetric**: $x_{\mod4} = y_{\mod4} \implies y_{\mod4} = x_{\mod4}$, so $(x,y) \in R \implies (y,x) \in R$.
> - Prove **transitive**: $x_{\mod4} = y_{\mod4} \ \land \ y_{\mod4} = z_{\mod4} \implies x_{\mod4} = z_{\mod4}$, so $(x,y), (y,z) \in R \implies (x,z) \in R$.
