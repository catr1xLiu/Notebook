
## Definition: Binary Relations

A relation describes **which** elements in a set is **related** (connected) to which elements in another. 

$R \subseteq A \times B \iff$ "R is a relation between A and B"

![[3.5.1 - Relations|80%]]

Specifically, the relation between a set $A$ and itself can be written as $R \subseteq A \times A$ or $R \subseteq A^2$.

## Symmetric, Asymmetric and Anti-symmetric 

A relation $R \subseteq A^2$ is **symmetric** when:$$
\forall x,y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \iff (y,x) \in R
$$
A relation is **asymmetric** when every element is strictly not reversible: $$
\forall x, y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \implies (y,x) \notin R
$$
A relation is **anti-symmetric** when only equal element can be reversible:$$
\forall x, y \in A \mid\quad (x,y) \in R \ \land \ (y,x) \in R \implies x=y
$$

## Definition: Equivalence Relation

A relation $R \in A^2$ is called an **equivalence** when $R$ is:
- **reflexive**: Because one should be equal to itself 
- **symmetric**: Because $a = b \iff b=a$
- **transitive**: Because $a=b, b=c \implies a=c$
We can now write that two items $\forall a, b \in A$ are **equivalent** under an equivalence relation $R$ as: $$
\begin{aligned}
a \equiv_{R}b & \iff b\equiv_R a \\
\iff (a,b) \in R & \iff (b,a) \in R
\end{aligned}
$$
