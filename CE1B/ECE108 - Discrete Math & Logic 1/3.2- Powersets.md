
> [!quote] Definition
> ## Powerset

Given a set $A$, the <u><strong style="color:#dab1da">powerset</strong></u> of $A$ is the set of all subsets of $A$, denoted $\mathcal{P}(A)$.

---

> [!fact] Claim
> ## Basic Property

Claim: $\emptyset \in \mathcal{P}(A)$ and $A \in \mathcal{P}(A)$ for every set $A$.

We prove this by: $\emptyset \subseteq A$ vacuously, and $A \subseteq A$ reflexively, so both are subsets of $A$ and therefore elements of $\mathcal{P}(A)$.

---

> [!fact] Claim
> ## Size of Powersets

Claim: if $\text{size}(A) = n$, the size of its powerset $\text{size}(\mathcal{P}(A)) = 2^n$.

We prove this by:
- We label each element in $A$ as $A_i$, we then denote a subset of $A$, $S \in \mathcal{P}(A)$, as a binary number $b$. Its $i^\text{th}$ digit (counting from most significant digit), represents whether $A_i$ is included in $S$, where $b_i = 1$ if $A_i \in S$ and $b_i=0$ if $A_i \notin S$. It is obvious that this representation is deterministic, there is one and only one $b$ given $S$.
- The amount of possible $b \in \{0,1\}^n$ is $2^n$, therefore, the amount of possible $S \in \mathcal{P}(A)$ is also $2^n$.
