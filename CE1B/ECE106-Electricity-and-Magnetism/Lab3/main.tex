\section{Theory}

We consider the system of two spherical uniform charges ($a=0.01m$, $Q=+1.0 \mu C$) placed $r=0.2m$ apart. We compute the total electrical energy:

\[
  W_\text{total} = n \times W_\text{formation} + W_\text{assembly} = 2 \times \frac{3}{5} \frac{Q^2}{4\pi \epsilon_0 a} + \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{Q^2}{r} = 1.1234 J
\]

\section{Analysis of Electric Potential}

With the required 5-charge set-up, with point charges of $+1.0 \mu C$ each, located in: 
\[ 
(0.75, 0.25, 0.0),\ (-0.5, 0.0, 0.0),\ (1.0,-0.75,0.0),\ (0.5, -1.0, 0.0)\ (0.0, -0.5, 0.0)
\]

We can compute the electric potential change when moving a $+0.65\mu C$ test charge.

The change in energy is:
\[
  \delta E = \delta P \times q = (75041 - 104800) \text{V} \times 0.65 \mu C = -0.01934 J
\]

\textbf{Answers:}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The elevation/ height corresponds to the \textbf{electric potential} at the projected point on the x-y plane. 
  \item The net amount of energy transferred does not vary depending on path take, because electric force is a conservative force and the energy transffered is equal to the change in electric potential energy.
  \item Electric field lines would correspond to tangent lines on the surface $(-\nabla)$, they will point downwards the surface, and the magnitude corresponds to the slope of the surface. 
\end{itemize}
