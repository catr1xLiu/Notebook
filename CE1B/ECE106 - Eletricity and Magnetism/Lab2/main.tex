\section{Pre-Lab: Theory}

\subsection{Eletric Flux through Closed Sphereical Surface}

A point charge of $+0.75\mu C$ is placed at $(x, y, z) = (0, 0, 0)$, we are to compute the total eletric flux through a sphereical surface of radius $R=2.0m$ from the definition of eletric flux. We notice that the eletric field strength $\vec{E}$ is the same, and that $\hat{n} \cdot \hat{r} = 1$. Therefore:

\[
  \Phi_E = \iint \vec{E} \cdot \hat{n} \ dS
  = S \times \|\vec{E}\|
  = 4 \pi R^2 \frac{q}{4 \pi R^2 \epsilon_0} 
\]
Given $\epsilon_{0} = 8.854187 \times 10^{-12} \ F / m$ and $q = +0.75 \times 10^{-6} C$, we compute for $R=2.0m$ and $R=5.0m$, $\Phi_{2m} = \Phi_{5m} = 84705.69 Nm^2/C$.

\subsection{Charge Density}

The charge density of a spherical charge cloud with radius $r=0.01m$ and charge of $+0.75\mu C$. The volume of the sphere is $V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 = 4.1888 \times 10^{-6} m^3$, density $\rho=\frac{q}{V} = 0.17905 C/m^3$

\subsection{Eletric Flux Through Square Surfaces}

We denote the the eletric flux through the three squares as $\Phi_1$, $\Phi_2$ and $\Phi_3$, respectively. We construct cubic gaussian surfaces for square 1 and 2, denote as Cube1 and Cube2. Using the Gauss' Law and considering the symetry:

\[
6\Phi_1 = 6\Phi_2 = \frac{q_\text{enclosed}}{\epsilon_0}
\]

We observe that Square 3 contains all of Square 1 and some additional area that has non-zero eletric flux. Therefore, $\Phi_3 > \Phi_1$. The overall relationship is:

\[
\Phi_3 > \Phi_2 = \Phi_1
\]

\section{Simulation for Spherical Gaussian Surfaces}
We build the simulation model of a point charge ($R=0.01$ and $Q=+0.75 \mu C$) and two sphereical surfaces of $R_\text{sphere1}=2.0m$ and $R_\text{sphere2}=5.0m$ with grounded boundary at $R_\text{boundary}=10.0m$ in COMSOL.

\begin{figure}[H] % [H] forces the figure to be placed exactly where it appears in the text
	\centering % Horizontally center the figure
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{./Figures/SphereSurfaceSim} % Include the figure
  \caption{COMSOL model of a point charge and 2 spherical surfaces.}
\end{figure}

During simulation, the surface integration is computed as:
\[
  \iint_S \vec{D} \cdot \hat{n} \ dA = \Phi \epsilon_0
\]

Therefore, the theoretical value for a closed surface is:
\[
  \oiint_S \vec{D} \cdot \hat{n} \ dA = \Phi \epsilon_0 = Q_\text{enclosed} = 0.75 \mu C
\]
