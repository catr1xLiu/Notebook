
> [!quote] Definition
> ## Permutation, Combination and Multichoose

We want to represent how many ways $k$ items can be selected from a set $|S|=n$, based on different rules: 
#### <u>Permutation</u>

> Ordered, without replacement — a sequence $\langle s_1, \dots, s_k \rangle$ of distinct $s_i \in S$, $0 \leq k \leq n$.

![[4.1.1 - Permutation|85%]]

For the $i$-th item, we are left with $(n-i+1)$ choices, therefore:

$$\begin{aligned} 
P(n,k) =& \ \prod_{i=1}^{k} (n-i+1) =  \underbrace{n \times (n-1) \times \cdots \times (n-k+1)}_{k \text{ factors}} \\
= & \ \boxed{\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}}
\end{aligned}$$
In the special case $k=n$, this becomes **arrangements**: 
$$ P(n,n) = \frac{n!}{0!} = n! $$

#### <u>Combination</u>

> Unordered, without replacement — a subset $A \subseteq S$ with $|A| = k$, $0 \leq k \leq n$.

For each combination, there are $P(k,k)$ arrangements to form a permutation:

$$ P(n,k) =\ P(k,k) \times \binom{n}{k} $$
Therefore:
$$\begin{aligned} \binom{n}{k} =& \ \boxed{\frac{P(n,k)}{k!}} \\ 
= & \ \frac{n!}{(n-k)! \ k!} = \binom{n}{n-k} \end{aligned}$$

#### <u>Multichoose</u>

> Unordered, with replacement — a bag of size $k$ from $S$, i.e. multiplicities $\langle m_1, \dots, m_n \rangle$ with $\sum m_i = k$. Here $k > n$ is allowed.

![[4.1.2 - Multichoose|85%]]

We can represent a repeated element as a cell pointing to another cell. That means each cell chooses from $n + (k-1)$ options — the $n$ elements of $S$, or one of the $k-1$ other cells. Selecting $k$ of those options, unordered:
$$
\left(\!\!\binom{n}{k}\!\!\right) = \boxed{\binom{n+k-1}{k}} = \binom{n+k-1}{n-1}
$$

#### Summary

| Notation                        | Order matters? | Repeats allowed? | Each selection is...       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| $\binom{n}{k}$                  | no             | no               | subset of size k           |
| $P(n,k)$                        | yes            | no               | sequence of distinct items |
| $\left(\!\binom{n}{k}\!\right)$ | no             | yes              | bag (multiset)             |
| $n^k$                           | yes            | yes              | string                     |

> [!fact] Result
> ## Counting Subsets

Given a finite set such that $|S| = n$ (countable), the number of subsets of $S$ is:

$$ |\mathcal{P}(S)| = 2^n $$

---

> [!fact] Theorem
> ## Binomial Theorem

Given $a, b \in \mathbb{R}, \ n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$:

$$ (a+b)^n = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \binom{}{} $$


> [!hint] Hint
> ## Application in Probability

With a uniform probability distribution, combination can be used to estimate probability:

$$ \Pr(A) = \frac{\#\text{ of ways yielding } A}{\#\text{ of possible outcomes}} $$
Which is what we will discuss in detail in [[4.2 - Discrete Probably]].

> [!example] Example
> ## Types of Combination Problems

#### <u>Selection</u>

*Select 5 bits from the alphabet **in order**, repetition allowed.*

$$ N = 26^5 $$

#### <u>Ordered Selection</u>

*Select 5 distinct bits from the alphabet in order.*

$$ N = P(26,5) = \frac{26!}{(26-5)!} = 26 \times 25 \times 24 \times 23 \times 22 $$

#### <u>Number of Arrangements</u>

*Number of 26-letter strings, each letter used exactly once.*

$$ N = P(26, 26) = \frac{26!}{0!} = 26! $$

#### <u>Unordered Selection, without replacement</u>

*Number of ways to get sets of size 5 from the alphabet.*

$$
N = \binom{26}{5}
= \frac{26!}{(26-5)! \cdot 5!}
= \binom{26}{26-5}
= \frac{26!}{(26-21)! \cdot 21!}
$$

#### <u>Unordered Selection, with replacement</u>

*Number of ways to get a bag of size 5 from the alphabet (letters may repeat in a bag, but the order in a bag does not matter).*

$$
N = \left(\!\!\binom{26}{5}\!\!\right) = \binom{26+5-1}{5} = \binom{30}{5} = \frac{30!}{25! \cdot 5!}
$$
