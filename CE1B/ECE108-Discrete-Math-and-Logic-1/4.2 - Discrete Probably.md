
> [!quote] Definition
> ## Event

First, we define a <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">sample space</strong></u> $S$, which is the set of all elementary events:
- We do a countable number of experiments (e.g. toss a coin twice).
- We collect all possible outcomes (e.g. $\{TT, TH, HT, HH\}$).

An <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">event</strong></u> $E \subseteq S$ is the combination of one or more elementary events happening.

Events $A, B \subseteq S$ are <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">mutually exclusive</strong></u> if $A \cap B = \emptyset$.

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Probability Distribution

A function $\Pr$ can be used to describe the probability distribution of a sample space $S$:

$$
\Pr: \mathcal{P}(S) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}
$$

- $\forall A \subseteq S: \quad \Pr(A) \geq 0$
- $\Pr(S) = 1$
- For two mutually exclusive events $A, B$: $\quad A \cap B = \emptyset \implies \Pr(A) + \Pr(B) = \Pr(A \cup B)$

#### <u>Discreteness</u>

A probability distribution is <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">discrete</strong></u> if $S$ is **finite**. *We only consider finite $S$ in this course.*

#### <u>Uniform</u>

A probability distribution is <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">uniform</strong></u> if:

$$
\forall \text{ elementary events } e_i, e_j \in S: \quad \Pr(e_i) = \Pr(e_j)
$$

This implies that:

$$
\forall A \subseteq S: \quad \Pr(A) = \frac{|A|}{|S|}
$$

---

> [!hint] Hint
> ## Relevant Claims

For a sample space $S$ with $|S| = n$ and a probability distribution $\Pr: \mathcal{P}(S) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$:

#### <u>Null Event</u>

**Claim:**

$$
\Pr(\emptyset) = 0
$$

#### <u>Probability Magnitude</u>

**Claim:**

$$
\forall A, B \subseteq S: \quad A \subseteq B \implies \Pr(A) \leq \Pr(B)
$$

#### <u>Combining</u>

**Claim:**

$$
\Pr(A \cup B) = \Pr(A) + \Pr(B) - \Pr(A \cap B)
$$

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Conditional Probability

We denote the probability that $A$ happens given that $B$ has already happened as:

$$
\Pr(A\mid B) = \frac{\Pr(A \cap B)}{\Pr(B)} \quad (\Pr(B)\neq 0)
$$

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Independence

Two events $A$ and $B$ are said to be <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">independent</strong></u> if:

$$ \Pr(A \mid B) = \Pr(A) \quad \iff \quad \Pr(A \cap B) = \Pr(A) \ \Pr(B) $$

---

> [!quote] Definition
> ## Expectation

Given a sample space $S$ with a probability distribution $\Pr$, we can use a function $X:S\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ to assign a value to each possible outcome.

The <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">range</strong></u> of $X$ is the set of outcome values we can get from sampling, and the probability that we get a specific outcome value is:

$$ \Pr(X=x) = \sum_{s\in S,\, X(s) = x} \Pr(s) $$

The <u><strong style="color:#a0399f">expectation / expected value</strong></u> (mean) is denoted $E[X]$, which equals:

$$ \begin{aligned}
E[X] =& \sum_{x\in R} x \times \Pr(X=x) \\
 = & \sum_{s\in S} X(s) \times \Pr(s)
\end{aligned} $$

---

> [!hint] Hint
> ## Linearity of Expectation

If $S$ is the sample space, and $X: S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $Y:S\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are random variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Notation: }& [X+Y](s) = X(s)+Y(s) \\ \\
E[X+Y] =& E[X]+E[Y] \\
E\left[\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right] =& \sum_{i=1}^n E[X_i]
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!example] Example
> ## Two Kinds of Fruit

Given a large sack of $d \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ kinds of fruit, with each kind having approximately the same number of fruits, what is the probability that grabbing 2 fruits yields two different kinds of fruit?

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the event of grabbing the $i$-th kind of fruit ($0 \leq i < d$) on the **first** grab be $A_i$, and the event of grabbing the $i$-th kind of fruit on the **second** grab be $B_i$. We have:
> $$
> \forall i \in [0,d): \quad \Pr(\overline{B_i}\mid A_i) = \Pr(\overline{B_i}) = \frac{d-1}{d}
> $$
> Since no matter which kind of fruit we choose on the first grab the probability of getting a different fruit on the next grab is $\frac{d-1}{d}$, the probability of getting two different kinds of fruit is $\frac{d-1}{d}$.

> [!example] Example
> ## Conditional Probability

Flip a coin twice. Find the probability of getting $HH$ given that at least one of the tosses is $H$.

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> $$
> S = \{HH, TH, HT, TT\}
> $$
> $$
> \Pr(HH \mid H) = \frac{\Pr(HH)}{\Pr(H)} = \frac{|S_{HH}|}{|S_H|} = \frac{1}{3}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> - Let $\set{W_1, W_2, W_3}$ be the events that door 1, 2, 3 has the prize.
> - Let $\set{S_1, S_2, S_3}$ be the events that door 1, 2, 3 is selected by the contestant.
> - Let $\set{R_1, R_2, R_3}$ be the events that door 1, 2, 3 is revealed by the host.
>
> Assume, without loss of generality, that the contestant selected door 1 and that the host revealed door 2.
>
> We need to compare $\Pr(W_1 \mid (S_1 \cap R_2))$ and $\Pr(W_3 \mid (S_1 \cap R_2))$. Write:
> $$ \begin{aligned}
> \Pr(W_1 \mid (S_1 \cap R_2)) =& \frac{\Pr(W_1 \cap S_1 \cap R_2)}{\Pr(S_1 \cap R_2)} \\
> =& \frac{\Pr(W_1) \times \Pr(S_1\mid W_1) \times \boxed{\Pr(R_2 \mid (W_1 \cap S_1))}}{\Pr(W_1 \cap S_1 \cap R_2) + \Pr(W_2 \cap S_1 \cap R_2) + \Pr(W_3 \cap S_1 \cap R_2)} \\
> =& \frac
> {\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}}
> {(\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2}) + 0 + (\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times 1)} \\
> = & \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}
> \\
> \\
> \Pr(W_3 \mid (S_1 \cap R_2)) = & \frac{\Pr(W_3 \cap S_1 \cap R_2)}{\Pr(S_1 \cap R_2)} \\
> =& \frac{\Pr(W_3) \times \Pr(S_1\mid W_3) \times \boxed{\Pr(R_2 \mid (W_3 \cap S_1))}}{\Pr(W_1 \cap S_1 \cap R_2) + \Pr(W_2 \cap S_1 \cap R_2) + \Pr(W_3 \cap S_1 \cap R_2)} \\
> =& \frac
> {\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times \boxed{1}}
> {(\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2}) + 0 + (\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} \times 1)} \\
> = & \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> \end{aligned} $$
>
> Using Python code, we can verify:
>
> ```python
> from random import randint, choice
>
> def sample() -> tuple[bool, bool]:
>     # Returns: One random sample in the boolean form
>     # (Keep Door Wins, Swap Door Wins), either [T,F] or [F, T]
>     prize: int = randint(1, 3)
>     selection: int = randint(1, 3)
>
>     POOL: set = {1, 2, 3}
>
>     reveal: int = choice(list(POOL - {prize, selection}))
>
>     swap: int = (POOL - {reveal, selection}).pop()
>
>     return selection == prize, swap == prize
>
>
> keep_door_wins, swap_door_wins = 0, 0
> SAMPLE_SIZE = int(1e6)
>
> for i in range(SAMPLE_SIZE):
>     k, s = sample()
>     keep_door_wins += int(k)
>     swap_door_wins += int(s)
>
> print(keep_door_wins / SAMPLE_SIZE, swap_door_wins / SAMPLE_SIZE)
> ```

> $$

> \end{aligned}
> $$
