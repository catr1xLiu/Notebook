\opening{Dear Waabi Recruiting Team,}

I am a 1B Computer Engineering student at UWaterloo. I recently worked on deep learning research at the National Research Council (NRC) Canada. I am applying for the Research Intern position because I want to explore an area in physical AI that I believe is underexplored. I want to integrate state-of-the-art visuomotor policies and diffusion-based action prediction models into autonomous vehicles.

The job description emphasizes the need for new ideas in prediction and behaviors. In my current research, I use conditional diffusion networks to predict motion, representing trajectory points as rich feature vectors. For my haptic-fused robotics project, which I recently submitted to RoboTac 2026, I implemented Diffusion Policy with Real-Time Chunking (RTC) to predict a robot's future actions in real time. At the same time, my human motion synthesis project uses Motion Diffusion Models (MDM) and ST-GCNs to create complex kinematic sequences. This work was recently accepted to an ICRA 2026 workshop. Running these models required me to scale PyTorch training pipelines across a 36-node SLURM cluster. This experience gives me the research background needed to address the complex agent and pedestrian prediction challenges at Waabi.

In addition to my AI research, I have a strong interest in simulation and software engineering. I created MapleSim, an open-source rigid-body physics library used by over 500 FIRST Robotics teams worldwide. Working on this large-scale Java/WPILib project gave me a solid foundation in object-oriented design and physics simulation. With the strong C++ skills from my degree, I am confident in writing the scalable, production-ready code necessary to connect AI with simulation environments like Waabi World.

I would love to bring my research drive and software engineering skills to your team to help advance autonomous transportation. Thank you for your time, and I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely yours, \\
