Age affects walking patterns through kinematic signatures such as reduced velocity and shifts in posture, but capturing these subtle differences in individual walking styles can be challenging. Significant progress has been made in detecting and generating human motion in different styles. However, whether biomechanical signatures such as aging can be learned by these models remains unexplored.

We present a proof-of-concept that attempts to learn and reproduce the aging signature in human motion using graph and diffusion networks. A graph convolutional network is trained to predict a subject's age from captured motion, while a diffusion network is trained to generate new motion conditioned on age. Consistent age classification demonstrates that age-specific signals exist within clinical data, and the diffusion network's results show that this signal can be effectively reproduced using generative models. This study bridges clinical gait analysis with modern machine-learning models and lays the groundwork for age-aware motion synthesis systems.
