This study investigates whether machine learning models can learn age-related kinematic signatures from human motion capture data. While age affects walking patterns through reduced joint range of motion and velocity changes, capturing these subtle variations remains challenging. We present a proof-of-concept approach: first, we train a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) to classify motion sequences into age groups (Young, Mid, Elderly) using the Van Criekinge dataset. Second, we fine-tune a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to generate age-conditioned walking motions. Our work addresses pipeline challenges including marker-to-vertex mapping and coordinate system corrections that previously corrupted training data. Preliminary analysis confirms strong correlation between age and walking speed ($r=-0.96$ for subjects 50+), validating the dataset's age signal. Successful ST-GCN classification will mathematically verify preserved kinematic signatures, while LoRA-MDM generation demonstrates feasibility for applications in elderly care and rehabilitation. This research establishes foundational capabilities for age-aware motion synthesis systems.
