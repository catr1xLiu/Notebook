It is well-established that age affects walking patterns through reduced velocity, constrained range of motion, and shift in posture. However, capturing these subtle kinematics signatures in the walking patterns of individual subjects remains challenging.

We present a 3-stage workflow that bridges Clinical Gait Analysis with Machine-Learning Motion Synthesis: 
(1) A multi-stage data processing pipeline that converts a clinical motion capture dataset (Plug-in Gait) to machine-learnable skeleton graphs of modern standard (HumanML3D), and removes instrumental noise and jitters to ensure quality;
(2) A Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) to distinguish age groups (young, mid, old) from human motion. 
(3) Various Low-Rank Adapters for Motion Diffusion Models (LoRA-MDM) for different age groups that allow age-conditioned motion generation, trained from a training-validation loop where the ST-GCN Network is used for guidance.

Consistent ST-GCN age classification proves that age-specific signals exist within clinical data, while our LoRA-MDM results show that this signal can be reproduced using a generative model. This proof-of-concept lays the groundwork for age-aware motion synthesis systems.
