# ✅ Tasks Completed

### 1. Initial LoRA Training on Van Criekinge Data

Two age-adapter runs were completed on the VC dataset as planned in Week 13 (`AgeEncoder`: rank 5, `lambda_prior_preserv=1.0`, 4000 steps, batch 32). Training loss converged cleanly (final loss 0.321), but generated samples exhibited pronounced **random-motion artifacts** — jittery limb movement even on "standing still" prompts. The 100STYLE-trained baseline (`Old_Retrained_humanml_normalized`) produced clean motion under identical generation settings, ruling out the inference pipeline. **Generated sample motions are polluted with random noise movement.** This kicked off a multi-day root-cause investigation.

### 2. Normalization Path Verification

First hypothesis: a train/inference normalization mismatch. Traced the full path and confirmed both training and generation use HumanML3D's `Mean.npy`/`Std.npy` via `VanCriekingeDataset.inv_transform()`, applied in `generate.py:167`. Additionally hardened the loader so `--lora_path` is required explicitly rather than silently falling back to style-name auto-discovery. **Normalization is correct in both directions** — hypothesis falsified.

### 3. Manifold-Level Diagnosis of VC Distribution

Wrote `diagnose_vc_manifold.py` to compare VC against HumanML3D walking and 100STYLE-FW in HumanML3D-normalized space, section by section across the 263-dim vector. The script outputs numerical summaries, per-dim mean/std plots, per-section histograms, per-joint `local_vel` breakdowns, and trajectory alignment statistics. Key findings from the initial pass:

- **43% of VC clips exhibited upward pelvis drift >10cm**, worst case drifting 1.9 m over 3 seconds (physically impossible)
- **Systematic coordinate misalignment** — VC trajectories not canonically oriented along +Z
- Top-15 most-shifted dimensions in VC were dominated by `root_y`

### 4. Upstream Pipeline Fixes

Two real bugs were identified and fixed in the VC→HumanML3D conversion:

- **Coordinate alignment fix** — resolved the root cause of root_y drift
- **Removed 6 polluted clips** (T-pose misclassifications and sub-threshold lengths)

Re-ran the diagnosis post-fix: VC median `root_y` now **0.951 m**, matching HumanML3D (0.944 m) and 100STYLE (0.951 m) exactly. **Pelvis drift resolved.**

### 5. Frame-Rate Hypothesis Investigation

Post-fix, `local_vel` (per-frame joint displacement) remained 2.4–5× HumanML3D's scale, which raised a plausible fps-mismatch hypothesis in the conversion. However, translational velocity conflates real walking speed with fps error — VC subjects may simply walk faster than the median HumanML3D walker.

Extended the diagnostic with a **per-joint angular velocity test** (rad/s, computed from consecutive-frame deltas of the `rot_data` 6D rotations). Angular velocity is physiologically bounded during walking regardless of speed, so an fps error would produce **uniform inflation across all joints** — a clean signature.

Result: **fps hypothesis falsified.** Per-joint VC/HML ratios ranged from 0.65× to 2.88× (std 0.57) — non-uniform. The inflation was concentrated in the lower-limb chain (ankles 2.75×, knees 1.87×, toes 1.57×) while upper body joints sat near 1.0× or below. This pattern is consistent with either (a) reduced mocap smoothing on fast-moving distal markers, or (b) lower-limb gait characteristics specific to the stroke-rehab cohort — both are real VC properties, not conversion bugs.

### 6. Conclusion

Both real conversion bugs (drift, alignment) are fixed. The remaining distribution shift (fast walking, canonical alignment, noisier lower legs) reflects genuine VC characteristics. **Rank-5 LoRA with `lambda_prior_preserv=1.0` is under-parametrized and over-regularized for this distribution shift** — the adapter can't pull the base model far enough from its HumanML3D prior to produce coherent VC-style motion.
