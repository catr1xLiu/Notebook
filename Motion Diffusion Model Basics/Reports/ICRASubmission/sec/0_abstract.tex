We present a framework to detect and reproduce aging signatures in human motion using data-driven neural networks.
Our data processing pipeline converts clinical gait recordings into learning-ready representations, recovering from defects caused by outdated capture equipment.
We450 clips from 138 subjects across the lifespan was obtained for our experiments.
A Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) is trained to distinguish age group (Young, Mid-age, Elderly) from a single motion clip, achieving 64.6\% classification accuracy.
A Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) fine-tuned with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) learns to generate realistic motion from natural language, grounded with an age condition.
We measured various clinical gait metrics from the generated motion clips including \textbf{xxx}, \textbf{yyy}, and \textbf{zzz}, all of which show that age signal is consistently reproduced by our model.
We find significant room for improvement, with dataset size and quality identified as the primary bottleneck.
This work lays the foundation for age-aware motion synthesis systems deployed in eldercare and assistive robotics contexts, and motivates the collection of larger age-diverse human motion datasets.
