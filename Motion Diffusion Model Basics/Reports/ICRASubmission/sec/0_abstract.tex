We present a framework to detect and reproduce biomechanical aging signatures in human motion using data-driven neural networks.
Our preprocessing pipeline maps clinical gait recordings into learning-ready graphical representations, resolving retargeting artifacts and measurement errors to extract 450 valid clips from 138 lifespan subjects.
We trained a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) to classify age groups (Young, Adult, Elderly) directly from kinematics, achieving a peak validation accuracy of 64.6\%.
Concurrently, a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) fine-tuned via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was used to synthesize age-conditioned locomotion.
To objectively evaluate synthesis quality, we computed standard clinical gait metrics—including spatiotemporal parameters, stride variability (CV), and lower-limb range of motion (RoM)—on both the ground-truth and generated datasets.
Our analysis confirms that the converted dataset perfectly preserves monotonic age-related decline, and the generated motions reflects some differences in aging signatures but struggles with spatiotemporal consistency.
These findings demonstrate the absolute necessity of quantitative biomechanical benchmarking for generative models, and highlight dataset scale and quality as the primary bottlenecks.
Finally, we propose a continuous latent-encoding conditioning architecture as future work to stabilize age-aware motion synthesis for deployment in eldercare and assistive robotics.
