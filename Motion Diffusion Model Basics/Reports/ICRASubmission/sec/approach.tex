We adopt the motion capture dataset of Van Criekinge et
al.~\cite{vancriekinge2023dataset} (ages 21--86) and
convert clinical marker recordings into HumanML3D-format
skeletal graphs~\cite{guo2022generating} via SMPL body-model
fitting~\cite{mahmood2019amass} and feature extraction.
An overview of our framework is available in
Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}.


\textbf{Age classification.}
A Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)~\cite{yan2018stgcn} is trained to classify motion clips into three age groups (Young, Mid, Elderly).  The penultimate layer of the network is a latent vector that encapsulates the biomechanical signatures of aging (e.g., velocity variance, joint motion range), denoted as $z_{age}$.


\textbf{Age-conditioned generation.}
We use a diffusion-based motion generative
model~\cite{tevet2023human, chen2022mld} conditioned on
both text and age. At training time, we extract
$z_\text{age}$ from real motion clips using the frozen
ST-GCN encoder, and train the diffusion model to generate
motion conditioned on this embedding via cross-attention.
A small MLP $g(a)$ is jointly trained to map a scalar age
value to the same latent space, supervised by the
ST-GCN-derived targets. At inference, $g(a)$ alone converts
a desired age into $z_\text{age}$, enabling continuous
age-controlled generation without requiring input motion.
