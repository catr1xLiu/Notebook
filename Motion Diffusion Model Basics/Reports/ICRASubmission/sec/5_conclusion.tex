We presented a proof-of-concept framework for age-aware human motion synthesis by integrating clinical gait processing, age-signature detection, and LoRA-based adaptation of a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model.
Our results show that age-related information can be detected from processed motion clips, while also revealing that current generated motions do not yet consistently preserve statistically robust aging signatures under selected clinical and kinematic metrics.
These findings highlight the need for larger age-diverse datasets, stronger conditioning mechanisms, and biomechanics-guided constraints.
By identifying these opportunities, this work provides an initial step toward demographic-aware motion priors for human motion prediction, assistive robot planning, and safer human-robot interaction in aging-in-place contexts.
