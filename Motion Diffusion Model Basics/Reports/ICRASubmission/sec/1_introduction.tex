Accurate human motion anticipation is foundational for safe human-robot interaction, especially when robots share space with humans, plan around future human movement, or provide proactive assistance.
Recent deep generative architectures, particularly Motion Diffusion Models (MDMs)~\cite{tevet2023human, chen2022mld}, have demonstrated strong capability in synthesizing realistic and diverse human motions, offering a promising way to model possible future motion trajectories.
However, widely used motion-capture datasets, such as AMASS~\cite{mahmood2019amass}, are heavily biased toward young and healthy actors~\cite{umagami2025intend}.
As a result, existing models can synthesize diverse actions and styles~\cite{sawdayee2025dance}, but often overlook subject-specific biomechanical factors that influence how different populations move.
While recent work has begun to incorporate physical attributes such as body shape into motion generation~\cite{liao2025shapemovestextdrivenshapeaware}, explicit modeling of age-related kinematic characteristics remains underexplored.
This limitation is particularly critical for assistive robots and intelligent systems in aging-in-place and eldercare scenarios, where anticipating older adults' movements is essential for safe navigation, timely assistance, collision avoidance, and proactive human-robot interaction.

Concurrently, clinical gait analysis has long provided a rigorous foundation for biomechanical assessment.
Recent integration of sensor-based data acquisition and data analysis have quantified physiological and spatiotemporal gait signatures associated with aging and health conditions~\cite{diaz2025evaluating, galasso2024development}.
Incorporating such biomechanical gait signatures into deep learning-based human motion models is essential for improving the physical validity of synthesized motion.

To address this gap, we present a proof-of-concept framework that integrates clinical gait analysis with deep generative modeling toward age-aware human motion synthesis.
First, we develop a data processing pipeline that converts clinical gait recordings from subjects across different age groups into standardized skeletal representations.
We then fine-tune an existing Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)~\cite{yan2018stgcn} to infer age group from individual motion clips and to encode age-related biomechanical characteristics into a latent representation.
We train a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapter for a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM)~\cite{tevet2023human} to generate text-driven human motions conditioned on age group.
The generated motion clips are evaluated using established clinical and kinematic gait metrics to examine whether the model reproduces biomechanical signatures associated with aging.
Our analysis shows that the model successfully captures spatial kinematic constraints --- Elderly-conditioned clips produce measurably reduced hip Range-of-Motion relative to Young and Adult conditions --- but fails to reproduce spatiotemporal consistency, with generated stride variability massively overshooting clinical baselines due to the inherent stochasticity of the diffusion process.
These findings identify dataset size, data quality, and conditioning strength as critical bottlenecks for age-aware motion synthesis, and motivate the collection of larger, more diverse human motion datasets spanning age groups, health conditions, and functional mobility levels.
