Accurate human motion synthesis is foundational for safe human-robot interaction, and deep generative architectures, particularly Motion Diffusion Models (MDMs)~\cite{tevet2023human, chen2022mld}, have demonstrated great success in synthesizing realistic and diverse motion.
However, current motion capture datasets, such as AMASS~\cite{mahmood2019amass}, are heavily biased toward young, healthy actors~\cite{umagami2025intend}.
Consequently, the vast majority of existing models successfully synthesize varied action expressions and styles~\cite{sawdayee2025dance}, but overlook the intrinsic biomechanical properties of the individual subjects.
While recent efforts have attempted to incorporate physical attributes like body shape~\cite{liao2025shapemovestextdrivenshapeaware} as a condition for motion generation, the explicit modeling of age-induced kinematic degradation remains severely underexplored.
This bias presents a critical bottleneck for deploying assistive robots and intelligent systems in eldercare and clinical hospitality scenarios, where accurately predicting and navigating around age-specific motion is paramount for safety.

Concurrently, clinical gait analysis has long provided a rigorous grounding for biomechanical assessment.
Driven by objective, sensor-based data science, recent clinical works have successfully quantified the physiological and spatiotemporal signatures of age- and health-related gait~\cite{diaz2025evaluating, galasso2024development}.

Incorporating biomechanical gait signatures into deep learning human motion models is essential to ensuring the physical validity of synthesized motion. Rather than relying solely on subjective visual fidelity, deep learning frameworks must be anchored to clinical reality.

To address this gap, we present a proof-of-concept framework that integrates clinical gait analysis with deep generative modeling to synthesize age-aware human motion.
First, we develop a data processing pipeline that converts clinical gait recordings from subjects across different age groups into standard skeletal representations.
We then train a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) to discriminate age by observing a single motion clip, and to encode biomechanical characteristics of motion into a latent representation.
Using this representation as a condition, we fine-tune a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to generate text-driven motion that accurately reflects the physical characteristics of specific age groups.
