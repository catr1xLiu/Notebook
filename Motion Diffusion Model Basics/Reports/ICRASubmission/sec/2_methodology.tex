To ensure biological fidelity is preserved, we utilize a modified optimization pipeline adapted from VPoser~\cite{pavlakos2019expressivebodycapture3d}.
We formulated a frame-by-frame minimum-loss optimization using the L-BFGS engine.
Crucially, we implemented a multi-step pipeline where a local minimum is found \textit{before} the optimizer considers body pose priors and spatiotemporal consistency. This careful staging ensures that minor, individual biomechanical details are preserved during fitting without being overwhelmed by standard generic priors.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:artifact}, standard priors induce crouch artifacts (left) and naive mapping causes mesh inflation (right); our optimized pipeline effectively mitigates these issues to recover a natural posture (center).

\subsection{Age Signature Detecting Network}

To capture the biomechanical signatures of aging, we employ an Age Representation Learner built on a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)~\cite{yan2018spatial}. The network is fine-tuned to classify motion clips into three distinct age groups: Young ($<$40), Adult (40--64), and Elderly ($\geq$65).
By training the ST-GCN to successfully predict age groups from walking motions alone, the network learns to isolate demographic kinematic differences from general gait mechanics. The penultimate layer of the ST-GCN encapsulates these age-related biomechanical signatures; we extract this layer's output to serve as the latent age embedding, denoted as $\mathbf{z}_{age}$.

\subsection{Gait-Analysis Metrics for Evaluation}

To rigorously evaluate whether aging signals are preserved in the processed dataset and successfully reproduced by our generative model, we utilize standard, training-free gait-analysis metrics derived from clinical literature~\cite{bailey2023smartwatch, cordeiro2025constraint, galasso2024development}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatiotemporal Parameters:} We compute the average walking speed, stride length, and cadence using heel-strike events detected via zero-velocity crossings of the ankle joints.
    \item \textbf{Gait Variability:} The consistency of a subject's gait is quantified using the Coefficient of Variation (CV) for both stride time and stride length, defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{CV} = \left( \frac{\sigma}{\mu} \right) \times 100\%
    \end{equation}
    where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation and $\mu$ is the mean of the parameter across steps.
    \item \textbf{Joint Range of Motion (RoM):} We measure the peak flexion and extension angles of the hip and knee joints during the gait cycle.
    \item \textbf{Margin of Stability (MoS):} To assess dynamic balance, we compute the anterior-posterior Margin of Stability:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{MoS} = \mathrm{BoS}_{\mathrm{boundary}} - x_{\mathrm{CoM}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathrm{BoS}_{\mathrm{boundary}}$ is the boundary of the Base of Support, and $x_{\mathrm{CoM}}$ is the extrapolated center of mass, given by:
    \begin{equation}
        x_{\mathrm{CoM}} = \mathrm{CoM} + \frac{v_{\mathrm{CoM}}}{\sqrt{g/l}}
    \end{equation}
    where $v_{\mathrm{CoM}}$ is the CoM velocity, $g$ is gravity, and $l$ is the effective pendulum length.
    \item \textbf{Minimum Foot Clearance (MFC):} This is defined as the minimum vertical toe displacement (Z-axis) during the mid-swing phase of the gait cycle, trimmed to exclude the initial and terminal 20\% of the swing window.
\end{itemize}

These metrics are computed deterministically on both the real ground-truth clips and the generated motion clips to provide a quantitative, biomechanically grounded evaluation.
