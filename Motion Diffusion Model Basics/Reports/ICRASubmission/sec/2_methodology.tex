\subsection{Data Processing Pipeline}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} (top), we adopt the motion capture dataset of Van Criekinge et al.~\cite{vancriekinge2023dataset} (ages 21--86) and convert clinical marker recordings into HumanML3D-format skeletal graphs~\cite{guo2022generating} via SMPL body-model fitting~\cite{mahmood2019amass} and feature extraction.

\subsection{Age Signature Detecting Network}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} (bottom left), an Age Representation Learner built on a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)~\cite{yan2018stgcn} is trained to classify motion clips into three age groups (Young, Mid, Elderly) and regress biological age.
The penultimate layer of the network acts as a kinematic latent vector that encapsulates the biomechanical signatures of aging (e.g., velocity variance, joint motion range), denoted as $\mathbf{z}_{age}$.

\subsection{Generative Network with LoRA}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} (bottom right), we use a Conditional Diffusion Model~\cite{tevet2023human, chen2022mld} that denoises a latent motion representation $\mathbf{x}_T$ conditioned on both text and age.
At training time, we extract $\mathbf{z}_\text{age}$ from real motion clips using the frozen ST-GCN encoder, and inject this embedding into the diffusion model via cross-attention.
A small MLP $g(a)$ is jointly trained to map a scalar age value to the same latent space, supervised by the ST-GCN-derived targets.
At inference, $g(a)$ alone converts a desired age into $\mathbf{z}_\text{age}$, enabling continuous age-controlled generation without requiring input motion.
