Our framework consists of four primary components: a robust data processing pipeline for clinical marker conversion, a graph-based representation learner for extracting age signatures, a theoretical generative architecture, and a practical LoRA-based prototype.

\subsection{Data Processing Pipeline}
We adopt the motion capture dataset of Van Criekinge et al.~\cite{vancriekinge2023dataset} (ages 21--86) and convert clinical marker recordings into HumanML3D-format skeletal graphs~\cite{guo2022generating} via SMPL body-model fitting~\cite{mahmood2019amass} and feature extraction.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:artifact}, naive mapping causes mesh inflation (right), while standard priors induce crouch artifacts (left).
Our multi-stage optimization pipeline, adapted from VPoser~\cite{pavlakos2019expressivebodycapture3d}, successfully addresses these issues to recover a natural posture (center).
Furthermore, our pipeline resolves common defects in clinical datasets, such as axis misalignment, marker mislabeling, and foot contact issues, ensuring high data quality.

\subsection{Age Signature Detecting Network}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} (bottom left), an Age Representation Learner built on an ST-GCN~\cite{yan2018stgcn} is fine-tuned to classify motion clips into three age groups (Young, Mid, Elderly) and regress biological age.
Once the network can successfully predict age, the penultimate layer encapsulates the biomechanical signatures of aging (e.g., joint motion range).
We extract this vector to serve as the age latent embedding, denoted as $\mathbf{z}_{age}$.

\subsection{Generative Network (Proposed)}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} (bottom right), we use a Conditional Diffusion Model~\cite{tevet2023human, chen2022mld} that denoises a latent motion representation $\mathbf{x}_T$ conditioned on both text and age.
At training time, we extract $\mathbf{z}_\text{age}$ from real motion clips using the frozen ST-GCN encoder, and inject this embedding into the diffusion model via cross-attention.
A small MLP $g(a)$ is jointly trained to map a scalar age value to the same latent space, supervised by the ST-GCN-derived targets.
At inference, $g(a)$ alone converts a desired age into $\mathbf{z}_\text{age}$, enabling continuous age-controlled generation without requiring input motion.

\subsection{LoRA-based Prototype Network}
Due to limitations in time and computational resources, we first implement a lightweight prototype using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model.
Instead of training the full proposed generative architecture from scratch to condition on latent embedding $\mathbf{z}_{age}$, we first use a LoRA adapter to allow models to accept discrete age group (young, middle-age, elderly) token and generate. This is an efficient validation of the feasibility of our framework with tiny training cost, serving as our first-step to continuous age condition.

\subsection{Gait-Analysis Metrics for Evaluation}
In order to validate that the aging signal is preserved in the processed dataset and successfully reproduced in the generated motion clips, we adapted several standard, training-free gait-analysis metrics documented in clinical literature~\cite{bailey2023smartwatch, cordeiro2025constraint, galasso2024development}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatiotemporal Parameters:} We compute average walking speed, stride length, and cadence by detecting heel-strike events based on the zero-velocity crossings of the ankle joints. 
    \item \textbf{Gait Variability:} We calculate the Coefficient of Variation (CV) for stride length and stride time across multiple steps. 
    \item \textbf{Joint Range of Motion (RoM):} We measure the peak flexion and extension angles of the hip and knee joints during the gait cycle. 
\end{itemize}
These metrics are computed deterministically from sample clips within our dataset and from motion generated by our LoRA-based prototype, providing a quantitative, biomechanically grounded evaluation of our framework.
