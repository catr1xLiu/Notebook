> [!example] Question 1102 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Intern Interview

Describe a stack and a queue to someone who does not have a background in data structures.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1103)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A **stack** and a **queue** are simple ways to organize items so you can add and remove them in a controlled order.
>
> ## Stack
>
> A **stack** works like a pile of plates.
>
> - You **add** a plate to the **top**
> - You **remove** a plate from the **top**
>
> This means the **last** item you put in is the **first** one you take out.
>
> This is called:
>
> $$\text{LIFO} = \text{Last In, First Out}$$
>
> ### Example
> Suppose you put in:
>
> 1. Book A
> 2. Book B
> 3. Book C
>
> The stack looks like this, with Book C on top.
>
> If you remove one item, you get:
>
> $$\text{Book C}$$
>
> Then next:
>
> $$\text{Book B}$$
>
> Then:
>
> $$\text{Book A}$$
>
> ### Real-life analogy
> A stack is like:
>
> - a stack of trays
> - a pile of pancakes
> - the browser “back” button history
>
> ### Common actions
> - **Push**: add an item to the top
> - **Pop**: remove the top item
> - **Peek**: look at the top item without removing it
>
> ---
>
> ## Queue
>
> A **queue** works like a line of people waiting.
>
> - You **add** new people at the **back**
> - You **remove** people from the **front**
>
> This means the **first** item you put in is the **first** one you take out.
>
> This is called:
>
> $$\text{FIFO} = \text{First In, First Out}$$
>
> ### Example
> Suppose three people enter a line:
>
> 1. Alice
> 2. Ben
> 3. Carla
>
> If one person is served and leaves, it is:
>
> $$\text{Alice}$$
>
> Then next:
>
> $$\text{Ben}$$
>
> Then:
>
> $$\text{Carla}$$
>
> ### Real-life analogy
> A queue is like:
>
> - people waiting in line at a store
> - cars at a drive-through
> - tasks waiting to be printed by a printer
>
> ### Common actions
> - **Enqueue**: add an item to the back
> - **Dequeue**: remove an item from the front
> - **Front/Peek**: look at the next item to be removed
>
> ---
>
> ## Main difference
>
> The key difference is the order in which items come out:
>
> - **Stack**: the newest item comes out first  
>   $$\text{LIFO}$$
> - **Queue**: the oldest item comes out first  
>   $$\text{FIFO}$$
>
> ## Simple summary
>
> - Use a **stack** when you want to deal with the most recent item first.
> - Use a **queue** when you want to deal with items in the order they arrived.
>
> So:
>
> $$\text{Stack} \rightarrow \text{top in, top out}$$
>
> $$\text{Queue} \rightarrow \text{back in, front out}$$
