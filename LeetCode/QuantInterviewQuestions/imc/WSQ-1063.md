> [!example] Question 1063 · IMC
> ## Trading Intern Interview

There is a 20 cm long table. You start 3 cm from the left edge. Each move, you go either 10 cm to the left or to the right with equal probability. If you reach either edge of the table, you fall off immediately. How many moves do you expect to make before falling off?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1064)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E(x)$ denote the expected number of moves until falling when starting at position $x$ centimeters from the left edge.
>
> The table occupies the interval from $0$ to $20$. Falling occurs as soon as a move takes you past an edge. Since each move is $\pm 10$ cm, the only positions that can occur starting from $3$ cm are
>
> $$
> 3,\ 13.
> $$
>
> Indeed:
>
> - From $3$, a move left goes to $-7$, so you fall immediately.
> - From $3$, a move right goes to $13$.
> - From $13$, a move right goes to $23$, so you fall immediately.
> - From $13$, a move left goes back to $3$.
>
> Thus we only need the expectations $E(3)$ and $E(13)$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the equations
>
> From position $3$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you fall on the next move;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you move to $13$.
>
> So
>
> $$
> E(3)=1+\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 E(13)
> =1+\frac12 E(13).
> $$
>
> From position $13$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you move to $3$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you fall.
>
> So
>
> $$
> E(13)=1+\frac12 E(3)+\frac12\cdot 0
> =1+\frac12 E(3).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> Substitute the second equation into the first:
>
> $$
> E(3)=1+\frac12\left(1+\frac12 E(3)\right).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> E(3)=1+\frac12+\frac14 E(3),
> $$
>
> $$
> E(3)-\frac14 E(3)=\frac32,
> $$
>
> $$
> \frac34 E(3)=\frac32.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E(3)=\frac{3}{2}\cdot \frac{4}{3}=2.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of moves before falling is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2}.
> $$
