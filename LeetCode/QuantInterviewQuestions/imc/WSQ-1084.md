> [!example] Question 1084 · IMC
> ## Quant Trading Intern Interview

Given a telephone keypad display with the numbers arranged as:
1 2 3
4 5 6
7 8 9
You press a random number, then move to a random adjacent number (no diagonals), then move again to a random adjacent number (again, no diagonals). You record the three numbers in order to form a three-digit number (e.g., 569). What is the probability that the resulting number is divisible by 2?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1085)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Arrange the digits on the keypad as a graph, where each digit is connected to its horizontal or vertical neighbors:
>
> - $1$ adjacent to $2,4$
> - $2$ adjacent to $1,3,5$
> - $3$ adjacent to $2,6$
> - $4$ adjacent to $1,5,7$
> - $5$ adjacent to $2,4,6,8$
> - $6$ adjacent to $3,5,9$
> - $7$ adjacent to $4,8$
> - $8$ adjacent to $5,7,9$
> - $9$ adjacent to $6,8$
>
> A $3$-digit number is formed by:
>
> 1. choosing the first digit uniformly from $\{1,\dots,9\}$,
> 2. moving to a uniformly chosen adjacent digit for the second digit,
> 3. moving again to a uniformly chosen adjacent digit for the third digit.
>
> To determine whether the resulting number is divisible by $2$, only the last digit matters. Thus we need the probability that the third digit is even, i.e. is one of $\{2,4,6,8\}$.
>
> Let $p_n(v)$ be the probability of being at digit $v$ after $n$ digits have been written. Initially,
>
> $$
> p_1(v)=\frac{1}{9} \qquad \text{for all } v.
> $$
>
> Now compute the distribution after one move.
>
> The degrees of the vertices are:
>
> - corners $1,3,7,9$: degree $2$,
> - edges $2,4,6,8$: degree $3$,
> - center $5$: degree $4$.
>
> Using transition probabilities:
>
> $$
> p_2(1)=\frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{2}{27}
> $$
>
> since $1$ can be reached from $2$ and $4$.
>
> Similarly,
>
> $$
> p_2(3)=p_2(7)=p_2(9)=\frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> For an edge digit such as $2$,
>
> $$
> p_2(2)=\frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)
> =\frac{5}{36}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> p_2(2)=p_2(4)=p_2(6)=p_2(8)=\frac{5}{36}.
> $$
>
> For the center,
>
> $$
> p_2(5)=\frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{4}{27}.
> $$
>
> Now compute the probability that the third digit is even. Each even digit receives probability from its neighbors.
>
> For digit $2$:
>
> $$
> p_3(2)=\frac{p_2(1)}{2}+\frac{p_2(3)}{2}+\frac{p_2(5)}{4}
> =\frac{1}{27}+\frac{1}{27}+\frac{1}{27}
> =\frac{1}{9}.
> $$
>
> By symmetry,
>
> $$
> p_3(4)=p_3(6)=p_3(8)=\frac{1}{9}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{third digit is even})
> = p_3(2)+p_3(4)+p_3(6)+p_3(8)
> =4\cdot \frac{1}{9}
> =\frac{4}{9}.
> $$
>
> Since a number is divisible by $2$ exactly when its last digit is even, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{9}}.
> $$
