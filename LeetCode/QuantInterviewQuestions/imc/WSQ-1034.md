> [!example] Question 1034 · IMC
> ## Trader Interview

If you remove the two squares that are diagonally opposite each other from a chessboard, can you use 2x1 domino tiles to exactly cover the remaining squares?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1035)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the standard $8 \times 8$ chessboard with its usual alternating black-and-white coloring. A domino of size $2 \times 1$ always covers exactly two adjacent squares, and any two adjacent squares on a chessboard have opposite colors. Therefore, each domino covers:
>
> $$
> 1 \text{ black square} + 1 \text{ white square}.
> $$
>
> Now remove the two diagonally opposite corner squares. These two corners are the same color. On a standard chessboard, all four corners are white, so removing two opposite corners removes:
>
> $$
> 2 \text{ white squares}.
> $$
>
> Originally, the board has:
>
> $$
> 32 \text{ black squares} \quad \text{and} \quad 32 \text{ white squares}.
> $$
>
> After removing the two opposite white corners, the remaining board has:
>
> $$
> 32 \text{ black squares} \quad \text{and} \quad 30 \text{ white squares}.
> $$
>
> To tile the board completely with dominoes, each domino would cover one black and one white square, so the total number of black and white squares covered would have to be equal. But the remaining board does not have equal numbers of black and white squares.
>
> Hence, it is impossible to cover the remaining squares with $2 \times 1$ dominoes.
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{No, the remaining squares cannot be covered.}}
> $$
