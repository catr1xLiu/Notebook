> [!example] Question 1096 · IMC
> ## Graduate Trader Program Interview

Five people are sitting at a circular table. What is the probability that they are seated in the order of their birthdays?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1097)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Treat the birthdays as inducing a strict cyclic order among the $5$ people (assuming, for simplicity, that no two share a birthday; with random birthdays this is the intended interpretation).
>
> Around a circular table, the total number of distinct seatings of $5$ people is
>
> $$
> (5-1)! = 4! = 24,
> $$
>
> since rotations are considered the same.
>
> Now ask: in how many of these circular arrangements do the people appear in birthday order around the table?
>
> Fix the cyclic birthday order, say from earliest birthday to latest. Around a circle, this order can occur in exactly two ways:
>
> 1. clockwise in birthday order,
> 2. counterclockwise in birthday order.
>
> Thus the number of favorable circular seatings is
>
> $$
> 2.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{24} = \frac{1}{12}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{12}}.
> $$
