> [!example] Question 1069 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Explain the concepts of a queue and a stack to a layperson.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1070)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A simple way to explain them to a layperson is to use everyday examples.
>
> ## Queue
>
> A **queue** works like a line at a coffee shop.
>
> - The **first person** to get in line is the **first person** served.
> - New people join at the **back**.
> - People leave from the **front**.
>
> This is called **First In, First Out**, or $FIFO$.
>
> ### Everyday example
> If Alice, Bob, and Carol line up in that order, then:
> - Alice is served first,
> - then Bob,
> - then Carol.
>
> So a queue is fair and keeps things in order of arrival.
>
> ### Where it is used
> - People waiting in line
> - Cars at a toll booth
> - Print jobs waiting for a printer
> - Customer service calls waiting to be answered
>
> ---
>
> ## Stack
>
> A **stack** works like a stack of plates.
>
> - You add a new plate to the **top**.
> - When you take one away, you also take from the **top**.
>
> This is called **Last In, First Out**, or $LIFO$.
>
> ### Everyday example
> If you place plates in this order:
> - first plate $A$,
> - then plate $B$,
> - then plate $C$,
>
> the first plate you remove will be $C$.
>
> So the **last item added** is the **first one removed**.
>
> ### Where it is used
> - A stack of plates or books
> - The “undo” feature in apps
> - Browser history when pressing “back”
> - Putting boxes on top of each other
>
> ---
>
> ## Key difference
>
> - **Queue:** first come, first served $\rightarrow FIFO$
> - **Stack:** last come, first served $\rightarrow LIFO$
>
> ---
>
> ## Very short layman explanation
>
> A good interview-friendly version is:
>
> A **queue** is like standing in line for food: the first person in line gets served first.  
> A **stack** is like a pile of plates: the last plate you put on top is the first one you take off.
>
> That comparison is usually the clearest for a non-technical person.
