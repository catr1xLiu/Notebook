> [!example] Question 1049 · IMC
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have 27 horses. You can race up to 5 horses at a time. What is the minimum number of races required to determine the top 3 fastest horses?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1050)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine the top $3$ fastest horses among $27$ horses when only $5$ can race at a time, the goal is to minimize the number of races while guaranteeing the correct answer, using only finishing order within each race.
>
> ## Step 1: Group most of the horses, but hold two back
>
> Divide $25$ of the $27$ horses into five groups of five, and keep two horses, $F_1$ and $F_2$, out of the initial groups entirely:
>
> - Group $A$: $A_1,\dots,A_5$
> - Group $B$: $B_1,\dots,B_5$
> - Group $C$: $C_1,\dots,C_5$
> - Group $D$: $D_1,\dots,D_5$
> - Group $E$: $E_1,\dots,E_5$
>
> Race each group once. This takes races $1$–$5$, after which the order within each group is known. $F_1$ and $F_2$ have not raced at all yet.
>
> ## Step 2: Race the five group winners
>
> Race $6$ pits the winners against each other:
>
> $$
> A_1,\; B_1,\; C_1,\; D_1,\; E_1.
> $$
>
> Relabel the groups so the result is
>
> $$
> A_1 < B_1 < C_1 < D_1 < E_1.
> $$
>
> Because $A_1$ beat every other group winner, and every other horse in groups $B$–$E$ is slower than its own group's winner, $A_1$ is the fastest of all $25$ horses in groups $A$–$E$.
>
> This also lets us discard most of the field:
>
> - Horses in groups $D$ and $E$ are eliminated, since $A_1, B_1, C_1$ are all already ahead of them.
> - In group $C$, only $C_1$ remains relevant.
> - In group $B$, only $B_1$ and $B_2$ remain relevant.
> - In group $A$, besides $A_1$, only $A_2$ and $A_3$ remain relevant.
>
> So the only horses that could still be $2$nd or $3$rd fastest among groups $A$–$E$ are
>
> $$
> A_2,\; A_3,\; B_1,\; B_2,\; C_1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Determine 2nd and 3rd place among the 25
>
> Race $7$ is run among exactly these five horses:
>
> $$
> A_2,\; A_3,\; B_1,\; B_2,\; C_1.
> $$
>
> If $X$ and $Y$ denote the top two finishers of this race, then $A_1 < X < Y$ are precisely the three fastest horses among the $25$ horses in groups $A$ through $E$.
>
> ## Step 4: Bring the two held-back horses into contention
>
> The horses $F_1$ and $F_2$ were never compared against any of the others, so either might belong in the true top $3$ overall. Race $8$ settles this:
>
> $$
> A_1,\; X,\; Y,\; F_1,\; F_2.
> $$
>
> Every horse from groups $A$–$E$ other than $A_1, X, Y$ is already known to trail $Y$, so none of them can be in the overall top $3$. Consequently, the top $3$ finishers of race $8$ are exactly the top $3$ fastest horses among all $27$.
>
> ## Why seven races are not enough
>
> Even after races $1$ through $7$, the horses $F_1$ and $F_2$ have not been raced against anyone outside their own pair, so it is unknown whether either belongs in the top $3$. Determining this requires at least one more race, so $7$ races cannot guarantee a correct answer.
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{8}
> $$
