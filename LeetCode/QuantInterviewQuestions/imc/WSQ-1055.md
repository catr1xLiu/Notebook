> [!example] Question 1055 · IMC
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

In a beer pong game with a 4x4 arrangement of cups, each time you land a ball in a cup, both the row and the column containing that cup are removed. Assuming each throw lands in any remaining cup with equal probability, what is the probability that you win the game in the minimum possible number of throws?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1056)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Step 1: Determine the minimum number of throws
>
> There are $4$ rows and $4$ columns. Each successful throw removes one remaining row and one remaining column. Therefore, after one throw, the remaining cups lie in a $3\times 3$ arrangement; after two throws, in a $2\times 2$ arrangement; after three throws, in a $1\times 1$ arrangement; and after four throws, no cups remain.
>
> Hence the minimum possible number of throws to clear the board is $4$.
>
> ## Step 2: Every throw always removes a new row and a new column
>
> At any point in the game, a "remaining" cup necessarily sits in a row that has not yet been removed and a column that has not yet been removed — if either had already been removed, that cup would already be gone. So no matter which remaining cup a throw lands in, it always eliminates exactly one row and one column that were still present.
>
> This means that after $k$ throws, exactly $k$ rows and $k$ columns have been removed, leaving a $(4-k)\times(4-k)$ arrangement of cups — regardless of which specific cups were hit along the way. This holds for **every** possible sequence of throws, not just special ones.
>
> ## Step 3: The game always finishes in exactly 4 throws
>
> Applying Step 2 repeatedly: the grid shrinks from $4\times 4$ to $3\times 3$ to $2\times 2$ to $1\times 1$ to empty, one row and one column at a time, on every single throw. Since this shrinking is forced by the rules of the game rather than depending on which cups happen to be hit, the board is always completely cleared after exactly $4$ throws, no matter how the game plays out.
>
> To see this concretely: on the first throw there are $16$ possible cups, on the second there are always $9$ remaining, on the third always $4$ remaining, and on the fourth exactly $1$ remaining — every one of these
>
> $$
> 16\cdot 9\cdot 4\cdot 1 = 576
> $$
>
> equally likely throw sequences clears the board in exactly $4$ throws.
>
> ## Step 4: Compute the probability
>
> Since every possible sequence of throws finishes the game in exactly $4$ throws, and $4$ is the minimum possible number of throws, winning in the minimum number of throws is not a special event to be counted separately — it is the only possible outcome.
>
> $$
> P(\text{win in minimum number of throws}) = \frac{576}{576} = 1.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}
> $$
