> [!example] Question 1054 · IMC
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

When rolling a fair six-sided die repeatedly, what is the probability that, before rolling any odd number, you have rolled each even number (2, 4, and 6) at least once?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1055)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the die be rolled repeatedly until the first odd number appears. We want the probability that, before this first odd roll, all three even faces $2,4,6$ have appeared at least once.
>
> ## Step 1: Reformulate the process
>
> Each roll has:
>
> - probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of being odd, which stops the process,
> - probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of being even, in which case the even result is equally likely to be $2,4,$ or $6$.
>
> Thus, before the first odd appears, we obtain a sequence of even rolls, followed by an odd roll. We need the probability that this even sequence contains all of $2,4,6$.
>
> ## Step 2: Condition on the number of even rolls before the first odd
>
> Let $N$ be the number of even rolls before the first odd. Then
>
> $$
> P(N=n)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots
> $$
>
> Given $N=n$, the $n$ even rolls are independent and uniform over $\{2,4,6\}$. Let $a_n$ be the probability that all three even numbers appear in $n$ such rolls. The desired probability is
>
> $$
> P=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1} a_n.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compute $a_n$
>
> If $n<3$, then $a_n=0$. For $n\ge 3$, by inclusion-exclusion the number of sequences of length $n$ over $\{2,4,6\}$ containing all three symbols is $3^n-3\cdot 2^n+3$, so
>
> $$
> a_n=1-3\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n+3\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P=\sum_{n=3}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1}\left[1-3\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n+3\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n\right].
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Evaluate the series
>
> Split into three sums.
>
> ### First sum
>
> $$
> \sum_{n=3}^\infty \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1}=\sum_{m=4}^\infty\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^m=\frac{(1/2)^4}{1-(1/2)}=\frac{1}{8}.
> $$
>
> ### Second sum
>
> Since $\left(\frac12\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac23\right)^n=\frac12\left(\frac13\right)^n$,
>
> $$
> 3\sum_{n=3}^\infty\left(\frac12\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac23\right)^n=\frac32\sum_{n=3}^\infty\left(\frac13\right)^n=\frac32\cdot\frac{(1/3)^3}{1-(1/3)}=\frac{1}{12}.
> $$
>
> ### Third sum
>
> Since $\left(\frac12\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac13\right)^n=\frac12\left(\frac16\right)^n$,
>
> $$
> 3\sum_{n=3}^\infty\left(\frac12\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac13\right)^n=\frac32\sum_{n=3}^\infty\left(\frac16\right)^n=\frac32\cdot\frac{(1/6)^3}{1-(1/6)}=\frac32\cdot\frac{1/216}{5/6}=\frac32\cdot\frac{1}{180}=\frac{1}{120}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P=\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{120}.
> $$
>
> Using the common denominator $120$,
>
> $$
> P=\frac{15}{120}-\frac{10}{120}+\frac{1}{120}=\frac{6}{120}=\frac{1}{20}.
> $$
>
> ## Remark: a direct combinatorial check
>
> For iid uniform rolls, the order in which the six faces first appear is a uniformly random permutation of $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. The event we want is exactly that $2,4,6$ occupy the first three positions of this permutation, in any order. Building that ordering face by face,
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{6}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{20},
> $$
>
> since among the first three faces to appear, each must be one of the three even numbers, drawn from a shrinking pool of faces not yet seen. This matches the series computation.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{20}}
> $$
