> [!example] Question 1010 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Is the total number of distinct unsigned 32-bit integers different from the total number of signed 32-bit integers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1011)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> No. They are the same.
>
> A $32$-bit pattern has $2^{32}$ distinct possible bit strings. Whether those bit strings are interpreted as **unsigned** or **signed** changes only the mapping from bit patterns to numerical values, not the number of patterns.
>
> - **Unsigned $32$-bit integers:** values range from
>   $$
>   0 \text{ to } 2^{32}-1
>   $$
>   so there are
>   $$
>   2^{32}
>   $$
>   distinct values.
>
> - **Signed $32$-bit integers** (using the usual two’s-complement representation): values range from
>   $$
>   -2^{31} \text{ to } 2^{31}-1
>   $$
>   and the count is
>   $$
>   2^{31} + 2^{31} = 2^{32}.
>   $$
>
> Therefore, the total number of distinct unsigned $32$-bit integers is **not different** from the total number of signed $32$-bit integers; both are
>
> $$
> 2^{32} = 4,\!294,\!967,\!296.
> $$
>
> What differs is the **range of values**, not the **number of representable values**.
