> [!example] Question 1104 · IMC
> ## Graduate Quantitative Trader Interview

A robot is placed on a table that is 20 cm wide. The robot starts at a point 3 cm away from one of the edges. Each step, it moves either 10 cm to the left or 10 cm to the right with equal probability. What is the expected number of steps until the robot falls off the table?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1105)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the table occupy the interval from $0$ to $20$ (in cm), and let the robot start at position $3$.
>
> At each step, the robot moves either $10$ cm to the left or $10$ cm to the right, each with probability $\tfrac12$. The robot falls as soon as its position is outside the interval $[0,20]$.
>
> We seek the expected number of steps until falling.
>
> ## Step 1: Determine the possible positions before falling
>
> Starting from $3$:
>
> - If the robot moves left first, it goes to $3-10=-7$, so it falls immediately.
> - If the robot moves right first, it goes to $3+10=13$, which is still on the table.
>
> From position $13$:
>
> - Moving right gives $13+10=23$, so it falls.
> - Moving left gives $13-10=3$, returning to the start.
>
> Thus, before falling, the only possible on-table positions are $3$ and $13$.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up expected-value equations
>
> Let
>
> - $E_3$ be the expected number of steps until falling starting from position $3$,
> - $E_{13}$ be the expected number of steps until falling starting from position $13$.
>
> From position $3$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the robot falls in $1$ step;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, it moves to $13$, taking $1$ step, and then needs an additional expected $E_{13}$ steps.
>
> So
> $$
> E_3=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\cdot(1+E_{13}).
> $$
>
> From position $13$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, it falls in $1$ step;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, it returns to $3$, taking $1$ step, and then needs an additional expected $E_3$ steps.
>
> So
> $$
> E_{13}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\cdot(1+E_3).
> $$
>
> Simplifying both,
> $$
> E_3=1+\frac12E_{13},
> $$
> $$
> E_{13}=1+\frac12E_3.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> Substitute the second equation into the first:
> $$
> E_3=1+\frac12\left(1+\frac12E_3\right).
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_3=1+\frac12+\frac14E_3
> =\frac32+\frac14E_3.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E_3-\frac14E_3=\frac32,
> $$
> $$
> \frac34E_3=\frac32,
> $$
> and therefore
> $$
> E_3=\frac32\cdot\frac43=2.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of steps before the robot falls is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2}.
> $$
>
> So, on average, the robot falls after $2$ steps.
