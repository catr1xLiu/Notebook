> [!example] Question 1039 · IMC
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

A robot is 3 cm from the left edge and 17 cm from the right edge of a table. The robot moves with equal probability 10 cm to the left or to the right in each step. What is the expected number of steps before the robot falls off the table?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1040)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the robot’s position be measured by its distance from the left edge of the table.
>
> Since the robot is $3\text{ cm}$ from the left edge and $17\text{ cm}$ from the right edge, the table length is
>
> $$
> 3+17=20\text{ cm}.
> $$
>
> The robot moves $\pm 10\text{ cm}$ each step with equal probability. Thus the only reachable positions on the table are
>
> $$
> 3,\ 13 \text{ cm from the left edge.}
> $$
>
> From either of these positions, one move may cause the robot to fall off.
>
> Define:
>
> - $E_3$ = expected number of steps until falling off, starting $3\text{ cm}$ from the left edge,
> - $E_{13}$ = expected number of steps until falling off, starting $13\text{ cm}$ from the left edge.
>
> We form equations for these expectations.
>
> ### From position $3$
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, the robot moves left to $-7$, which is off the table, so it falls in $1$ step.
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, it moves right to $13$, so it uses $1$ step and then has expected remaining time $E_{13}$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E_3=\frac12(1)+\frac12(1+E_{13})
> =1+\frac12 E_{13}.
> $$
>
> ### From position $13$
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, the robot moves left to $3$, so it uses $1$ step and then has expected remaining time $E_3$.
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, it moves right to $23$, which is off the table, so it falls in $1$ step.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E_{13}=\frac12(1+E_3)+\frac12(1)
> =1+\frac12 E_3.
> $$
>
> Now solve the system:
>
> $$
> E_3=1+\frac12 E_{13},
> $$
>
> $$
> E_{13}=1+\frac12 E_3.
> $$
>
> Substitute the second into the first:
>
> $$
> E_3=1+\frac12\left(1+\frac12 E_3\right)
> =1+\frac12+\frac14 E_3
> =\frac32+\frac14 E_3.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E_3-\frac14 E_3=\frac32
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> \frac34 E_3=\frac32
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_3=2.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the average number of steps for the robot to fall off the table is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2}.
> $$
