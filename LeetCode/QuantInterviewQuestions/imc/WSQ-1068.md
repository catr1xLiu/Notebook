> [!example] Question 1068 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Interview

How would you explain the difference between a stack and a queue to a non-technical person?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1069)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Stack vs. Queue, explained simply
>
> A good non-technical way to explain the difference is to use everyday examples.
>
> A **stack** works like a **pile of plates**.  
> You add a plate to the top, and when you take one away, you also take the **top** plate first.  
> So the **last** plate you put on is the **first** one you remove.
>
> This is called:
>
> $$\text{LIFO} = \text{Last In, First Out}$$
>
> A **queue** works like a **line of people** waiting for coffee.  
> New people join at the **back** of the line, and the person at the **front** is served first.  
> So the **first** person to arrive is the **first** one to leave.
>
> This is called:
>
> $$\text{FIFO} = \text{First In, First Out}$$
>
> ## Simple comparison
>
> - **Stack:** like stacking trays, books, or plates  
>   - Add to the top  
>   - Remove from the top  
>   - Last added, first removed
>
> - **Queue:** like a checkout line or ticket line  
>   - Add to the back  
>   - Remove from the front  
>   - First added, first removed
>
> ## One-sentence distinction
>
> The main difference is:
>
> - A **stack** reverses the order of things.
> - A **queue** preserves the order of arrival.
>
> ## Short interview-style answer
>
> “A **stack** is like a stack of plates: the last one placed on top is the first one you take off. A **queue** is like a line at a store: the first person to get in line is the first person served. So a stack is **last in, first out**, while a queue is **first in, first out**.”
>
> ## If a slightly more practical explanation is needed
>
> You could add:
>
> - A **stack** is useful when you want to **undo** the most recent action first.
> - A **queue** is useful when you want to handle things in the **same order they arrived**.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A **stack** is like a **pile of plates**: the last item added is the first one removed, which is $$\text{LIFO}$$.  
> A **queue** is like a **line of people**: the first item added is the first one removed, which is $$\text{FIFO}$$.  
> So the key difference is whether you remove the **most recent** item first or the **oldest** item first.
