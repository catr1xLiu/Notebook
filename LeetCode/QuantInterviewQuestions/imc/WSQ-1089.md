> [!example] Question 1089 · IMC
> ## Quant Trader Interview

What is the expected number of coin flips required for two players, who take turns flipping, to get two tails in a row?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1090)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Two players alternate flipping a fair coin, one flip per turn, and the game ends as soon as two tails occur in a row. Since the coin is fair and each flip is independent of who performs it, alternating the flips between the two players has no effect on the statistics of the sequence of heads and tails: only the raw sequence of outcomes matters for deciding when two consecutive tails first appear. The problem is therefore equivalent to the classical question: what is the expected number of flips of a fair coin until two consecutive tails appear?
>
> Let $E$ denote the expected total number of flips until the game ends.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the equations
>
> Track the state of the flip sequence after each flip:
>
> - State $S_0$: either no flips have occurred yet, or the most recent flip was heads.
> - State $S_T$: the most recent flip was tails (so a second consecutive tail would end the game).
>
> Let $E_0$ and $E_T$ denote the expected number of additional flips needed to finish, starting from states $S_0$ and $S_T$ respectively. The game begins in state $S_0$, so the desired answer is $E_0$.
>
> From state $S_0$, one flip occurs:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ it is heads, and the state remains $S_0$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ it is tails, and the state moves to $S_T$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0 + \frac12 E_T.
> $$
>
> From state $S_T$, one flip occurs:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ it is heads, breaking the run, and the state returns to $S_0$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ it is tails, giving two tails in a row, and the game ends.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_T = 1 + \frac12 E_0 + \frac12 \cdot 0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> Substitute the expression for $E_T$ into the equation for $E_0$:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0 + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right)
> = 1 + \frac12 E_0 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_0 - \frac34 E_0 = \frac32,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \frac14 E_0 = \frac32,
> $$
> which gives
> $$
> E_0 = 6.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected total number of flips until two tails occur in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6}.
> $$
>
> This matches the general formula for the expected waiting time until a run of $k$ consecutive tails first appears in fair-coin flips, namely $2^{k+1}-2$; for $k=2$ this gives $2^3 - 2 = 6$.
