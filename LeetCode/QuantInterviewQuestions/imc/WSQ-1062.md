> [!example] Question 1062 · IMC
> ## Trading Intern Interview

You are given two dice: one numbered 1 to 6 and the other numbered 1 to 4. You choose one at random and roll it twice. The first roll is a 2. What is the expected value of the second roll?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1063)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D_6$ denote the $6$-sided die and $D_4$ the $4$-sided die. Each die is chosen with probability $\tfrac12$, then rolled twice. We are given that the first roll is $2$, and we want
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll} \mid \text{first roll}=2].
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Update the probability of which die was chosen
>
> Using Bayes' rule,
>
> $$
> P(D_6 \mid \text{first}=2)
> =
> \frac{P(\text{first}=2 \mid D_6)P(D_6)}{P(\text{first}=2)}.
> $$
>
> Now,
>
> $$
> P(\text{first}=2 \mid D_6)=\frac16,
> \qquad
> P(\text{first}=2 \mid D_4)=\frac14,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(\text{first}=2)
> =
> \frac12\cdot \frac16+\frac12\cdot \frac14
> =
> \frac1{12}+\frac18
> =
> \frac5{24}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(D_6 \mid \text{first}=2)
> =
> \frac{\frac16\cdot \frac12}{\frac5{24}}
> =
> \frac{1/12}{5/24}
> =
> \frac25.
> $$
>
> Similarly,
>
> $$
> P(D_4 \mid \text{first}=2)=1-\frac25=\frac35.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expected second roll given each die
>
> If the chosen die is $D_6$, then
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll} \mid D_6]=\frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6}=\frac72.
> $$
>
> If the chosen die is $D_4$, then
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll} \mid D_4]=\frac{1+2+3+4}{4}=\frac52.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Average using the posterior probabilities
>
> Since the second roll is independent of the first roll once the die is fixed,
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll} \mid \text{first}=2]
> =
> P(D_6 \mid \text{first}=2)\cdot \frac72
> +
> P(D_4 \mid \text{first}=2)\cdot \frac52.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll} \mid \text{first}=2]
> =
> \frac25\cdot \frac72+\frac35\cdot \frac52
> =
> \frac75+\frac{15}{10}
> =
> \frac75+\frac32
> =
> \frac{14}{10}+\frac{15}{10}
> =
> \frac{29}{10}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected value of the second roll is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{29}{10}}.
> $$
