> [!example] Question 1106 · IMC
> ## Summer Intern Interview

You have one coin and your opponent has two. You flip another coin; if it lands on tails, you win one of your opponent's coins. Otherwise, your opponent wins one of your coins. The winner is the first to hold all three coins. What is the probability that you win?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1107)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p_k$ denote the probability that you eventually win when you currently hold $k$ coins.
>
> There are only three possible states for your number of coins:
>
> - $k=0$: you have lost, so $p_0=0$,
> - $k=3$: you have won, so $p_3=1$,
> - $k=1,2$: transient states.
>
> Initially, you have one coin, so we seek $p_1$.
>
> Each flip is fair:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you gain one coin,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, you lose one coin.
>
> Thus the recursion is
>
> $$
> p_1=\frac12 p_0+\frac12 p_2,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> p_2=\frac12 p_1+\frac12 p_3.
> $$
>
> Using $p_0=0$ and $p_3=1$, these become
>
> $$
> p_1=\frac12 p_2,
> $$
>
> $$
> p_2=\frac12 p_1+\frac12.
> $$
>
> Substitute $p_2=2p_1$ into the second equation:
>
> $$
> 2p_1=\frac12 p_1+\frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \frac32 p_1=\frac12,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> p_1=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that you eventually win all three coins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}.
> $$
