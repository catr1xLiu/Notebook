> [!example] Question 1086 · IMC
> ## Quant Trading Intern Interview

How many integers less than 100000 contain two consecutive '1's in their decimal representation?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1087)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Count the integers from $0$ to $100000$ whose decimal representation contains the substring $11$.
>
> It is easier to count the complement: numbers with **no** two consecutive $1$'s.
>
> ## 1. Count strings of fixed length with no consecutive $1$'s
>
> Let $a_n$ be the number of $n$-digit strings (allowing leading zeros) over $\{0,1,2,\dots,9\}$ that contain no consecutive $1$'s.
>
> To form such a string of length $n$:
>
> - if the first digit is not $1$, there are $9$ choices, followed by any valid string of length $n-1$;
> - if the first digit is $1$, then the second digit cannot be $1$, so there are $9$ choices for the second digit, followed by any valid string of length $n-2$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> a_n = 9a_{n-1} + 9a_{n-2}.
> $$
>
> Initial values:
> $$
> a_0 = 1,\qquad a_1 = 10.
> $$
>
> Now compute up to $n=5$:
>
> $$
> a_2 = 9a_1 + 9a_0 = 9\cdot 10 + 9\cdot 1 = 99,
> $$
>
> $$
> a_3 = 9a_2 + 9a_1 = 9\cdot 99 + 9\cdot 10 = 981,
> $$
>
> $$
> a_4 = 9a_3 + 9a_2 = 9\cdot 981 + 9\cdot 99 = 9720,
> $$
>
> $$
> a_5 = 9a_4 + 9a_3 = 9\cdot 9720 + 9\cdot 981 = 96309.
> $$
>
> So among the $100000$ integers from $0$ to $99999$, exactly $96309$ have no consecutive $1$'s.
>
> Therefore, among $0$ to $99999$, the number that **do** contain $11$ is
> $$
> 100000 - 96309 = 3691.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Include $100000$
>
> The number $100000$ does not contain two consecutive $1$'s, so it does not change the count.
>
> Thus the number of integers within $100000$ containing two consecutive $1$'s is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3691}.
> $$
