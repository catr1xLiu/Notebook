> [!example] Question 1048 · IMC
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have 12 identical balls, one of which is heavier than the rest (you don't know which). Using only a balance scale that can show which side is heavier, what is the minimum number of weighings needed to identify the heavier ball?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1049)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To identify one heavier ball among $12$ identical-looking balls using a balance scale, the minimum number of weighings is
>
> $$
> 3.
> $$
>
> ### Reason
>
> Each weighing on a balance has $3$ possible outcomes:
>
> - left side heavier,
> - right side heavier,
> - balanced.
>
> Thus, with $k$ weighings, one can distinguish at most
>
> $$
> 3^k
> $$
>
> different possibilities.
>
> Here there are $12$ possible heavier balls, so we need
>
> $$
> 3^k \ge 12.
> $$
>
> Check powers of $3$:
>
> $$
> 3^2 = 9 < 12, \qquad 3^3 = 27 \ge 12.
> $$
>
> So $2$ weighings are not enough, but $3$ weighings are sufficient.
>
> ### Constructive strategy
>
> - First weighing: split the $12$ balls into three groups of $4$, and weigh $4$ against $4$.
>   - If they balance, the heavier ball is in the remaining $4$.
>   - If not, it is in the heavier group of $4$.
>
> So after one weighing, the candidate set is reduced to $4$ balls.
>
> - Second weighing: take those $4$ candidate balls, weigh $2$ against $2$.
>   - If they balance, this would not happen since one of the $4$ is heavier, so one side must be heavier.
>   - The heavier ball is among the heavier pair.
>
> Now the candidate set is reduced to $2$ balls.
>
> - Third weighing: weigh those $2$ balls against each other.
>   - The heavier side identifies the heavier ball.
>
> Therefore, the minimum number of weighings is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}.
> $$
