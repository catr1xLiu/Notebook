> [!example] Question 1075 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Is searching through a sorted binary tree (binary search tree) always more efficient in terms of complexity than binary search?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1076)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> No.
>
> A binary search on a **sorted array** runs in
>
> $$
> O(\log n)
> $$
>
> time in the worst case.
>
> Searching in a **binary search tree** takes time proportional to the height of the tree:
>
> $$
> O(h)
> $$
>
> where $h$ is the tree height.
>
> - If the BST is **balanced**, then
>
> $$
> h = O(\log n)
> $$
>
> so search is
>
> $$
> O(\log n)
> $$
>
> which is the same asymptotic complexity as binary search.
>
> - If the BST is **unbalanced** (for example, it degenerates into a chain), then
>
> $$
> h = O(n)
> $$
>
> so search can take
>
> $$
> O(n)
> $$
>
> in the worst case.
>
> Therefore, searching a BST is **not always better** than binary search. In fact:
>
> - **Binary search on a sorted array:** always $O(\log n)$ worst case.
> - **BST search:** $O(\log n)$ only if the tree is balanced; otherwise it may be $O(n)$.
>
> So the correct conclusion is:
>
> $$
> \text{Searching in a BST is not always better; at best it matches binary search, and at worst it is much worse.}
> $$
