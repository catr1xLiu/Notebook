> [!example] Question 1041 · IMC
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

Draw a graph of profit versus price for a call option with a fee of £3. What features of this graph are of interest to traders?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1042)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Profit graph for a call option with fee £3
>
> Let $S_T$ be the price of the underlying asset at expiry, and let $K$ be the strike price.
>
> The payoff of a call option at expiry is
>
> $$
> \max(S_T - K, 0).
> $$
>
> If the option costs a fee (premium) of £$3$, then the **profit** is
>
> $$
> \Pi(S_T) = \max(S_T - K, 0) - 3.
> $$
>
> This can be written piecewise as
>
> $$
> \Pi(S_T)=
> \begin{cases}
> -3, & S_T \le K,\\[4pt]
> S_T - K - 3, & S_T > K.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ### Sketch of the graph
>
> - For $S_T \le K$, the profit is constant at £$-3$.
> - At $S_T = K$, the graph has a kink.
> - For $S_T > K$, the profit rises linearly with slope $1$.
> - The break-even point occurs when
>
> $$
> S_T - K - 3 = 0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> S_T = K + 3.
> $$
>
> A sketch is therefore:
>
> ```text
> Profit
>   ^
>   |
>   |                          /
>   |                        /
>   |                      /
>   |                    /
>   |-------------------/------------------> Price at expiry, S_T
>   |                 /
> -3|----------------•----------------------
>   |               K      K+3
> ```
>
> More precisely:
>
> - the horizontal segment is at profit £$-3$ up to $S_T=K$;
> - after $K$, the line increases with gradient $1$;
> - it crosses the horizontal axis at $S_T=K+3$.
>
> ## What is interesting about this graph to traders?
>
> Traders find several features important:
>
> 1. **Limited downside**
>    
>    The maximum loss is fixed at the premium paid, namely £$3$. Even if the asset price falls a great deal, the profit cannot go below
>
>    $$
>    -3.
>    $$
>
> 2. **Unlimited upside**
>    
>    If the underlying price rises far above the strike, profit keeps increasing:
>
>    $$
>    \Pi(S_T) = S_T - K - 3,
>    $$
>
>    so there is no upper cap on profit.
>
> 3. **Convex payoff**
>    
>    The graph bends upward at the strike. This convex shape is attractive because losses are bounded while gains accelerate once the asset moves sufficiently upward.
>
> 4. **Break-even is above the strike**
>    
>    The holder does not make profit as soon as $S_T>K$; the premium must also be recovered. Hence break-even is at
>
>    $$
>    K+3,
>    $$
>
>    not at $K$.
>
> 5. **Asymmetric risk-return profile**
>    
>    The option buyer risks a small known amount for exposure to large favorable moves. This asymmetry is one of the main reasons options are used for speculation and hedging.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The profit function for a call option with fee £$3$ is
>
> $$
> \Pi(S_T)=\max(S_T-K,0)-3
> =
> \begin{cases}
> -3, & S_T\le K,\\[4pt]
> S_T-K-3, & S_T>K.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Its graph is flat at £$-3$ until the strike $K$, then rises linearly, crossing profit $0$ at $S_T=K+3$. Traders find this graph interesting because it shows **limited loss, unlimited upside, and a convex payoff shape**.
