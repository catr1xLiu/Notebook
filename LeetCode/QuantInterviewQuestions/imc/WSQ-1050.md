> [!example] Question 1050 · IMC
> ## Intern Interview

What is the probability of landing on the Broadway street tile in Monopoly after 10 rotations? You can approximate the answer.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1051)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the board have $40$ spaces, numbered $0,1,\dots,39$, and let Broadway be one fixed target space. A “rotation” around the board means a net advance of $40$ spaces. Thus, after $10$ rotations, the player has advanced a total of approximately
>
> $$
> 10 \cdot 40 = 400
> $$
>
> spaces.
>
> To ask for the probability of **landing on Broadway at that time**, we interpret this as the probability that the player’s position after $400$ spaces is exactly the Broadway space modulo $40$.
>
> Since movement in Monopoly is determined by dice rolls, and after many turns the position on the board is well approximated by a distribution close to uniform over the $40$ spaces, the probability of being on any particular space is approximately
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{40}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of being on Broadway after $10$ full rotations is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{40} \approx 0.025 = 2.5\% }.
> $$
>
> So the approximate probability is **$2.5\%$**.
