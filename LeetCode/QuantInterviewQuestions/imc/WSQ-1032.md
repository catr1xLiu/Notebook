> [!example] Question 1032 · IMC
> ## Trader Interview

How much would the average global sea level rise if the mass of Mount Everest was submerged into the ocean?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1033)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Treat the question as a volume-displacement problem: if the entire mass of Mount Everest were moved into the ocean, it would displace a volume of water equal to the mountain’s volume. The average sea-level rise is then
>
> $$
> \Delta h=\frac{V_{\text{Everest}}}{A_{\text{ocean}}},
> $$
>
> where $V_{\text{Everest}}$ is the mountain’s volume and $A_{\text{ocean}}$ is the surface area of the world’s oceans.
>
> ## 1. Ocean surface area
>
> A standard estimate for the ocean’s area is
>
> $$
> A_{\text{ocean}} \approx 3.61\times 10^{14}\ \text{m}^2.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Estimate Mount Everest’s volume
>
> Mount Everest is not a simple geometric solid, so only an order-of-magnitude estimate is possible. Approximate it as a cone.
>
> Take:
>
> - height $h \approx 8.85\times 10^3\ \text{m}$,
> - base radius on the order of $r \approx 10^4\ \text{m}$.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V_{\text{cone}}=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h
> \approx \frac{1}{3}\pi (10^4)^2(8.85\times 10^3)
> \approx 9.3\times 10^{11}\ \text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> So a reasonable estimate is
>
> $$
> V_{\text{Everest}} \approx 10^{12}\ \text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Compute the average sea-level rise
>
> Now divide by the ocean area:
>
> $$
> \Delta h \approx \frac{10^{12}}{3.61\times 10^{14}}
> \approx 2.8\times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}.
> $$
>
> Converting units,
>
> $$
> 2.8\times 10^{-3}\ \text{m} = 2.8\ \text{mm}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The average global sea level would rise by only a few millimeters, approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{3\ \text{mm}}
> $$
>
> using a rough geometric estimate for Mount Everest’s volume. Depending on how one defines the mountain’s base, the true value could vary by a factor of a few, but it would still be on the order of millimeters, not centimeters or meters.
