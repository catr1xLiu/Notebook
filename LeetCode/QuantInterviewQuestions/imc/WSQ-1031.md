> [!example] Question 1031 · IMC
> ## Trader Interview

You have a 4x4 grid and three balls. If you manage to get at least 2 balls in the same row or column, you win. What is the probability of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1032)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Setting up the problem
>
> Three balls are placed uniformly at random into distinct cells of a $4\times 4$ grid. A win occurs if there exists at least one row or at least one column containing at least two of the balls.
>
> To lose, the three balls must occupy:
>
> - three distinct rows, and
> - three distinct columns.
>
> This is certainly possible, so the probability of winning is not $1$, and we compute it directly.
>
> ## Step 1: Total number of ways
>
> Choose any $3$ distinct cells from the $16$ available:
>
> $$
> \binom{16}{3} = 560.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Losing configurations
>
> A losing configuration has all three balls in different rows and different columns.
>
> Count these:
>
> 1. Choose $3$ rows out of $4$: $\binom{4}{3} = 4$.
> 2. Choose $3$ columns out of $4$: $\binom{4}{3} = 4$.
> 3. Match the $3$ chosen rows to the $3$ chosen columns, one cell per row and one per column: $3! = 6$.
>
> Thus the number of losing configurations is
>
> $$
> 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 = 96.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Winning configurations
>
> Therefore the number of winning configurations is
>
> $$
> 560 - 96 = 464.
> $$
>
> So the probability of winning is
>
> $$
> \frac{464}{560} = \frac{29}{35}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{29}{35}}
> $$
