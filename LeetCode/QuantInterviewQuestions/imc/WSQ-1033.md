> [!example] Question 1033 · IMC
> ## Trader Interview

Draw a payoff diagram for a put option.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1034)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Payoff Diagram for a Put Option
>
> A **put option** gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to **sell** the underlying asset at the **strike price** $K$ at expiration.
>
> Let $S_T$ denote the asset price at expiration. The **payoff** of a long put option is
>
> $$
> \text{Payoff} = \max(K - S_T,\, 0).
> $$
>
> ### Piecewise form
>
> $$
> \text{Payoff} =
> \begin{cases}
> K - S_T, & \text{if } S_T < K,\\[4pt]
> 0, & \text{if } S_T \ge K.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ### Shape of the diagram
>
> - For $S_T < K$, the payoff declines linearly with slope $-1$.
> - At $S_T = K$, the payoff is $0$.
> - For $S_T > K$, the payoff remains $0$.
>
> ### Sketch
>
> ```text
> Payoff
>   ^
>   |
> K |\
>   | \
>   |  \
>   |   \
>   |    \
>   |     \____________
>   |
>   +--------------------------> S_T
>                K
> ```
>
> ### Interpretation
>
> - If the asset price falls below the strike price $K$, the put becomes valuable because the holder can sell at $K$ when the market price is only $S_T$.
> - If the asset price is at or above $K$, exercising the put is not beneficial, so the payoff is $0$.
>
> Thus, the payoff diagram is a **downward-sloping line up to $K$**, and **flat at $0$ beyond $K$**.
