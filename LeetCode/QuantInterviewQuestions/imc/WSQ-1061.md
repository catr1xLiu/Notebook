> [!example] Question 1061 · IMC
> ## Graduate Quant Trader Interview

There are some fish in a bucket. Three fishermen come to the bucket one after another. The first tries to divide the fish into groups of three but finds one fish left over, so he takes this one and a third of the remaining fish. The next fisherman tries to divide the rest into three, again finds one left over, and takes that one and a third of the remainder. The third fisherman does the same. What is the minimum number of fish that could have been in the bucket initially?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1062)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of fish be $N$.
>
> Each fisherman finds that the current number of fish leaves remainder $1$ when divided by $3$. He removes that one fish, leaving a multiple of $3$, and then takes one-third of what remains. Thus after each fisherman, the number of fish left is two-thirds of the number remaining after removing the extra fish.
>
> If the current number is $x$, then after one fisherman the number left is
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3}(x-1).
> $$
>
> We require this process to work three times in succession, with the number before each fisherman congruent to $1 \pmod 3$.
>
> Set up the successive counts:
>
> $$
> N_1=\frac{2}{3}(N-1),
> $$
>
> $$
> N_2=\frac{2}{3}(N_1-1),
> $$
>
> $$
> N_3=\frac{2}{3}(N_2-1).
> $$
>
> We seek the smallest positive integer $N$ such that $N$, $N_1$, and $N_2$ are all congruent to $1 \pmod 3$, and all quantities are integers.
>
> Work backward from the third fisherman.
>
> For the third fisherman to act, the number before him, $N_2$, must satisfy
>
> $$
> N_2 \equiv 1 \pmod 3.
> $$
>
> Write
>
> $$
> N_2 = 3a+1.
> $$
>
> Then after he takes his share, the number left is
>
> $$
> N_3=\frac{2}{3}(N_2-1)=\frac{2}{3}(3a)=2a.
> $$
>
> To minimize $N$, minimize $N_2$, then $N_1$, then $N$.
>
> Now $N_2=\frac{2}{3}(N_1-1)$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3}(N_1-1)=3a+1.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> N_1-1=\frac{3}{2}(3a+1).
> $$
>
> For this to be an integer, $3a+1$ must be even, so $a$ must be odd. The smallest odd $a$ is $1$, giving
>
> $$
> N_2=3(1)+1=4.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> N_1=\frac{3}{2}\cdot 4 +1 = 6+1=7.
> $$
>
> Now use
>
> $$
> N_1=\frac{2}{3}(N-1)=7.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> N-1=\frac{3}{2}\cdot 7.
> $$
>
> This is not an integer, so $N_1=7$ cannot occur from an integer $N$. Therefore take the next possible value of $N_2$.
>
> The next odd $a$ is $3$, giving
>
> $$
> N_2=3(3)+1=10.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> N_1=\frac{3}{2}\cdot 10 +1 = 15+1=16.
> $$
>
> Now solve for $N$:
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3}(N-1)=16
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> N-1=24,
> $$
>
> $$
> N=25.
> $$
>
> Check:
>
> - First fisherman: $25=3\cdot 8+1$. Remove $1$, leaving $24$; take one-third, $8$; left with $16$.
> - Second fisherman: $16=3\cdot 5+1$. Remove $1$, leaving $15$; take one-third, $5$; left with $10$.
> - Third fisherman: $10=3\cdot 3+1$. Remove $1$, leaving $9$; take one-third, $3$; left with $6$.
>
> All conditions are satisfied.
>
> $$
> \boxed{25}
> $$
