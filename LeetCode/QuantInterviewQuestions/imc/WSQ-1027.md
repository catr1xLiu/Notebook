> [!example] Question 1027 · IMC
> ## Software Developer Interview

Determine if two given words are anagrams of each other.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1028)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Two words are **anagrams** if they contain exactly the same letters with the same frequencies, possibly in a different order.
>
> ### Approach
>
> To determine whether two words are anagrams:
>
> 1. Compare their lengths.
>    - If the lengths are different, they cannot be anagrams.
> 2. Count the frequency of each character in both words.
> 3. If all character frequencies match, the words are anagrams; otherwise, they are not.
>
> ### Example Method
>
> Given two words $w_1$ and $w_2$:
>
> - If $|w_1| \ne |w_2|$, return **false**.
> - Otherwise, sort both words or build character-frequency maps.
> - If the sorted forms or frequency maps are equal, return **true**.
>
> ### Illustration
>
> For the words $\texttt{listen}$ and $\texttt{silent}$:
>
> - Both have length $6$.
> - Their character counts are identical:
>   - $\texttt{l}: 1$
>   - $\texttt{i}: 1$
>   - $\texttt{s}: 1$
>   - $\texttt{t}: 1$
>   - $\texttt{e}: 1$
>   - $\texttt{n}: 1$
>
> Hence, they are anagrams.
>
> For the words $\texttt{hello}$ and $\texttt{world}$:
>
> - Their character frequencies differ.
>
> Hence, they are **not** anagrams.
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> Two words are anagrams **if and only if** they have the same length and the same character frequencies. A practical check is:
>
> $$
> \text{anagram}(w_1, w_2) =
> \begin{cases}
> \text{true}, & \text{if sorted}(w_1) = \text{sorted}(w_2),\\
> \text{false}, & \text{otherwise.}
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> If specific two words are provided, this rule can be applied directly.
