> [!example] Question 1037 · IMC
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

The profits of traders A, B, and C follow independent uniform distributions from 0 EUR to 100,000 EUR. What is the probability that A \> B and B \> C?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1038)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the profits of traders $A$, $B$, and $C$ be independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on $[0,100]$ (in thousands of euros). We seek
>
> $$
> P(A>B \text{ and } B>C).
> $$
>
> Since $A$, $B$, and $C$ are independent and identically distributed with a continuous distribution, all $3! = 6$ possible strict orderings are equally likely:
>
> $$
> A>B>C,\quad A>C>B,\quad B>A>C,\quad B>C>A,\quad C>A>B,\quad C>B>A.
> $$
>
> Because the distribution is continuous, ties occur with probability $0$, so these six orderings partition the sample space equally. Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(A>B \text{ and } B>C)=P(A>B>C)=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}.
> $$
>
> In decimal form,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6} \approx 0.1667.
> $$
