What is the difference between threading and multiprocessing?

> ## Difference Between Threading and Multiprocessing
>
> Threading and multiprocessing are two ways to achieve concurrency, but they differ in how work is organized and how memory is handled.
>
> ### 1. Basic idea
>
> - **Threading** uses multiple threads within a single process.
> - **Multiprocessing** uses multiple separate processes, each with its own memory space.
>
> ### 2. Memory model
>
> - In **threading**, threads share the same memory of the parent process. This makes communication easier, since threads can access the same variables directly.
> - In **multiprocessing**, each process has its own independent memory. Data must be passed between processes using mechanisms such as pipes, queues, or shared memory.
>
> ### 3. Overhead
>
> - **Threads** are generally lighter weight. Creating and switching between threads usually costs less.
> - **Processes** are heavier weight. Creating processes and exchanging data between them usually requires more system resources.
>
> ### 4. Safety and isolation
>
> - Because threads share memory, they are more prone to issues such as race conditions and deadlocks. Proper synchronization, such as locks or semaphores, is often required.
> - Processes are more isolated. A crash in one process is less likely to corrupt another process’s memory.
>
> ### 5. Performance characteristics
>
> - **Threading** is often well suited for **I/O-bound** tasks, such as network requests, file access, or waiting on external resources.
> - **Multiprocessing** is often better for **CPU-bound** tasks, since separate processes can run truly in parallel on multiple CPU cores.
>
> ### 6. Communication
>
> - **Threads:** communication is simple because of shared memory, but synchronization is necessary.
> - **Processes:** communication is more explicit and slower, but often safer.
>
> ### 7. Practical summary
>
> If the task spends most of its time waiting, **threading** is usually convenient and efficient. If the task spends most of its time computing, **multiprocessing** is often preferable for better parallel execution.
>
> ## Final comparison
>
> - **Threading:** shared memory, lower overhead, easier communication, higher risk of synchronization bugs, good for I/O-bound work.
> - **Multiprocessing:** separate memory, higher overhead, safer isolation, better CPU parallelism, good for CPU-bound work.
