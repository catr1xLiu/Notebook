> [!example] Question 1042 · IMC
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

What is the minimum number of cards required to build a card tower with 100 levels, given that you need 2 cards for 1 level, 7 for 2 levels, and so on?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1043)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To build a card tower with $n$ levels, count the cards in two parts:
>
> 1. **Leaning pairs:** Each level $k$ has $k$ pairs of leaning cards, and each pair uses $2$ cards.  
>    Thus the total number of cards in leaning pairs is
>    $$
>    2(1+2+\cdots+n)=2\cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}=n(n+1).
>    $$
>
> 2. **Horizontal cards:** Between adjacent pairs, each level contributes one fewer horizontal card than the number of pairs on that level.  
>    So the total number of horizontal cards is
>    $$
>    (1+2+\cdots+(n-1))=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}.
>    $$
>
> Therefore, the total number of cards is
> $$
> n(n+1)+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> For $n=100$:
> $$
> 100\cdot 101+\frac{100\cdot 99}{2}
> =10100+4950
> =15050.
> $$
>
> Thus, the minimum number of cards needed is
> $$
> \boxed{15050}.
> $$
