> [!example] Question 1012 · IMC
> ## Software Engineer Interview

How would you explain the difference between a stack and a queue to a non-technical person?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1013)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A simple way to explain the difference is to use everyday situations.
>
> A **queue** is like a line at a coffee shop.  
> The **first person to get in line is the first person served**.  
> This is called **FIFO**: **First In, First Out**.
>
> A **stack** is like a stack of plates or books.  
> When you add a new plate, you put it on the top. When you take one away, you also take the top one first.  
> So the **last item added is the first one removed**.  
> This is called **LIFO**: **Last In, First Out**.
>
> So the key difference is:
>
> - **Queue:** items leave in the same order they arrive.
> - **Stack:** the newest item leaves first.
>
> If explaining to a non-technical person in an interview, a good answer would be:
>
> A queue works like waiting in line: the first person in is the first person out. A stack works like a pile of plates: the last one placed on top is the first one taken off. So a queue is about fairness in arrival order, while a stack is about accessing the most recent item first.
>
> A short practical example:
>
> - **Queue:** printer jobs, supermarket lines, customer support waiting lists.
> - **Stack:** undo button in a document, a pile of trays, browser back history.
>
> That makes the distinction concrete and easy to remember.
