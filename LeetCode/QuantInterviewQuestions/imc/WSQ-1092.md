> [!example] Question 1092 · IMC
> ## SWE Intern Interview

What does amortized runtime mean?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1093)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Amortized runtime is the **average cost per operation over a sequence of operations**, especially when some individual operations are occasionally expensive.
>
> A data structure may have:
>
> - most operations very cheap,
> - but once in a while one operation is costly.
>
> Amortized analysis shows that, over many operations, the expensive ones are rare enough that the **average cost stays small**.
>
> ## Main idea
>
> If a sequence of $n$ operations takes total time $T(n)$, then the amortized cost per operation is
>
> $$
> \frac{T(n)}{n}.
> $$
>
> This is **not** the same as average-case runtime based on probability.  
> Amortized analysis gives a guarantee for **any sequence of operations**, not just a random one.
>
> ## Example: dynamic array append
>
> Suppose appending to an array usually takes $O(1)$, but when the array is full, it must allocate a larger array and copy all elements, which costs $O(n)$ for that operation.
>
> Even though some individual appends cost $O(n)$, over many appends the total cost is only $O(n)$, so the amortized cost per append is
>
> $$
> O(1).
> $$
>
> ## Why this happens
>
> When the array doubles in size each time it fills, resizing happens rarely:
>
> - capacity $1 \to 2$
> - $2 \to 4$
> - $4 \to 8$
> - etc.
>
> The total copying work after $n$ appends is
>
> $$
> 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + \cdots < 2n,
> $$
>
> so the total work is linear in $n$. Hence each append has amortized cost
>
> $$
> O(1).
> $$
>
> ## Summary
>
> Amortized runtime means:
>
> - look at the **total cost** of a sequence of operations,
> - divide by the number of operations,
> - obtain a guaranteed average cost per operation.
>
> Thus, an operation can be occasionally expensive while still having a small amortized runtime.
