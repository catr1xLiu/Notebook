> [!example] Question 1052 · IMC
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

Two teams are playing each other, and both have positive (plus) odds to win. How much should you bet on each team, and why?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1053)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two moneyline odds be $+a$ for Team $1$ and $+b$ for Team $2$, with $a,b>0$.
>
> ## Step 1: Convert the odds into payout formulas
>
> For American odds of $+a$:
>
> - a bet of $x$ returns profit
>   $$
>   x\cdot \frac{a}{100}
>   $$
>   if it wins.
>
> So if you bet:
>
> - $x$ on Team $1$ at $+a$,
> - $y$ on Team $2$ at $+b$,
>
> then:
>
> - if Team $1$ wins, net profit is
>   $$
>   x\cdot \frac{a}{100}-y
>   $$
> - if Team $2$ wins, net profit is
>   $$
>   y\cdot \frac{b}{100}-x
>   $$
>
> ## Step 2: Choose bets to equalize the outcome
>
> If the goal is to make the same result no matter who wins, set these equal:
>
> $$
> x\cdot \frac{a}{100}-y
> =
> y\cdot \frac{b}{100}-x
> $$
>
> Rearrange:
>
> $$
> x\left(1+\frac{a}{100}\right)
> =
> y\left(1+\frac{b}{100}\right)
> $$
>
> Thus the required ratio is
>
> $$
> \frac{x}{y}
> =
> \frac{1+\frac{b}{100}}{1+\frac{a}{100}}
> =
> \frac{100+b}{100+a}
> $$
>
> So the bets should be in the proportion
>
> $$
> x:y=(100+b):(100+a)
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Express the bets for any total amount staked
>
> If the total amount bet is
>
> $$
> T=x+y,
> $$
>
> then using the ratio above,
>
> $$
> x=T\cdot \frac{100+b}{200+a+b},
> \qquad
> y=T\cdot \frac{100+a}{200+a+b}
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - bet
>   $$
>   T\cdot \frac{100+b}{200+a+b}
>   $$
>   on Team $1$,
> - bet
>   $$
>   T\cdot \frac{100+a}{200+a+b}
>   $$
>   on Team $2$.
>
> ## Step 4: What happens to the profit?
>
> Substitute into either outcome:
>
> $$
> \text{Profit}
> =
> x\cdot \frac{a}{100}-y
> $$
>
> This simplifies to
>
> $$
> \text{Profit}
> =
> T\cdot \frac{ab-10000}{100(200+a+b)}
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - if
>   $$
>   ab>10000,
>   $$
>   you lock in a profit;
> - if
>   $$
>   ab=10000,
>   $$
>   you break even;
> - if
>   $$
>   ab<10000,
>   $$
>   you lock in a loss.
>
> ## Why?
>
> This works because the stakes are chosen so that the total return is the same regardless of which team wins. That is the standard hedge/arbitrage setup.
>
> However, with two teams both listed at positive odds, this usually indicates a profitable arbitrage opportunity only when
>
> $$
> \frac{100}{100+a}+\frac{100}{100+b}<1,
> $$
>
> which is equivalent to
>
> $$
> ab>10000.
> $$
>
> ## Example
>
> Suppose the teams are:
>
> - Team $1$: $+120$
> - Team $2$: $+110$
>
> Then $a=120$, $b=110$, so the stake ratio is
>
> $$
> x:y=(100+110):(100+120)=210:220=21:22
> $$
>
> If $T=\text{\textdollar}430$, then
>
> $$
> x=430\cdot \frac{210}{430}=\$210,
> \qquad
> y=430\cdot \frac{220}{430}=\$220
> $$
>
> Check the outcomes:
>
> - If Team $1$ wins:
>   $$
>   210\cdot 1.20-220=252-220=\$32
>   $$
> - If Team $2$ wins:
>   $$
>   220\cdot 1.10-210=242-210=\$32
>   $$
>
> So either way the profit is $\text{\textdollar}32$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If Team $1$ is $+a$ and Team $2$ is $+b$, bet in the ratio
>
> $$
> (100+b):(100+a).
> $$
>
> For a total stake $T$, bet
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> x=T\cdot \frac{100+b}{200+a+b}
> }
> \quad\text{on Team 1,}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> y=T\cdot \frac{100+a}{200+a+b}
> }
> \quad\text{on Team 2.}
> $$
>
> This equalizes the payout regardless of the winner. It is profitable only when
>
> $$
> \boxed{ab>10000.}
> $$
