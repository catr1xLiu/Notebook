> [!example] Question 522 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A bus has some initial number of passengers. At the first stop, three quarters of the passengers get off and 7 get on the bus. At the second stop, the same process occurs: three quarters of the passengers get off and 7 get on. At the third stop, the same thing happens again. What is the minimum number of passengers to start with?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=523)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of passengers be $x$.
>
> At each stop, the number of passengers changes by the rule
>
> $$
> \text{new number}=\frac{1}{4}(\text{old number})+7,
> $$
>
> because three quarters get off, so one quarter remains, and then $7$ get on.
>
> We want the number of passengers after each stop to remain a whole number. Work backward from the third stop, choosing the smallest possible values.
>
> Let the number of passengers after the third stop be $n_3$.
>
> Then the number just before the third stop, say $n_2$, must satisfy
>
> $$
> \frac{n_2}{4}+7=n_3,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> n_2=4(n_3-7).
> $$
>
> Similarly, if $n_1$ is the number before the second stop,
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{4}+7=n_2
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> n_1=4(n_2-7).
> $$
>
> And if $x$ is the initial number,
>
> $$
> \frac{x}{4}+7=n_1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> x=4(n_1-7).
> $$
>
> To make $x$ as small as possible, take the smallest possible positive integer after the third stop. The smallest reasonable choice is
>
> $$
> n_3=1.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> n_2=4(1-7)=4(-6)=-24,
> $$
>
> which is impossible. So increase $n_3$ until all previous values are nonnegative integers.
>
> Try $n_3=7$:
>
> $$
> n_2=4(7-7)=0,
> $$
>
> then
>
> $$
> n_1=4(0-7)=-28,
> $$
>
> impossible.
>
> Try one more step systematically by requiring each previous value to be at least $0$.
>
> From
>
> $$
> n_1=4(n_2-7)\ge 0,
> $$
>
> we need
>
> $$
> n_2\ge 7.
> $$
>
> From
>
> $$
> n_2=4(n_3-7)\ge 7,
> $$
>
> we need
>
> $$
> 4(n_3-7)\ge 7
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> n_3\ge \frac{35}{4}.
> $$
>
> So the smallest integer choice is
>
> $$
> n_3=9.
> $$
>
> Now compute backward:
>
> $$
> n_2=4(9-7)=8,
> $$
>
> $$
> n_1=4(8-7)=4,
> $$
>
> $$
> x=4(4-7)=-12,
> $$
>
> still impossible.
>
> So also require
>
> $$
> x=4(n_1-7)\ge 0,
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> n_1\ge 7.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> n_1=4(n_2-7)\ge 7,
> $$
>
> we need
>
> $$
> n_2\ge 9.
> $$
>
> And since
>
> $$
> n_2=4(n_3-7)\ge 9,
> $$
>
> we need
>
> $$
> n_3\ge 10.
> $$
>
> Take the smallest such value:
>
> $$
> n_3=10.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> n_2=4(10-7)=12,
> $$
>
> $$
> n_1=4(12-7)=20,
> $$
>
> $$
> x=4(20-7)=52.
> $$
>
> Check:
>
> - After first stop: $$\frac{1}{4}\cdot 52+7=13+7=20$$
> - After second stop: $$\frac{1}{4}\cdot 20+7=5+7=12$$
> - After third stop: $$\frac{1}{4}\cdot 12+7=3+7=10$$
>
> All are whole numbers.
>
> Therefore, the minimum number of passengers initially is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52}.
> $$
