> [!example] Question 294 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

Find the smallest integer such that the product of its digits is 3,000,000.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=295)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Factor the required product:
> $$
> 3{,}000{,}000 = 3 \cdot 10^6 = 3 \cdot (2\cdot 5)^6 = 2^6 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^6.
> $$
>
> We seek the smallest positive integer whose digits multiply to this value. Since a digit can be at most $9$, we must group these prime factors into decimal digits.
>
> The available digit factorizations are:
> $$
> 2,\; 3,\; 4=2^2,\; 5,\; 6=2\cdot 3,\; 8=2^3,\; 9=3^2.
> $$
>
> Because there are six factors of $5$, and the only digit containing a factor $5$ is $5$ itself, the number must contain exactly six digits $5$.
>
> That accounts for:
> $$
> 5^6.
> $$
>
> The remaining factor is:
> $$
> 2^6 \cdot 3.
> $$
>
> To make the integer as small as possible, we want as few digits as possible, since any additional digit increases the length and thus makes the number larger. The factor $2^6 \cdot 3$ can be grouped as
> $$
> 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 3,
> $$
> because
> $$
> 8\cdot 8 \cdot 3 = 2^3 \cdot 2^3 \cdot 3 = 2^6 \cdot 3.
> $$
>
> So the full set of digits is:
> $$
> 3,\; 8,\; 8,\; 5,\; 5,\; 5,\; 5,\; 5,\; 5.
> $$
>
> To obtain the smallest integer with these digits, arrange them in increasing order:
> $$
> 355555588.
> $$
>
> Check:
> $$
> 3 \cdot 5^6 \cdot 8^2
> = 3 \cdot 15625 \cdot 64
> = 3 \cdot 1{,}000{,}000
> = 3{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest such integer is
> $$
> \boxed{355555588}.
> $$
