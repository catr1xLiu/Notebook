> [!example] Question 232 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

You are bidding on a car whose true price is uniformly distributed between 0 and 100. If your bid exceeds the actual price, you win the car and can resell it for 1.5 times its actual price. What bid maximizes your expected profit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=233)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the car’s true price be $X$, where
>
> $$
> X \sim \text{Uniform}(0,100).
> $$
>
> Suppose you submit a bid of $b$, with $0 \le b \le 100$.
>
> If your bid exceeds the true price, that is, if $b > X$, then you obtain the car and can resell it for $1.5X$. Since you pay $b$, your profit in that case is
>
> $$
> 1.5X - b.
> $$
>
> If $b \le X$, you do not get the car, so the profit is $0$.
>
> Thus the profit as a function of $X$ is
>
> $$
> \pi(X)=
> \begin{cases}
> 1.5X-b, & X<b,\\
> 0, & X\ge b.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ## Expected profit
>
> Because $X$ is uniform on $[0,100]$, its density is
>
> $$
> f_X(x)=\frac{1}{100}, \qquad 0\le x\le 100.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected profit is
>
> $$
> E[\pi(X)]
> =
> \int_0^b (1.5x-b)\frac{1}{100}\,dx.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> E[\pi(X)]
> =
> \frac{1}{100}\left[\frac{1.5}{2}x^2-bx\right]_0^b
> =
> \frac{1}{100}\left(0.75b^2-b^2\right)
> =
> -\frac{b^2}{400}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\pi(X)] = -\frac{b^2}{400}.
> $$
>
> ## Maximizing expected profit
>
> Since
>
> $$
> -\frac{b^2}{400} \le 0
> $$
>
> for all $b \in [0,100]$, the expected profit is maximized when $b=0$.
>
> At $b=0$,
>
> $$
> E[\pi(X)] = 0.
> $$
>
> Any positive bid gives negative expected profit.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The bid that maximizes expected profit is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> So the optimal strategy is **not to bid at all**.
