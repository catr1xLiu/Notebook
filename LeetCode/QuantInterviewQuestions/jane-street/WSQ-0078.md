> [!example] Question 78 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

Estimate the number of cubic meters of water on Earth.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=79)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To estimate the total volume of water on Earth, use a Fermi approximation.
>
> ## 1. Estimate Earth’s surface area
>
> Take Earth’s radius as
>
> $$
> R \approx 6.4 \times 10^6 \ \text{m}.
> $$
>
> Then the surface area is
>
> $$
> A = 4\pi R^2 \approx 4\pi (6.4\times 10^6)^2.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> (6.4\times 10^6)^2 \approx 4.1\times 10^{13},
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> A \approx 4\pi \cdot 4.1\times 10^{13} \approx 5\times 10^{14}\ \text{m}^2.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Estimate the fraction covered by water
>
> About $70\%$ of Earth’s surface is covered by oceans, so the ocean-covered area is
>
> $$
> A_w \approx 0.7 \times 5\times 10^{14} \approx 3.5\times 10^{14}\ \text{m}^2.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Estimate average ocean depth
>
> Take the average ocean depth to be about
>
> $$
> d \approx 4\times 10^3\ \text{m}.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Compute the volume
>
> Then the water volume is approximately
>
> $$
> V \approx A_w d \approx (3.5\times 10^{14})(4\times 10^3).
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> V \approx 1.4\times 10^{18}\ \text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> A reasonable estimate for the amount of water on Earth is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\approx 10^{18}\ \text{m}^3}
> $$
>
> more precisely, about
>
> $$
> \boxed{1.4\times 10^{18}\ \text{m}^3}.
> $$
>
> This is an estimate of total water dominated by the oceans; freshwater in ice, lakes, rivers, and groundwater changes the result only modestly at this scale.
