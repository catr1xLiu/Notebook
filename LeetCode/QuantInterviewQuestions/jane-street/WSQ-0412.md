> [!example] Question 412 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If you roll a 10-sided die and a 20-sided die, what is the probability that the 10-sided die shows a higher number than the 20-sided die?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=413)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the result of the $10$-sided die, so $X \in \{1,2,\dots,10\}$, and let $Y$ be the result of the $20$-sided die, so $Y \in \{1,2,\dots,20\}$.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(X > Y).
> $$
>
> Since the dice are fair and independent, there are
> $$
> 10 \cdot 20 = 200
> $$
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> Count the favorable outcomes where $X > Y$:
>
> - If $X=1$, then there are $0$ values of $Y$ with $Y<1$.
> - If $X=2$, then there is $1$ value of $Y$ with $Y<2$.
> - If $X=3$, then there are $2$ values of $Y$ with $Y<3$.
> - $\dots$
> - If $X=10$, then there are $9$ values of $Y$ with $Y<10$.
>
> Thus the number of favorable outcomes is
> $$
> 0+1+2+\cdots+9.
> $$
>
> This is an arithmetic sum:
> $$
> 0+1+2+\cdots+9 = \frac{9 \cdot 10}{2} = 45.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\frac{45}{200}=\frac{9}{40}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{9}{40}}.
> $$
