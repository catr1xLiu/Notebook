> [!example] Question 111 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

When you roll a coin five times, what is the probability of getting an even number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=112)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained in $5$ tosses of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(5,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want the probability of getting an even number of heads, namely
>
> $$
> P(X \in \{0,2,4\}).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{5}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^5.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> P(\text{even number of heads})
> = P(X=0)+P(X=2)+P(X=4)
> $$
>
> $$
> = \left[\binom{5}{0}+\binom{5}{2}+\binom{5}{4}\right]\left(\tfrac12\right)^5.
> $$
>
> Compute the binomial coefficients:
>
> $$
> \binom{5}{0}=1,\qquad \binom{5}{2}=10,\qquad \binom{5}{4}=5.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> P(\text{even number of heads})
> = \frac{1+10+5}{32}
> = \frac{16}{32}
> = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
