> [!example] Question 618 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You dip a cube into paint and let it dry. Then you cut it into 27 smaller sub-cubes and put them in a bag. If you randomly pick one sub-cube and roll it on the table, what is the probability that no paint is visible?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=619)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Cutting the painted cube into $27$ equal sub-cubes means the original cube was divided into a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid.
>
> A randomly chosen sub-cube can be of three types:
>
> - **Corner cubes**: $8$ of them, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - **Edge-center cubes**: $12$ of them, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - **Face-center cubes**: $6$ of them, each with $1$ painted face.
> - **Interior cube**: $1$ of them, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> Thus the counts by number of painted faces are:
>
> - $0$ painted faces: $1$
> - $1$ painted face: $6$
> - $2$ painted faces: $12$
> - $3$ painted faces: $8$
>
> Now roll the chosen sub-cube and ask for the probability that **no paint is visible on top**.
>
> For a sub-cube with:
>
> - $0$ painted faces, the probability is $1$
> - $1$ painted face, the probability is $\frac{5}{6}$
> - $2$ painted faces, the probability is $\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3}$
> - $3$ painted faces, the probability is $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$
>
> Average over all $27$ sub-cubes:
>
> $$
> P=\frac{1}{27}\left(1\cdot 1+6\cdot \frac{5}{6}+12\cdot \frac{2}{3}+8\cdot \frac{1}{2}\right)
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> P=\frac{1}{27}\left(1+5+8+4\right)=\frac{18}{27}=\frac{2}{3}
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that no paint is visible is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
