> [!example] Question 256 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

You have two bowling balls of the same density. One has a radius of 8 and a weight of 16; the other has a radius of 12. What is the weight of the second ball?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=257)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For objects of the same density, weight is proportional to volume. Since a sphere’s volume is proportional to the cube of its radius,
>
> $$
> W \propto r^3.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \frac{W_2}{W_1}=\frac{r_2^3}{r_1^3}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the given values:
>
> $$
> \frac{W_2}{16}=\frac{12^3}{8^3}.
> $$
>
> Compute the ratio:
>
> $$
> \frac{12^3}{8^3}=\left(\frac{12}{8}\right)^3=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3=\frac{27}{8}.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> W_2=16\cdot \frac{27}{8}=2\cdot 27=54.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the weight of the second bowling ball is
>
> $$
> \boxed{54}.
> $$
