> [!example] Question 129 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting an odd number of heads in a sequence of coin flips where some coins are not fair?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=130)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the coin flips be independent, and let the probability that the $i$-th coin lands heads be $p_i$, for $i=1,2,\dots,n$.
>
> We seek the probability that the total number of heads is odd.
>
> ## Step 1: Use parity instead of counting directly
>
> Let $X_i$ be the indicator of a head on flip $i$:
> $$
> X_i =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if flip } i \text{ is heads},\\
> 0, & \text{if flip } i \text{ is tails}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then the total number of heads is
> $$
> S = X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n.
> $$
>
> We want
> $$
> P(S \text{ is odd}).
> $$
>
> A standard trick is to consider $(-1)^S$:
>
> - if $S$ is even, then $(-1)^S = 1$,
> - if $S$ is odd, then $(-1)^S = -1$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E[(-1)^S] = P(S \text{ even}) - P(S \text{ odd}).
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> P(S \text{ even}) + P(S \text{ odd}) = 1,
> $$
> we can solve for $P(S \text{ odd})$ once we compute $E[(-1)^S]$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute $E[(-1)^S]$
>
> Because
> $$
> (-1)^S = (-1)^{X_1+\cdots+X_n} = \prod_{i=1}^n (-1)^{X_i},
> $$
> and the flips are independent,
> $$
> E[(-1)^S] = \prod_{i=1}^n E[(-1)^{X_i}].
> $$
>
> Now for a single flip,
> $$
> E[(-1)^{X_i}] = (1-p_i)(1) + p_i(-1) = 1 - 2p_i.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> E[(-1)^S] = \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - 2p_i).
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve for the odd probability
>
> Let
> $$
> P_{\text{even}} = P(S \text{ even}), \qquad P_{\text{odd}} = P(S \text{ odd}).
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> P_{\text{even}} - P_{\text{odd}} = \prod_{i=1}^n (1-2p_i),
> $$
> and
> $$
> P_{\text{even}} + P_{\text{odd}} = 1.
> $$
>
> Subtracting gives
> $$
> 2P_{\text{odd}} = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^n (1-2p_i).
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(S \text{ is odd}) = \frac{1 - \prod_{i=1}^n (1-2p_i)}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> For independent coin flips with head probabilities $p_1,p_2,\dots,p_n$, the probability of an odd number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{odd number of heads}) = \frac{1 - \prod_{i=1}^n (1-2p_i)}{2}.}
> $$
>
> In the special case of fair coins, $p_i=\tfrac12$ for all $i$, so the product is $0$, and the formula gives
> $$
> P(\text{odd number of heads}) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
