> [!example] Question 191 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

1. If you have 100 chips, how can you split them into piles in order to maximize the product of the number of chips in all piles? 2. You are flipping four coins and you get \$1 for each head that shows up. a) You are given the choice to reflip all 4 coins once. What is your optimal strategy? b) You can reflip all 4 coins as many times as you want, but you must pay \$1 for each reflip. What is your optimal strategy?

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Splitting $100$ chips into piles to maximize the product
>
> Let the pile sizes be positive integers $a_1,a_2,\dots,a_k$ such that
>
> $$
> a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k=100,
> $$
>
> and we want to maximize
>
> $$
> P=a_1a_2\cdots a_k.
> $$
>
> ### Step 1: Eliminate pile sizes $\ge 5$
>
> If some pile has size $n\ge 5$, then replacing it by two piles of sizes $3$ and $n-3$ changes the product by a factor
>
> $$
> \frac{3(n-3)}{n}.
> $$
>
> This is greater than $1$ when
>
> $$
> 3(n-3)>n
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> 2n>9,
> $$
>
> which holds for all integers $n\ge 5$. Thus any pile of size at least $5$ can be split to increase the product.
>
> So in an optimal partition, every pile has size $2$, $3$, or $4$.
>
> ### Step 2: Eliminate $4$'s
>
> A pile of size $4$ contributes the same as two piles of size $2$, since
>
> $$
> 4=2\cdot 2.
> $$
>
> So we may restrict attention to piles of size $2$ and $3$.
>
> ### Step 3: Use as many $3$'s as possible
>
> Among positive integers, $3$'s are best for maximizing the product, except that we should avoid leaving a remainder of $1$, because
>
> $$
> 3+1=4
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 3\cdot 1=3<4=2\cdot 2.
> $$
>
> Now divide $100$ by $3$:
>
> $$
> 100=3\cdot 33+1.
> $$
>
> So using all $3$'s would leave a remainder of $1$, which is suboptimal. Replace one $3+1$ by $2+2$. Therefore
>
> $$
> 100=32\cdot 3+2\cdot 2.
> $$
>
> Hence the optimal split is
>
> $$
> 100=3+3+\cdots+3+2+2
> $$
>
> with $32$ piles of size $3$ and $2$ piles of size $2$.
>
> The maximum product is
>
> $$
> 3^{32}\cdot 2^2 = 4\cdot 3^{32}.
> $$
>
> ### Final answer for Part 1
>
> Split the $100$ chips into $32$ piles of $3$ chips and $2$ piles of $2$ chips. The maximum product is
>
> $$
> 4\cdot 3^{32}.
> $$
>

>
> ## 2. Flipping four coins
>
> Let $H$ be the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped. Then
>
> $$
> H\sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12),
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \Pr(H=k)=\binom{4}{k}\frac1{16}, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> The payoff from a flip is the number of heads.
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> E[H]=4\cdot \frac12=2.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2(a). You may reflip all $4$ coins once
>
> You first flip the $4$ coins and observe $H$. Then you may either keep the current payoff $H$, or discard it and reflip all $4$ coins once. If you reflip, the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[H]=2.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - if the current value is greater than $2$, keep it;
> - if it is less than $2$, reflip;
> - if it equals $2$, you are indifferent.
>
> So the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Reflip if } H=0 \text{ or } 1; \quad
> \text{keep if } H=3 \text{ or } 4; \quad
> \text{indifferent if } H=2.
> $$
>
> ### Expected value under this strategy
>
> The expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[\max(H,2)].
> $$
>
> Compute it from the distribution:
>
> $$
> \Pr(H=0)=\frac1{16},\quad
> \Pr(H=1)=\frac4{16},\quad
> \Pr(H=2)=\frac6{16},\quad
> \Pr(H=3)=\frac4{16},\quad
> \Pr(H=4)=\frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> E[\max(H,2)]
> =
> 2\left(\frac1{16}+\frac4{16}+\frac6{16}\right)
> +3\cdot \frac4{16}
> +4\cdot \frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\max(H,2)]
> =
> 2\cdot \frac{11}{16}
> +\frac{12}{16}
> +\frac{4}{16}
> =
> \frac{22+12+4}{16}
> =
> \frac{38}{16}
> =
> \frac{19}{8}.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{19}{8}=2.375.
> $$
>
> ### Final answer for Part 2(a)
>
> Reflip if you see $0$ or $1$ heads, keep if you see $3$ or $4$ heads, and be indifferent at $2$ heads.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2(b). You may reflip as many times as you want, paying $\text{\textdollar}1$ each time
>
> Now each time you choose to reflip, you pay $\text{\textdollar}1$ and then receive a fresh draw with expected payoff $2$. Thus the expected **net** value of choosing to reflip from any state is
>
> $$
> -1 + E[H] = -1+2 = 1.
> $$
>
> So after observing $H$, compare:
>
> - keeping gives value $H$;
> - reflipping gives expected value $1$.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - if $H>1$, keep;
> - if $H<1$, reflip;
> - if $H=1$, you are indifferent.
>
> So the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Reflip only when } H=0;
> \quad
> \text{keep when } H=2,3,4;
> \quad
> \text{indifferent when } H=1.
> $$
>
> ### Why repeated reflipping does not change the threshold
>
> Suppose the optimal expected value before a fresh decision is $V$. After seeing $H$, you choose between
>
> - stopping for payoff $H$, or
> - paying $1$ and starting over, for expected value $V-1$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V = E[\max(H, V-1)].
> $$
>
> Trying the threshold above gives $V-1=1$, so
>
> $$
> V=E[\max(H,1)].
> $$
>
> Now compute:
>
> $$
> E[\max(H,1)]
> =
> 1\left(\frac1{16}+\frac4{16}\right)
> +2\cdot \frac6{16}
> +3\cdot \frac4{16}
> +4\cdot \frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> E[\max(H,1)]
> =
> \frac5{16}+\frac{12}{16}+\frac{12}{16}+\frac4{16}
> =
> \frac{33}{16}.
> $$
>
> So indeed
>
> $$
> V=\frac{33}{16},
> \qquad
> V-1=\frac{17}{16}>1.
> $$
>
> To find the exact optimal threshold, solve the fixed-point equation directly. Since the continuation value is $V-1$, the stopping rule is to keep whenever $H\ge V-1$ and reroll otherwise. Because $H$ is integer-valued, it is natural to test whether $1<V-1\le 2$, i.e. whether one should reroll on $0$ and $1$, but keep $2,3,4$.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V
> =
> 2\cdot \frac{6}{16}
> +3\cdot \frac{4}{16}
> +4\cdot \frac{1}{16}
> +(V-1)\left(\frac{1}{16}+\frac{4}{16}\right).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> V
> =
> \frac{12+12+4}{16}+\frac{5}{16}(V-1)
> =
> \frac{28}{16}+\frac{5}{16}V-\frac{5}{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V=\frac{23}{16}+\frac{5}{16}V,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \frac{11}{16}V=\frac{23}{16},
> \qquad
> V=\frac{23}{11}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V-1=\frac{12}{11},
> $$
>
> which indeed lies between $1$ and $2$. Therefore the correct optimal rule is to reflip when $H=0$ or $1$, and keep when $H=2,3,4$.
>
> ### Final answer for Part 2(b)
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Reflip if } H=0 \text{ or } 1,
> \qquad
> \text{keep if } H=2,3,4.
> $$
>
> The corresponding optimal expected net payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{23}{11}.
> $$
>

> ## Final answers
>
> 1. The chips should be split into
>
> $$
> 32 \text{ piles of } 3 \text{ and } 2 \text{ piles of } 2,
> $$
>
> giving maximum product
>
> $$
> 4\cdot 3^{32}.
> $$
>
> 2. For the coin game:
>    - **(a)** Reflip if you get $0$ or $1$ heads; keep if you get $3$ or $4$; indifferent at $2$.
>    - **(b)** Reflip if you get $0$ or $1$ heads; keep if you get $2$, $3$, or $4$.
