> [!example] Question 568 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If you toss 4 coins and receive 1 dollar for each heads, how much are you willing to pay to play the game? What is your answer if you have the option to toss one coin again, or two coins again?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=569)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $X$ denote the number of heads among the $4$ fair coin tosses, and let $T=4-X$ denote the number of tails. The natural amount to pay for the game is its expected payoff.
>
> ## 1. Tossing 4 fair coins, no option to redo any toss
>
> Each coin pays $1$ dollar if it lands heads. For one fair coin, the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff from one coin}] = 1\cdot \frac12 + 0\cdot \frac12 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> With $4$ independent coins, the total expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[X] = 4 \cdot \frac12 = 2.
> $$
>
> So a fair price to pay is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. With the option to toss one coin again
>
> Here "toss one coin again" means: after seeing all $4$ results, you may pick one coin that landed tails and toss that single coin one more time, keeping whatever it shows.
>
> If there are no tails at all ($T=0$), there is nothing to gain from the option. If $T\ge 1$, the option is always worth using, since redoing a tail turns it into a head with probability $\frac12$ (a gain of $1$) and leaves it tails with probability $\frac12$ (a gain of $0$), for an expected gain of $\frac12$ whenever at least one tail is available. So
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = E[X] + \frac12\,P(T\ge 1).
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> P(T\ge 1) = 1-P(T=0) = 1-\left(\frac12\right)^4 = 1-\frac1{16}=\frac{15}{16},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = 2+\frac12\cdot\frac{15}{16}=2+\frac{15}{32}=\frac{79}{32}\approx 2.469.
> $$
>
> So the fair price to pay is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{79}{32}\approx 2.469\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. With the option to toss up to two coins again
>
> Now you may pick up to two of the tails and redo each of them once (if fewer than two tails occur, you simply redo all of them). The number of coins you actually get to redo is $\min(T,2)$, and each redo independently gains $\frac12$ in expectation, so
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = E[X] + \frac12\,E[\min(T,2)].
> $$
>
> Since $T$ is Binomial with parameters $4$ and $\frac12$,
>
> $$
> P(T=0)=\frac1{16},\quad P(T=1)=\frac4{16},\quad P(T=2)=\frac6{16},\quad P(T=3)=\frac4{16},\quad P(T=4)=\frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> The corresponding values of $\min(T,2)$ are $0,1,2,2,2$, so
>
> $$
> E[\min(T,2)]=\frac{0\cdot1+1\cdot4+2\cdot6+2\cdot4+2\cdot1}{16}=\frac{4+12+8+2}{16}=\frac{26}{16}=\frac{13}{8}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = 2+\frac12\cdot\frac{13}{8}=2+\frac{13}{16}=\frac{45}{16}=2.8125.
> $$
>
> So the fair price to pay is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{45}{16}=2.8125\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{2\text{ dollars},\quad \frac{79}{32}\approx 2.469\text{ dollars},\quad \frac{45}{16}=2.8125\text{ dollars}}
> $$
>
> for the cases of no redo, one redo of a tail, and up to two redos of tails, respectively.
