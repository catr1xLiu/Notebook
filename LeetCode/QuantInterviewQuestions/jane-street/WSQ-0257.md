> [!example] Question 257 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected value of an optional reroll of a fair six-sided die, given that your payout is the number of pips shown on the die? In other words, how much would you pay for the option to reroll once, given that your final payout is the number shown on the die?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=258)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ denote the outcome of the initial fair die roll, and suppose you may choose, after seeing $X$, whether to keep it or pay a fee $c$ for a reroll. If you reroll, the new outcome is again uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, and your payment is the number of pips on the final die.
>
> The value of the reroll option is the maximum fee $c$ for which you would still be willing to use the option optimally.
>
> ## Step 1: Expected value of a fresh roll
>
> For a fair die,
> $$
> E[\text{fresh roll}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, if your current roll is $x$, then:
>
> - keeping it gives payoff $x$;
> - rerolling gives expected payoff $3.5 - c$.
>
> So you should reroll exactly when
> $$
> 3.5 - c > x.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, for a given fee $c$, there is a cutoff: reroll low outcomes, keep high ones.
>
> ## Step 2: Find the value of the option itself
>
> The value of having a free optional reroll is
> $$
> E\big[\max(X, 3.5)\big] - E[X].
> $$
>
> Since without the option your expected payoff is just
> $$
> E[X] = 3.5,
> $$
> we compute $E[\max(X,3.5)]$.
>
> For each possible initial roll:
> - if $X=1,2,3$, you reroll, giving expected value $3.5$;
> - if $X=4,5,6$, you keep the roll.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E[\text{payoff with free option}]
> = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> 3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6 = 25.5,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E[\text{payoff with free option}] = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore the value of the option is
> $$
> 4.25 - 3.5 = 0.75.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The most you should be willing to pay for the optional reroll is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$0.75}
> $$
>
> assuming each pip is worth one dollar.
