> [!example] Question 146 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Two dice are rolled. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers shown is a perfect square?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=147)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two fair six-sided dice show outcomes $a$ and $b$, where $a,b \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.  
> Assume the question asks for the probability that the **sum** $a+b$ is a perfect square.
>
> ## Step 1: Count all possible outcomes
>
> Since each die has $6$ possible outcomes and the dice are independent, the total number of equally likely outcomes is
>
> $$
> 6 \times 6 = 36.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Identify possible perfect-square sums
>
> The sum of two dice ranges from $2$ to $12$.  
> The perfect squares in this range are
>
> $$
> 4,\; 9.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Count outcomes giving each perfect-square sum
>
> ### Sum $4$
> The pairs are
>
> $$
> (1,3),\ (2,2),\ (3,1),
> $$
>
> so there are $3$ outcomes.
>
> ### Sum $9$
> The pairs are
>
> $$
> (3,6),\ (4,5),\ (5,4),\ (6,3),
> $$
>
> so there are $4$ outcomes.
>
> Thus the total number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$
> 3+4=7.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{perfect-square sum})=\frac{7}{36}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{7}{36}}
> $$
