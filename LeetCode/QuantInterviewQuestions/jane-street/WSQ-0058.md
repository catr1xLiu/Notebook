> [!example] Question 58 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

In a game, I toss a coin 5 times and you toss it 4 times. If I get more heads than you, I win; otherwise, you win. What is the probability that you will win?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=59)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained in $5$ tosses, and let $Y$ be the number of heads obtained in $4$ tosses. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(5,\tfrac12), \qquad Y \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12),
> $$
>
> and the two variables are independent.
>
> The second player wins whenever $Y \ge X$. Thus we seek
>
> $$
> P(Y \ge X).
> $$
>
> Since all tosses are fair,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{\binom{5}{k}}{2^5}, \qquad P(Y=j)=\frac{\binom{4}{j}}{2^4}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(Y \ge X)=\sum_{k=0}^5 P(X=k)\,P(Y\ge k).
> $$
>
> Compute the needed tail probabilities for $Y$:
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 0)=1,
> $$
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 1)=1-P(Y=0)=1-\frac1{16}=\frac{15}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 2)=\frac{\binom42+\binom43+\binom44}{16}
> =\frac{6+4+1}{16}
> =\frac{11}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 3)=\frac{\binom43+\binom44}{16}
> =\frac{4+1}{16}
> =\frac{5}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 4)=\frac{\binom44}{16}=\frac1{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge 5)=0.
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{1,\,5,\,10,\,10,\,5,\,1}{32}
> \quad \text{for } k=0,1,2,3,4,5.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge X)
> =
> \frac1{32}\cdot 1
> +\frac5{32}\cdot\frac{15}{16}
> +\frac{10}{32}\cdot\frac{11}{16}
> +\frac{10}{32}\cdot\frac{5}{16}
> +\frac5{32}\cdot\frac1{16}
> +\frac1{32}\cdot 0.
> $$
>
> Put everything over the common denominator $512$:
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge X)
> =
> \frac{16+75+110+50+5}{512}
> =
> \frac{256}{512}
> =
> \frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence the chance that the second player wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
