> [!example] Question 482 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have a drawer with an infinite number of socks in two colors, with each color equally likely. What is the expected number of socks you must draw individually from the drawer before you obtain a matching pair?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=483)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two colors be $A$ and $B$, each drawn with probability $\tfrac12$, independently from draw to draw.
>
> We seek the expected number of draws until the first time a matching pair is obtained.
>
> ## Step 1: Observe the earliest possible match
>
> - After the first draw, no pair is possible.
> - After the second draw, a pair is formed if the second sock matches the first.
>
> Since there are only two colors, if the second sock does **not** match the first, then the two socks drawn are one of each color. In that case, the **third** sock must match one of them, so a matching pair is guaranteed by the third draw.
>
> Thus the number of draws $N$ until a match is found can only be:
>
> - $N=2$, with probability $\tfrac12$,
> - $N=3$, with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expectation
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E[N] = 2 \cdot \frac12 + 3 \cdot \frac12
> = 1 + \frac32
> = \frac52.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{2}}
> $$
>
> So the expected number of attempts is $2.5$.
