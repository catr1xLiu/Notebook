> [!example] Question 591 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

There is a token you can purchase for \$XX, which allows you to propose a bet. If you win the bet, you retain the token for future use. If you lose, you may use the token to recover half of your bet. Based on the chips both sides have on that day, how much is the token worth?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=592)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Interpret the token as a **one-time insurance option** on an all-in bet.
>
> Suppose:
>
> - you have $A$ chips,
> - your opponent has $B$ chips,
> - before the hand starts, you may pay some amount to buy the token.
>
> You then play a bet for all the chips that can actually be contested. Since one player cannot win more than the other possesses, the effective all-in amount is
>
> $$
> m=\min(A,B).
> $$
>
> So without the token:
>
> - if you win, your bankroll increases by $m$,
> - if you lose, your bankroll decreases by $m$.
>
> Thus your wealth after the hand is:
>
> - win: $A+m$,
> - lose: $A-m$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Effect of the token
>
> The token changes only the losing outcome:
>
> - if you win, nothing changes; you keep the token for future use,
> - if you lose, you may use the token to recover half of the money you lost.
>
> Since the loss on the hand is $m$, recovering half means receiving
>
> $$
> \frac{m}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, with the token, the losing outcome becomes
>
> $$
> A-m+\frac{m}{2}=A-\frac{m}{2}.
> $$
>
> So compared with not having the token, the token adds value only in the event of a loss, and that added value is exactly
>
> $$
> \frac{m}{2}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Fair value of the token
>
> Let $p$ be your probability of winning the bet.
>
> Then the token pays:
>
> - $0$ if you win,
> - $\dfrac{m}{2}$ if you lose.
>
> Hence its expected value is
>
> $$
> (1-p)\frac{m}{2}.
> $$
>
> So the fair price of the token is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1-p}{2}\min(A,B)}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Special case: even bet
>
> If the bet is fair, so that
>
> $$
> p=\frac12,
> $$
>
> then
>
> $$
> \text{token value}
> =
> \frac{1}{2}\cdot \frac{1}{2}\min(A,B)
> =
> \frac14 \min(A,B).
> $$
>
> Thus in the symmetric $50$-$50$ case, the token is worth
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac14 \min(A,B)}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If you have $A$ chips and your opponent has $B$ chips, and your probability of winning the bet is $p$, then the token’s fair value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1-p}{2}\min(A,B)}.
> $$
>
> In particular, for an even-money bet with win probability $p=\frac12$, the token is worth
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac14 \min(A,B)}.
> $$
