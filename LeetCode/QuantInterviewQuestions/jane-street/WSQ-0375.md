> [!example] Question 375 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

On a multiple-choice exam with three possible answers for each of the five questions, what is the probability that a student would get four or more correct answers just by guessing?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=376)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each question has probability
>
> $$
> p=\frac{1}{3}
> $$
>
> of being answered correctly by guessing, and the five questions are independent. Thus the number of correct answers $X$ follows a binomial distribution:
>
> $$
> X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n=5,p=\tfrac13).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=P(X=4)+P(X=5).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=4)=\binom{5}{4}\left(\frac13\right)^4\left(\frac23\right)^1
> =5\cdot \frac{1}{81}\cdot \frac{2}{3}
> =\frac{10}{243},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(X=5)=\binom{5}{5}\left(\frac13\right)^5
> =\frac{1}{243}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=\frac{10}{243}+\frac{1}{243}=\frac{11}{243}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{243}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> 0.0453.
> $$
