> [!example] Question 648 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

There exists a six-sided die. The die is rolled, and you are paid \$x if the die shows x dots (e.g., if you roll a 3, then you are paid \$3). What is a fair price for this game?

Additional layer: After rolling the die once, you have the option of taking the rolled amount or rolling again. However, if you roll again, you must take the amount corresponding to the second roll. What is a fair price for this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=649)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Single roll
>
> A fair price is the expected payoff of one roll of a fair six-sided die.
>
> Let $X$ be the payoff. Then $X \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, each with probability $\frac{1}{6}$. Thus
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So the fair price for the first game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$3.50}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Option to roll again once
>
> Now after the first roll, you may either:
>
> - keep the first amount, or
> - reroll once and be forced to accept the second result.
>
> Let $Y$ be the second-roll payoff. Since the second roll is an ordinary fair die roll,
>
> $$
> E[Y] = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, after observing the first roll $x$, the optimal decision is:
>
> - keep $x$ if $x \ge 3.5$,
> - reroll if $x < 3.5$.
>
> Since $x$ must be an integer, this means:
>
> - reroll on $1,2,3$,
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> Now compute the expected value under this optimal strategy:
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \frac{1}{6}\big(3.5 + 3.5 + 3.5 + 4 + 5 + 6\big).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \frac{10.5 + 15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fair price for the second game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$4.25}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - Single-roll game: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}3.50}$
> - Game with optional reroll: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}4.25}$
