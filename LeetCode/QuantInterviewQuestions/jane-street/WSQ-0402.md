> [!example] Question 402 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If there are N points on a circle and a chord is drawn between each pair of points, how many regions does the circle get subdivided into?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=403)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume that the $N$ points are distinct and arranged so that no three chords intersect at a single interior point. This is the general-position case.
>
> ## Approach
>
> Count how the number of regions increases as chords are added. Each new chord is cut into several segments by its intersections with previously drawn chords, and each segment creates one new region.
>
> Thus, the total number of regions equals
>
> $$
> 1 + \text{(number of chords)} + \text{(number of interior intersection points)}.
> $$
>
> So we need to count:
>
> 1. the number of chords determined by $N$ points;
> 2. the number of interior intersections of those chords.
>
> ## Step 1: Count the chords
>
> Any chord is determined by choosing $2$ of the $N$ points, so the number of chords is
>
> $$
> \binom{N}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Count the interior intersections
>
> An interior intersection occurs exactly when we choose $4$ points on the circle: the two diagonals of the quadrilateral formed by those $4$ points intersect once inside the circle.
>
> Thus, each choice of $4$ points gives exactly one interior intersection, and every interior intersection arises this way. Therefore the number of interior intersections is
>
> $$
> \binom{N}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Add the contributions
>
> Starting from one region (the whole disk), each chord adds one region plus one additional region for each interior intersection on that chord. Summing over all chords gives
>
> $$
> R(N) = 1 + \binom{N}{2} + \binom{N}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The number of regions is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 + \binom{N}{2} + \binom{N}{4}}
> $$
>
> provided that no three chords meet at the same interior point.
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 + \frac{N(N-1)}{2} + \frac{N(N-1)(N-2)(N-3)}{24}}.
> $$
>
> For example:
>
> - $N=1$: $1$
> - $N=2$: $2$
> - $N=3$: $4$
> - $N=4$: $8$
> - $N=5$: $16$
