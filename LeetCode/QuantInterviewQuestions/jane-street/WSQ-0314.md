> [!example] Question 314 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the optimal way to bet on the outcomes of a six-sided die roll?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=315)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine an *optimal* betting strategy on a fair six-sided die, one must first specify the **payoff structure**. Without knowing how much each possible bet pays, there is no unique optimal strategy.
>
> ## 1. Model
>
> Let the die outcomes be $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each with probability
>
> $$
> P(i)=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Suppose a bettor may place money on various events $A$ (for example, “the outcome is $1$,” or “the outcome is even”), and a bet on event $A$ pays according to some odds.
>
> If one stakes $1$ unit on an event $A$ with probability $p=P(A)$ and receives $r$ units of **net profit** when $A$ occurs, then the expected profit is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{profit}] = p\,r - (1-p).
> $$
>
> A bet is favorable precisely when
>
> $$
> p\,r - (1-p) > 0,
> $$
>
> that is,
>
> $$
> r > \frac{1-p}{p}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, if the total return including the stake is $b$, then the expected value is positive when
>
> $$
> p\,b > 1.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Fair odds
>
> For a fair die, a fair bet on a single face should pay net odds of $5:1$, since
>
> $$
> P(\text{chosen face})=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Then the expected profit from staking $1$ unit on one face is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\cdot 5 - \frac{5}{6}\cdot 1 = 0.
> $$
>
> Likewise, any correctly priced bet on the die has expected profit $0$.
>
> Hence:
>
> - if the casino offers **fair odds**, every betting strategy has expected profit $0$;
> - if the casino offers **worse than fair odds**, every strategy has negative expected profit;
> - if some bets are **better than fair odds**, one should place all stake on the bet with the highest positive expected value.
>
> ## 3. Optimal strategy under common criteria
>
> ### Maximizing expected wealth for a single roll
> If there is a single bet and no utility or risk constraint, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - bet nothing if all expected values are nonpositive;
> - otherwise, bet everything on the wager with the greatest expected return.
>
> ### Maximizing long-run growth
> If one repeatedly bets and favorable odds are available, the standard optimal criterion is the **Kelly criterion**.
>
> For a bet that wins with probability $p$ and pays net odds $r$, the optimal fraction $f^*$ of wealth to bet is
>
> $$
> f^*=\frac{pr-(1-p)}{r},
> $$
>
> provided this is positive; otherwise $f^*=0$.
>
> For a single-face die bet, $p=\frac{1}{6}$. Thus
>
> $$
> f^*=\frac{\frac{r}{6}-\frac{5}{6}}{r}
> =\frac{r-5}{6r}.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - if $r<5$, do not bet;
> - if $r=5$, the edge is zero, so do not bet under Kelly;
> - if $r>5$, bet the fraction
>
> $$
> \frac{r-5}{6r}
> $$
>
> of current wealth on that face.
>
> ## 4. Final conclusion
>
> There is no unique “optimal way” to bet on a six-sided die without specifying the payoff odds and the objective.
>
> - With a **fair die and fair payouts**, no betting pattern is better than any other in expectation; the expected gain is $0$.
> - With **unfavorable payouts**, the optimal strategy is **not to bet**.
> - With **favorable payouts**, the optimal strategy is to bet on the outcome or event with the highest positive expected value; for repeated play, the Kelly-optimal fraction is
>
> $$
> f^*=\frac{pr-(1-p)}{r}.
> $$
>
> Thus, under standard gambling assumptions, the optimal way to bet on a six-sided die is:
>
> $$
> \text{Bet only when the offered odds exceed the fair odds implied by } \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> For a single-face bet, that means the net payoff must exceed $5:1$.
