> [!example] Question 53 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given an unfair coin that lands heads with probability 2/3 and tails with probability 1/3, how can you use it to simulate a fair coin toss?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=54)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Use **von Neumann’s method**.
>
> ### Procedure
> Flip the unfair coin **twice**:
>
> - If the outcome is **HT**, output **H**
> - If the outcome is **TH**, output **T**
> - If the outcome is **HH** or **TT**, ignore the result and repeat with two new flips
>
> ### Why this works
> Let $P(H)=\frac{2}{3}$ and $P(T)=\frac{1}{3}$.
>
> Then
> $$
> P(HT)=P(H)P(T)=\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{9},
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(TH)=P(T)P(H)=\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}=\frac{2}{9}.
> $$
>
> Thus the two accepted outcomes, $HT$ and $TH$, are equally likely. Since one is mapped to heads and the other to tails, the output is fair.
>
> ### Conclusion
> A fair coin can be simulated by repeatedly flipping the biased coin twice and using:
>
> $$
> HT \mapsto H,\qquad TH \mapsto T,
> $$
>
> while discarding $HH$ and $TT$.
