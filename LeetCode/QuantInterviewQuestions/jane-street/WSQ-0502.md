> [!example] Question 502 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Imagine I flip 100 coins in sequence, and you must guess the exact sequence. You can ask one yes/no question. What question should you ask to maximize your probability of correctly guessing the sequence?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=503)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To maximize the probability of correctly identifying one sequence out of all $2^{100}$ possible outcomes using a single yes/no question, the question should split the set of possible sequences into two parts as evenly as possible.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> A yes/no question can produce only two answers. After hearing the answer, one can make only one final guess. Thus:
>
> - If the answer is “yes,” one must choose one sequence associated with that answer.
> - If the answer is “no,” one must choose one sequence associated with that answer.
>
> So with one yes/no question, at best one can arrange things so that:
>
> - one particular sequence is guessed if the answer is “yes,” and
> - another particular sequence is guessed if the answer is “no.”
>
> This gives at most two possible sequences that can ever be guessed correctly, one for each answer.
>
> Since all $2^{100}$ sequences are equally likely, the largest possible success probability is therefore
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{2^{100}} = \frac{1}{2^{99}}.
> $$
>
> ## Optimal question
>
> Choose any two distinct sequences, say $A$ and $B$, and ask:
>
> $$
> \text{“Is the sequence } A \text{?”}
> $$
>
> - If the answer is “yes,” guess $A$.
> - If the answer is “no,” guess $B$.
>
> This succeeds exactly when the true sequence is either $A$ or $B$, so the success probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{2^{100}} = \frac{1}{2^{99}}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> An optimal question is any yes/no question that singles out one sequence in one branch and another sequence in the other branch, for example:
>
> $$
> \text{“Is the sequence } HHH\cdots H \text{?”}
> $$
>
> and if the answer is “no,” guess some other fixed sequence such as $TTT\cdots T$.
>
> The maximum possible probability of guessing correctly is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2^{99}}}.
> $$
