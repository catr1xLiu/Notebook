> [!example] Question 279 · Jane Street
> ## Summer Intern Interview

I have a 12-sided die and you have a 20-sided die. Each of us gets up to two rolls, and on either roll, we can choose to stop and keep the number from that roll. Whoever has the higher number wins, with ties going to the person with the 12-sided die. What is the probability that the person with the 20-sided die wins this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=280)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the $12$-sided player be $A$ and the $20$-sided player be $B$.
>
> Each player may roll up to two times and may stop after the first roll. Thus each player faces the same type of optimal stopping problem: after seeing the first roll, either keep it, or reject it and take the second roll, which must then be accepted. Since the second roll is independent and must be accepted, optimal play is a **threshold rule**: keep sufficiently large first rolls, reroll sufficiently small ones.
>
> We first find the pair of thresholds that are mutual best responses, then compute the resulting win probability for the $20$-sided die.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Final-value distribution from a threshold
>
> If a player with an $n$-sided die keeps the first roll iff it is at least $t$, then the final value distribution is:
>
> - for $x<t$,
>   $$
>   \Pr(\text{final}=x)=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{n^2},
>   $$
>   since this occurs only by first rolling below $t$ and then rolling $x$ on the second roll;
>
> - for $x\ge t$,
>   $$
>   \Pr(\text{final}=x)=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{t-1}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{n}
>   =\frac{n+t-1}{n^2},
>   $$
>   since one may keep $x$ on the first roll, or reroll after a low first roll and then land on $x$.
>
> If $A$ keeps on first-roll values at least $a$, and $B$ keeps on first-roll values at least $b$, write $F(t)=\Pr(A\le t)$ and $G(t)=\Pr(B\le t)$ for the resulting final-value distributions.
>
> Since ties go to $A$: $A$ wins by keeping $x$ with probability $G(x)$, or by rerolling with probability $\tfrac1{12}\sum_{y=1}^{12}G(y)$, so $A$ keeps $x$ iff $G(x)$ exceeds that average. Likewise $B$ wins by keeping $x$ with probability $F(x-1)$ (since ties favor $A$), or by rerolling with probability $\tfrac1{20}\sum_{y=1}^{20}F(y-1)$, so $B$ keeps $x$ iff $F(x-1)$ exceeds that average.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Verifying the equilibrium thresholds $a=8$, $b=10$
>
> **Distribution of $A$ with $a=8$.** If $A$ rerolls on $1$–$7$ and keeps $8$–$12$, then
> $$
> \Pr(A=x)=
> \begin{cases}
> \frac{7}{144}, & 1\le x\le 7,\\[4pt]
> \frac{19}{144}, & 8\le x\le 12,
> \end{cases}
> \qquad
> F(t)=
> \begin{cases}
> 0, & t\le 0,\\[4pt]
> \frac{7t}{144}, & 1\le t\le 7,\\[4pt]
> \frac{49}{144}+\frac{19(t-7)}{144}, & 8\le t\le 12,\\[4pt]
> 1, & t\ge 12.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> **Check $B$'s best response.** $B$'s reroll value is
> $$
> \frac{1}{20}\sum_{x=1}^{20}F(x-1).
> $$
> Splitting the sum: $F(0)$ through $F(7)$ contribute $\frac{7}{144}(0+1+\cdots+7)=\frac{49}{36}$; then $F(8),\dots,F(12)$ are $\frac{68}{144},\frac{87}{144},\frac{106}{144},\frac{125}{144},1$, summing to $\frac{265}{72}$; and $F(13),\dots,F(19)$ each equal $1$, contributing $7$. In total,
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{20}F(x-1)=\frac{49}{36}+\frac{265}{72}+7=\frac{289}{24},
> $$
> so the reroll value is $\dfrac{289}{480}\approx 0.60208$. Comparing:
> $$
> F(8)=\frac{68}{144}=\frac{17}{36}\approx 0.4722<\frac{289}{480}<F(9)=\frac{87}{144}=\frac{29}{48}\approx 0.6042,
> $$
> so $B$'s best response is to keep iff $x\ge 10$ — confirming $b=10$.
>
> **Distribution of $B$ with $b=10$.** If $B$ rerolls on $1$–$9$ and keeps $10$–$20$, then
> $$
> \Pr(B=y)=
> \begin{cases}
> \frac{9}{400}, & 1\le y\le 9,\\[4pt]
> \frac{29}{400}, & 10\le y\le 20,
> \end{cases}
> \qquad
> G(y)=
> \begin{cases}
> \frac{9y}{400}, & 1\le y\le 9,\\[4pt]
> \frac{81}{400}+\frac{29(y-9)}{400}, & 10\le y\le 20.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> **Check $A$'s best response.** $A$'s reroll value is
> $$
> \frac{1}{12}\sum_{y=1}^{12}G(y).
> $$
> For $y=1,\dots,9$: $\sum_{y=1}^9\frac{9y}{400}=\frac{9}{400}\cdot 45=\frac{405}{400}$. For $y=10,11,12$: $G(10)=\frac{110}{400}$, $G(11)=\frac{139}{400}$, $G(12)=\frac{168}{400}$, summing to $\frac{417}{400}$. In total,
> $$
> \sum_{y=1}^{12}G(y)=\frac{405+417}{400}=\frac{822}{400}=\frac{411}{200},
> $$
> so the reroll value is $\dfrac{1}{12}\cdot\dfrac{411}{200}=\dfrac{137}{800}\approx 0.17125$. Comparing:
> $$
> G(7)=\frac{63}{400}=0.1575<\frac{137}{800}<G(8)=\frac{72}{400}=0.18,
> $$
> so $A$'s best response is to keep iff $x\ge 8$ — confirming $a=8$.
>
> Since each threshold is the best response to the other, $(a,b)=(8,10)$ is the equilibrium pair of thresholds:
>
> - $A$ (the $12$-sided die): keep $8,9,10,11,12$; reroll $1$ through $7$.
> - $B$ (the $20$-sided die): keep $10,11,\dots,20$; reroll $1$ through $9$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Computing the probability that the $20$-sided die wins
>
> Since ties go to $A$, player $B$ wins exactly when $B>A$:
> $$
> \Pr(B\text{ wins})=\sum_{y=1}^{20}\Pr(B=y)\,F(y-1).
> $$
>
> Split this into $B$'s reroll region ($y=1,\dots,9$) and keep region ($y=10,\dots,20$):
>
> **First part ($y=1,\dots,9$):** this uses $F(0),F(1),\dots,F(8)$:
> $$
> \sum_{y=1}^{9}F(y-1)=\underbrace{\left(0+\tfrac{7}{144}+\tfrac{14}{144}+\cdots+\tfrac{49}{144}\right)}_{F(0)\text{ through }F(7)}+\,F(8)=\frac{49}{36}+\frac{68}{144}=\frac{196+68}{144}=\frac{264}{144}=\frac{11}{6}.
> $$
> So this part contributes
> $$
> \frac{9}{400}\cdot\frac{11}{6}=\frac{33}{800}.
> $$
>
> **Second part ($y=10,\dots,20$, 11 terms):** this uses $F(9),F(10),F(11)$, and then $F(12),F(13),\dots,F(19)$ — the latter being $8$ terms, all equal to $1$ since $A$'s die never exceeds $12$:
> $$
> \sum_{y=10}^{20}F(y-1)=F(9)+F(10)+F(11)+8\cdot 1
> =\frac{87+106+125}{144}+8
> =\frac{318}{144}+8
> =\frac{53}{24}+\frac{192}{24}
> =\frac{245}{24}.
> $$
> So this part contributes
> $$
> \frac{29}{400}\cdot\frac{245}{24}=\frac{7105}{9600}.
> $$
>
> **Total:**
> $$
> \Pr(B\text{ wins})=\frac{33}{800}+\frac{7105}{9600}=\frac{396}{9600}+\frac{7105}{9600}=\frac{7501}{9600}.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability that the $20$-sided die wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{7501}{9600}\approx 0.781354.}
> $$
