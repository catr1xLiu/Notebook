> [!example] Question 625 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Three coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting at least two heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=626)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability of getting **at least two heads** when $3$ fair coins are tossed, count the favorable outcomes and divide by the total number of outcomes.
>
> ### Step 1: Total number of outcomes
> Each coin has $2$ possible outcomes, so with $3$ coins the total number of outcomes is
>
> $$
> 2^3 = 8.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Favorable outcomes
> “At least two heads” means getting either:
>
> - exactly $2$ heads, or
> - exactly $3$ heads.
>
> The outcomes with exactly $2$ heads are:
>
> $$
> \{HHT,\ HTH,\ THH\},
> $$
>
> so there are $3$ such outcomes.
>
> The outcome with exactly $3$ heads is:
>
> $$
> \{HHH\},
> $$
>
> so there is $1$ such outcome.
>
> Thus, the number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$
> 3 + 1 = 4.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Compute the probability
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least two heads}) = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ### Final Answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> $$
