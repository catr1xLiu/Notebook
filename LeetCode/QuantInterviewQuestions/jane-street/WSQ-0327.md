> [!example] Question 327 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If 4 coins are flipped, what is the probability that at most 2 heads are flipped?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=328)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \text{Binomial}(n=4, p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> The probability of getting at most $2$ heads is
>
> $$
> P(X \le 2)=P(X=0)+P(X=1)+P(X=2).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^4.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> P(X \le 2)
> = \left[\binom{4}{0}+\binom{4}{1}+\binom{4}{2}\right]\left(\frac12\right)^4
> = \left[1+4+6\right]\frac{1}{16}
> = \frac{11}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{16}}.
> $$
