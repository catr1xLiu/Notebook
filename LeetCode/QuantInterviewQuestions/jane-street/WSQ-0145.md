> [!example] Question 145 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

There is a p% chance of raining on Saturday and a q% chance of raining on Sunday. What are the maximum and minimum probabilities of it raining on both days?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=146)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it rains on Saturday and $B$ the event that it rains on Sunday. Then
>
> $$
> P(A)=\frac{p}{100}, \qquad P(B)=\frac{q}{100}.
> $$
>
> We want the possible range of
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B),
> $$
>
> the probability that it rains on both days.
>
> ## Maximum value
>
> Since $A\cap B \subseteq A$ and $A\cap B \subseteq B$, we must have
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)\le P(A), \qquad P(A\cap B)\le P(B).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)\le \min\!\left(\frac{p}{100},\frac{q}{100}\right).
> $$
>
> This upper bound is attainable, so the maximum possible probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\min\!\left(\frac{p}{100},\frac{q}{100}\right)}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, in percent form:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\min(p,q)\% }.
> $$
>
> ## Minimum value
>
> Using
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B),
> $$
>
> and the fact that $P(A\cup B)\le 1$, we get
>
> $$
> P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\le 1.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)\ge P(A)+P(B)-1
> = \frac{p}{100}+\frac{q}{100}-1
> = \frac{p+q-100}{100}.
> $$
>
> Also, probabilities cannot be negative, so
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)\ge 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)\ge \max\!\left(0,\frac{p+q-100}{100}\right).
> $$
>
> This lower bound is attainable, so the minimum possible probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\max\!\left(0,\frac{p+q-100}{100}\right)}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, in percent form:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\max(0,\,p+q-100)\% }.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The probability of raining on both Saturday and Sunday can range from
>
> $$
> \boxed{\max(0,\,p+q-100)\%}
> $$
>
> to
>
> $$
> \boxed{\min(p,q)\% }.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - **Minimum** $= \boxed{\max(0,\,p+q-100)\%}$
> - **Maximum** $= \boxed{\min(p,q)\%}$
