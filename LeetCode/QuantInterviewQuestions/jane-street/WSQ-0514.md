> [!example] Question 514 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

There are two painters: Mr. Fast can paint a room in 1 hour, and Mr. Slow needs 1 hour 15 minutes. If they work together, how long does it take them to paint one room?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=515)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the whole job be painting one room.
>
> Mr. Fast’s rate is
> $$
> 1 \text{ room/hour}.
> $$
>
> Mr. Slow needs $1$ hour $15$ minutes, that is
> $$
> 1.25=\frac{5}{4} \text{ hours},
> $$
> so his rate is
> $$
> \frac{1}{5/4}=\frac{4}{5} \text{ room/hour}.
> $$
>
> Working together, their rates add:
> $$
> 1+\frac{4}{5}=\frac{9}{5} \text{ rooms/hour}.
> $$
>
> Hence the time to paint one room is the reciprocal:
> $$
> \frac{1}{9/5}=\frac{5}{9} \text{ hour}.
> $$
>
> Convert this to minutes:
> $$
> \frac{5}{9}\cdot 60=\frac{100}{3}=33\tfrac{1}{3} \text{ minutes}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the two painters together take
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{9}\text{ hour}=33\tfrac{1}{3}\text{ minutes}}
> $$
> to paint one room.
