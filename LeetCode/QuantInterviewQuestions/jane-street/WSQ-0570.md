> [!example] Question 570 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Suppose you have a sequence of 36 integers consisting only of 1's and 0's (e.g., 010101..., 10000100...). Can you construct a sequence where every subsequence of length 5 is unique? Assume the sequence starts with 00000. How would you construct such a sequence? For example, if you start with 00000100001..., it is not valid because it contains two identical subsequences (00001, 00001) of length 5.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=571)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make every contiguous subsequence of length $5$ appear at most once in a binary sequence, one uses a **de Bruijn sequence** of order $5$ on the alphabet $\{0,1\}$.
>
> There are exactly
>
> $$
> 2^5 = 32
> $$
>
> possible binary strings of length $5$. In a cyclic binary sequence, if every length-$5$ block appears exactly once, then the sequence has length $32$ cyclically. To write it as an ordinary linear sequence, one appends the first $4$ bits to the end, giving total length
>
> $$
> 32 + 4 = 36.
> $$
>
> So the problem is exactly to construct a binary de Bruijn sequence of order $5$ that begins with $00000$.
>
> ## Construction idea
>
> Consider the directed graph whose vertices are the binary strings of length $4$. From a vertex $a_1a_2a_3a_4$, there are two outgoing edges:
>
> - to $a_2a_3a_4 0$,
> - to $a_2a_3a_4 1$.
>
> Each edge corresponds to a binary string of length $5$, namely $a_1a_2a_3a_4b$. Thus:
>
> - vertices are length-$4$ strings,
> - edges are length-$5$ strings.
>
> A cyclic sequence in which every length-$5$ block occurs exactly once is obtained by finding an **Eulerian cycle** in this graph, since such a cycle uses each edge exactly once.
>
> Because every vertex has indegree $2$ and outdegree $2$, an Eulerian cycle exists.
>
> ## One explicit sequence
>
> A valid cyclic de Bruijn sequence of order $5$ is
>
> $$
> 00000100011001010011101011011111.
> $$
>
> This has length $32$ and contains every binary word of length $5$ exactly once, cyclically.
>
> To turn it into an ordinary length-$36$ sequence starting with $00000$, append the first $4$ bits, namely $0000$:
>
> $$
> 000001000110010100111010110111110000.
> $$
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Reading consecutive blocks of length $5$ in this length-$36$ sequence gives
>
> $$
> 36 - 5 + 1 = 32
> $$
>
> contiguous subsequences of length $5$, and they are exactly the $32$ possible binary strings of length $5$, each appearing once.
>
> Since the sequence begins with $00000$, it satisfies the required starting condition.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> One such sequence is
>
> $$
> \boxed{000001000110010100111010110111110000}
> $$
>
> and its $32$ consecutive subsequences of length $5$ are all distinct.
