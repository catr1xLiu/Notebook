> [!example] Question 380 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You have 4 coins. I flip them, and you receive one dollar for each heads. How much are you willing to pay to play this game? What if, after the initial flip, I give you the option to reflip any number of coins? What if I require you to reflip any number of coins of your choice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=381)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ denote the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped. The game pays $X$ dollars.
>
> The fair price to play is the expected payoff.
>
> ## 1. No reflip
>
> Since each coin is fair, the expected value from one coin is
> $$
> 0 \cdot \frac12 + 1 \cdot \frac12 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> With $4$ independent coins, the total expected payoff is
> $$
> E[X] = 4 \cdot \frac12 = 2.
> $$
>
> So the fair price is $2$ dollars.
>
> ## 2. You may reflip any coin
>
> Now after seeing the first result of each coin, you may choose whether to keep it or reflip it.
>
> Consider one coin:
>
> - If it lands heads, keeping it gives $1$ dollar.
> - If it lands tails, keeping it gives $0$, while reflipping gives expected value
> $$
> 0 \cdot \frac12 + 1 \cdot \frac12 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Thus the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep every head,
> - reflip every tail.
>
> So for one coin, the expected value is
> $$
> \frac12 \cdot 1 + \frac12 \cdot \frac12 = \frac12 + \frac14 = \frac34.
> $$
>
> For $4$ coins, the expected payoff is
> $$
> 4 \cdot \frac34 = 3.
> $$
>
> So the fair price is $3$ dollars.
>
> ## 3. You must reflip at least one coin
>
> Now interpret the rule as stated: you must reflip some coin (at least one), but you choose which and how many.
>
> Reflipping a coin that shows heads always hurts: it exchanges a guaranteed $1$ for an expected $\frac12$, a loss of $\frac12$. Reflipping a coin that shows tails always helps: it exchanges a guaranteed $0$ for an expected $\frac12$, a gain of $\frac12$. So, coin by coin, the optimal move is to reflip every tail and keep every head — exactly as in Part 2 — provided this satisfies the "reflip at least one" requirement.
>
> That requirement only bites when all $4$ coins already show heads, since then there are no tails to reflip and some head must be sacrificed. In that case, reflipping additional heads only compounds the loss, so the optimal choice is to reflip exactly one head, leaving expected value
> $$
> 3\cdot 1+1\cdot\frac12=\frac72.
> $$
>
> So the strategy is:
>
> - if at least one tail appears, reflip every tail (and only the tails);
> - if all four coins show heads, reflip exactly one of them.
>
> ### Computing the expected payoff
>
> Let $n$ be the number of heads on the initial flip, with
> $$
> P(n)=\binom{4}{n}\Big/16, \qquad n=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> For $n=0,1,2,3$ (at least one tail present), the strategy keeps the $n$ heads and reflips the $4-n$ tails, giving expected payoff
> $$
> n+(4-n)\cdot\frac12=2+\frac n2.
> $$
>
> For $n=4$, the expected payoff is $\frac72$ as computed above.
>
> Averaging over all $16$ equally likely initial outcomes, with $\binom40=1,\binom41=4,\binom42=6,\binom43=4,\binom44=1$:
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]=\frac{1(2)+4(2.5)+6(3)+4(3.5)}{16}+\frac{1}{16}\cdot\frac72
> =\frac{2+10+18+14}{16}+\frac{3.5}{16}
> =\frac{44}{16}+\frac{3.5}{16}
> =\frac{47.5}{16}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]=\frac{95}{32}=2.96875.
> $$
>
> So the fair price is $\dfrac{95}{32}$ dollars, that is, $2.96875$ dollars.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - No reflip: $2$ dollars
> - Optional reflip (reflip zero or more coins freely): $3$ dollars
> - Mandatory reflip (must reflip at least one coin, your choice which): $\dfrac{95}{32}=2.96875$ dollars
