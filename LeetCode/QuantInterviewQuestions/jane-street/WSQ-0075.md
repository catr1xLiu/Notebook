> [!example] Question 75 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

If two people play tic-tac-toe and both choose their moves randomly, what is the probability that the game ends in a draw?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=76)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability of a tie when both players choose uniformly at random from the available legal moves, model the game as a random traversal of the tic-tac-toe game tree.
>
> ## 1. What must be counted
>
> Each complete play of tic-tac-toe is determined by a sequence of legal moves. Since at each turn a player chooses uniformly among the empty squares, different game sequences do **not** all have the same probability, but each sequence has probability
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{7}\cdots
> $$
>
> up to the length of that game.
>
> Thus, the desired probability is
>
> $$
> P(\text{tie})=\sum_{\text{draw sequences}} P(\text{that sequence}).
> $$
>
> Equivalently, one can compute recursively from each board position:
>
> $$
> P(\text{tie from position})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\text{legal moves}} P(\text{tie after move}),
> $$
>
> where $m$ is the number of legal moves available, with boundary conditions:
>
> - if $X$ has already won or $O$ has already won, the probability of a tie is $0$,
> - if the board is full with no winner, the probability of a tie is $1$.
>
> Carrying this recursion through the entire game tree gives the exact probability.
>
> ## 2. Result of the enumeration
>
> A full enumeration of all legal random games yields:
>
> - probability $X$ wins: approximately $0.5849206$,
> - probability $O$ wins: approximately $0.2880952$,
> - probability of a tie: approximately $0.1269841$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(\text{tie}) \approx 0.1269841.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Converting to odds
>
> As a percentage, this is
>
> $$
> 0.1269841 \times 100\% \approx 12.7\%.
> $$
>
> So the odds that two random tic-tac-toe players tie are about
>
> $$
> \boxed{12.7\%}
> $$
>
> which is roughly
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 \text{ tie in } 7.9 \text{ games}}.
> $$
