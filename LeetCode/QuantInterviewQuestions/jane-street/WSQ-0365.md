> [!example] Question 365 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If you roll two dice, and you have the option to roll again and get paid the highest roll, when is it optimal to reroll?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=366)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $M$ be the current highest value showing after rolling two fair six-sided dice. You may either
>
> - **stop** and receive $M$, or
> - **roll both dice again once**, in which case your payoff is the highest value on the new roll.
>
> To decide when it is optimal to roll again, compare the sure payoff $M$ with the expected payoff from rerolling.
>
> ## Step 1: Distribution of the maximum of two dice
>
> Let $X$ be the maximum of two fair dice on a fresh roll. Then for $k = 1,2,\dots,6$,
> $$
> P(X \le k) = \left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2,
> $$
> since both dice must be at most $k$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(X = k) = P(X \le k) - P(X \le k-1)
> = \left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{k-1}{6}\right)^2
> = \frac{2k-1}{36}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Expected value of rerolling
>
> Compute
> $$
> E[X] = \sum_{k=1}^6 k \, P(X=k)
> = \sum_{k=1}^6 k \cdot \frac{2k-1}{36}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[X]
> = \frac{1}{36}\sum_{k=1}^6 (2k^2-k)
> = \frac{1}{36}\left(2\sum_{k=1}^6 k^2 - \sum_{k=1}^6 k\right).
> $$
>
> Using
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^6 k = 21,
> \qquad
> \sum_{k=1}^6 k^2 = 91,
> $$
> we get
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1}{36}(2\cdot 91 - 21)
> = \frac{161}{36}
> \approx 4.4722.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Optimal rule
>
> If your current highest die is $M$, then:
>
> - stopping gives payoff $M$,
> - rerolling gives expected payoff $\dfrac{161}{36}$.
>
> Therefore, rerolling is optimal exactly when
> $$
> M < \frac{161}{36} \approx 4.4722.
> $$
>
> Since $M$ must be an integer, this means reroll if and only if
> $$
> M \le 4.
> $$
>
> If
> $$
> M \ge 5,
> $$
> it is better to keep the current result.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> It is optimal to **roll again when the current highest die is $1,2,3,$ or $4$**, and to **stop when the current highest die is $5$ or $6$**.
