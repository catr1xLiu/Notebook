> [!example] Question 425 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

There are three pancakes: one with both sides burned, one with only one side burned, and one with neither side burned. The pancakes are stacked in a plate, and the top visible side is burned. What is the probability that the other side of this pancake is also burned?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=426)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Label the three pancakes as follows:
>
> - $BB$: both sides burned,
> - $BN$: one side burned and one side not burned,
> - $NN$: neither side burned.
>
> All three pancakes are placed on a plate in a random orientation, and the visible top side is observed to be burned. We want the probability that the hidden bottom side is also burned.
>
> ## Step 1: List all equally likely visible sides
>
> Each pancake has $2$ sides, so there are $6$ equally likely ways for a top side to be showing:
>
> - Pancake $BB$: top could be burned on either side $\Rightarrow 2$ burned top-sides.
> - Pancake $BN$: top could be the burned side or the unburned side $\Rightarrow 1$ burned top-side.
> - Pancake $NN$: no burned top-sides.
>
> Thus, among all possible top-side observations, the burned top-sides are:
>
> - $2$ from $BB$,
> - $1$ from $BN$.
>
> So there are $3$ possible cases consistent with “the top side is burned.”
>
> ## Step 2: Count favorable cases
>
> We want the hidden side to be burned.
>
> - If the visible burned side comes from pancake $BB$, then the other side is burned.
> - If the visible burned side comes from pancake $BN$, then the other side is not burned.
>
> Since the $BB$ pancake contributes $2$ of the $3$ possible burned-top cases, the desired probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
