> [!example] Question 609 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

On a bus with three stops, at each stop, three-quarters of the passengers get off and seven new passengers board. What is the minimum integer number of passengers that must have been on the bus at the start so that the number of passengers after each stop is always an integer?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=610)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of passengers be $x$.
>
> At each stop, $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of the passengers get off, so $\tfrac{1}{4}$ remain, and then $7$ people get on.
>
> Thus the number of passengers changes as follows:
>
> - After the first stop:
>   $$
>   \frac{x}{4} + 7
>   $$
> - After the second stop:
>   $$
>   \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{4} + 7\right) + 7= \frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}
>   $$
> - After the third stop:
>   $$
>   \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}\right) + 7= \frac{x}{64} + \frac{147}{16}
>   $$
>
> Since the number of passengers must be a whole number after each stop, we track the required divisibility conditions on $x$ one stop at a time.
>
> ## Step 1: First stop
>
> For $\frac{x}{4}+7$ to be an integer, $x$ must be divisible by $4$. Write
> $$
> x=4a.
> $$
> The count after the first stop is then $a+7$.
>
> ## Step 2: Second stop
>
> For $\frac{1}{4}(a+7)+7$ to be an integer, $a+7$ must be divisible by $4$, that is,
> $$
> a\equiv 1 \pmod 4.
> $$
> Write $a=4b+1$, so
> $$
> x=4a=16b+4.
> $$
> The count after the first stop is $a+7=4b+8=4(b+2)$, and the count after the second stop is
> $$
> \frac{4(b+2)}{4}+7=(b+2)+7=b+9.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Third stop
>
> For $\frac{1}{4}(b+9)+7$ to be an integer, $b+9$ must be divisible by $4$, that is,
> $$
> b\equiv 3\pmod4.
> $$
> Write $b=4c+3$, so
> $$
> x=16b+4=16(4c+3)+4=64c+52.
> $$
>
> ## Minimal solution
>
> Every valid $x$ satisfies $x\equiv52\pmod{64}$, so the smallest positive value occurs at $c=0$:
> $$
> x=52.
> $$
>
> ## Check
>
> - Start with $52$.
> - First stop: $\tfrac14\cdot52=13$ remain, then $7$ board, giving $20$.
> - Second stop: $\tfrac14\cdot20=5$ remain, then $7$ board, giving $12$.
> - Third stop: $\tfrac14\cdot12=3$ remain, then $7$ board, giving $10$.
>
> All three counts are whole numbers, confirming the chain
> $$
> 52\to20\to12\to10.
> $$
>
> Since $52$ is the smallest positive member of the residue class $x\equiv52\pmod{64}$, it is the minimal starting number of passengers for which the count remains an integer after every stop.
>
> $$
> \boxed{52}
> $$
