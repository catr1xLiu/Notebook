> [!example] Question 45 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given deck A (a normal 52-card deck), deck B (a 26-card deck with 50% black and 50% red cards), and deck C (a 26-card deck that is a random subset of deck A), which deck would you prefer to choose from if you need to draw two cards of the same color on consecutive draws?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=46)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the event of interest be that the first two cards drawn without replacement are the same color.
>
> ## Deck A: standard $52$-card deck
>
> A standard deck has $26$ red and $26$ black cards. After the first card is drawn, there are $51$ cards left, of which $25$ match its color. Hence
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{26}{52}\cdot\frac{25}{51}+\frac{26}{52}\cdot\frac{25}{51}
> =\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> ## Deck B: $26$-card deck with $50\%$ red and $50\%$ black
>
> This deck has $13$ red and $13$ black cards. Similarly,
>
> $$
> P_B=\frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> Indeed,
>
> $$
> P_B=\frac{13}{26}\cdot\frac{12}{25}+\frac{13}{26}\cdot\frac{12}{25}
> =\frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> Now compare:
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}-\frac{12}{25}
> =\frac{625-612}{1275}
> =\frac{13}{1275}>0.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P_A>P_B.
> $$
>
> Thus, between $A$ and $B$, one should choose **Deck A**.
>
> ---
>
> ## Deck C: a random $26$-card subset of Deck A
>
> Now suppose Deck C is formed by choosing $26$ cards uniformly at random from the standard $52$-card deck. Let $R$ be the number of red cards in Deck C. Then $R$ is random, and Deck C has $R$ red and $26-R$ black cards.
>
> Conditioned on $R=r$, the probability that two consecutive draws are the same color is
>
> $$
> P(\text{same} \mid R=r)
> =
> \frac{r}{26}\cdot\frac{r-1}{25}
> +
> \frac{26-r}{26}\cdot\frac{25-r}{25}.
> $$
>
> This simplifies to
>
> $$
> P(\text{same} \mid R=r)
> =
> \frac{r(r-1)+(26-r)(25-r)}{26\cdot 25}.
> $$
>
> To find the overall probability for Deck C, average over the random variable $R$. A simpler route is to use symmetry of random sampling:
>
> Choosing Deck C as a random $26$-card subset of Deck A, then drawing two cards from Deck C, is equivalent to drawing two cards directly from Deck A. Every ordered pair of distinct cards from Deck A is equally likely to appear as the first two cards drawn from Deck C.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P_C=\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> So Deck C has the same success probability as Deck A:
>
> $$
> P_C=P_A=\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The probabilities are
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{25}{51}\approx 0.4902,
> \qquad
> P_B=\frac{12}{25}=0.48,
> \qquad
> P_C=\frac{25}{51}\approx 0.4902.
> $$
>
> Hence:
>
> - Between Deck $A$ and Deck $B$, choose **Deck $A$**.
> - With Deck $C$ included, **Decks $A$ and $C$ are equally good**, and both are better than Deck $B$.
