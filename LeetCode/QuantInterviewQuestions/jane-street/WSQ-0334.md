> [!example] Question 334 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Two players each choose a different integer between 1 and 30. Then, a random integer x between 1 and 30 is generated. The player whose chosen number is closer to x receives x dollars. Analyze the optimal strategies for each player.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=335)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the first player choose an integer $a \in \{1,2,\dots,30\}$, and let the second player choose a different integer $b \in \{1,2,\dots,30\}$.
>
> After that, a random integer $x \in \{1,2,\dots,30\}$ is selected uniformly. The player whose chosen number is closer to $x$ receives $x$ dollars.
>
> The natural question is: **what number should the first player choose, anticipating that the second player will respond optimally?**
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. How the winner is determined
>
> For fixed choices $a$ and $b$, the first player wins when
>
> $$
> |x-a| < |x-b|.
> $$
>
> Similarly, the second player wins when
>
> $$
> |x-b| < |x-a|.
> $$
>
> If $|x-a| = |x-b|$, there is a tie. Since $a$ and $b$ are distinct integers, this happens exactly when $x$ is the midpoint of $a$ and $b$, which can occur only if $a+b$ is even.
>
> Because the payment is $x$ dollars, the expected payoff depends not only on how many values of $x$ each player wins, but also on **which** values they win.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Best response to a fixed first choice
>
> Suppose the first player chooses $a$.
>
> The second player should choose a number adjacent to $a$, because moving closer to $a$ shifts the boundary between winning regions and allows the second player to capture more values of $x$ on one side.
>
> So the only serious candidates are:
>
> - $b = a-1$, if $a > 1$,
> - $b = a+1$, if $a < 30$.
>
> Now compare these.
>
> ### Case 1: The second player chooses $b=a+1$
>
> Then the boundary between them is halfway between $a$ and $a+1$, namely $a+\tfrac12$. Hence:
>
> - the first player wins for $x \le a$,
> - the second player wins for $x \ge a+1$.
>
> Thus the second player’s expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{30}\sum_{x=a+1}^{30} x.
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: The second player chooses $b=a-1$
>
> Then the boundary is $a-\tfrac12$. Hence:
>
> - the second player wins for $x \le a-1$,
> - the first player wins for $x \ge a$.
>
> So the second player’s expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{30}\sum_{x=1}^{a-1} x.
> $$
>
> Therefore, against first choice $a$, the second player will choose the side giving the larger sum:
>
> $$
> \max\left\{ \sum_{x=1}^{a-1} x,\ \sum_{x=a+1}^{30} x \right\}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Compute these sums
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{n} x = \frac{n(n+1)}{2},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{a-1} x = \frac{(a-1)a}{2},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=a+1}^{30} x
> = \sum_{x=1}^{30} x - \sum_{x=1}^{a} x
> = 465 - \frac{a(a+1)}{2},
> $$
>
> since
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{30} x = \frac{30\cdot 31}{2} = 465.
> $$
>
> So the second player’s best possible total winning sum is
>
> $$
> \max\left\{ \frac{(a-1)a}{2},\ 465 - \frac{a(a+1)}{2} \right\}.
> $$
>
> To minimize the second player’s advantage, the first player should choose $a$ so that these two quantities are as balanced as possible.
>
> Set them approximately equal:
>
> $$
> \frac{(a-1)a}{2} = 465 - \frac{a(a+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Multiply by $2$:
>
> $$
> a(a-1) = 930 - a(a+1).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> a^2-a = 930 - a^2 - a,
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> 2a^2 = 930,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> a^2 = 465,
> \qquad
> a \approx \sqrt{465} \approx 21.56.
> $$
>
> Thus the best integer choices are near $22$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Check $a=21$ and $a=22$
>
> ### If $a=21$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{20} x = \frac{20\cdot 21}{2} = 210,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=22}^{30} x = 465 - \frac{21\cdot 22}{2} = 465 - 231 = 234.
> $$
>
> So the second player chooses $22$, giving expected payoff
>
> $$
> \frac{234}{30} = 7.8.
> $$
>
> ### If $a=22$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{21} x = \frac{21\cdot 22}{2} = 231,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=23}^{30} x = 465 - \frac{22\cdot 23}{2} = 465 - 253 = 212.
> $$
>
> So the second player chooses $21$, giving expected payoff
>
> $$
> \frac{231}{30} = 7.7.
> $$
>
> Thus $a=22$ is better for the first player than $a=21$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. First player’s expected payoff at $a=22$
>
> If the first player chooses $22$, the second player’s best response is $21$.
>
> Then the first player wins for $x=22,23,\dots,30$, so the first player’s total winning sum is
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=22}^{30} x = 234.
> $$
>
> Hence the first player’s expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{234}{30} = 7.8.
> $$
>
> The second player gets
>
> $$
> \frac{231}{30} = 7.7.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The first player should choose
>
> $$
> \boxed{22}.
> $$
>
> Then the second player’s best response is $21$, and the first player’s expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{234}{30} = 7.8 \text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> So the optimal first move is $\boxed{22}$.
