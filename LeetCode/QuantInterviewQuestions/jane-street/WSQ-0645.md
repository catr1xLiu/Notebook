> [!example] Question 645 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Flip a coin four times. If you flip a head on the first flip, you win \$1. For each consecutive head after the first, you win double your previous winnings. What is the expected value of your total winnings?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=646)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the four coin flips be independent and fair.
>
> The payout rule admits a natural literal reading and a looser one, and it is worth being precise about which is intended.
>
> **Literal reading.** "If you flip a head on the first flip, you win 1 dollar" is a condition on flip $1$ specifically: if the first flip is tails, you win nothing at all, no matter what happens afterward. "For each consecutive head after the first, you win double your previous winnings" then means: every time a head is immediately followed by another head, your winnings double once; a tail never removes anything already won, it simply stops that occasion from producing a further double. Under this reading, your total winnings are $0$ unless flip $1$ is heads, and otherwise equal $2^p$ dollars, where $p$ is the number of times a head is immediately followed by another head somewhere among the four flips. This gives an expected value of $\frac{21}{16}$, worked out in full below.
>
> **Looser reading.** One could instead read "the first" as "the first head, wherever it occurs" rather than "the first flip," so the base dollar is earned as soon as any head appears among the four flips, not only if flip $1$ itself is heads; the same doubling rule ($2^p$) then applies whenever at least one head occurs. This gives $\frac{33}{16}$ instead.
>
> We work out the literal computation in full, since it matches the stated wording most closely, and then record the alternate figure.
>
> ## Step 1: The literal reading (flip $1$ required)
>
> Winnings are $0$ unless flip $1$ is heads. There are $8$ such sequences; for each, count $p$, the number of adjacent head-head pairs anywhere in the four flips:
>
> - $p=0$: $HTHT,\ HTTH,\ HTTT$ — no adjacent pair of heads anywhere — winnings $2^0=1$.
> - $p=1$: $HHTH,\ HHTT,\ HTHH$ — exactly one adjacent pair (at flips $1$–$2$ for the first two, at flips $3$–$4$ for $HTHH$) — winnings $2^1=2$.
> - $p=2$: $HHHT$ — two adjacent pairs — winnings $2^2=4$.
> - $p=3$: $HHHH$ — three adjacent pairs — winnings $2^3=8$.
>
> The remaining $8$ sequences all have flip $1$ tails, contributing winnings $0$:
> $$
> THHH,\ THHT,\ THTH,\ THTT,\ TTHH,\ TTHT,\ TTTH,\ TTTT.
> $$
>
> Checking the count: $8+3+3+1+1=16$, as required.
>
> ## Step 2: Expected value under the literal reading
>
> Since each of the $16$ sequences has probability $\frac1{16}$,
> $$
> E[W]=\frac1{16}\bigl(0\cdot8+1\cdot3+2\cdot3+4\cdot1+8\cdot1\bigr).
> $$
> The sum inside is
> $$
> 0+3+6+4+8=21,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E[W]=\frac{21}{16}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: The looser reading (any first head)
>
> If instead the base dollar is earned as soon as any head appears — so only $TTTT$ contributes $0$ — the same rule $2^p$ applies to all $16$ sequences:
>
> - $p=0$: $HTHT,\ HTTH,\ HTTT,\ THTH,\ THTT,\ TTHT,\ TTTH$ — $7$ outcomes, winnings $1$ each.
> - $p=1$: $HHTH,\ HHTT,\ HTHH,\ THHT,\ TTHH$ — $5$ outcomes, winnings $2$ each.
> - $p=2$: $HHHT,\ THHH$ — $2$ outcomes, winnings $4$ each.
> - $p=3$: $HHHH$ — $1$ outcome, winnings $8$.
>
> Then
> $$
> E[W]=\frac1{16}\bigl(0\cdot1+1\cdot7+2\cdot5+4\cdot2+8\cdot1\bigr)=\frac{7+10+8+8}{16}=\frac{33}{16}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Reading the payout rule literally — the dollar requires a head specifically on flip $1$ — the expected winnings are
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{21}{16}\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> If instead the rule is read more loosely, so that the first head anywhere among the four flips starts the payout, the expected winnings are $\frac{33}{16}$ dollars instead. The two readings differ only in how sequences that begin with tails but still contain a head are treated; $\frac{21}{16}$ is the value consistent with the literal statement of the problem.
