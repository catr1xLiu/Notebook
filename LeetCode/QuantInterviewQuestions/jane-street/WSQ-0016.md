> [!example] Question 16 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability that a seventh game will be needed in a best-of-seven game series?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=17)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be the probability that Team A wins any given game, and assume games are independent.
>
> A 7th game is played **if and only if** after 6 games the series is tied $3$–$3$. Thus, in the first 6 games, Team A must win exactly 3 games and lose exactly 3 games.
>
> The number of ways this can happen is
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{3} = 20.
> $$
>
> For any particular sequence with 3 wins and 3 losses, the probability is
>
> $$
> p^3(1-p)^3.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that the series reaches Game 7 is
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{3} p^3(1-p)^3
> = 20p^3(1-p)^3.
> $$
>
> If the teams are evenly matched, so that $p=\tfrac12$, then
>
> $$
> 20\left(\frac12\right)^3\left(\frac12\right)^3
> = 20\left(\frac12\right)^6
> = \frac{20}{64}
> = \frac{5}{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the chance that the 7th game is needed is
>
> $$
> \boxed{20p^3(1-p)^3}
> $$
>
> and in the equal-strength case,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{16} \approx 0.3125}.
> $$
