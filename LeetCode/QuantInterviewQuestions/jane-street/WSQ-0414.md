> [!example] Question 414 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You throw a die two times. What is the probability of getting at least one six?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=415)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event “at least one $6$ appears in two throws.”
>
> A quick way is to use the complement:
>
> - The probability of **not** getting a $6$ on one throw is $\frac{5}{6}$.
> - Since the two throws are independent, the probability of getting no $6$ on both throws is
>   $$
>   \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2=\frac{25}{36}.
>   $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(A)=1-\frac{25}{36}=\frac{11}{36}.
> $$
>
> So the probability of getting at least one $6$ in two throws is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{36}}.
> $$
