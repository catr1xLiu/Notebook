> [!example] Question 197 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

What is the expected value and optimal strategy for a game where you have 3 blue balls and 2 red balls in a bag, and you get +\$1 when you draw a blue ball and -\$1 when you draw a red ball?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=198)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the state of the game be $(b,r)$, where $b$ is the number of blue balls remaining and $r$ is the number of red balls remaining. At any state, you may either stop and take payoff $0$ from that point onward, or continue and draw one ball uniformly at random.
>
> Define $V(b,r)$ to be the maximum expected future payoff from state $(b,r)$.
>
> ## Recurrence
>
> If you continue from state $(b,r)$, then:
>
> - with probability $\dfrac{b}{b+r}$, you draw a blue ball and gain $+1$, moving to state $(b-1,r)$;
> - with probability $\dfrac{r}{b+r}$, you draw a red ball and gain $-1$, moving to state $(b,r-1)$.
>
> Thus the expected value of continuing is
>
> $$
> C(b,r)=\frac{b}{b+r}\bigl(1+V(b-1,r)\bigr)+\frac{r}{b+r}\bigl(-1+V(b,r-1)\bigr).
> $$
>
> Since you may also stop immediately, the optimal value satisfies
>
> $$
> V(b,r)=\max\{0,\; C(b,r)\}.
> $$
>
> We compute this recursively.
>
> ## Boundary values
>
> If there are no blue balls left, continuing cannot help:
>
> $$
> V(0,r)=0.
> $$
>
> If there are only blue balls left, you should draw them all:
>
> $$
> V(b,0)=b.
> $$
>
> ## Computation
>
> ### State $(1,1)$
>
> $$
> C(1,1)=\frac12(1+V(0,1))+\frac12(-1+V(1,0))
> =\frac12(1+0)+\frac12(-1+1)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> V(1,1)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,1)$
>
> $$
> C(2,1)=\frac23(1+V(1,1))+\frac13(-1+V(2,0))
> =\frac23\left(1+\frac12\right)+\frac13(-1+2)
> =1+\frac13=\frac43.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V(2,1)=\frac43.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,1)$
>
> $$
> C(3,1)=\frac34(1+V(2,1))+\frac14(-1+V(3,0))
> =\frac34\left(1+\frac43\right)+\frac14(-1+3)
> =\frac34\cdot\frac73+\frac12
> =\frac74+\frac12=\frac94.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> V(3,1)=\frac94.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,2)$
>
> $$
> C(1,2)=\frac13(1+V(0,2))+\frac23(-1+V(1,1))
> =\frac13(1+0)+\frac23\left(-1+\frac12\right)
> =\frac13-\frac13=0.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> V(1,2)=0.
> $$
>
> At this state, you are indifferent between stopping and continuing.
>
> ### State $(2,2)$
>
> $$
> C(2,2)=\frac12(1+V(1,2))+\frac12(-1+V(2,1))
> =\frac12(1+0)+\frac12\left(-1+\frac43\right)
> =\frac12+\frac16=\frac23.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V(2,2)=\frac23.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,2)$
>
> This is the initial state. Compute:
>
> $$
> C(3,2)=\frac35(1+V(2,2))+\frac25(-1+V(3,1))
> =\frac35\left(1+\frac23\right)+\frac25\left(-1+\frac94\right).
> $$
>
> Now simplify:
>
> $$
> \frac35\cdot\frac53=1,
> \qquad
> \frac25\cdot\frac54=\frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> V(3,2)=C(3,2)=1+\frac12=\frac32.
> $$
>
> ## Optimal strategy
>
> Continue drawing whenever the continuation value is positive, and stop otherwise.
>
> From the calculations:
>
> - $(3,2)$: continue;
> - $(2,2)$: continue;
> - $(3,1)$: continue;
> - $(2,1)$: continue;
> - $(1,1)$: continue;
> - $(1,2)$: value $0$, so you are indifferent; stopping is optimal, and continuing is also optimal in expectation.
>
> Thus the only nontrivial stopping point is when there is $1$ blue and $2$ red remaining; there the expected gain from continuing is exactly $0$, so you may stop. In all other reachable states with at least one blue ball, it is optimal to continue.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The maximum expected payoff starting from $3$ blue and $2$ red balls is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac32}.
> $$
>
> An optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Keep drawing in every state except possibly at }(1,2),\text{ where stopping and continuing are equally good.}}
> $$
>
> So, practically, you may draw until either all balls are gone or until the state becomes $1$ blue and $2$ red, at which point stopping is optimal.
