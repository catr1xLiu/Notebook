> [!example] Question 571 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting exactly 2 heads in 4 flips of a fair coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=572)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ### Mental-math confidence questions
>
> Let the four mental-math questions have correctness probabilities
>
> $$
> p_1,\; p_2,\; p_3,\; p_4,
> $$
>
> where $p_i$ is your subjective probability that question $i$ was answered correctly.
>
> If you believe the correctness of the four answers is independent, then:
>
> #### Probability you got them all right
> $$
> P(\text{all 4 correct}) = p_1 p_2 p_3 p_4.
> $$
>
> If you think each answer is equally likely correct with probability $p$, this becomes
>
> $$
> P(\text{all 4 correct}) = p^4.
> $$
>
> #### Probability you got the hardest one right
> If the hardest question is question $h$, then
>
> $$
> P(\text{hardest one correct}) = p_h.
> $$
>
> #### Probability you got at least one wrong
> Use the complement:
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one wrong}) = 1 - P(\text{all 4 correct}) = 1 - p_1 p_2 p_3 p_4.
> $$
>
> If all four have the same confidence level $p$, then
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one wrong}) = 1 - p^4.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Coin problem: exactly $2$ heads in $4$ flips
>
> Assume the coin is fair, so each sequence of $4$ flips is equally likely.
>
> The total number of outcomes is
>
> $$
> 2^4 = 16.
> $$
>
> To get exactly $2$ heads, choose which $2$ of the $4$ positions are heads:
>
> $$
> \binom{4}{2} = 6.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{exactly 2 heads in 4 flips}) = \frac{\binom{4}{2}}{2^4} = \frac{6}{16} = \frac{3}{8}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{8}}.
> $$
