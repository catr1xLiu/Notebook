> [!example] Question 601 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If you roll a fair coin 10 times, what is the expected value of the product of the number of heads and the number of tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=602)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $10$ fair coin tosses, and let $T$ be the number of tails. Since every toss is either a head or a tail,
>
> $$
> T = 10 - H.
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> E[HT].
> $$
>
> Substitute $T = 10 - H$:
>
> $$
> HT = H(10 - H) = 10H - H^2.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[HT] = 10E[H] - E[H^2].
> $$
>
> Now $H \sim \text{Binomial}(10, \tfrac12)$, so
>
> $$
> E[H] = np = 10 \cdot \frac12 = 5,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(H) = np(1-p) = 10 \cdot \frac12 \cdot \frac12 = \frac52.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> E[H^2] = \operatorname{Var}(H) + (E[H])^2,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[H^2] = \frac52 + 25 = \frac{55}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[HT] = 10 \cdot 5 - \frac{55}{2}
> = 50 - \frac{55}{2}
> = \frac{45}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected product of the number of heads and the number of tails is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{45}{2}}.
> $$
