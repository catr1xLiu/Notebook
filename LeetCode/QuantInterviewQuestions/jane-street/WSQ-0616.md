> [!example] Question 616 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If I flip three coins, what is the probability that at least two of them are heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=617)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each coin flip has $2$ possible outcomes, so flipping $3$ coins gives
>
> $$
> 2^3 = 8
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> “At least $2$ heads” means getting either exactly $2$ heads or exactly $3$ heads.
>
> - Exactly $2$ heads: the outcomes are $HHT$, $HTH$, $THH$, so there are $3$ such outcomes.
> - Exactly $3$ heads: the outcome is $HHH$, so there is $1$ such outcome.
>
> Thus the number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$
> 3 + 1 = 4.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that at least $2$ of the $3$ coins are heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
