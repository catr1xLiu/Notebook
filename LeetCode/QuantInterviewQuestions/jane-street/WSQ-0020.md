> [!example] Question 20 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given that the probability of rain on Saturday is p1 and the probability of rain on Sunday is p2, what is the probability that it rains on at least one day of the weekend?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=21)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $S$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $T$ be the event that it rains on Sunday. We are given
>
> $$
> P(S) = p_1, \qquad P(T) = p_2.
> $$
>
> “Rain on the weekend” means that it rains on at least one of the two days, so the desired probability is
>
> $$
> P(S \cup T).
> $$
>
> Using the addition rule,
>
> $$
> P(S \cup T) = P(S) + P(T) - P(S \cap T).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{rain on the weekend}) = p_1 + p_2 - P(\text{rain on both Saturday and Sunday}).
> $$
>
> So, in general,
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{rain on the weekend}) = p_1 + p_2 - P(S \cap T).}
> $$
>
> If Saturday and Sunday rain are independent, then
>
> $$
> P(S \cap T) = p_1 p_2,
> $$
>
> and hence
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{rain on the weekend}) = p_1 + p_2 - p_1 p_2.}
> $$
