> [!example] Question 227 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

Consider a list of all the integers from 0 to 1,000,000. What is the sum of all the digits of these numbers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=228)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Write the numbers from $0$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ with exactly $6$ digits by allowing leading zeros, except for the last number, which is $1{,}000{,}000$.
>
> So first consider all numbers from $000000$ to $999999$.
>
> ## Step 1: Sum of digits from $000000$ to $999999$
>
> There are $10^6$ such strings, each with $6$ digit positions.
>
> In any fixed position, each digit $0,1,2,\dots,9$ appears equally often. Since there are $10^6$ total numbers, each digit appears
>
> $$
> \frac{10^6}{10} = 10^5
> $$
>
> times in each position.
>
> Thus the sum contributed by one position is
>
> $$
> (0+1+2+\cdots+9)\cdot 10^5 = 45 \cdot 10^5.
> $$
>
> Since there are $6$ positions, the total digit sum is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 45 \cdot 10^5 = 270 \cdot 10^5 = 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Add the number $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The digit sum of $1{,}000{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore the sum of all digits of all integers from $0$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000 + 1 = 27{,}000{,}001.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{27{,}000{,}001}
> $$
