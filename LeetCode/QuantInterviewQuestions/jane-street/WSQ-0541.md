> [!example] Question 541 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

1. How many digits are in 99 raised to the power of 20?
2. What is the expected value of rolling 20 dice with 3 sides each versus 30 dice with 2 sides each? If you were playing a game, which option would you choose, or does it not matter? What is the probability that you will win?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=542)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Number of digits in $99^{20}$
>
> To find the number of digits of a positive integer $N$, use
>
> $$
> \text{digits}(N)=\lfloor \log_{10} N \rfloor + 1.
> $$
>
> Here,
>
> $$
> N=99^{20}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \log_{10}(99^{20})=20\log_{10}(99).
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \log_{10}(99)=\log_{10}(100)-\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{100}{99}\right)\approx 2-0.0043648=1.9956352,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> 20\log_{10}(99)\approx 20(1.9956352)=39.912704.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \text{digits}(99^{20})=\lfloor 39.912704 \rfloor +1=39+1=40.
> $$
>
> Therefore, $99^{20}$ has
>
> $$
> \boxed{40}
> $$
>
> digits.
>
> ## 2. Comparing 20 dice with 3 sides each versus 30 dice with 2 sides each
>
> Here a die "with $3$ sides" is a fair die uniform on $\{1,2,3\}$, and a die "with $2$ sides" is a fair die uniform on $\{1,2\}$, equivalent to a coin labeled $1$ and $2$.
>
> Let $X$ be the total from rolling $20$ three-sided dice, and let $Y$ be the total from rolling $30$ two-sided dice, with the larger total winning.
>
> ### Step 1: Expected values
>
> For a single three-sided die,
>
> $$
> E[\text{one three-sided die}] = \frac{1+2+3}{3} = 2,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> E[X] = 20 \times 2 = 40.
> $$
>
> For a single two-sided die,
>
> $$
> E[\text{one two-sided die}] = \frac{1+2}{2} = 1.5,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> E[Y] = 30 \times 1.5 = 45.
> $$
>
> Since $45>40$, the thirty two-sided dice give the higher expected total:
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[Y]=45 > E[X]=40.}
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: How often each side actually wins
>
> A higher mean does not by itself guarantee a higher win probability, so the comparison should be checked directly. Here $X$ ranges over $20$ to $60$ and $Y$ ranges over $30$ to $60$; both distributions can be obtained exactly by convolution, for example from the generating functions $(z+z^2+z^3)^{20}$ for $X$ and $(z+z^2)^{30}$ for $Y$.
>
> Carrying out this convolution exactly gives
>
> $$
> P(Y>X)\approx 0.8376,
> \qquad
> P(X>Y)\approx 0.1142,
> \qquad
> P(X=Y)\approx 0.0482.
> $$
>
> So the $30$ two-sided dice beat the $20$ three-sided dice about $83.8\%$ of the time, confirming decisively, and not merely on average, that this is the better option.
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - Number of digits in $99^{20}$: $\boxed{40}$.
> - Better choice: the $30$ two-sided dice, since $45>40$ in expectation and they win outright with probability about $0.8376$.
> - Probability that the $30$ two-sided dice win: $\boxed{0.8376}$, versus $0.1142$ for the $20$ three-sided dice and $0.0482$ for a tie.
