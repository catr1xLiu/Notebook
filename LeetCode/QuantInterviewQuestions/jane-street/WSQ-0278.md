> [!example] Question 278 · Jane Street
> ## Summer Intern Interview

Starting with 13 red and blue cards labeled from 1 to 13, remove 7 blue cards at random. What is the probability that a card drawn is blue, given that its number is 3?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=279)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> There are $13$ red cards and $13$ blue cards, one of each color for each label $1,2,\dots,13$.
>
> Then $7$ blue cards are removed uniformly at random from the $13$ blue cards. After that, a card is drawn from the remaining cards. We want
>
> $$
> P(\text{blue} \mid \text{number is }3).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: The two cards labeled $3$
>
> Originally there are exactly two cards labeled $3$:
>
> - the red $3$, which is never removed (only blue cards are removed);
> - the blue $3$, which survives only if it is **not** among the $7$ blue cards removed.
>
> Since $7$ of the $13$ blue cards are removed at random, each blue card survives with probability
> $$
> P(\text{blue }3\text{ survives})=\frac{6}{13}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Set up the full sample space
>
> After removal, there are always
> $$
> 13+6=19
> $$
> cards remaining: the $13$ red cards, and the $6$ blue cards that survived.
>
> We compute both probabilities needed for Bayes' rule directly over this final $19$-card deck, since a card is drawn uniformly at random from whatever remains.
>
> ## Step 3: Probability of drawing the blue $3$
>
> The blue $3$ is drawn only if it survived the removal **and** it is the card selected from the $19$ remaining:
> $$
> P(\text{blue }3\text{ drawn})
> = P(\text{blue }3\text{ survives})\cdot P(\text{draw blue }3 \mid \text{it survives})
> = \frac{6}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{19}
> = \frac{6}{247}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Probability of drawing a card numbered $3$
>
> The drawn card has number $3$ if it is the red $3$ (always present) or the blue $3$ (present only if it survived):
> $$
> P(\text{number is }3)
> = P(\text{draw red }3)+P(\text{draw blue }3)
> = \frac{1}{19}+\frac{6}{247}
> = \frac{13}{247}+\frac{6}{247}
> = \frac{19}{247}
> = \frac{1}{13}.
> $$
>
> Note that this is *not* simply the unconditional survival probability of the blue $3$ combined with a $50/50$ split — observing that the drawn card is numbered $3$ is itself informative: it is more likely to occur when the blue $3$ survived (since a surviving blue $3$ doubles the chance of seeing a "$3$" from $1/19$ to $2/19$) than when it did not. The full calculation above already accounts for this correctly.
>
> ## Step 5: Apply Bayes' rule
>
> $$
> P(\text{blue} \mid \text{number is }3)
> = \frac{P(\text{blue }3\text{ drawn})}{P(\text{number is }3)}
> = \frac{6/247}{1/13}
> = \frac{6}{19}.
> $$
>
> So the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{6}{19}}.
> $$
