> [!example] Question 230 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

You are given a ten-sided die (values 1-10) and are allowed to roll it once or twice. After your first roll, you may choose to roll again. If you roll a second time, you add both values for your final score. If your total is 13 or less, you receive that amount in pounds as a payout. If your total exceeds 13, you receive nothing. What is the optimal strategy, and how did you arrive at your answer?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=231)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $X$ be the first roll and $Y$ the second roll, where each is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,10\}$.
>
> After observing $X=x$, you must choose between:
>
> - **Stop** and take payout $x$.
> - **Roll again**, in which case the final score is $x+Y$, and the payout is
>   $$
>   \begin{cases}
>   x+Y, & x+Y \le 13,\\
>   0, & x+Y > 13.
>   \end{cases}
>   $$
>
> The optimal strategy is found by comparing these two expected payouts for each possible first roll $x$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Expected payout if you stop
>
> If you stop after seeing $x$, the payout is simply
>
> $$
> V_{\text{stop}}(x)=x.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Expected payout if you roll again
>
> If you roll again, then for each $y\in\{1,\dots,10\}$ the payout is $x+y$ if $x+y\le 13$, and $0$ otherwise.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(x)
> =\frac{1}{10}\sum_{y=1}^{10} (x+y)\mathbf{1}_{\{x+y\le 13\}}.
> $$
>
> Only those $y$ satisfying $y\le 13-x$ contribute. Hence:
>
> - if $x\le 3$, then $13-x\ge 10$, so all second rolls are safe;
> - if $x\ge 4$, then only $y=1,\dots,13-x$ are safe.
>
> So we compute case by case.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Compute $V_{\text{roll}}(x)$
>
> ### Case 1: $x\le 3$
>
> Then all $10$ values of $Y$ are allowed, so
>
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(x)=x+\mathbb{E}[Y]=x+5.5.
> $$
>
> Thus for $x=1,2,3$:
>
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(1)=6.5,\qquad
> V_{\text{roll}}(2)=7.5,\qquad
> V_{\text{roll}}(3)=8.5.
> $$
>
> These all exceed $x$, so rolling again is better.
>
> ---
>
> ### Case 2: $x\ge 4$
>
> Let $n=13-x$. Then the safe values are $y=1,\dots,n$, where $n\le 9$.
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(x)
> =\frac{1}{10}\sum_{y=1}^{13-x}(x+y).
> $$
>
> Compute the sum:
>
> $$
> \sum_{y=1}^{13-x}(x+y)
> =(13-x)x+\sum_{y=1}^{13-x} y
> =(13-x)x+\frac{(13-x)(14-x)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(x)
> =\frac{1}{10}\left((13-x)x+\frac{(13-x)(14-x)}{2}\right).
> $$
>
> Now evaluate for $x=4,5,\dots,10$:
>
> ### $x=4$
> Safe $y=1,\dots,9$:
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(4)=\frac{1}{10}(5+6+\cdots+13)=\frac{81}{10}=8.1.
> $$
> Compare with stopping: $4$. Roll again.
>
> ### $x=5$
> Safe $y=1,\dots,8$:
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(5)=\frac{1}{10}(6+7+\cdots+13)=\frac{76}{10}=7.6.
> $$
> Compare with stopping: $5$. Roll again.
>
> ### $x=6$
> Safe $y=1,\dots,7$:
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(6)=\frac{1}{10}(7+8+\cdots+13)=\frac{70}{10}=7.0.
> $$
> Compare with stopping: $6$. Roll again.
>
> ### $x=7$
> Safe $y=1,\dots,6$:
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(7)=\frac{1}{10}(8+9+\cdots+13)=\frac{63}{10}=6.3.
> $$
> Compare with stopping: $7$. Stop.
>
> ### $x=8$
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(8)=\frac{1}{10}(9+10+11+12+13)=\frac{55}{10}=5.5<8.
> $$
> Stop.
>
> ### $x=9$
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(9)=\frac{1}{10}(10+11+12+13)=\frac{46}{10}=4.6<9.
> $$
> Stop.
>
> ### $x=10$
> $$
> V_{\text{roll}}(10)=\frac{1}{10}(11+12+13)=\frac{36}{10}=3.6<10.
> $$
> Stop.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Optimal strategy
>
> Comparing $V_{\text{roll}}(x)$ with $V_{\text{stop}}(x)=x$ gives:
>
> - Roll again if $x=1,2,3,4,5,6$.
> - Stop if $x=7,8,9,10$.
>
> So the **optimal threshold strategy** is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Roll again if the first roll is }6\text{ or less; otherwise stop.}}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Expected payout under the optimal strategy
>
> The expected value of the game is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{10}\sum_{x=1}^{10} \max\bigl(x, V_{\text{roll}}(x)\bigr).
> $$
>
> Using the optimal choices:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{payout}]
> =\frac{1}{10}\left(6.5+7.5+8.5+8.1+7.6+7.0+7+8+9+10\right).
> $$
>
> Summing,
>
> $$
> 6.5+7.5+8.5+8.1+7.6+7.0+7+8+9+10=79.2.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{optimal payout}] = \frac{79.2}{10}=7.92.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Take a second roll if the first roll is }1,2,3,4,5,\text{ or }6;\ \text{otherwise keep the first roll.}}
> $$
>
> The maximum expected payout under this strategy is
>
> $$
> \boxed{£7.92}.
> $$
