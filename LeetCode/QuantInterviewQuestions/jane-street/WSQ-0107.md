> [!example] Question 107 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You ask someone to take a test in which each question has 5 possible answers and only one correct answer. You observe that the person's answer is correct. What is the probability that the test taker actually knew (derived) the answer rather than guessed?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=108)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $K$ denote the event that the test taker **knew** the answer, and let $R$ denote the event that the answer observed is **right**.
>
> We are asked to find the probability
>
> $$
> P(K \mid R).
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(K \mid R)=\frac{P(R \mid K)P(K)}{P(R)}.
> $$
>
> Now:
>
> - If the test taker knew the answer, then the answer is certainly correct, so
>   $$
>   P(R \mid K)=1.
>   $$
> - If the test taker did **not** know the answer and guessed randomly among $5$ choices, then
>   $$
>   P(R \mid K^c)=\frac{1}{5}.
>   $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(R)=P(R \mid K)P(K)+P(R \mid K^c)P(K^c)
> =1\cdot P(K)+\frac{1}{5}(1-P(K)).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(K \mid R)
> =
> \frac{P(K)}{P(K)+\frac{1}{5}(1-P(K))}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that the test taker really knew the answer, given that the answer is correct, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{P(K)}{P(K)+\frac{1}{5}(1-P(K))}}.
> $$
>
> This cannot be reduced to a numerical value unless the prior probability $P(K)$ that the test taker knew the answer is specified.
