> [!example] Question 74 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

If I have 4 coins and I flip them, and you get paid \$1 for every head, what is the expected value of your earnings? If you had a magic wand that lets you re-flip any even number of coins (either 2 or 4), what would your expected earnings be after using it optimally?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=75)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped.
>
> Since each coin contributes $\text{\textdollar}1$ if it lands heads, the earnings are exactly $X$ dollars.
>
> ## 1. Expectation without the magic wand
>
> Write
> $$
> X = X_1 + X_2 + X_3 + X_4,
> $$
> where
> $$
> X_i =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if coin } i \text{ is heads},\\
> 0, & \text{if coin } i \text{ is tails}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> For each fair coin,
> $$
> E[X_i] = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> E[X] = E[X_1] + E[X_2] + E[X_3] + E[X_4] = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected earnings are
> $$
> \boxed{\text{\textdollar}2}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Expectation with the magic wand
>
> After seeing the $4$ initial coin results, one may choose to re-flip either $2$ coins or all $4$ coins, with the restriction that the number of coins re-flipped must be even.
>
> The goal is to maximize the expected final number of heads.
>
> ### Key observation
>
> If a coin is currently heads and is re-flipped, its expected contribution afterward becomes
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}
> $$
> instead of $1$, so re-flipping a head loses expected value
> $$
> 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> If a coin is currently tails and is re-flipped, its expected contribution afterward becomes
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}
> $$
> instead of $0$, so re-flipping a tail gains expected value
> $$
> \frac{1}{2} - 0 = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, re-flipping a chosen set of coins changes expectation by
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}(\#\text{tails re-flipped} - \#\text{heads re-flipped}).
> $$
>
> Since we choose which coins to re-flip after seeing the results, one should re-flip a set of coins only when that set contains more tails than heads, and among such sets the best choice is to re-flip exactly the tails available (never a head), as long as this uses an even number of coins.
>
> ### Evaluate by number of initial heads
>
> Let $k$ be the number of heads in the initial $4$ flips.
>
> - If $k=0$, the outcome is $TTTT$: re-flip all $4$ tails, giving final expected heads $4\cdot\frac12=2$.
> - If $k=1$, the outcome has $1$ head and $3$ tails: re-flip two of the three tails, so the untouched head plus the two re-flipped tails give final expected heads $1+1=2$.
> - If $k=2$, there are $2$ heads and $2$ tails: re-flipping exactly the two tails is an even-sized set consisting entirely of tails, gaining $\frac12(2-0)=1$, for final expected heads $2+1=3$.
> - If $k=3$, there is only $1$ tail, so no even-sized set of coins consists purely of tails (any even-sized set must include at least one head, giving equal or more heads than tails re-flipped, which never gains); do not use the wand, so final expected heads stays at $3$.
> - If $k=4$, there are no tails at all, so any re-flip can only lose expected value; do not use the wand, so final expected heads stays at $4$.
>
> Thus the optimal expected final earnings, conditioned on $k$, are
> $$
> 2,\,2,\,3,\,3,\,4
> $$
> for $k=0,1,2,3,4$ respectively.
>
> ### Average over the initial distribution
>
> Since $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12)$,
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E[\text{optimal earnings}]
> = \sum_{k=0}^4 P(X=k)\cdot \text{(optimal expectation given }k).
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[\text{optimal earnings}]
> =
> \frac{1}{16}\left[
> \binom{4}{0}\cdot 2
> +\binom{4}{1}\cdot 2
> +\binom{4}{2}\cdot 3
> +\binom{4}{3}\cdot 3
> +\binom{4}{4}\cdot 4
> \right].
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> =
> \frac{1}{16}\left[
> 1\cdot 2 + 4\cdot 2 + 6\cdot 3 + 4\cdot 3 + 1\cdot 4
> \right]
> =
> \frac{1}{16}(2+8+18+12+4)
> =
> \frac{44}{16}
> =
> \frac{11}{4}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected earnings with optimal use of the wand are
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{4} = \text{\textdollar}2.75}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - Without the wand: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}2}$.
> - With optimal use of the wand: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}2.75}$.
