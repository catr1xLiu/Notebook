> [!example] Question 250 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

How much would you pay for a game where your payoff equals the number shown on a die, with an option to reroll once? Generalize to n opportunities to reroll.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=251)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the die be fair with outcomes $1,2,3,4,5,6$.
>
> The value of the game is the maximal expected payoff under an optimal reroll strategy. Since the player may choose whether to keep the current roll or reroll, this is an optimal stopping problem solved by backward induction.
>
> ## One opportunity to reroll
>
> Suppose you roll once, observe the result $x$, and may either:
>
> - keep $x$, or
> - reroll once, after which you must accept the new result.
>
> If you reroll, the expected value of the new roll is
>
> $$
> E[\text{new roll}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So after seeing $x$, the optimal choice is:
>
> - keep $x$ if $x \ge 3.5$,
> - reroll if $x < 3.5$.
>
> Since $x$ is an integer, this means:
>
> - reroll on $1,2,3$,
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> Thus the expected value is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\big(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6\big)
> = \frac{25.5}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore, a fair price is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Generalization to $n$ opportunities to reroll
>
> Let $V_n$ denote the value of the game when you have $n$ rerolls available before the first roll.
>
> Thus:
>
> - $V_0$ is the value with no rerolls available, so
>   $$
>   V_0 = 3.5.
>   $$
> - For $n \ge 1$, after seeing a roll $x$, you may either keep $x$ or use one reroll, which gives expected value $V_{n-1}$.
>
> Hence the optimal decision is to keep $x$ if $x \ge V_{n-1}$ and reroll if $x < V_{n-1}$. Therefore
>
> $$
> V_n = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{x=1}^6 \max(x, V_{n-1}).
> $$
>
> This is the general recursion.
>
> ### First few values
>
> Start with
>
> $$
> V_0 = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V_1 = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6)=4.25.
> $$
>
> Since $4 < 4.25 < 5$, the threshold again falls between $4$ and $5$: reroll on $1,2,3,4$ and keep $5,6$. Thus
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{1}{6}(4.25+4.25+4.25+4.25+5+6)
> = \frac{28}{6}
> = \frac{14}{3}
> \approx 4.6667.
> $$
>
> Since $4 < V_2 < 5$ as well,
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{1}{6}(V_2+V_2+V_2+V_2+5+6)
> = \frac{4V_2+11}{6}.
> $$
>
> For all $n \ge 1$, once $V_n$ lies between $4$ and $5$, the same threshold applies: reroll on $1,2,3,4$ and keep $5,6$. Thus for all $n \ge 2$,
>
> $$
> V_n = \frac{4V_{n-1}+11}{6}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> V_n - 5.5 = \frac{2}{3}(V_{n-1}-5.5).
> $$
>
> So for $n \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> V_n = 5.5 - (5.5 - V_1)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\,n-1}.
> $$
>
> Since $V_1 = 4.25$,
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{4} = 1.25 = 5.5 - V_1,
> $$
>
> hence for $n \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> V_n = 5.5 - \frac{5}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n-1}
> }.
> $$
>
> This can also be written as
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> V_n = \frac{11}{2} - \frac{5}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n-1}
> \qquad (n \ge 1).
> }
> $$
>
> with
>
> $$
> \boxed{V_0 = 3.5.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Limiting value
>
> As $n \to \infty$,
>
> $$
> V_n \to 5.5.
> $$
>
> So with arbitrarily many rerolls, the expected payoff approaches
>
> $$
> \boxed{5.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - With one opportunity to reroll, the fair price is
>   $$
>   \boxed{4.25}.
>   $$
>
> - With $n$ opportunities to reroll, the value satisfies
>   $$
>   \boxed{V_0=3.5,\qquad V_n=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{x=1}^6 \max(x,V_{n-1})}.
>   $$
>
> - Explicitly,
>   $$
>   \boxed{
>   V_n = \frac{11}{2} - \frac{5}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n-1}
>   \quad \text{for } n\ge 1.
>   }
>   $$
