> [!example] Question 360 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If I roll a pair of dice and know that one die shows a six, what is the probability that both dice show six?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=361)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the sample space consist of ordered pairs $(d_1,d_2)$, where each die can show $1$ through $6$. Thus there are $36$ equally likely outcomes.
>
> We are given that **at least one die is a six**. The outcomes satisfying this condition are
>
> $$
> (6,1),(6,2),(6,3),(6,4),(6,5),(6,6),
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1,6),(2,6),(3,6),(4,6),(5,6).
> $$
>
> This gives $11$ possible outcomes.
>
> Among these, the outcome where **both dice are six** is only
>
> $$
> (6,6).
> $$
>
> Therefore, the conditional probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{11}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that both dice are six, given that at least one die is a six, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{11}}.
> $$
