> [!example] Question 209 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

If I roll two dice and tell you that at least one is a 6, what is the probability that both are 6s?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=210)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the sample space consist of all ordered outcomes of two fair dice. There are $36$ equally likely outcomes.
>
> We are given the condition that **at least one die is a $6$**. Restrict the sample space to outcomes satisfying this condition.
>
> There are:
>
> - $6$ outcomes where the first die is $6$:  
>   $$(6,1),(6,2),(6,3),(6,4),(6,5),(6,6)$$
> - $6$ outcomes where the second die is $6$:  
>   $$(1,6),(2,6),(3,6),(4,6),(5,6),(6,6)$$
>
> Counting $(6,6)$ only once, the number of outcomes with at least one $6$ is
>
> $$
> 6+6-1=11.
> $$
>
> Among these $11$ outcomes, only one has both dice equal to $6$, namely
>
> $$
> (6,6).
> $$
>
> Therefore, the conditional probability is
>
> $$
> P(\text{both are }6 \mid \text{at least one is }6)=\frac{1}{11}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{11}}.
> $$
