> [!example] Question 350 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Two painters work at different rates. How long will it take them to paint a room if they work together, given their individual painting rates?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=351)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the first painter take $a$ hours to complete the job alone, and the second painter take $b$ hours to complete the job alone.
>
> Their individual painting rates are:
>
> - First painter: $\dfrac{1}{a}$ job per hour
> - Second painter: $\dfrac{1}{b}$ job per hour
>
> Working together, their rates add, so the combined rate is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
> $$
>
> Thus, if $T$ is the time they take together, then
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{T}=\frac{a+b}{ab}
> $$
>
> and therefore
>
> $$
> T=\frac{ab}{a+b}
> $$
>
> Hence, the time taken when they paint together is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{ab}{a+b}}
> $$
>
> hours.
