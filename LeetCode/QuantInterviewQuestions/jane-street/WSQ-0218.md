> [!example] Question 218 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

You have 4 fair coins. When you toss the coins, you win an amount in dollars equal to the total number of tails. Additionally, you have the option to re-toss all the coins, but this costs you 1 dollar. What is the expected value of this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=219)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of tails obtained when $4$ fair coins are tossed. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \text{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12),
> $$
>
> so the possible payoffs from a toss are $0,1,2,3,4$ dollars.
>
> A re-toss costs $1$ dollar, so if after seeing the current result $x$ you choose to re-toss, your expected future value is
>
> $$
> V - 1,
> $$
>
> where $V$ is the value of the game before a toss. Thus the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the current result $x$ if $x \ge V-1$,
> - re-toss if $x < V-1$.
>
> Hence $V$ must satisfy the dynamic programming equation
>
> $$
> V=\mathbb E\big[\max(X,\,V-1)\big].
> $$
>
> Since $X$ has distribution
>
> $$
> \mathbb P(X=0)=\frac1{16},\quad
> \mathbb P(X=1)=\frac4{16},\quad
> \mathbb P(X=2)=\frac6{16},\quad
> \mathbb P(X=3)=\frac4{16},\quad
> \mathbb P(X=4)=\frac1{16},
> $$
>
> we determine the correct stopping region.
>
> If $1<V-1\le 2$, then one would re-toss on $0$ and $1$, and keep $2,3,4$. In that case,
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{16}(V-1)+\frac{4}{16}(V-1)+\frac{6}{16}\cdot 2+\frac{4}{16}\cdot 3+\frac{1}{16}\cdot 4.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> V=\frac{5}{16}(V-1)+\frac{12+12+4}{16}
> =\frac{5}{16}(V-1)+\frac{28}{16}.
> $$
>
> Multiply by $16$:
>
> $$
> 16V=5V-5+28=5V+23,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> 11V=23,\qquad V=\frac{23}{11}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V-1=\frac{12}{11},
> $$
>
> which indeed lies between $1$ and $2$, so the assumption is consistent.
>
> Therefore the optimal policy is to re-toss whenever the number of tails is $0$ or $1$, and to keep the result when it is $2,3,$ or $4$.
>
> The value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{23}{11}\text{ dollars}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{2.09\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
