> [!example] Question 160 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

You have a number whose digits multiply to 96, and none of the digits is 1. What are the largest and smallest such numbers you can form?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=161)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To form numbers whose digits multiply to $96$, with no digit equal to $1$, first factorize:
>
> $$
> 96 = 2^5 \cdot 3.
> $$
>
> A digit must be one of $2,3,4,6,8,9$ since digits are from $0$ to $9$ and no digit may be $1$.
>
> We want:
>
> - the **largest** possible number,
> - the **smallest** possible number.
>
> ## Largest number
>
> To make the number as large as possible, we want as many digits as possible, because a number with more digits is larger than any number with fewer digits.
>
> So split $96$ into the greatest number of single-digit factors greater than $1$:
>
> $$
> 96 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3.
> $$
>
> This gives the digits $2,2,2,2,2,3$, six digits in total. To maximize the number, arrange them in descending order:
>
> $$
> 322222.
> $$
>
> So the largest possible number is
>
> $$
> \boxed{322222}.
> $$
>
> ## Smallest number
>
> To make the number as small as possible, we want as few digits as possible, and then arrange them in increasing order.
>
> Combine the factors into the largest possible digits:
>
> $$
> 96 = 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 8.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 8 = 96,
> $$
>
> this gives a $3$-digit number. It is impossible to make a $2$-digit number, because no two digits from $2$ to $9$ have product $96$.
>
> Now arrange the digits in increasing order to get the smallest such number:
>
> $$
> 268.
> $$
>
> So the smallest possible number is
>
> $$
> \boxed{268}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \text{Largest number} = \boxed{322222}, \qquad \text{Smallest number} = \boxed{268}.
> $$
