> [!example] Question 504 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Devise a betting strategy for a seven-game series such that if team 1 wins the series, you win \$1000 no matter what, and if team 1 loses the series, you lose \$1000 no matter what.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=505)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $A_k$ denote the event that team 1 wins the series in exactly $k$ games, where $k \in \{4,5,6,7\}$. These events are mutually exclusive and exhaustive for the event "team 1 wins the series."
>
> We seek bets on the outcomes of the individual games so that:
>
> - if team 1 wins the series, the total profit is always $1000$ dollars,
> - if team 1 loses the series, the total loss is always $1000$ dollars.
>
> The idea is to work backward, choosing the stake before each game so that the final result becomes fixed regardless of how the remaining games unfold. Assume each game is offered at even odds: betting $x$ on team 1 in a game wins $x$ if team 1 wins that game and loses $x$ if team 1 loses it.
>
> ### Backward induction
>
> Let $V(i,j)$ denote the wealth you must already have accumulated once team 1 has won $i$ games and team 2 has won $j$ games, in order to still be able to replicate the target payoff. The terminal conditions are
>
> $$
> V(4,j)=1000 \quad (j=0,1,2,3),
> \qquad
> V(i,4)=-1000 \quad (i=0,1,2,3).
> $$
>
> Betting $x$ on team 1 in the next game moves your wealth from $V(i,j)$ to $V(i,j)+x$ if team 1 wins, or to $V(i,j)-x$ if team 1 loses. Matching these to the correct successor values requires
>
> $$
> V(i,j)+x=V(i+1,j),
> \qquad
> V(i,j)-x=V(i,j+1),
> $$
>
> which gives
>
> $$
> V(i,j)=\frac{V(i+1,j)+V(i,j+1)}{2},
> \qquad
> x=\frac{V(i+1,j)-V(i,j+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Applying this averaging rule backward from the terminal values determines $V(i,j)$, and hence the correct bet, at every state of the series.
>
> ### Step 1: Game 1
>
> From the state 0-0,
>
> $$
> V(1,0)=\frac{625}{2}=312.5,
> \qquad
> V(0,1)=-\frac{625}{2}=-312.5,
> $$
>
> so the first bet is
>
> $$
> x=\frac{312.5-(-312.5)}{2}=312.5.
> $$
>
> Bet $312.50$ dollars on team 1 in game 1.
>
> ### Step 2: Game 2
>
> Using $V(2,0)=625$, $V(1,1)=0$, $V(0,2)=-625$:
>
> - if team 1 leads 1-0, bet $312.50$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 0-1, bet $312.50$ dollars.
>
> ### Step 3: Game 3
>
> Using $V(3,0)=875$, $V(2,1)=375$, $V(1,2)=-375$, $V(0,3)=-875$:
>
> - if team 1 leads 2-0, bet $250$ dollars,
> - if the series is tied 1-1, bet $375$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 0-2, bet $250$ dollars.
>
> ### Step 4: Game 4
>
> Using $V(4,0)=1000$, $V(3,1)=750$, $V(2,2)=0$, $V(1,3)=-750$, $V(0,4)=-1000$:
>
> - if team 1 leads 3-0, bet $125$ dollars,
> - if team 1 leads 2-1, bet $375$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 1-2, bet $375$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 0-3, bet $125$ dollars.
>
> ### Step 5: Game 5
>
> Using $V(4,1)=1000$, $V(3,2)=500$, $V(2,3)=-500$, $V(1,4)=-1000$:
>
> - if team 1 leads 3-1, bet $250$ dollars,
> - if the series is tied 2-2, bet $500$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 1-3, bet $250$ dollars.
>
> ### Step 6: Game 6
>
> Using $V(4,2)=1000$, $V(3,3)=0$, $V(2,4)=-1000$:
>
> - if team 1 leads 3-2, bet $500$ dollars,
> - if team 1 trails 2-3, bet $500$ dollars.
>
> ### Step 7: Game 7
>
> Using $V(4,3)=1000$, $V(3,4)=-1000$:
>
> - if the series is tied 3-3, bet $1000$ dollars on team 1.
>
> ## Summary of the strategy
>
> Bet on team 1 according to the series score (team 1 wins - team 2 wins) going into that game:
>
> - Score 0-0: bet $312.50$.
> - Score 1-0: bet $312.50$.
> - Score 0-1: bet $312.50$.
> - Score 2-0: bet $250$.
> - Score 1-1: bet $375$.
> - Score 0-2: bet $250$.
> - Score 3-0: bet $125$.
> - Score 2-1: bet $375$.
> - Score 1-2: bet $375$.
> - Score 0-3: bet $125$.
> - Score 3-1: bet $250$.
> - Score 2-2: bet $500$.
> - Score 1-3: bet $250$.
> - Score 3-2: bet $500$.
> - Score 2-3: bet $500$.
> - Score 3-3: bet $1000$.
>
> All amounts are in dollars, bet on team 1 at even odds; if team 1 has already clinched or been eliminated, no further bets are needed.
>
> This strategy guarantees:
>
> - a total profit of $1000$ dollars if team 1 wins the series,
> - a total loss of $1000$ dollars if team 1 loses the series,
>
> no matter how the individual games unfold.
