> [!example] Question 248 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

Find the lowest positive integer such that the product of its digits equals n.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=249)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the given positive integer be $n$. We want the smallest positive integer whose digits have product $n$.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> A decimal digit can only be one of $0,1,2,\dots,9$. Therefore, if a number has digit product $n$, then $n$ must be expressible as a product of digits from $2$ to $9$ (digits $1$ do not change the product, and $0$ makes the product $0$).
>
> To obtain the *smallest integer*, we should:
>
> 1. Factor $n$ into digits between $2$ and $9$.
> 2. Use as few digits as possible, preferring larger factors first.
> 3. Arrange the resulting digits in increasing order to minimize the numerical value.
>
> ## Step 1: Special cases
>
> - If $n = 0$, the answer is $10$, since $1 \cdot 0 = 0$ and $10$ is the smallest positive integer with digit product $0$.
> - If $1 \le n \le 9$, the answer is simply $n$ itself.
>
> ## Step 2: Factor $n$ using digits $9$ down to $2$
>
> Repeatedly divide $n$ by $9,8,7,\dots,2$ whenever possible.
>
> - Each successful division records one digit.
> - After all divisions, if the remaining value is greater than $1$, then such an integer does not exist, because $n$ contains a prime factor greater than $7$ or cannot be formed from decimal digits.
>
> ## Step 3: Form the smallest integer
>
> If the collected digits are $d_1,d_2,\dots,d_k$, sort them in increasing order and concatenate them. That gives the smallest possible integer.
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Using larger digits first reduces the total number of digits. Any number with fewer digits is smaller than any number with more digits, provided there are no leading zeroes. Once the number of digits is minimized, arranging them in increasing order gives the smallest lexicographic and hence smallest numerical value.
>
> ## Example
>
> For $n = 100$:
>
> $$
> 100 \div 5 = 20,\qquad 20 \div 5 = 4,\qquad 4 \div 4 = 1
> $$
>
> So the digits are $5,5,4$. Sorting gives $4,5,5$, hence the smallest integer is
>
> $$
> 455
> $$
>
> since
>
> $$
> 4 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 100.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The smallest integer whose digits multiply to $n$ is obtained by:
>
> - returning $10$ if $n=0$,
> - returning $n$ if $1 \le n \le 9$,
> - otherwise factoring $n$ by digits from $9$ down to $2$,
> - and, if successful, writing the found digits in increasing order.
>
> If after this process a factor greater than $1$ remains, then no such integer exists.
