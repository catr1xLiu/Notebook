> [!example] Question 243 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

Given a 4x4 chessboard, can a knight start from any square and visit every other square exactly once without revisiting any square?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=244)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A knight’s move is an $L$-shape: two squares in one direction and one square perpendicular. The question asks whether, on a $4\times 4$ board, there exists a path by knight moves that visits every square exactly once, starting from some square. This is a **knight’s tour** problem on the $4\times 4$ board.
>
> ## Step 1: Color the board
>
> Color the board in the usual checkerboard pattern, with $8$ black and $8$ white squares.
>
> A knight always moves from a square of one color to a square of the opposite color. Therefore, any knight’s path alternates colors.
>
> If a tour visits all $16$ squares exactly once, then the sequence of visited squares must alternate colors:
> $$
> \text{black, white, black, white, } \dots
> $$
> Since $16$ is even, such a path would use exactly $8$ squares of each color. So the coloring condition alone does not rule it out.
>
> ## Step 2: Examine the knight-move graph
>
> Represent each square as a vertex, and join two vertices if a knight can move between those squares. A knight’s tour would then be a Hamiltonian path in this graph.
>
> On the $4\times 4$ board, some squares have very few possible knight moves:
>
> - The four corner squares each have degree $2$.
> - In fact, there are several degree-$2$ squares, and their arrangement severely restricts any Hamiltonian path.
>
> Label the board by coordinates $(i,j)$ with $i,j \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. The knight moves are:
> $$
> (\pm 2,\pm 1), \quad (\pm 1,\pm 2).
> $$
>
> Listing the legal moves shows that the graph splits into forced patterns. In particular, the degree-$2$ vertices must be used in a very constrained way in any Hamiltonian path: if a vertex has degree $2$, then both incident edges must belong to the path unless that vertex is one of the two endpoints.
>
> But on the $4\times 4$ board there are too many such constrained vertices, and they cannot all be arranged consistently into a single path through all $16$ vertices.
>
> ## Step 3: A direct contradiction
>
> Consider the eight squares
> $$
> (1,1),(1,4),(4,1),(4,4),(1,2),(2,1),(3,4),(4,3).
> $$
> Each of these has only $2$ possible knight moves. In any Hamiltonian path, at most two of these degree-$2$ vertices can serve as endpoints; all the others must use both of their incident edges.
>
> This forces too many edges locally, creating disjoint chains or cycles before all $16$ squares are included. Hence no single Hamiltonian path through all squares can exist.
>
> Therefore a knight cannot visit all $16$ squares of a $4\times 4$ board without revisiting a square.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> No, a knight cannot start on a $4\times 4$ chessboard and visit every square exactly once. A knight’s tour on a $4\times 4$ board is impossible.
