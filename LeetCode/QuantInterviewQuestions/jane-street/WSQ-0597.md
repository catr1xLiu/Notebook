> [!example] Question 597 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A truck at a depot starts with 2,000 apples and can carry a maximum of 1,000 apples at a time. For every mile traveled, the truck loses 1 apple. What is the maximum number of apples that can be transported across a 1,000 mile desert?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=598)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> This is a transport optimization problem: because the truck can carry at most $1000$ apples at a time but starts with $2000$ apples, it must shuttle apples forward in stages, creating intermediate depots.
>
> Each mile traveled costs $1$ apple. When apples must be moved forward in multiple trips, the effective loss per mile is larger.
>
> ### Step 1: Determine the cost per mile while all $2000$ apples are being moved
>
> Since the truck can carry at most $1000$ apples, moving $2000$ apples forward requires:
>
> - $2$ forward trips to carry the apples,
> - and, except after the last trip, $1$ return trip.
>
> So, to advance all $2000$ apples by $1$ mile, the truck travels
>
> $$
> 2 + 1 + 2 = 3
> $$
>
> trip-miles? More carefully: with $2000$ apples, the truck must make:
>
> - one forward trip carrying $1000$ apples,
> - one return trip,
> - one forward trip carrying the other $1000$ apples.
>
> Thus the truck travels $3$ miles for every $1$ mile the stock of apples is advanced. Since $1$ apple is lost per mile traveled, the cost is
>
> $$
> 3 \text{ apples per mile}.
> $$
>
> This continues until only $1000$ apples remain, because once the stock has been reduced to $1000$, everything can be carried in a single trip.
>
> To reduce from $2000$ apples to $1000$ apples, $1000$ apples must be lost. At $3$ apples per mile, this requires
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3}
> $$
>
> miles.
>
> So after traveling
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3}
> $$
>
> miles, there are exactly $1000$ apples left at that point.
>
> ### Step 2: Move the remaining $1000$ apples the rest of the way
>
> The remaining distance is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{1000}{3} = \frac{2000}{3}
> $$
>
> miles.
>
> Now only one trip is needed, so the loss rate is $1$ apple per mile. Therefore the apples lost on this final stretch are
>
> $$
> \frac{2000}{3}.
> $$
>
> Hence the number of apples delivered is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{2000}{3} = \frac{1000}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1000}{3}}
> $$
>
> So the maximum number of apples that can be transported across the desert is
>
> $$
> \boxed{333\tfrac{1}{3}}
> $$
>
> apples.
