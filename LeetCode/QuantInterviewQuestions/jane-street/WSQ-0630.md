> [!example] Question 630 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have a box filled with cash, where the cash value is uniformly distributed between 1 and 1000. You participate in an auction: you win the box if you bid at least the value of the cash inside; you win nothing and lose nothing otherwise. If you win, you can resell the box for 150% of its value. How much should you bid to maximize the expected value of your profit (resale value minus bid)?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=631)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the cash value in the box be $V$, where
>
> $$
> V \sim \text{Uniform}(1,1000).
> $$
>
> If you bid $b$, then:
>
> - you **win** if $b \ge V$,
> - in that case your profit is $1.5V - b$,
> - otherwise your profit is $0$.
>
> We seek the bid $b$ that maximizes expected profit.
>
> ## 1. Expected profit as a function of $b$
>
> For a given bid $b$, the profit is
>
> $$
> \Pi(b,V)=
> \begin{cases}
> 1.5V-b, & V \le b,\\[4pt]
> 0, & V>b.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Since $V$ is uniform on $[1,1000]$, its density is
>
> $$
> f_V(v)=\frac{1}{999}, \qquad 1 \le v \le 1000.
> $$
>
> Thus, for $1 \le b \le 1000$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \frac{1}{999}\int_1^b (1.5v-b)\,dv.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> \int_1^b (1.5v-b)\,dv
> = \left[0.75v^2-bv\right]_1^b
> = \left(0.75b^2-b^2\right)-\left(0.75-b\right)
> = -0.25b^2+b-0.75.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \frac{-0.25b^2+b-0.75}{999}.
> $$
>
> Since dividing by $999$ does not affect the maximizer, maximize
>
> $$
> g(b)=-0.25b^2+b-0.75.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Maximize the quadratic
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> g'(b)=-0.5b+1.
> $$
>
> Set equal to zero:
>
> $$
> -0.5b+1=0
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> b=2.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> g''(b)=-0.5<0,
> $$
>
> this gives a maximum.
>
> ## 3. Check boundary regions
>
> - If $b<1$, you never win, so expected profit is $0$.
> - If $b>1000$, you always win, and expected profit is
>
> $$
> E[1.5V-b]=1.5E[V]-b.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> E[V]=\frac{1+1000}{2}=500.5,
> $$
>
> this becomes
>
> $$
> 750.75-b,
> $$
>
> which decreases as $b$ increases, so the best in that region is at $b=1000$, not better than the interior optimum.
>
> Thus the global maximizer is indeed
>
> $$
> \boxed{b=2}.
> $$
>
> So the optimal bid is **$\text{\textdollar}2$**.
