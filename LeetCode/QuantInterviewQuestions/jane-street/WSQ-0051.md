> [!example] Question 51 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

There is a solar system with three planets orbiting the sun. One has an orbital period of 60 years, another 84 years, and the third 140 years. Today, the three planets are aligned with the sun. When is the next time all three planets will be aligned with the sun together?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=52)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To be aligned with the sun again, each planet must return to the same relative angular position it has today. This happens when the elapsed time is a common multiple of their orbital periods.
>
> Thus we seek the least common multiple of
>
> $$60,\quad 84,\quad 140.$$
>
> Factor each number:
>
> $$60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5,$$
>
> $$84 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7,$$
>
> $$140 = 2^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7.$$
>
> The least common multiple takes the highest power of each prime appearing:
>
> $$\operatorname{lcm}(60,84,140)=2^2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7.$$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$2^2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7 = 4\cdot 105 = 420.$$
>
> Therefore, the next time all three planets will be aligned with the sun is in
>
> $$\boxed{420\text{ years}}.$$
