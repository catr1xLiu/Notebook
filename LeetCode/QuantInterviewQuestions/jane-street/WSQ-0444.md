> [!example] Question 444 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You play rock, paper, scissors with a friend, but your friend cannot play rock. What is the optimal strategy for each player (the winner gets \$1 from the loser), and what is the expected pay-off?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=445)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Since your friend cannot play rock, his pure strategies are only **paper** and **scissors**. This is a zero-sum game with payoff to you given by:
>
> - If you play **rock**:
>   - against paper: you lose $\text{\textdollar}1$, so payoff $-1$;
>   - against scissors: you win $\text{\textdollar}1$, so payoff $+1$.
> - If you play **paper**:
>   - against paper: payoff $0$;
>   - against scissors: payoff $-1$.
> - If you play **scissors**:
>   - against paper: payoff $+1$;
>   - against scissors: payoff $0$.
>
> Thus the payoff matrix to you is
>
> $$
> \begin{array}{c|cc}
>  & \text{Friend: Paper} & \text{Friend: Scissors} \\
> \hline
> \text{You: Rock} & -1 & 1 \\
> \text{You: Paper} & 0 & -1 \\
> \text{You: Scissors} & 1 & 0
> \end{array}
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Eliminate dominated strategies
>
> Compare your strategies **rock** and **scissors**:
>
> - against paper: scissors gives $1$, rock gives $-1$;
> - against scissors: scissors gives $0$, rock gives $1$.
>
> Neither dominates the other.
>
> Compare **paper** with a mixture of rock and scissors. In fact, paper is never as good as an appropriate mixture, and one can solve directly using only rock and scissors.
>
> So let you play:
>
> - rock with probability $p$,
> - scissors with probability $1-p$,
> - paper with probability $0$.
>
> ## Step 2: Make the friend indifferent
>
> If your friend chooses paper, your expected payoff is
>
> $$
> p(-1) + (1-p)(1) = 1 - 2p.
> $$
>
> If your friend chooses scissors, your expected payoff is
>
> $$
> p(1) + (1-p)(0) = p.
> $$
>
> To maximize your guaranteed payoff, choose $p$ so these are equal:
>
> $$
> 1 - 2p = p.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> 1 = 3p
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> p = \frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore you should play
>
> - rock with probability $\frac{1}{3}$,
> - scissors with probability $\frac{2}{3}$,
> - paper with probability $0$.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the value
>
> With $p=\frac{1}{3}$, the payoff against either response of your friend is
>
> $$
> 1 - 2\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{3},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> So your guaranteed expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}\text{ dollar per game}}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal strategy is to play
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \text{Rock with probability } \frac{1}{3},\quad
> \text{Scissors with probability } \frac{2}{3},\quad
> \text{Paper with probability } 0
> }
> $$
>
> and this yields expected payoff
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}\text{ dollar}}
> $$
>
> per play.
