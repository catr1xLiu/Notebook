> [!example] Question 387 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If you have four coins and I have four coins, we both toss our four coins. If the outcome of your four coins matches exactly with mine, I will give you \$2; otherwise, you give me \$1. Should you take this bet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=388)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in my $4$ coin tosses, and let $Y$ be the number of heads in your $4$ coin tosses. We are paid according to whether $X=Y$.
>
> - If $X=Y$, I receive $\text{\textdollar}2$.
> - If $X\ne Y$, I pay $\text{\textdollar}1$.
>
> To decide whether to play, compute the expected gain.
>
> ## 1. Probability that the numbers of heads are equal
>
> For $k=0,1,2,3,4$,
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{2^4}=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}.
> $$
> Since the two sets of tosses are independent,
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\sum_{k=0}^4 P(X=k)P(Y=k)
> =\sum_{k=0}^4 \left(\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}\right)^2.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\frac{1^2+4^2+6^2+4^2+1^2}{16^2}
> =\frac{1+16+36+16+1}{256}
> =\frac{70}{256}
> =\frac{35}{128}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> P(X\ne Y)=1-\frac{35}{128}=\frac{93}{128}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Expected gain
>
> Let $G$ be my gain. Then
> $$
> E[G]=2\cdot P(X=Y)-1\cdot P(X\ne Y).
> $$
> Substituting,
> $$
> E[G]=2\cdot \frac{35}{128}-\frac{93}{128}
> =\frac{70-93}{128}
> =-\frac{23}{128}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Conclusion
>
> The expected gain is
> $$
> -\frac{23}{128}\text{ dollars} \approx -\$0.18.
> $$
> Since the expectation is negative, this is not a favorable game.
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{No, I should not do it.}}
> $$
