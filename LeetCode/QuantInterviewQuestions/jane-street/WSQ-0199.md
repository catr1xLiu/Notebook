> [!example] Question 199 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

What is the optimal strategy and the expected value when rolling a 20-sided die, where you can pay 1 dollar to re-roll and always receive the face value of your final roll?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=200)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the current face value be $x \in \{1,2,\dots,20\}$. If you stop, your payoff is $x$. If you reroll, you pay $1$ dollar and then face the same decision again after seeing the new result.
>
> The problem is an optimal stopping problem. The natural strategy is a **threshold strategy**: keep rerolling when the current value is too small, and stop when it is large enough.
>
> ## 1. Let $V$ be the optimal expected value
>
> Let $V$ denote the expected payoff when using the optimal strategy starting from a fresh roll.
>
> After seeing a value $x$, you compare:
>
> - **Stop:** receive $x$.
> - **Reroll:** pay $1$ and start over, giving expected value $V-1$.
>
> So the optimal action is:
>
> - stop if $x \ge V-1$,
> - reroll if $x < V-1$.
>
> Thus the optimal stopping value after observing $x$ is
> $$
> \max(x,\; V-1).
> $$
>
> Since the first roll is uniform on $\{1,\dots,20\}$,
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{20}\sum_{x=1}^{20}\max(x,\;V-1).
> $$
>
> ## 2. Determine the threshold
>
> Suppose the threshold is $k$: reroll for $x \le k$ and stop for $x \ge k+1$.
>
> Then for $x \le k$, the value is $V-1$, and for $x \ge k+1$, the value is $x$. Hence
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{20}\left(k(V-1)+\sum_{x=k+1}^{20}x\right).
> $$
>
> We must also have consistency:
> $$
> k < V-1 \le k+1.
> $$
>
> Now test plausible values of $k$.
>
> ### Try $k=14$
>
> Then
> $$
> \sum_{x=15}^{20}x=15+16+17+18+19+20=105.
> $$
> So
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{20}\left(14(V-1)+105\right).
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> 20V=14V-14+105=14V+91,
> $$
> so
> $$
> 6V=91,
> \qquad
> V=\frac{91}{6}\approx 15.167.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> V-1=\frac{85}{6}\approx 14.167,
> $$
> which satisfies
> $$
> 14 < V-1 \le 15.
> $$
> So $k=14$ is consistent.
>
> Therefore the optimal strategy is:
>
> - **reroll on $1,2,\dots,14$**,
> - **keep $15,16,17,18,19,20$**.
>
> ## 3. Expected value under the optimal strategy
>
> The optimal expected value is
> $$
> V=\frac{91}{6}\approx 15.17.
> $$
>
> If one wants the expected **net gain relative to the face value minus reroll costs**, this is exactly the same quantity, since reroll costs were already included in the recursion.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is to **keep the roll if it is at least $15$ and reroll otherwise**.
>
> The corresponding expected value is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{91}{6}\approx 15.17}.
> $$
