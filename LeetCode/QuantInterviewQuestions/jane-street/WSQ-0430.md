> [!example] Question 430 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Suppose you have n points on a circle arranged so that, after connecting every pair of points with straight chords, the number of regions inside the circle is maximized. How many regions are there?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=431)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To maximize the number of regions formed by connecting $n$ points on a circle with chords, the points must be placed so that no three chords intersect at a single interior point. Then each intersection is determined by a choice of $4$ points, since any $4$ points determine exactly one interior intersection.
>
> The total number of regions is obtained by counting:
>
> - $1$ initial region,
> - one new region for each chord drawn between a pair of points,
> - one additional new region for each interior intersection.
>
> There are
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{2}
> $$
>
> chords, and
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{4}
> $$
>
> interior intersections.
>
> Therefore, the maximum number of regions is
>
> $$
> R(n)=1+\binom{n}{2}+\binom{n}{4}.
> $$
>
> Thus the number of regions is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1+\binom{n}{2}+\binom{n}{4}}.
> $$
