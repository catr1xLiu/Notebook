> [!example] Question 301 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Intern Interview

You have 3 buckets, each able to hold one ball. Every time you throw a ball, you are guaranteed to hit a bucket; if the bucket is already filled, the ball bounces out. Each ball that goes in gives +1, each ball that bounces out gives -1. If you have 2 balls to throw, what is the expected gain?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=302)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the three buckets be distinct, and assume each throw lands in each bucket with probability $\frac13$, independently of previous throws.
>
> Each bucket has capacity $1$. A throw into an empty bucket puts a ball in and gives payoff $+1$. A throw into a filled bucket causes the ball to bounce out, removing the existing ball and giving payoff $-1$.
>
> There are $2$ throws total.
>
> ## First throw
>
> Initially all buckets are empty, so the first throw must land in an empty bucket. Hence the gain on the first throw is
>
> $$
> +1.
> $$
>
> After this throw, exactly one bucket is filled.
>
> ## Second throw
>
> Now there is:
>
> - $1$ filled bucket,
> - $2$ empty buckets.
>
> Thus:
>
> - with probability $\frac13$, the second throw hits the filled bucket, so a ball comes out and the gain is $-1$;
> - with probability $\frac23$, it hits an empty bucket, so a ball goes in and the gain is $+1$.
>
> Therefore the expected gain on the second throw is
>
> $$
> \frac23(+1) + \frac13(-1)
> = \frac23 - \frac13
> = \frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Total expected gain
>
> By linearity of expectation,
>
> $$
> E[\text{total gain}] = 1 + \frac13 = \frac43.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected gain is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac43}.
> $$
