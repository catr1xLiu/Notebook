> [!example] Question 585 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If you extend the faces of a tetrahedron as planes infinitely in all directions, how many regions does this divide three-dimensional space into?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=586)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each face of a tetrahedron lies in a plane. Thus the problem asks for the number of regions determined in $\mathbb{R}^3$ by the $4$ planes containing the faces of a tetrahedron.
>
> These $4$ planes are in general position:
>
> - no two are parallel,
> - no three share a common line,
> - no four pass through a common point.
>
> Count regions by adding the planes one at a time.
>
> - With $0$ planes: $1$ region.
> - Adding the first plane divides space into $2$ regions.
> - Adding the second plane intersects the first in a line, so it is cut into $2$ regions; hence it creates $2$ new regions, for a total of $4$.
> - Adding the third plane intersects the first two in two lines on that plane, and those two lines intersect, dividing the plane into $4$ regions; hence it creates $4$ new regions, for a total of $8$.
> - Adding the fourth plane intersects the first three in three lines on that plane. Because the planes come from a tetrahedron, these three lines are in general position on the plane: no two are parallel and not all three concurrent. Three such lines divide the plane into
>   $$
>   1+\frac{3(3+1)}{2}=7
>   $$
>   regions, so the fourth plane creates $7$ new regions.
>
> Therefore the total number of regions is
> $$
> 1+1+2+4+7=15.
> $$
>
> \[
> \boxed{15}
> \]
