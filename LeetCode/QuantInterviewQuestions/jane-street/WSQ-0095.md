> [!example] Question 95 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the probability of obtaining the same result across three events: rolling a single die, drawing a random card from a standard 52-card deck (with aces counted as 1), and spinning a roulette wheel (consider the score as the number landed on)?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=96)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To match across all three events, there must be a number that can appear on
>
> - a standard die: $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$,
> - a standard deck rank (with ace $=1$): $\{1,2,\dots,13\}$,
> - a roulette wheel.
>
> On a standard roulette wheel, the possible scores include $0$ and the integers $1$ through $36$ (and possibly $00$ on an American wheel, which does not affect this calculation). Thus the common possible matching values are exactly
>
> $$
> \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}.
> $$
>
> For any fixed value $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$,
>
> - $P(\text{die}=k)=\frac{1}{6}$,
> - $P(\text{card value}=k)=\frac{4}{52}=\frac{1}{13}$,
> - $P(\text{roulette}=k)=\frac{1}{37}$ for European roulette, or $\frac{1}{38}$ for American roulette.
>
> Since the events are independent, the probability that all three equal a specific $k$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{37}
> $$
>
> for European roulette, and
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{38}
> $$
>
> for American roulette.
>
> There are $6$ possible matching values, so multiply by $6$:
>
> ### European roulette
> $$
> 6\cdot \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{37}
> = \frac{1}{13\cdot 37}
> = \frac{1}{481}.
> $$
>
> ### American roulette
> $$
> 6\cdot \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{38}
> = \frac{1}{13\cdot 38}
> = \frac{1}{494}.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{481}}
> $$
>
> for a European wheel, or
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{494}}
> $$
>
> for an American wheel.
