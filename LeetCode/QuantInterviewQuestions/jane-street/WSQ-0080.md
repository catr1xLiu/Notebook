> [!example] Question 80 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You have 4 coins, and you earn \$1 for each heads. If you have the option to reflip all of the coins, what is the expected value of this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=81)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained on the first flip of $4$ fair coins. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \text{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> If you keep the first result, your payoff is $X$ dollars. If instead you choose to reflip all $4$ coins, then the new expected payoff is simply the expected number of heads from $4$ fair coins:
>
> $$
> E[\text{reflip payoff}] = 4 \cdot \tfrac12 = 2.
> $$
>
> Thus, after seeing the first outcome, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the result if $X \ge 2$,
> - reflip if $X < 2$.
>
> So the value of the game is
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)].
> $$
>
> Compute this using the distribution of $X$:
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\frac{1}{16}, \quad P(X=1)=\frac{4}{16}, \quad P(X=2)=\frac{6}{16}, \quad P(X=3)=\frac{4}{16}, \quad P(X=4)=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)]
> =2\left(\frac{1}{16}+\frac{4}{16}+\frac{6}{16}\right)
> +3\left(\frac{4}{16}\right)
> +4\left(\frac{1}{16}\right).
> $$
>
> Now simplify:
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)]
> =2\left(\frac{11}{16}\right)+\frac{12}{16}+\frac{4}{16}
> =\frac{22}{16}+\frac{16}{16}
> =\frac{38}{16}
> =\frac{19}{8}.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{19}{8}}
> $$
>
> dollars.
