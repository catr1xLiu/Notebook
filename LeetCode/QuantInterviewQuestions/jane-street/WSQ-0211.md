> [!example] Question 211 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

You have 4 fair coins. If you flip all of them, what is the probability of getting at least 2 heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=212)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For the mental math:
>
> - $12{,}000{,}000 - 101 = 11{,}999{,}899$
> - $24 \cdot 24 = 576$
> - $12\%$ of $54$ is $0.12 \cdot 54 = 6.48$
>
> For the probability question:
>
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when flipping $4$ fair coins. Then $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4, \tfrac12)$.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(X \ge 2).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=P(X=2)+P(X=3)+P(X=4).
> $$
>
> Compute each term:
> $$
> P(X=2)=\frac{\binom{4}{2}}{16}=\frac{6}{16},
> $$
> $$
> P(X=3)=\frac{\binom{4}{3}}{16}=\frac{4}{16},
> $$
> $$
> P(X=4)=\frac{\binom{4}{4}}{16}=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=\frac{6+4+1}{16}=\frac{11}{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus the answers are:
>
> - $11{,}999{,}899$
> - $576$
> - $6.48$
> - Probability of at least $2$ heads: $\boxed{\frac{11}{16}}$
