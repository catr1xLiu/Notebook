> [!example] Question 208 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting at least 2 heads when tossing a fair coin 5 times?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=209)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability of getting **at least $2$ heads** in $5$ tosses of a fair coin, let $X$ be the number of heads obtained.
>
> Then $X$ follows a binomial distribution with parameters $n=5$ and $p=\tfrac{1}{2}$, so
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{5}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5.
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2).
> $$
>
> It is simplest to use the complement:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=1-P(X=0)-P(X=1).
> $$
>
> Compute each term:
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\binom{5}{0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5=\frac{1}{32},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(X=1)=\binom{5}{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5=\frac{5}{32}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=1-\frac{1}{32}-\frac{5}{32}
> =1-\frac{6}{32}
> =\frac{26}{32}
> =\frac{13}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{13}{16}}.
> $$
