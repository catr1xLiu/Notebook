> [!example] Question 12 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

a) Flip 4 coins and you get paid \$1 for every head. What is the expected value of your earnings? b) If you have a magic wand that allows you to tap on every pair of coins with opposite faces (one head and one tail) to flip both of them again, what is the expected value of your earnings after using the wand?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=13)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $H$ denote the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped. Since each coin is fair,
>
> $$
> H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> Your earnings are the number of heads, measured in dollars.
>
> ### (a) Expected earnings from the original $4$ flips
>
> For a binomial random variable,
>
> $$
> E[H] = np.
> $$
>
> Here $n=4$ and $p=\tfrac12$, so
>
> $$
> E[H] = 4 \cdot \tfrac12 = 2.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected earnings are
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### (b) Expected earnings after re-flipping every mixed pair
>
> Interpret the magic wand as follows: after the initial $4$ flips, partition the coins into $2$ pairs. For each pair, if it shows one head and one tail, re-flip both coins once. Then earnings are again the total number of heads showing after this procedure.
>
> It suffices to analyze one pair of fair coins.
>
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in a single pair after applying the rule.
>
> - If the initial pair is $HH$, then no re-flip occurs and $X=2$.
> - If the initial pair is $TT$, then no re-flip occurs and $X=0$.
> - If the initial pair is $HT$ or $TH$, then both coins are re-flipped. After re-flipping, the expected number of heads is
>   $$
>   2 \cdot \tfrac12 = 1.
>   $$
>
> Now compute the expectation for one pair:
>
> - $P(HH)=\tfrac14$,
> - $P(TT)=\tfrac14$,
> - $P(HT \text{ or } TH)=\tfrac12$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[X] = \tfrac14 \cdot 2 + \tfrac14 \cdot 0 + \tfrac12 \cdot 1
> = \tfrac12 + 0 + \tfrac12
> = 1.
> $$
>
> Since there are $2$ independent pairs, the total expected number of heads is
>
> $$
> 2 \cdot 1 = 2.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected earnings are still
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{(a)\ \ \$2 \qquad (b)\ \ \$2}
> $$
