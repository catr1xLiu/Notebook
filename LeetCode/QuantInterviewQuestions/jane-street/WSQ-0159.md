> [!example] Question 159 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Find the probability that a randomly chosen 3-digit number has its first digit even, its second digit odd, and its third digit different from the first two digits.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=160)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider all $3$-digit numbers, from $100$ to $999$.
>
> The total number of $3$-digit numbers is
> $$
> 999-100+1=900.
> $$
>
> We now count the favorable numbers.
>
> ### Step 1: First digit is even
> The first digit can be one of
> $$
> 2,4,6,8,
> $$
> so there are
> $$
> 4
> $$
> choices.
>
> ### Step 2: Second digit is odd
> The second digit can be one of
> $$
> 1,3,5,7,9,
> $$
> so there are
> $$
> 5
> $$
> choices.
>
> Since the first digit is even and the second digit is odd, they are automatically different.
>
> ### Step 3: Third digit is different from the first two digits
> The third digit can be any digit from $0$ to $9$, so initially there are
> $$
> 10
> $$
> choices.
>
> It must be different from both the first and second digits, eliminating $2$ digits. Hence the number of choices is
> $$
> 10-2=8.
> $$
>
> ### Step 4: Count favorable numbers
> Thus the number of favorable $3$-digit numbers is
> $$
> 4 \times 5 \times 8 = 160.
> $$
>
> ### Step 5: Compute the probability
> Therefore, the required probability is
> $$
> \frac{160}{900}=\frac{8}{45}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{8}{45}}.
> $$
