> [!example] Question 178 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Estimate the weight of Mount Kilimanjaro.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=179)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Estimation
>
> Interpret “weight” as the gravitational force on the mountain. This requires an estimate of the mountain’s mass.
>
> Use
> $$
> W = mg, \qquad m = \rho V,
> $$
> so
> $$
> W = \rho V g.
> $$
>
> ## 1. Approximate the mountain’s volume
>
> Model Mt. Kilimanjaro as a cone.
>
> Take:
> - height above surrounding plain: about $h \approx 4\,\text{km} = 4\times 10^3\,\text{m}$,
> - base radius: about $r \approx 15\,\text{km} = 1.5\times 10^4\,\text{m}$.
>
> Then
> $$
> V \approx \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h
> = \frac{1}{3}\pi (1.5\times 10^4)^2(4\times 10^3).
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> r^2 = 2.25\times 10^8,
> $$
> so
> $$
> V \approx \frac{1}{3}\pi (2.25\times 10^8)(4\times 10^3)
> = \frac{1}{3}\pi (9\times 10^{11})
> \approx 9.4\times 10^{11}\,\text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> Thus, to order of magnitude,
> $$
> V \approx 10^{12}\,\text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Estimate the mass
>
> A volcanic mountain is mostly rock, so use density
> $$
> \rho \approx 2.7\times 10^3\,\text{kg/m}^3.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> m \approx \rho V
> \approx (2.7\times 10^3)(9.4\times 10^{11})
> \approx 2.5\times 10^{15}\,\text{kg}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Convert mass to weight
>
> Using
> $$
> g \approx 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 10\,\text{m/s}^2,
> $$
> the weight is
> $$
> W \approx mg
> \approx (2.5\times 10^{15})(9.8)
> \approx 2.5\times 10^{16}\,\text{N}.
> $$
>
> ## Final estimate
>
> $$
> \boxed{W \approx 2\times 10^{16}\text{ to }3\times 10^{16}\,\text{N}}
> $$
>
> Equivalently, the mountain’s mass is roughly
> $$
> \boxed{m \approx 2.5\times 10^{15}\,\text{kg}}.
> $$
>
> So a reasonable order-of-magnitude estimate for the weight of Mt. Kilimanjaro is
>
> $$
> \boxed{10^{16}\,\text{N}}.
> $$
