> [!example] Question 611 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Today is 15/09/2011. What is the nearest future date such that all eight digits in the date (DDMMYYYY) are unique?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=612)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Write the date $15/09/2011$ in eight-digit form as $15092011$.
>
> We seek the nearest future date whose eight digits — day, month, and year written as $\text{DDMMYYYY}$ — are all distinct.
>
> ## 1. Ruling out the rest of $2011$ and all of $2000$–$2099$
>
> The year $2011$ itself already contains a repeated digit (the digit $1$ appears twice), so no date in $2011$ can have eight distinct digits.
>
> More generally, consider any year of the form $20ab$ (any year from $2000$ to $2099$) whose own four digits $2,0,a,b$ are distinct. Since the year already uses the digits $2$ and $0$:
>
> - a month from $01$ to $09$ reuses the year's $0$,
> - month $10$ reuses both $1$ and $0$,
> - month $11$ repeats a digit within itself,
> - month $12$ reuses both $1$ and $2$.
>
> Every possible month conflicts with the year, regardless of $a$ and $b$. Hence no date in $2000$–$2099$ can work.
>
> ## 2. A key requirement on the year
>
> For any later year, write its four digits as $2,a,b,c$ (the thousands digit stays $2$ until year $3000$, far beyond where we need to look). We now show:
>
> **A valid date can only exist in a year that contains neither the digit $0$ nor the digit $1$.**
>
> - Suppose the year contains $0$. Then, exactly as in Part 1, months $01$–$09$ reuse the year's $0$, month $10$ reuses $0$, month $11$ repeats internally, and month $12$ reuses the year's $2$. Every month fails.
> - Suppose instead the year avoids $0$ but contains $1$. Then months $10,11,12$ all reuse the year's $1$ (or repeat internally), so the month must have the form $0X$, which uses up the digit $0$. Now the day, which is at most $31$, needs its tens digit to be $0,1,2,$ or $3$. But $0$ has just been used by the month, and $1,2$ are already used by the year, so at best only $3$ remains — forcing the day to be $30$ or $31$, whose units digit is $0$ or $1$, both already taken. So the day is impossible either way.
>
> Hence a year can only work if it contains **neither** $0$ nor $1$.
>
> ## 3. The smallest qualifying year
>
> We want the smallest year greater than $2011$, with four distinct digits $2,a,b,c$, that avoids both $0$ and $1$ entirely.
>
> Since $0$, $1$, and the thousands digit $2$ are all excluded from $\{a,b,c\}$, these three digits must come from $\{3,4,5,6,7,8,9\}$. To make the year as small as possible, take the smallest available digits in order for the hundreds, tens, and units places:
> $$
> a=3,\qquad b=4,\qquad c=5,
> $$
> giving the year $2345$.
>
> Every year from $2012$ through $2344$ either falls in $2000$–$2099$ (ruled out in Part 1), repeats a digit internally (as happens throughout $2200$–$2299$ and at $2330$–$2339$), or contains a $0$ or a $1$ (ruled out in Part 2) — for instance $2134$ contains the digit $1$, so by Part 2 no valid day can be formed there no matter which month is chosen. Hence $2345$ is the first year for which a valid date can possibly exist.
>
> ## 4. The earliest valid date within $2345$
>
> The year $2345$ uses the digits $2,3,4,5$, leaving $\{0,1,6,7,8,9\}$ available for the day and month.
>
> Any month equal to $02,03,04,05$ immediately reuses a digit from the year, so the only candidate months starting with $0$ are $01,06,07,08,09$; we check them in increasing order.
>
> **Month $01$:** uses $0$ and $1$, leaving only $\{6,7,8,9\}$ for the day. Every two-digit day formed from these digits is at least $67$, which exceeds $31$, so no valid day exists.
>
> **Month $06$:** uses $0$ and $6$, leaving $\{1,7,8,9\}$ for the day. Since $0,2,3$ are all taken, the day's tens digit must be $1$ (the only survivor among $0,1,2,3$), so the day has the form $1D$ with $D\in\{7,8,9\}$, giving valid days $17,18,19$. The smallest is $17$.
>
> So the earliest valid date is $17/06/2345$.
>
> For comparison, $16/09/2345$ also has eight distinct digits, but September falls later in the year $2345$ than June, so it is not the earliest such date.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The nearest future date with all eight digits of $\text{DDMMYYYY}$ distinct is
>
> $$
> \boxed{17/06/2345}.
> $$
