> [!example] Question 139 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

In n coin tosses, what is the probability that the number of heads is even? Prove your result rigorously.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=140)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $n$ tosses of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Bin}(n,\tfrac12),
> $$
>
> so for $k=0,1,\dots,n$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> We want the probability that $X$ is even:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X \text{ is even})
> = \sum_{\substack{0\le k\le n\\ k \text{ even}}} \binom{n}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^n
> = \left(\frac12\right)^n \sum_{\substack{0\le k\le n\\ k \text{ even}}} \binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Thus it remains to evaluate the sum of the even-indexed binomial coefficients.
>
> ## Step 1: Use the binomial theorem
>
> From
>
> $$
> (1+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}=2^n
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}(-1)^k=0 \qquad (n\ge 1),
> $$
>
> we obtain
>
> $$
> \sum_{k \text{ even}} \binom{n}{k}
> -
> \sum_{k \text{ odd}} \binom{n}{k}
> =0.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \sum_{k \text{ even}} \binom{n}{k}
> =
> \sum_{k \text{ odd}} \binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Since their sum is $2^n$, each must equal $2^{n-1}$:
>
> $$
> \sum_{k \text{ even}} \binom{n}{k} = 2^{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X \text{ is even})
> =
> \left(\frac12\right)^n 2^{n-1}
> =
> \frac12,
> \qquad n\ge 1.
> $$
>
> So the percentage is
>
> $$
> 50\%.
> $$
>
> ## Edge case
>
> If $n=0$, there are no tosses, so the number of heads is $0$, which is even. Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X \text{ is even})=1.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> For $n\ge 1$, the probability that the number of heads in $n$ fair coin tosses is even is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}
> $$
>
> that is,
>
> $$
> \boxed{50\%}.
> $$
