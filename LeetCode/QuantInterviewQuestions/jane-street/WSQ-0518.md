> [!example] Question 518 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the probability that in n coin tosses, the number of heads will be even?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=519)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $P_n$ denote the probability that in $n$ tosses of a fair coin, the number of heads is even.
>
> ## Step 1: Express the probability as a sum
>
> If exactly $k$ heads occur, then the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P_n=\frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> So the problem reduces to evaluating the sum of the even binomial coefficients.
>
> ## Step 2: Use the binomial identities
>
> From the binomial theorem,
>
> $$
> (1+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}=2^n.
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> (1-1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> Separating even and odd terms in the second identity gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{k\text{ even}}\binom{n}{k}-\sum_{k\text{ odd}}\binom{n}{k}=0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \sum_{k\text{ even}}\binom{n}{k}
> =
> \sum_{k\text{ odd}}\binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Since the total sum is $2^n$, each of these sums must equal
>
> $$
> 2^{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P_n=\frac{1}{2^n}\cdot 2^{n-1}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> For every integer $n \ge 1$, the probability that the number of heads in $n$ fair coin tosses is even is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> If $n=0$, there are $0$ heads, which is even, so the probability is $1$.
