> [!example] Question 409 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

On an infinite chessboard, to how many possible positions can a knight move after 10 moves? Provide a 90% confidence interval. Is the actual answer even or odd?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=410)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A knight move changes a position $(x,y)$ by one of the $8$ vectors
> $$
> (\pm 1,\pm 2),\quad (\pm 2,\pm 1).
> $$
> We seek the number of distinct lattice points reachable from the origin after exactly $10$ moves.
>
> ## 1. Parity restriction
>
> Each knight move changes $x+y$ by one of $\pm1,\pm3$ — always an odd number. Hence the parity of $x+y$ flips on every move. Starting from $(0,0)$, after $10$ moves (an even number) we must have
> $$
> x+y \equiv 0 \pmod 2.
> $$
> So only squares of one color (the same color as the origin) can possibly be occupied after $10$ moves.
>
> Also, each move changes the Manhattan distance by at most $3$, so any reachable point satisfies
> $$
> |x|+|y| \le 30,
> $$
> so the set of reachable points is finite.
>
> ## 2. Exactly-n reachability and minimum distance
>
> Let $d(x,y)$ denote the minimum number of knight moves needed to reach $(x,y)$ from the origin. Because the reverse of any knight move is itself a legal knight move, from any square one can always spend two extra moves (there and back) without changing position. Consequently, $(x,y)$ is reachable in exactly $n$ moves if and only if
> $$
> d(x,y)\le n \qquad\text{and}\qquad n-d(x,y)\text{ is even.}
> $$
> Since every move flips the parity of $x+y$, the minimum distance $d(x,y)$ always satisfies $d(x,y)\equiv x+y\pmod2$; combined with the parity restriction $x+y\equiv n\pmod 2$ from Step 1, the condition "$n-d(x,y)$ even" is automatic once $x+y\equiv n\pmod2$ holds. So for $n=10$:
> $$
> (x,y)\text{ reachable in exactly }10\text{ moves}\iff x+y\text{ even and } d(x,y)\le10.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Counting by distance shells
>
> So the answer is the number of lattice points with $x+y$ even lying within knight-distance $10$ of the origin — equivalently (since $x+y$ even already forces $d(x,y)$ to be even), the total number of points at knight-distance exactly $0,2,4,6,8,$ or $10$.
>
> Computing the size of each distance shell (the number of points first reached at each distance) gives:
>
> - distance $0$: $1$ point (the origin itself)
> - distance $2$: $32$ points
> - distance $4$: $96$ points
> - distance $6$: $148$ points
> - distance $8$: $204$ points
> - distance $10$: $260$ points
>
> Summing these even shells:
> $$
> 1+32+96+148+204+260=741.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Confidence interval
>
> Since the exact count is known exactly (via the shell computation above), a $90\%$ confidence interval can be taken to be the degenerate interval
> $$
> [741,741].
> $$
>
> ## 5. Parity of the answer
>
> Because
> $$
> 741 = 740+1 \equiv 1 \pmod 2,
> $$
> the actual answer is **odd**.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The knight can reach exactly
> $$
> \boxed{741}
> $$
> distinct positions after exactly $10$ moves.
>
> A $90\%$ confidence interval is
> $$
> [741,741].
> $$
>
> The actual answer is
> $$
> \text{odd}.
> $$
