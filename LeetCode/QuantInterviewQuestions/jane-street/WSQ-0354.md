> [!example] Question 354 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You throw a fair die and are paid the number you roll. If you are not satisfied with the first result, you may choose to throw a second time and must accept the result of the second throw. What is the expected payoff?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=355)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1$ be the result of the first throw and $X_2$ the result of the second throw. Each is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, and if the second throw is taken, its result must be accepted.
>
> The problem is an optimal stopping problem: after observing $X_1$, decide whether to keep it or reject it and take $X_2$.
>
> ## Step 1: Value of choosing the second throw
>
> If the second throw is taken, the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[X_2] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, after seeing the first result, it is optimal to:
>
> - keep $X_1$ if $X_1 \ge 4$,
> - reroll if $X_1 \le 3$,
>
> since $4,5,6 > 3.5$ and $1,2,3 < 3.5$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expected payoff under this strategy
>
> The payoff is:
>
> - $1,2,3$ on the first throw: reject and receive expected value $3.5$,
> - $4,5,6$ on the first throw: accept that amount.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]
> = \frac{1}{6}\left(3.5 + 3.5 + 3.5 + 4 + 5 + 6\right).
> $$
>
> Compute the sum:
>
> $$
> 3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6 = 10.5 + 15 = 25.5.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25 = \frac{17}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{17}{4}}
> $$
>
> The optimal rule is to keep the first throw if it is $4$, $5$, or $6$, and otherwise throw again.
