> [!example] Question 374 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Two teams, each with an equal probability of winning, are playing a best-of-7 tournament series. What is the probability that the series lasts until the 7th game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=375)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To reach the $7$th game in a best-of-$7$ series, neither team can have already won $4$ games in the first $6$ games. Thus, after $6$ games, the only way the series is still undecided is if each team has won exactly $3$ games.
>
> Each game is independent, and each team has probability $\tfrac12$ of winning any game. Therefore, among the first $6$ games, we need the probability of exactly $3$ wins for one team and $3$ wins for the other.
>
> The number of such sequences is
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{3} = 20.
> $$
>
> The total number of possible outcomes of the first $6$ games is
>
> $$
> 2^6 = 64.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability that the series goes to a $7$th game is
>
> $$
> \frac{\binom{6}{3}}{2^6}
> = \frac{20}{64}
> = \frac{5}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{16}}.
> $$
