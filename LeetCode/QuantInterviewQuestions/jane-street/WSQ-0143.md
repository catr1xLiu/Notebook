> [!example] Question 143 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

What is the smallest number whose digits multiply to give 96?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=144)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To obtain the smallest positive integer whose digits have product $96$, factor $96$ into single-digit factors and then arrange those digits in increasing order.
>
> First factor:
> $$
> 96 = 2^5 \cdot 3
> $$
>
> We want to group these primes into digits from $2$ to $9$ so that:
>
> 1. the number of digits is as small as possible, and  
> 2. among such numbers, the arrangement is as small as possible.
>
> Combine factors into the largest possible single digits:
> $$
> 96 = 8 \cdot 6 \cdot 2
> $$
> since
> $$
> 8 = 2^3,\qquad 6 = 2 \cdot 3,\qquad 2 = 2.
> $$
>
> Thus the digits can be $8, 6, 2$. Any representation with fewer than $3$ digits is impossible, because no two digits from $1$ to $9$ multiply to $96$.
>
> To make the number as small as possible, write these digits in increasing order:
> $$
> 268
> $$
>
> Check:
> $$
> 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 8 = 96
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest number is
> $$
> \boxed{268}
> $$
