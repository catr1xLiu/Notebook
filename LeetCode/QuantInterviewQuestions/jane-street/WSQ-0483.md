> [!example] Question 483 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

If I flip 100 coins and then multiply the number of heads by the number of tails, what is the expected value of that product? Can you provide a confidence interval for this value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=484)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $100$ fair coin flips. Then
> $$
> H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100, \tfrac12),
> $$
> and the number of tails is
> $$
> T = 100 - H.
> $$
> The quantity of interest is
> $$
> X = HT = H(100-H).
> $$
>
> ## Expected value
>
> Compute
> $$
> E[X] = E\!\big[H(100-H)\big] = 100E[H] - E[H^2].
> $$
> For a binomial random variable,
> $$
> E[H] = np = 100\cdot \tfrac12 = 50,
> $$
> and
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(H) = np(1-p) = 100\cdot \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac12 = 25.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> E[H^2] = \operatorname{Var}(H) + (E[H])^2 = 25 + 50^2 = 2525.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> E[X] = 100\cdot 50 - 2525 = 2475.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
> $$
> \boxed{2475}.
> $$
>
> ## A confidence interval
>
> Since
> $$
> X = H(100-H) = 2500 - (H-50)^2,
> $$
> the maximum possible value is $2500$, attained when $H=50$.
>
> A convenient approximate $95\%$ interval comes from the normal approximation
> $$
> H \approx N(50,25),
> $$
> so
> $$
> Z=\frac{H-50}{5}\approx N(0,1).
> $$
> A central $95\%$ interval for $H$ is approximately
> $$
> 50 \pm 1.96\cdot 5 = 50 \pm 9.8,
> $$
> that is,
> $$
> H \in [40.2,\,59.8].
> $$
> Rounding to integers gives roughly
> $$
> H \in [40,60].
> $$
> Transforming to $X=H(100-H)$,
> $$
> X_{\min} \approx 40\cdot 60 = 2400,
> \qquad
> X_{\max} = 50\cdot 50 = 2500.
> $$
> Thus an approximate central $95\%$ interval for the product is
> $$
> \boxed{[2400,\,2500]}.
> $$
>
> ## Remark on interpretation
>
> This is a prediction interval for the random quantity $HT$ from one experiment of $100$ flips, not a confidence interval for an unknown parameter. Since $HT$ is discrete and skewed toward its upper bound, the interval is necessarily one-sided in shape after transformation.
