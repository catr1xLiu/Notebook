> [!example] Question 404 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Consider the following game: You are given a 12-sided die and will be paid the number that the die lands on. If you are unhappy with the roll, you may instead choose to roll two 6-sided dice and will be paid the sum of those two dice. What is the expected value of this game, and how much would you be willing to pay to play?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=405)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the outcome of the $12$-sided die, and let $Y$ be the sum of two fair $6$-sided dice.
>
> The game works as follows:
>
> - First, roll the $12$-sided die and observe $X$.
> - Then choose either:
>   - keep $X$, or
>   - reject it and instead receive $Y$.
>
> Thus the optimal strategy is to keep $X$ when it is at least as large as the expected value of $Y$, and reroll otherwise.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the value of the fallback option
>
> For two fair $6$-sided dice,
>
> $$
> E[Y] = E[D_1 + D_2] = E[D_1] + E[D_2] = 3.5 + 3.5 = 7.
> $$
>
> So if the initial roll is less than $7$, it is better to switch; if it is greater than $7$, it is better to keep it. When $X=7$, both choices have the same expected value.
>
> Hence the optimal payoff is
>
> $$
> \max(X, 7).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expected payoff
>
> Since $X$ is uniform on $\{1,2,\dots,12\}$,
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,7)] = \frac{1}{12}\sum_{x=1}^{12} \max(x,7).
> $$
>
> For $x=1,2,3,4,5,6$, the value is $7$.
> For $x=7,8,9,10,11,12$, the value is $x$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,7)]
> = \frac{1}{12}\left(6\cdot 7 + (7+8+9+10+11+12)\right).
> $$
>
> Now,
>
> $$
> 7+8+9+10+11+12 = 57,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,7)] = \frac{42+57}{12} = \frac{99}{12} = \frac{33}{4} = 8.25.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The fair price to pay for this game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$8.25}.
> $$
