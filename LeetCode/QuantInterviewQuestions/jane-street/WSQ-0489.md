> [!example] Question 489 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Find the smallest positive integer x such that the product of all the digits of x is 10,000.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=490)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make the product of the digits equal to $10{,}000$, first factorize:
>
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 10^4 = 2^4 \cdot 5^4.
> $$
>
> Each digit must be an integer from $0$ to $9$. Since the product is nonzero, no digit can be $0$.
>
> A digit contributes factors of $2$ and $5$ as follows:
>
> - $1$ contributes nothing,
> - $2$ contributes $2$,
> - $4$ contributes $2^2$,
> - $5$ contributes $5$,
> - $8$ contributes $2^3$.
>
> To obtain $2^4 \cdot 5^4$, we need four factors of $5$. The only digit containing a factor of $5$ is $5$, so the number must contain exactly four digits equal to $5$.
>
> These four $5$'s contribute
>
> $$
> 5^4.
> $$
>
> We still need a factor of
>
> $$
> 2^4 = 16.
> $$
>
> To make the integer as small as possible, we want as few digits as possible, and then arrange them in increasing order. The digit $8$ gives $2^3$, so using $8$ and $2$ gives
>
> $$
> 8 \cdot 2 = 16.
> $$
>
> Thus the digits can be
>
> $$
> 2, 5, 5, 5, 5, 8.
> $$
>
> Their product is
>
> $$
> 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 8 = 10{,}000.
> $$
>
> Arranging these digits in increasing order gives the smallest such integer:
>
> $$
> 255558.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest positive integer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{255558}.
> $$
