> [!example] Question 510 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Given a fair six-sided die (faces numbered 1 to 6), what is the expected value of a single roll? Suppose you have two chances: after the first roll, you can choose whether to keep the result or roll again. What is the maximum expected value you can achieve if you use the best possible strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=511)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For one fair six-sided die, the expected value is the average of the outcomes:
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Now suppose you may roll up to two times, and after the first roll you can either keep it or reject it and take a second roll. The goal is to choose the strategy that maximizes the expected value.
>
> ## Step 1: Value of rejecting the first roll
>
> If you reject the first roll, then you must take the second roll, whose expected value is
>
> $$
> E[X] = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Therefore, after observing the first roll, you should:
>
> - keep it if it is greater than $3.5$,
> - reroll if it is less than $3.5$.
>
> Since die outcomes are integers, this means:
>
> - keep $4,5,6$,
> - reroll on $1,2,3$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expected value under this strategy
>
> With probability $\frac{1}{6}$ each, the first roll is $1,2,3,4,5,6$.
>
> - If the first roll is $1,2,$ or $3$, you reroll, giving expected value $3.5$.
> - If the first roll is $4,5,$ or $6$, you keep it.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[\text{optimal payoff}]
> = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> 3.5+3.5+3.5 = 10.5
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 10.5+4+5+6 = 25.5.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\text{optimal payoff}] = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - With one roll, the expected value is
>
> $$
> 3.5.
> $$
>
> - With up to two rolls and the best strategy, the maximum expected value is
>
> $$
> 4.25.
> $$
>
> The optimal strategy is to keep the first roll if it is $4,5,$ or $6$, and reroll if it is $1,2,$ or $3$.
