> [!example] Question 101 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

In a standard pack of cards, what is the expected number of cards one must draw to obtain cards from all four suits?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=102)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of cards drawn until cards from all four suits have appeared, when a standard $52$-card deck (thirteen cards in each suit) is dealt out in a uniformly random order. We seek $E[X]$.
>
> ## Reformulating in terms of first occurrences
>
> For each suit $i\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, let $M_i$ be the position (draw number) at which the first card of suit $i$ appears. The process finishes exactly when the last suit to appear finally shows up, so
>
> $$
> X = \max(M_1,M_2,M_3,M_4).
> $$
>
> ## A key fact about first occurrences
>
> For a set of $m$ "marked" cards sitting among $N$ cards in a uniformly random order, the expected position of the first marked card is
>
> $$
> \frac{N+1}{m+1}.
> $$
>
> To see this, note that the position of the first marked card equals $1$ plus the number of unmarked cards that precede it. By symmetry, the $N-m$ unmarked cards are equally likely to fall into any of the $m+1$ gaps determined by the $m$ marked cards (before the first, between consecutive marked cards, or after the last), so the expected number of unmarked cards before the first marked one is $\frac{N-m}{m+1}$. Hence the expected position of the first marked card is
>
> $$
> 1+\frac{N-m}{m+1}=\frac{N+1}{m+1}.
> $$
>
> ## Applying inclusion–exclusion to the maximum
>
> For any four numbers, the maximum can be written in terms of minima via inclusion–exclusion:
>
> $$
> \max(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4) = \sum_i a_i - \sum_{i<j}\min(a_i,a_j) + \sum_{i<j<k}\min(a_i,a_j,a_k) - \min(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4).
> $$
>
> (This is the same identity as $\max(a,b)=a+b-\min(a,b)$, generalized to four terms.)
>
> Taking $a_i=M_i$, the minimum of first-occurrence positions over any subset of suits is just the first-occurrence position of the *union* of those suits — a marked set whose size is $13$ times the number of suits in the subset. So, using the formula above:
>
> - for a single suit (a marked set of size $13$): $E[M_i]=\frac{53}{14}$.
> - for a pair of suits (marked set of size $26$): $E[\min(M_i,M_j)]=\frac{53}{27}$.
> - for a triple of suits (marked set of size $39$): $E[\min(M_i,M_j,M_k)]=\frac{53}{40}$.
> - for all four suits (the marked set is the whole deck), the first card is always in position $1$, so $E[\min(M_1,M_2,M_3,M_4)]=1$.
>
> There are $\binom{4}{1}=4$ singles, $\binom{4}{2}=6$ pairs, $\binom{4}{3}=4$ triples, and $\binom{4}{4}=1$ full set, so
>
> $$
> E[X] = 4\cdot\frac{53}{14} - 6\cdot\frac{53}{27} + 4\cdot\frac{53}{40} - 1.
> $$
>
> ## Evaluating the sum
>
> Compute each term:
>
> $$
> 4\cdot\frac{53}{14}=\frac{212}{14}=\frac{106}{7}, \qquad
> 6\cdot\frac{53}{27}=\frac{318}{27}=\frac{106}{9}, \qquad
> 4\cdot\frac{53}{40}=\frac{212}{40}=\frac{53}{10}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{106}{7}-\frac{106}{9}+\frac{53}{10}-1.
> $$
>
> Using the common denominator $630$:
>
> $$
> \frac{106}{7}=\frac{9540}{630}, \qquad
> \frac{106}{9}=\frac{7420}{630}, \qquad
> \frac{53}{10}=\frac{3339}{630}, \qquad
> 1=\frac{630}{630}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{9540-7420+3339-630}{630}=\frac{4829}{630}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[X] = \frac{4829}{630}\approx 7.665}.
> $$
>
> So the expected number of cards that must be drawn to see all four suits is about $7.665$. This is not a clean integer: because the deck is drawn without replacement, the composition of the remaining cards keeps changing as suits get exhausted, so the problem does not reduce to a simple sum of independent geometric waiting times, and the exact answer comes out as this slightly awkward fraction rather than a round number.
