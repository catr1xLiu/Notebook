> [!example] Question 213 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

You play a game where you roll a 100-sided die. You can either accept the value of the roll as your payout in dollars, or pay \$1 to reroll the die. What is the optimal strategy for playing this game, and what is the fair value of the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=214)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V$ denote the fair value of the game before any roll is made.
>
> After a roll showing $x \in \{1,2,\dots,100\}$, there are two choices:
>
> - accept $x$, yielding payoff $x$;
> - reroll, which costs $1$ and returns the game to its original state, so its value is $V-1$.
>
> Thus, after seeing $x$, the optimal action is to choose the larger of $x$ and $V-1$. Therefore the optimal rule is:
>
> - **accept** if $x \ge V-1$,
> - **reroll** if $x < V-1$.
>
> So the strategy is a threshold rule.
>
> ## Step 1: Determine the threshold
>
> Let
> $$
> c = V-1.
> $$
> Then the value satisfies
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\sum_{x=1}^{100} \max(x,c).
> $$
>
> Since $c$ will lie between two integers, the decision threshold is integer-based. Suppose the rule is: reroll on $1,2,\dots,k$ and keep $k+1,\dots,100$. Then
> $$
> c \in [k,k+1),
> $$
> and
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\left(k(V-1) + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x\right).
> $$
>
> We need $k$ and $V$ to be mutually consistent: the value $V$ computed from threshold $k$ must itself place $V-1$ back in $[k,k+1)$.
>
> Try $k=86$, so we reroll on $1,\dots,86$ and accept $87,\dots,100$. Then
> $$
> \sum_{x=87}^{100} x
> = \frac{14(87+100)}{2}
> = 1309.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\bigl(86(V-1)+1309\bigr).
> $$
> So
> $$
> 100V = 86V - 86 + 1309,
> $$
> $$
> 14V = 1223,
> $$
> $$
> V = \frac{1223}{14} = 87\frac{5}{14}\approx 87.357.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> V-1 = \frac{1209}{14} = 86\frac{5}{14}\approx 86.357.
> $$
> This lies between $86$ and $87$, exactly as the threshold $k=86$ requires: accepting is optimal precisely when
> $$
> x \ge V-1 = 86\tfrac{5}{14},
> $$
> that is, for integers $x \ge 87$ — which means rerolling on $1$ through $86$, matching the assumed threshold. The solution is self-consistent.
>
> ## Step 2: Fair value
>
> The fair value of the game is therefore
> $$
> V = \frac{1223}{14} \approx 87.357.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal strategy is to **reroll any result from $1$ through $86$**, paying $1$ each time, and **keep any result from $87$ through $100$**.
>
> The **fair value of the game** is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1223}{14} = 87\frac{5}{14}\approx 87.357}.
> $$
