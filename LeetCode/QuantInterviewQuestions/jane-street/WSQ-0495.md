> [!example] Question 495 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

There are an unknown number of people on a bus. At the first stop, three-quarters of the passengers get off and 7 people get on. This process repeats for two more stops. After this sequence, what is the minimum possible number of people that could be on the bus?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=496)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of people on the bus be $x$.
>
> At each stop, three-quarters of the people get off, so one-quarter remain, and then $7$ more get on. Thus the number of people after one stop is
>
> $$
> \frac{x}{4} + 7.
> $$
>
> This same process happens for a total of three stops.
>
> After the second stop, the number of people is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{4} + 7\right) + 7
> = \frac{x}{16} + \frac{7}{4} + 7
> = \frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}.
> $$
>
> After the third stop, the number of people is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}\right) + 7
> = \frac{x}{64} + \frac{35}{16} + 7
> = \frac{x}{64} + \frac{147}{16}.
> $$
>
> Since the number of people must be a whole number after each stop, work backward.
>
> Let the number of people after the third stop be $n_3$.
> Then before the third stop, the number must have been
>
> $$
> n_2 = 4(n_3 - 7).
> $$
>
> Similarly, before the second stop,
>
> $$
> n_1 = 4(n_2 - 7),
> $$
>
> and before the first stop,
>
> $$
> x = 4(n_1 - 7).
> $$
>
> To minimize $x$, choose the smallest possible positive integer for $n_3$ such that all earlier values are nonnegative integers.
>
> Take $n_3 = 7$. Then
>
> $$
> n_2 = 4(7 - 7) = 0,
> $$
>
> $$
> n_1 = 4(0 - 7) = -28,
> $$
>
> which is impossible.
>
> Take $n_3 = 8$. Then
>
> $$
> n_2 = 4(8 - 7) = 4,
> $$
>
> $$
> n_1 = 4(4 - 7) = -12,
> $$
>
> impossible.
>
> Take $n_3 = 9$. Then
>
> $$
> n_2 = 4(9 - 7) = 8,
> $$
>
> $$
> n_1 = 4(8 - 7) = 4,
> $$
>
> $$
> x = 4(4 - 7) = -12,
> $$
>
> impossible.
>
> Continue until all values are nonnegative:
>
> Take $n_3 = 10$:
>
> $$
> n_2 = 12,\quad n_1 = 20,\quad x = 52.
> $$
>
> Check:
>
> - Start: $52$
> - After first stop: $\frac{52}{4} + 7 = 13 + 7 = 20$
> - After second stop: $\frac{20}{4} + 7 = 5 + 7 = 12$
> - After third stop: $\frac{12}{4} + 7 = 3 + 7 = 10$
>
> All are whole numbers, so this works.
>
> Therefore, the minimum possible number of people initially on the bus is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52}.
> $$
