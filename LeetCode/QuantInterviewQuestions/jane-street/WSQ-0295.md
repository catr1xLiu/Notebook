> [!example] Question 295 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

Devise a strategy for playing a game where a fair 100-sided die is rolled, and you are paid the value shown on the die in pounds. Each roll of the die costs £1.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=296)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Each roll yields a random payoff equal to the die face, uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,100\}$, and costs £$1$.
>
> Let $X$ be the payoff from one roll. Then
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X=k)=\frac{1}{100}, \qquad k=1,2,\dots,100.
> $$
>
> The expected payoff from one roll is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X]=\frac{1+2+\cdots+100}{100}
> =\frac{\frac{100\cdot 101}{2}}{100}
> =\frac{101}{2}
> =50.5.
> $$
>
> Since each roll costs £$1$, the expected **net** gain from one roll is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X-1]=\mathbb{E}[X]-1=50.5-1=49.5.
> $$
>
> Thus each roll gives an average profit of £$49.50$.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> Because the expected net gain per roll is positive, and indeed very large, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - **Play whenever allowed.**
> - **Roll as many times as possible.**
>
> If you are allowed $n$ rolls, the expected total profit is
>
> $$
> n \cdot 49.5.
> $$
>
> So there is no reason to decline a roll: every additional roll increases expected winnings by £$49.50$.
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The best strategy is to **keep rolling as often as possible**, since the expected value of a roll is £$50.50$ while the cost is only £$1$, giving an expected profit of
>
> $$
> £49.50 \text{ per roll}.
> $$
