> [!example] Question 70 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the probability of getting a square number as the sum when you roll two 8-sided dice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=71)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each $8$-sided die has outcomes $1$ through $8$, so rolling two dice produces
>
> $$
> 8 \cdot 8 = 64
> $$
>
> equally likely ordered outcomes.
>
> Interpret “getting a square number” as the **sum** of the two dice being a perfect square. The possible sums range from $2$ to $16$, and the square numbers in this range are
>
> $$
> 4,\; 9,\; 16.
> $$
>
> Count the outcomes giving each sum:
>
> - Sum $4$:
>   $$
>   (1,3),\,(2,2),\,(3,1)
>   $$
>   so there are $3$ outcomes.
>
> - Sum $9$:
>   $$
>   (1,8),(2,7),(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3),(7,2),(8,1)
>   $$
>   so there are $8$ outcomes.
>
> - Sum $16$:
>   $$
>   (8,8)
>   $$
>   so there is $1$ outcome.
>
> Thus the total number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$
> 3+8+1=12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{12}{64}=\frac{3}{16}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{16}}.
> $$
