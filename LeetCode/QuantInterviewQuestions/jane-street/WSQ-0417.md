> [!example] Question 417 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You have two packs of poker cards: one with 52 cards, the other with 104 cards. You will pick two cards separately from the same pack. If both cards are red, you win. Which pack should you choose to maximize your chances of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=418)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Choose the pack that gives the larger probability that two cards drawn without replacement are both red.
>
> There are $26$ red cards in a standard $52$-card pack, and $52$ red cards in a $104$-card pack made of two such packs.
>
> ## Pack with $52$ cards
>
> The probability that the first card is red is
>
> $$
> \frac{26}{52}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Given that the first card is red, the probability that the second card is also red is
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P_{52}=\frac{26}{52}\cdot\frac{25}{51}=\frac{25}{102}.
> $$
>
> ## Pack with $104$ cards
>
> The probability that the first card is red is
>
> $$
> \frac{52}{104}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Given that the first card is red, the probability that the second card is also red is
>
> $$
> \frac{51}{103}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P_{104}=\frac{52}{104}\cdot\frac{51}{103}=\frac{51}{206}.
> $$
>
> ## Comparison
>
> Compare
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{102} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{51}{206}.
> $$
>
> Using decimal values,
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{102}\approx 0.2451, \qquad \frac{51}{206}\approx 0.2476.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> \frac{51}{206}>\frac{25}{102}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> You should choose the $104$-card pack, because it gives a slightly higher probability that both cards are red.
