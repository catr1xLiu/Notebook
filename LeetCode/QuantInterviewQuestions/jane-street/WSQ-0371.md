> [!example] Question 371 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If you have an asset that increases or decreases by 20% each day with probability 1/2, what is the probability that after n days, the asset's price is unchanged?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=372)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial asset price be $S_0$. Each day, the price is multiplied by either
>
> - $1.2$ with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, or
> - $0.8$ with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
>
> After $n$ days, suppose the asset has gone up on $k$ days and down on $n-k$ days. Then its price is
>
> $$
> S_n = S_0 (1.2)^k (0.8)^{n-k}.
> $$
>
> We want the price to be unchanged, so we require
>
> $$
> S_n = S_0.
> $$
>
> Dividing by $S_0$ gives
>
> $$
> (1.2)^k (0.8)^{n-k} = 1.
> $$
>
> Rewrite the factors as fractions:
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^k \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{n-k} = 1.
> $$
>
> Multiply through:
>
> $$
> \frac{6^k 4^{\,n-k}}{5^n} = 1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> 6^k 4^{\,n-k} = 5^n.
> $$
>
> Now the left-hand side has only prime factors $2$ and $3$, while the right-hand side has only prime factor $5$. This is possible only if both sides equal $1$, which occurs only when
>
> $$
> n = 0.
> $$
>
> Hence, for any positive integer $n$,
>
> $$
> \Pr(S_n = S_0) = 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that the asset’s price is unchanged after $n$ days is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0 \text{ for all } n \ge 1.}
> $$
>
> (And trivially it is $1$ when $n=0$.)
