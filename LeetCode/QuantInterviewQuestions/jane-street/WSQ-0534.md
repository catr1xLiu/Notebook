> [!example] Question 534 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Given a fair 6-sided die, you roll once and observe the outcome n (where n is the number rolled). What is your expected value (EV) of the roll? If you are allowed one optional reroll (meaning you can choose to accept the first roll or reroll), what is your EV now? If you are allowed to reroll one additional time (meaning you can roll up to two times, always choosing whether to accept the current roll or reroll), what is your EV?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=535)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the die be fair, with outcomes $1,2,3,4,5,6$ each occurring with probability $\tfrac16$.
>
> We interpret the game as follows:
>
> - First, you roll once and receive the amount shown.
> - In the second part, after seeing the first roll, you may either keep it or reroll once, but then you must accept the second roll.
> - In the third part, after seeing the first roll, you may reroll; after seeing the second roll, you may reroll one final time.
>
> ## 1. Expected value with no reroll
>
> The expected value of a single roll is
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Expected value with one optional reroll
>
> Suppose the first roll is $n$.
>
> If you keep it, your payoff is $n$.
>
> If you reroll, your expected payoff is the expected value of a fresh die roll, namely
>
> $$
> 3.5.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the first roll if $n \ge 4$,
> - reroll if $n \le 3$.
>
> So the resulting payoff is:
>
> - $3.5$ when the first roll is $1,2,3$,
> - $4,5,6$ when the first roll is $4,5,6$.
>
> Hence the expected value is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\left(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6\right)
> = \frac{25.5}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Expected value with up to two rerolls total
>
> Now you may reroll after the first roll, and if you do, you may also reroll after the second roll.
>
> Let $V_1$ be the expected value when you have exactly one roll remaining and may choose whether to keep or reroll after seeing the current roll. From part 2,
>
> $$
> V_1 = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Now consider the first roll.
>
> - If you keep the first roll $n$, you get $n$.
> - If you reroll, you enter the “one optional reroll remaining” situation, whose expected value is $4.25$.
>
> So you should:
>
> - keep if $n \ge 5$,
> - reroll if $n \le 4$.
>
> Thus the expected value is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\left(4.25+4.25+4.25+4.25+5+6\right)
> = \frac{28}{6}
> = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5,\quad 4.25,\quad \frac{14}{3}\approx 4.667}
> $$
