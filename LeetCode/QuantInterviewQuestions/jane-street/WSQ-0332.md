> [!example] Question 332 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Given 99 coins (not necessarily fair) and 1 fair coin, what is the probability that the total number of heads flipped is even?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=333)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ denote the probability that the total number of heads from the $99$ arbitrary coins is even. Then the probability that those $99$ coins produce an odd number of heads is $1-p$.
>
> Now flip the fair coin.
>
> - If the first $99$ coins produce an even number of heads, then the total remains even exactly when the fair coin shows tails, which has probability $\tfrac12$.
> - If the first $99$ coins produce an odd number of heads, then the total becomes even exactly when the fair coin shows heads, which also has probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \Pr(\text{total number of heads is even})
> = p\cdot \frac12 + (1-p)\cdot \frac12
> = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability that the total number of heads is even is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
