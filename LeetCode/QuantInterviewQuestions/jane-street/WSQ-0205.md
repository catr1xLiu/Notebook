> [!example] Question 205 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

There is some amount of money in a box, determined as follows: 200 fair coins are flipped. Let the number of heads that come up be H. The amount of money in the box is H\*(100-H)/100. How much would you pay for the box?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=206)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $200$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(200,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> The amount of money in the box is
>
> $$
> X=\frac{H(100-H)}{100}.
> $$
>
> A fair price to pay is the expected value $E[X]$.
>
> ## Compute the expectation
>
> We have
>
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{1}{100}E\!\left[H(100-H)\right]
> =\frac{1}{100}\left(100E[H]-E[H^2]\right).
> $$
>
> For $H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(200,\tfrac12)$,
>
> $$
> E[H]=200\cdot \frac12=100,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(H)=200\cdot \frac12\cdot \frac12=50.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> E[H^2]=\operatorname{Var}(H)+(E[H])^2,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[H^2]=50+100^2=10050.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[X]
> =\frac{1}{100}\left(100\cdot 100-10050\right)
> =\frac{1}{100}(10000-10050)
> =-\frac{50}{100}
> =-0.5.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected amount of money in the box is
>
> $$
> -\$0.50.
> $$
>
> So a fair price would be to demand $\text{\textdollar}0.50$ to take the box; equivalently, one should pay
>
> $$
> -\$0.50.
> $$
