> [!example] Question 547 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

I have one fair coin and one biased two-headed coin, and I put both in my pocket. I randomly choose one coin and flip it. It shows heads. What is the probability that the coin has tails on the other side?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=548)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $F$ denote the event that the chosen coin is the fair coin, and let $H$ denote the event that the flip shows heads.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(F \mid H),
> $$
> because the coin has tails on the other side exactly when the chosen coin is the fair coin.
>
> By Bayes' rule,
> $$
> P(F \mid H)=\frac{P(H \mid F)P(F)}{P(H)}.
> $$
>
> Since the coin is chosen at random,
> $$
> P(F)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> P(H \mid F)=\frac{1}{2},
> \qquad
> P(H \mid \text{two-headed})=1.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(H)=P(H \mid F)P(F)+P(H \mid \text{two-headed})P(\text{two-headed})
> $$
> $$
> =\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}+1\cdot\frac{1}{2}
> =\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}
> =\frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(F \mid H)=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{3}{4}}
> =\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{3}{4}}
> =\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that the coin has tails on the other side is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}.
> $$
