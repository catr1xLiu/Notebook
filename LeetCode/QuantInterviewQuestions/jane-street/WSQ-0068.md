> [!example] Question 68 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the first date in the future, written in the format YYYY-MM-DD, in which all digits are distinct?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=69)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Setting up the problem
>
> We seek the first date after today, $2026$-$08$-$05$, written in the ISO form $\text{YYYY-MM-DD}$, whose $8$ digits are all different from one another.
>
> ## Step 1: No date in the 2000s works
>
> Every year from $2000$ through $2099$ begins with the two digits $2,0$. So both $2$ and $0$ are already used by the year alone.
>
> A valid two-digit month is always $01$ through $12$, so its leading digit must be $0$ or $1$. But $0$ is already used by the year, so the month's leading digit would have to be $1$, forcing the month to be $10$, $11$, or $12$. Checking each of these:
>
> - month $10$ reuses the digit $0$, already taken by the year;
> - month $11$ repeats its own digit $1$;
> - month $12$ reuses the digit $2$, already taken by the year.
>
> So no month at all can be formed once the year has the form $20YZ$. Hence no date from $2026$ through $2099$ can have all $8$ digits distinct.
>
> ## Step 2: Later years remain tightly constrained
>
> For a year beyond $2099$, a similar constraint persists in a weaker form. The four year digits must themselves be mutually distinct, and, of the six digits left over, enough of the "small" digits $0,1,2,3$ must remain unused for a valid month (needing a leading $0$ or $1$) and a valid day (needing a leading digit of at most $3$, since every calendar day is at most $31$) to both be built from what remains. This is a demanding requirement: even when a year's own four digits are distinct, the year frequently consumes so many of $0,1,2,3$ that no valid month can be formed, or no valid day can be completed within an otherwise valid month.
>
> Checking years systematically on this basis, working upward from $2026$, shows that every year through $2344$ fails for one of these reasons.
>
> ## Step 3: The first year that works
>
> The first year for which a valid month and day can be completed is $2345$, whose digits $2,3,4,5$ are mutually distinct and, notably, do not include $0$ or $1$.
>
> The digits left over are $0,1,6,7,8,9$. Since $2,3,4,5$ are already used, the months $02,03,04,05$ are all blocked. The month $01$ is also unusable: it leaves only $6,7,8,9$ available, and none of these can serve as the leading digit of a valid day (at most $31$). The next candidate, month $06$, does work: it uses $0$ and $6$, leaving $1,7,8,9$ available, and among these the smallest valid day is $17$, using digits $1$ and $7$ — a valid day, since June has $30$ days.
>
> This gives the date
>
> $$
> 2345-06-17,
> $$
>
> whose $8$ digits, $2,3,4,5,0,6,1,7$, are all distinct.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The first future date, written as $\text{YYYY-MM-DD}$, with all $8$ digits distinct is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2345-06-17}.
> $$
