> [!example] Question 148 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Three fair coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting at least two heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=149)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability of getting **at least two heads** when tossing $3$ fair coins, list the possible outcomes.
>
> Each coin has $2$ possible outcomes, so the total number of equally likely outcomes is
>
> $$
> 2^3 = 8.
> $$
>
> The sample space is
>
> $$
> \{HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT\}.
> $$
>
> “At least two heads” means getting either exactly $2$ heads or exactly $3$ heads.
>
> The favorable outcomes are
>
> $$
> \{HHT, HTH, THH, HHH\},
> $$
>
> so there are $4$ favorable outcomes.
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of getting at least two heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
