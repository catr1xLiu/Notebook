> [!example] Question 233 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

You are given two identical eggs and a 100-story building. Your task is to determine the highest floor from which you can drop an egg without it breaking.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=234)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> With two identical eggs, the goal is to determine the highest safe floor in the **worst case** using as few drops as possible.
>
> Let the building have floors $1$ through $100$, and let $F$ be the highest floor from which an egg does **not** break.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> If one egg breaks, only one egg remains, so the only safe method afterward is to test floors **one by one** upward from the last known safe floor.
>
> Thus, the first egg should be dropped at carefully chosen increasing floors so that, no matter where it breaks, the total number of drops remains as small as possible.
>
> Suppose the first egg is dropped first from floor $x$.
>
> - If it breaks there, we must test floors $1$ through $x-1$ linearly, requiring at most $x-1$ more drops.
> - Total worst-case drops: $x$.
>
> If it does not break, we should go up by a smaller amount next, so that the remaining number of drops still matches the worst case. Hence we use decreasing step sizes:
>
> - first drop: $x$
> - second drop: $x + (x-1)$
> - third drop: $x + (x-1) + (x-2)$
> - and so on.
>
> This ensures that after each safe drop, the number of remaining floors to check linearly if the egg breaks matches the number of drops left.
>
> So we need the smallest integer $x$ such that
>
> $$
> x + (x-1) + (x-2) + \cdots + 1 \ge 100.
> $$
>
> That sum is
>
> $$
> \frac{x(x+1)}{2} \ge 100.
> $$
>
> Solve:
>
> $$
> x^2 + x - 200 \ge 0.
> $$
>
> The positive root is
>
> $$
> x = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{801}}{2} \approx 13.66.
> $$
>
> So the smallest integer is
>
> $$
> x = 14.
> $$
>
> ## Optimal dropping floors
>
> Use the first egg at floors:
>
> $$
> 14,\ 27,\ 39,\ 50,\ 60,\ 69,\ 77,\ 84,\ 90,\ 95,\ 99,\ 100
> $$
>
> since the increments are
>
> $$
> 14,\ 13,\ 12,\ 11,\ 10,\ 9,\ 8,\ 7,\ 6,\ 5,\ 4,\ 1.
> $$
>
> If the first egg breaks at any stage, use the second egg to test floors one by one from the previous safe floor upward.
>
> ## Why this is optimal
>
> Any strategy with worst-case $n$ drops can cover at most
>
> $$
> 1 + 2 + \cdots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
> $$
>
> floors with two eggs. Therefore we need
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \ge 100.
> $$
>
> The smallest such $n$ is $14$, so no strategy can guarantee success in fewer than $14$ drops.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The minimum number of drops needed in the worst case is
>
> $$
> 14.
> $$
>
> An optimal strategy is to drop the first egg from floors
>
> $$
> 14,\ 27,\ 39,\ 50,\ 60,\ 69,\ 77,\ 84,\ 90,\ 95,\ 99,\ 100,
> $$
>
> and when it breaks, use the second egg to test the intervening floors one by one.
