> [!example] Question 39 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the expected length of the longest segment when a unit-length stick is broken at two random points?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=40)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two break points be chosen independently and uniformly on the unit interval. After ordering them as
> $$
> 0 \le U \le V \le 1,
> $$
> the three segment lengths are
> $$
> X = U,\qquad Y = V-U,\qquad Z = 1-V.
> $$
> We seek
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M], \qquad M=\max\{X,Y,Z\}.
> $$
>
> ## 1. Joint distribution of the segment lengths
>
> The vector $(X,Y,Z)$ satisfies
> $$
> X>0,\quad Y>0,\quad Z>0,\quad X+Y+Z=1.
> $$
> Since the ordered break points $(U,V)$ are uniformly distributed on the triangle $0<u<v<1$ with density $2$, the pair $(X,Y)$ is uniformly distributed on
> $$
> \{(x,y): x>0,\ y>0,\ x+y<1\}
> $$
> with density $2$. Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> 2\iint_{x>0,\ y>0,\ x+y<1} \max\{x,y,1-x-y\}\,dx\,dy.
> $$
>
> A simpler route is to compute the tail probability of $M$.
>
> ## 2. Distribution of the maximum segment length
>
> For $m<\tfrac13$, it is impossible that all three segments are at most $m$, since
> $$
> X+Y+Z=1>3m.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M\le m)=0,\qquad 0\le m<\frac13.
> $$
>
> For $m\in\left[\tfrac13,\tfrac12\right]$, the event $M\le m$ means
> $$
> X\le m,\quad Y\le m,\quad Z\le m.
> $$
> Since $Z=1-X-Y$, this becomes
> $$
> X\le m,\quad Y\le m,\quad X+Y\ge 1-m,
> $$
> together with $x>0,\ y>0,\ x+y<1$.
>
> Inside the basic triangle $x>0,\ y>0,\ x+y<1$, these inequalities define a smaller equilateral-type triangle with vertices
> $$
> (1-2m,m),\quad (m,1-2m),\quad (m,m).
> $$
> Its area is
> $$
> \frac12(3m-1)^2.
> $$
> Multiplying by the density $2$ gives
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M\le m)=(3m-1)^2,\qquad \frac13\le m\le \frac12.
> $$
>
> For $m\ge \tfrac12$, it is easier to compute the complement. If $M>m$, then at least one segment exceeds $m$. Since $m>\tfrac12$, at most one segment can exceed $m$, so these events are disjoint:
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M>m)=\mathbb{P}(X>m)+\mathbb{P}(Y>m)+\mathbb{P}(Z>m).
> $$
> By symmetry, each has the same probability. Now
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(X>m)=\mathbb{P}(U>m)=(1-m)^2,
> $$
> because both unordered break points must lie to the right of $m$. Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M>m)=3(1-m)^2,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M\le m)=1-3(1-m)^2,\qquad \frac12\le m\le 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M>m)=
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & 0\le m<\frac13,\\[4pt]
> 1-(3m-1)^2, & \frac13\le m\le \frac12,\\[4pt]
> 3(1-m)^2, & \frac12\le m\le 1.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ## 3. Compute the expectation
>
> Using
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]=\int_0^1 \mathbb{P}(M>m)\,dm,
> $$
> we obtain
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \int_0^{1/3} 1\,dm
> +\int_{1/3}^{1/2}\bigl(1-(3m-1)^2\bigr)\,dm
> +\int_{1/2}^{1}3(1-m)^2\,dm.
> $$
>
> Evaluate each term:
>
> $$
> \int_0^{1/3}1\,dm=\frac13.
> $$
>
> For the second integral,
> $$
> 1-(3m-1)^2=1-(9m^2-6m+1)=6m-9m^2,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \int_{1/3}^{1/2}(6m-9m^2)\,dm
> =
> \left[3m^2-3m^3\right]_{1/3}^{1/2}
> =
> \frac38-\frac29
> =
> \frac{11}{72}.
> $$
>
> For the third integral,
> $$
> \int_{1/2}^{1}3(1-m)^2\,dm
> =
> 3\int_0^{1/2} t^2\,dt
> =
> 3\cdot \frac{(1/2)^3}{3}
> =
> \frac18.
> $$
>
> Adding,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \frac13+\frac{11}{72}+\frac18
> =
> \frac{24+11+9}{72}
> =
> \frac{44}{72}
> =
> \frac{11}{18}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected length of the longest segment is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{18}}.
> $$
