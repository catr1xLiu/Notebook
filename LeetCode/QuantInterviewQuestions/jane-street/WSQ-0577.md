> [!example] Question 577 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You play a game where you roll a 100-sided die labeled with the numbers 1 to 100. After each roll, you may either accept the payout equal to the number rolled in dollars or pay \$1 to roll again. You can continue to pay \$1 to reroll as many times as you like until you choose to accept your current roll. What is the optimal strategy for this game, and what are your expected winnings using this strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=578)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V$ denote the optimal expected net payoff from the game at the start of a decision point, that is, immediately after seeing a roll and before deciding whether to stop or pay $1$ to reroll.
>
> ## 1. Set up the optimality equation
>
> If the current roll is $x$, then there are two choices:
>
> - **Stop** and receive $x$.
> - **Continue** by paying $1$, after which the game restarts in the same strategic situation, with expected value $V$.
>
> Thus, if you continue, your expected value is
>
> $$
> -1 + V.
> $$
>
> So for a current roll $x$, the optimal value is
>
> $$
> \max\{x,\; V-1\}.
> $$
>
> Because each roll is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,100\}$, the value function satisfies
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{x=1}^{100}\max\{x,\; V-1\}.
> $$
>
> This implies that there is a threshold rule: stop when $x$ is at least some cutoff, and reroll otherwise.
>
> ## 2. Determine the threshold
>
> Let
>
> $$
> T=V-1.
> $$
>
> Then the rule is:
>
> - stop if $x\ge T$,
> - reroll if $x<T$.
>
> Since $x$ is integer-valued, the actual cutoff will be an integer threshold.
>
> Suppose the threshold is $k$: reroll on $1,2,\dots,k-1$ and stop on $k,k+1,\dots,100$.
>
> Then the expected value from a fresh roll is
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{100}\left( \sum_{x=1}^{k-1}(V-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{100}x \right).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 100V=(k-1)(V-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{100}x.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=k}^{100}x=\frac{100\cdot 101}{2}-\frac{(k-1)k}{2}=5050-\frac{(k-1)k}{2},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> 100V=(k-1)(V-1)+5050-\frac{(k-1)k}{2}.
> $$
>
> Rearrange:
>
> $$
> (101-k)V=5050-(k-1)-\frac{(k-1)k}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V=\frac{5050-(k-1)-\frac{(k-1)k}{2}}{101-k}.
> $$
>
> Now the threshold condition requires that continuing be better than stopping for $x<k$, and stopping be better for $x\ge k$. Since continuing is worth $V-1$, this means
>
> $$
> k-1 < V-1 \le k,
> $$
>
> equivalently,
>
> $$
> k < V \le k+1.
> $$
>
> We test likely values near the mean. For $k=90$,
>
> $$
> V=\frac{5050-89-\frac{89\cdot 90}{2}}{11}
> =\frac{5050-89-4005}{11}
> =\frac{956}{11}
> \approx 86.91,
> $$
>
> which does not satisfy $90<V\le 91$.
>
> For $k=87$,
>
> $$
> V=\frac{5050-86-\frac{86\cdot 87}{2}}{14}
> =\frac{5050-86-3741}{14}
> =\frac{1223}{14}
> \approx 87.36.
> $$
>
> This satisfies
>
> $$
> 87<V\le 88.
> $$
>
> Thus the correct threshold is
>
> $$
> k=87.
> $$
>
> So the optimal strategy is to reroll on $1$ through $86$, and stop on $87$ through $100$.
>
> ## 3. Compute the expected winnings
>
> Under this strategy, the value $V$ is
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1223}{14}\approx 87.36.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected net winnings are
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1223}{14}\text{ dollars} \approx 87.36\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Keep rerolling until you get }87\text{ or higher, then stop.}}
> $$
>
> The expected winnings under this strategy are:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1223}{14}\approx 87.36\text{ dollars}.}
> $$
