> [!example] Question 416 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You want to buy a car at an auction where the car's price is uniformly distributed between \$0 and \$1000. If you bid more than the car's price, you win it at the price you bid; if you bid less than the car's price, you do not win it but also do not lose anything. You can sell the car for 1.5 times the value for which you bought it. What should you bid to maximize your expected profit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=417)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the car’s unknown value at auction be $V$, where
>
> $$
> V \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1000).
> $$
>
> Suppose you submit a bid of $b$, with $0 \le b \le 1000$.
>
> ## Step 1: Profit as a function of the bid and the car value
>
> - If $b \ge V$, you win the car and pay your bid, namely $b$.
> - You can then sell it for $1.5V$.
>
> So if you win, your profit is
>
> $$
> 1.5V - b.
> $$
>
> - If $b < V$, you do not buy the car, so your profit is
>
> $$
> 0.
> $$
>
> Thus the profit function is
>
> $$
> \Pi(b,V)=
> \begin{cases}
> 1.5V-b, & V \le b,\\
> 0, & V>b.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute expected profit
>
> Because $V$ is uniform on $[0,1000]$, its density is
>
> $$
> f(v)=\frac{1}{1000}, \qquad 0 \le v \le 1000.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected profit from bidding $b$ is
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \int_0^b (1.5v - b)\frac{1}{1000}\,dv.
> $$
>
> Evaluate the integral:
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)]
> = \frac{1}{1000}\int_0^b (1.5v-b)\,dv
> = \frac{1}{1000}\left[0.75v^2-bv\right]_0^b.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \frac{1}{1000}\left(0.75b^2-b^2\right)
> = \frac{1}{1000}\left(-0.25b^2\right)
> = -\frac{b^2}{4000}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Maximize expected profit
>
> Since
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = -\frac{b^2}{4000},
> $$
>
> this is largest when $b=0$.
>
> At $b=0$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(0)] = 0.
> $$
>
> For every $b>0$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)]<0.
> $$
>
> So any positive bid gives a negative expected profit.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The bid that maximizes expected profit is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> In other words, under these rules, you should **not bid at all**.
>
> The intuition is that when you win, it is because the car’s value $V$ is at most your bid $b$, so conditional on winning, the average value is only
>
> $$
> E[V\mid V\le b]=\frac{b}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus your average resale revenue when you win is
>
> $$
> 1.5\cdot \frac{b}{2}=0.75b,
> $$
>
> while you pay $b$, giving an average loss of
>
> $$
> 0.75b-b=-0.25b
> $$
>
> whenever you win.
