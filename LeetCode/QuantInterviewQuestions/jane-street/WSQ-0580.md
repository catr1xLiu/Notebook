> [!example] Question 580 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A tosses n+1 fair coins. B tosses n fair coins. B wins if he has at least as many heads as A. What is the probability that B wins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=581)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ toss $n+1$ fair coins and $B$ toss $n$ fair coins. We seek the probability that $B$ gets at least as many heads as $A$.
>
> ## Step 1: Compare the first $n$ coins of $A$ with the $n$ coins of $B$
>
> Let $A'$ denote the number of heads in the first $n$ coins tossed by $A$, and let $X$ denote the result of $A$'s extra coin, where
>
> - $X=1$ if the extra coin is a head,
> - $X=0$ if the extra coin is a tail.
>
> Then the total number of heads for $A$ is
>
> $$
> A = A' + X.
> $$
>
> Let $B$ also denote the number of heads obtained by player $B$ in his $n$ coins.
>
> Since $A'$ and $B$ each count heads in $n$ fair coin tosses, they have the same distribution, and the pair $(A',B)$ is symmetric.
>
> ## Step 2: Express the winning condition
>
> Player $B$ wins when
>
> $$
> B \ge A = A' + X.
> $$
>
> This depends on the extra coin $X$.
>
> ### Case 1: $X=0$
>
> Then $A=A'$, so $B$ wins if
>
> $$
> B \ge A'.
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: $X=1$
>
> Then $A=A'+1$, so $B$ wins if
>
> $$
> B \ge A'+1,
> $$
>
> that is,
>
> $$
> B > A'.
> $$
>
> Since $X$ is independent of $A'$ and $B$, and $\Pr(X=0)=\Pr(X=1)=\frac12$, we get
>
> $$
> \Pr(B \text{ wins})
> = \frac12 \Pr(B \ge A') + \frac12 \Pr(B > A').
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Use symmetry
>
> Because $A'$ and $B$ have identical distributions,
>
> $$
> \Pr(B > A') = \Pr(A' > B).
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> \Pr(B \ge A') = \Pr(B > A') + \Pr(B = A').
> $$
>
> Now
>
> $$
> \Pr(B > A') + \Pr(B = A') + \Pr(A' > B) = 1.
> $$
>
> By symmetry, $\Pr(B > A')=\Pr(A' > B)$, so
>
> $$
> 2\Pr(B > A') + \Pr(B = A') = 1.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \Pr(B \ge A') + \Pr(B > A')
> = \bigl(\Pr(B > A') + \Pr(B = A')\bigr) + \Pr(B > A')
> = 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \Pr(B \text{ wins})
> = \frac12 \cdot 1
> = \frac12.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}
> $$
