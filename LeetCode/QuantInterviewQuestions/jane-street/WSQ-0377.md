> [!example] Question 377 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

There are infinitely many cards numbered 1 to 5. You construct a deck by selecting any composition of these cards. A player guesses a value between 1 and 5 and draws a card at random from the deck. If they guessed the correct number, they receive a payout equal to that value; otherwise, they get nothing. What composition of the deck minimizes the player's expected payout?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=378)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the deck contain cards with values in $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$.  
> Write $p_i$ for the proportion of cards of value $i$, so that
>
> $$
> p_i \ge 0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^5 p_i = 1.
> $$
>
> A player first chooses a guess $g \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$, then a card is drawn from the deck. If the guess is correct, the player receives the value of the card; otherwise the payoff is $0$.
>
> We seek the deck composition that minimizes the player’s expected payoff, assuming the player chooses the best possible guess.
>
> ## Expected payoff for a fixed guess
>
> If the player guesses $g$, then the only way to win is to draw a card of value $g$, which happens with probability $p_g$. In that case the payoff is $g$. Thus the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E_g = g\,p_g.
> $$
>
> Since the player will choose the guess maximizing this quantity, the deck-maker wishes to minimize
>
> $$
> \max_{1 \le g \le 5} g p_g
> $$
>
> subject to
>
> $$
> p_i \ge 0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^5 p_i = 1.
> $$
>
> ## Minimax principle
>
> To minimize the maximum of the five numbers
>
> $$
> p_1,\; 2p_2,\; 3p_3,\; 4p_4,\; 5p_5,
> $$
>
> they should all be equal at the optimum. Indeed, if one of them were smaller than the maximum, probability mass could be shifted to reduce the maximum further.
>
> So set
>
> $$
> 1\cdot p_1 = 2\cdot p_2 = 3\cdot p_3 = 4\cdot p_4 = 5\cdot p_5 = c
> $$
>
> for some constant $c$. Then
>
> $$
> p_i = \frac{c}{i}.
> $$
>
> Using $\sum_{i=1}^5 p_i = 1$ gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^5 \frac{c}{i} = 1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> c = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^5 \frac{1}{i}}.
> $$
>
> Now
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^5 \frac{1}{i}
> = 1 + \frac12 + \frac13 + \frac14 + \frac15
> = \frac{137}{60}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> c = \frac{60}{137}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> p_i = \frac{60}{137\,i}.
> $$
>
> ## Deck composition
>
> Thus the optimal proportions are
>
> $$
> p_1 = \frac{60}{137}, \qquad
> p_2 = \frac{30}{137}, \qquad
> p_3 = \frac{20}{137}, \qquad
> p_4 = \frac{15}{137}, \qquad
> p_5 = \frac{12}{137}.
> $$
>
> These sum to $1$, and for each guess $g$,
>
> $$
> g p_g = \frac{60}{137}.
> $$
>
> So no matter what value the player guesses, their expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{60}{137}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The deck should contain values in proportions inversely proportional to the card value:
>
> $$
> p_i \propto \frac{1}{i}.
> $$
>
> Concretely, the minimizing composition is
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \left(
> \frac{60}{137},
> \frac{30}{137},
> \frac{20}{137},
> \frac{15}{137},
> \frac{12}{137}
> \right)
> }
> $$
>
> for card values $1,2,3,4,5$ respectively. The resulting minimum possible expected payoff to the guesser is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{60}{137}}.
> $$
