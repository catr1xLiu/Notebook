> [!example] Question 269 · Jane Street
> ## Junior Trader Interview

Two fair 6-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that the product of the top numbers is a perfect square?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=270)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For two fair six-sided dice, there are
>
> $$6 \times 6 = 36$$
>
> equally likely ordered outcomes.
>
> We want the product of the two top numbers to be a perfect square.
>
> ## Step 1: List the possible die values
>
> The possible numbers are
>
> $$1,2,3,4,5,6.$$
>
> Their prime factorizations are:
>
> - $1$
> - $2$
> - $3$
> - $2^2$
> - $5$
> - $2\cdot 3$
>
> A product is a square exactly when every prime appears with an even exponent.
>
> ## Step 2: Check which ordered pairs give a square product
>
> Evaluate by first die value:
>
> - If the first die is $1$, then the second must itself be a square: $1$ or $4$.
>   - $(1,1)$, $(1,4)$
>
> - If the first die is $2$, then we need $2 \times y$ to be a square. This happens only for $y=2$.
>   - $(2,2)$
>
> - If the first die is $3$, then we need $3 \times y$ to be a square. This happens only for $y=3$.
>   - $(3,3)$
>
> - If the first die is $4$, then since $4$ is already a square, the second must be a square: $1$ or $4$.
>   - $(4,1)$, $(4,4)$
>
> - If the first die is $5$, then we need $5 \times y$ to be a square. This happens only for $y=5$.
>   - $(5,5)$
>
> - If the first die is $6$, then we need $6 \times y$ to be a square. This happens only for $y=6$.
>   - $(6,6)$
>
> Thus the favorable ordered outcomes are
>
> $$
> (1,1),(1,4),(2,2),(3,3),(4,1),(4,4),(5,5),(6,6),
> $$
>
> for a total of
>
> $$8$$
>
> favorable outcomes.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{product is a square})=\frac{8}{36}=\frac{2}{9}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{9}}
> $$
