> [!example] Question 13 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Two people each make a bid from 1 to 100. The highest bid is reduced by 10. What should be your strategy to maximize your probability of winning the auction?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=14)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two bids be integers from $1$ to $100$. If the higher bid is larger than the lower bid by at least $10$, then after reducing the higher bid by $10$, it is still at least as large as the lower bid, so the higher bidder wins. If the higher bid exceeds the lower bid by less than $10$, then after the reduction the higher bidder loses. Thus:
>
> - if you bid $x$ and your opponent bids $y<x$, then you win iff $x-y\ge 10$, i.e. $y\le x-10$;
> - if your opponent bids $y>x$, then you win iff $y-x\le 9$, i.e. $y\in\{x+1,\dots,x+9\}$;
> - if $y=x$, the result is a tie.
>
> To maximize the probability of winning, assume the opponent’s bid is equally likely to be any integer from $1$ to $100$.
>
> ## Number of opponent bids you beat
>
> Fix your bid $x$.
>
> You beat:
>
> 1. all bids $y\le x-10$, giving
>    $$
>    x-10
>    $$
>    winning cases, provided $x\ge 10$;
>
> 2. all bids $y=x+1,\dots,\min(x+9,100)$, giving
>    $$
>    \min(9,\,100-x)
>    $$
>    winning cases.
>
> So the total number of winning opponent bids is
> $$
> W(x)=\max(x-10,0)+\min(9,100-x).
> $$
>
> We now simplify by ranges.
>
> ### Case 1: $1\le x\le 9$
>
> Then
> $$
> W(x)=0+9=9.
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: $10\le x\le 91$
>
> Then
> $$
> W(x)=(x-10)+9=x-1.
> $$
>
> ### Case 3: $92\le x\le 100$
>
> Then
> $$
> W(x)=(x-10)+(100-x)=90.
> $$
>
> ## Maximization
>
> From the above:
>
> - for $1\le x\le 9$, $W(x)=9$;
> - for $10\le x\le 91$, $W(x)=x-1$, which increases with $x$;
> - for $92\le x\le 100$, $W(x)=90$.
>
> Hence the maximum number of winning opponent bids is
> $$
> 90,
> $$
> achieved by every bid
> $$
> x\in\{91,92,\dots,100\}.
> $$
>
> Since there are $100$ possible opponent bids, this gives winning probability
> $$
> \frac{90}{100}=0.9
> $$
> (if ties are counted as non-wins; at $x=91$ there is one tie when the opponent also bids $91$, and similarly for other such bids).
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Any bid from
> $$
> \boxed{91 \text{ to } 100}
> $$
> is optimal. Each of these bids maximizes your probability of winning, giving a win probability of
> $$
> \boxed{90\%}.
> $$
>
> If one wants a single recommendation, a natural choice is
> $$
> \boxed{91},
> $$
> but every bid in $\boxed{91,\dots,100}$ is equally optimal.
