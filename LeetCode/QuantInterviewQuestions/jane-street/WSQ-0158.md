> [!example] Question 158 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

I'm thinking of an integer with no 0s or 1s. When you multiply all the digits of the number together, you get 10,000. What is the largest number I could be thinking of? What is the smallest such number?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=159)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Factor the target product:
>
> $$10{,}000 = 10^4 = 2^4 \cdot 5^4.$$
>
> The digits of the number cannot be $0$ or $1$, so each digit must be one of
>
> $$2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9.$$
>
> Since the product of the digits is $2^4\cdot 5^4$, no digit may introduce any prime factor other than $2$ or $5$. Thus the only possible digits are
>
> $$2,\;4,\;5,\;8,$$
>
> because:
>
> - $2 = 2$
> - $4 = 2^2$
> - $5 = 5$
> - $8 = 2^3$
>
> To make the product $2^4\cdot 5^4$, we must supply exactly four factors of $5$. Since the only allowed digit containing a factor of $5$ is $5$ itself, the number must contain exactly four $5$'s.
>
> So the remaining digits must multiply to $2^4=16$. Using digits from $\{2,4,8\}$, the possible ways are:
>
> - $8\cdot 2 = 16$
> - $4\cdot 4 = 16$
> - $4\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 16$
> - $2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 16$
>
> Hence the possible multisets of digits are:
>
> - $5,5,5,5,8,2$
> - $5,5,5,5,4,4$
> - $5,5,5,5,4,2,2$
> - $5,5,5,5,2,2,2,2$
>
> To get the **largest** number, we want as many digits as possible, since a number with more digits is larger than any number with fewer digits when leading digits are nonzero. The maximum number of digits occurs with
>
> $$5,5,5,5,2,2,2,2.$$
>
> Arranging in descending order gives the largest such number:
>
> $$55552222.$$
>
> To get the **smallest** number, we want as few digits as possible. The minimum number of digits occurs with
>
> $$5,5,5,5,8,2.$$
>
> Arranging in ascending order gives the smallest such number:
>
> $$255558.$$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$\boxed{\text{Largest }= 55552222,\qquad \text{Smallest }= 255558.}$$
