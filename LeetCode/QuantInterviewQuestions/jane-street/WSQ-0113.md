> [!example] Question 113 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected value when throwing a fair six-sided die once?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=114)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ denote the outcome of a fair six-sided die. Then $X$ takes the values $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each with probability $\frac{1}{6}$.
>
> The expected value is
>
> $$
> E[X] = \sum_{x=1}^{6} x \, P(X=x).
> $$
>
> Since $P(X=x)=\frac{1}{6}$ for each $x$,
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1}{6}(1+2+3+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute the sum:
>
> $$
> 1+2+3+4+5+6 = 21.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected value of a fair die throw is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{7}{2}}.
> $$
