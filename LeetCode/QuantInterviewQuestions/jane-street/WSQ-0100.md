> [!example] Question 100 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

We play a game with a 100-sided die. Each roll costs one dollar, and you can choose to stop at any time and accept the dollar amount of your most recent roll. What is the optimal stopping time to maximize your expected profit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=101)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V$ denote the optimal expected net payoff at the start of a turn, before paying to roll. Because the game resets after any decision to continue, the problem is stationary, so the same value $V$ applies at every stage.
>
> ## Step 1: Optimality equation
>
> A roll costs $1$, and then produces a value $X$ uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,100\}$. After observing $X=x$, there are two choices:
>
> - **Stop**, yielding net payoff $x$.
> - **Continue**, in which case the current roll is discarded and the game restarts from the initial position, worth $V$.
>
> Thus after seeing $x$, the optimal action is to take
> $$
> \max\{x,\,V\}.
> $$
> Since one must pay $1$ to obtain the roll, the value $V$ satisfies
> $$
> V=-1+\mathbb{E}\big[\max\{X,V\}\big].
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Threshold form of the optimal rule
>
> Because $\max\{x,V\}$ compares $x$ with a constant, the optimal rule is a threshold rule:
>
> - stop if $x \ge V$,
> - continue if $x<V$.
>
> Since $X$ is integer-valued, this means there is some integer threshold $k$ such that one stops on rolls $k,k+1,\dots,100$ and continues on smaller rolls.
>
> To determine the threshold, solve the value equation.
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the value equation
>
> Suppose $V$ lies between two consecutive integers. Anticipating a threshold near the top, let us test $V\in[87,88]$. Then
> $$
> \max\{x,V\}=
> \begin{cases}
> V,&x=1,\dots,87,\\
> x,&x=88,\dots,100.
> \end{cases}
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\max\{X,V\}]
> =
> \frac{1}{100}\left(87V+\sum_{x=88}^{100}x\right).
> $$
> Now
> $$
> \sum_{x=88}^{100}x
> =
> \frac{(88+100)\cdot 13}{2}
> =
> 1222.
> $$
> So the equation becomes
> $$
> V=-1+\frac{87V+1222}{100}.
> $$
> Multiply by $100$:
> $$
> 100V=-100+87V+1222,
> $$
> so
> $$
> 13V=1122,
> \qquad
> V=\frac{1122}{13}\approx 86.3077.
> $$
> This is not in the interval $[87,88]$, so that guess is inconsistent.
>
> Now instead suppose $V\in[86,87]$. Then
> $$
> \max\{x,V\}=
> \begin{cases}
> V,&x=1,\dots,86,\\
> x,&x=87,\dots,100.
> \end{cases}
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\max\{X,V\}]
> =
> \frac{1}{100}\left(86V+\sum_{x=87}^{100}x\right).
> $$
> Compute
> $$
> \sum_{x=87}^{100}x
> =
> \frac{(87+100)\cdot 14}{2}
> =
> 1309.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V=-1+\frac{86V+1309}{100}.
> $$
> So
> $$
> 100V=-100+86V+1309,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> 14V=1209,
> \qquad
> V=\frac{1209}{14}\approx 86.3571.
> $$
> This is consistent with $V\in[86,87]$.
>
> Therefore the optimal value is
> $$
> V=\frac{1209}{14}\approx 86.36.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Determine the stopping rule
>
> After observing a roll $x$, stop iff
> $$
> x\ge V\approx 86.36.
> $$
> Since $x$ is an integer, this is equivalent to
> $$
> x\ge 87.
> $$
>
> Thus the optimal stopping time is:
>
> - **continue** on rolls $1$ through $86$,
> - **stop** on rolls $87$ through $100$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is to **keep rolling until the first time the die shows at least $87$**, then stop and take that amount.
>
> The corresponding optimal expected net payoff is
> $$
> \frac{1209}{14}\approx 86.36 \text{ dollars}.
> $$
