> [!example] Question 125 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

You are playing a game with a 6-sided die. You may roll the die once, observe the result, and choose either to stop (keeping the result) or roll again. Your final payoff is the sum of your rolls, unless this sum exceeds 9, in which case you receive nothing. What is your optimal strategy for this game? Specifically, for each possible outcome of the first roll, should you choose to stop or roll again?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=126)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the first roll be $x \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. After observing $x$, there are two possible actions:
>
> - **Stop**, receiving payoff $x$.
> - **Roll again**, in which case the second roll $Y$ is uniform on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, and the payoff is
>   $$
>   x+Y \quad \text{if } x+Y \le 9,
>   $$
>   and $0$ otherwise.
>
> The optimal strategy is found by comparing the sure payoff $x$ from stopping with the expected payoff from rolling again.
>
> ## Expected payoff from rolling again
>
> For each first-roll outcome $x$, compute
> $$
> E_x=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{y=1}^6 \begin{cases}
> x+y, & x+y \le 9,\\
> 0, & x+y>9.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ### Case $x=1$
> No bust is possible, since $1+y \le 7$ for all $y$.
> $$
> E_1=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{y=1}^6 (1+y)
> =\frac{1}{6}(2+3+4+5+6+7)
> =\frac{27}{6}=4.5.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $1$: roll again.
>
> ### Case $x=2$
> No bust is possible, since $2+y \le 8$.
> $$
> E_2=\frac{1}{6}(3+4+5+6+7+8)
> =\frac{33}{6}=5.5.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $2$: roll again.
>
> ### Case $x=3$
> No bust is possible, since $3+y \le 9$.
> $$
> E_3=\frac{1}{6}(4+5+6+7+8+9)
> =\frac{39}{6}=6.5.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $3$: roll again.
>
> ### Case $x=4$
> Now bust occurs if $Y=6$.
> $$
> E_4=\frac{1}{6}(5+6+7+8+9+0)
> =\frac{35}{6}\approx 5.83.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $4$: roll again.
>
> ### Case $x=5$
> Bust occurs if $Y\ge 5$.
> $$
> E_5=\frac{1}{6}(6+7+8+9+0+0)
> =\frac{30}{6}=5.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $5$: indifferent.
>
> ### Case $x=6$
> Bust occurs if $Y\ge 4$.
> $$
> E_6=\frac{1}{6}(7+8+9+0+0+0)
> =\frac{24}{6}=4.
> $$
> Compare with stopping payoff $6$: stop.
>
> ## Optimal strategy
>
> Thus the optimal decision after the first roll is:
>
> - If the first roll is $1,2,3,4$: **roll again**.
> - If the first roll is $6$: **stop**.
> - If the first roll is $5$: **either action is equally good**.
>
> So one optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Roll again on }1,2,3,4;\ \text{stop on }5,6,}
> $$
>
> with the understanding that at $5$ you are indifferent, since both choices give expected payoff $5$.
