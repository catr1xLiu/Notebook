> [!example] Question 252 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

If you randomly pick a 3-digit number, what is the probability that all three digits are even numbers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=253)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A $3$-digit number ranges from $100$ to $999$, so there are
>
> $$
> 999 - 100 + 1 = 900
> $$
>
> such numbers in total.
>
> To have all $3$ digits even:
>
> - The hundreds digit must be even and nonzero: $2,4,6,8$  
>   $\Rightarrow 4$ choices.
> - The tens digit can be any even digit: $0,2,4,6,8$  
>   $\Rightarrow 5$ choices.
> - The units digit can also be any even digit: $0,2,4,6,8$  
>   $\Rightarrow 5$ choices.
>
> Thus, the number of favorable $3$-digit numbers is
>
> $$
> 4 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 100.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{100}{900} = \frac{1}{9}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{9}}.
> $$
