> [!example] Question 204 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

What is the probability that there will be an even number of heads in n coin flips?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=205)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $P_n$ denote the probability that in $n$ flips of a fair coin, the number of heads is even.
>
> ## Method 1: Binomial expansion
>
> The probability of getting exactly $k$ heads in $n$ flips is
>
> $$
> \Pr(H=k)=\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P_n=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\ k\text{ even}}}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^n
> = \frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{\substack{k=0\\ k\text{ even}}}^{n}\binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Now use the identities
>
> $$
> (1+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}=2^n
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> E=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\ k\text{ even}}}^{n}\binom{n}{k},
> \qquad
> O=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\ k\text{ odd}}}^{n}\binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> E+O=2^n,
> \qquad
> E-O=0.
> $$
>
> So $E=O=2^{n-1}$. Therefore,
>
> $$
> P_n=\frac{E}{2^n}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{2^n}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, for every $n\ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{P_n=\frac12.}
> $$
>
> If $n=0$, there are zero heads, which is even, so the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1.}
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \Pr(\text{even number of heads in } n \text{ fair coin flips})=
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & n=0,\\[4pt]
> \dfrac12, & n\ge 1.
> \end{cases}
> $$
