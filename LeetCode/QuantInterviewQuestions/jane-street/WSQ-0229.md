> [!example] Question 229 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

There are one hundred doors, each with one dollar behind it. You roll a one-hundred-sided die one hundred times. After rolling, you may take the dollar behind the door corresponding to any number that was rolled. What is the expected amount of money you can obtain? Explain why.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=230)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the total number of dollars collected. Since there is $1$ dollar behind each door, $X$ is exactly the number of distinct door numbers that appear in $100$ independent rolls of a fair $100$-sided die.
>
> ## Step 1: Indicator variables
>
> For each door $i \in \{1,2,\dots,100\}$, define the indicator
> $$
> I_i =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if door } i \text{ is rolled at least once},\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> X = \sum_{i=1}^{100} I_i.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{i=1}^{100} \mathbb{E}[I_i].
> $$
>
> So it remains to compute $\mathbb{E}[I_i] = \Pr(I_i=1)$.
>
> ## Step 2: Probability a given door is hit
>
> Fix a door $i$. On any one roll, the probability that $i$ is *not* rolled is
> $$
> \frac{99}{100}.
> $$
>
> Since the $100$ rolls are independent, the probability that $i$ is never rolled in all $100$ rolls is
> $$
> \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that door $i$ is rolled at least once is
> $$
> 1 - \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[I_i] = 1 - \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Sum over all doors
>
> All $100$ doors are symmetric, so
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X]
> = 100\left(1 - \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}\right).
> $$
>
> Numerically,
> $$
> \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100} \approx 0.3660,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X] \approx 100(1 - 0.3660) \approx 63.4.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[X] = 100\left(1 - \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}\right) \approx 63.4}
> $$
>
> The reason is that the total money collected equals the number of distinct doors rolled, and each door is collected with probability $1 - \left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{100}$. By linearity of expectation, summing these probabilities over the $100$ doors gives the expected total.
