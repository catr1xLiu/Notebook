> [!example] Question 525 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

There are three coins: one with heads on both sides, one with tails on both sides, and one with heads on one side and tails on the other. You pick a coin at random and toss it, resulting in heads. What is the probability that the coin you picked has heads on both sides?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=526)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> There are $3$ coins:
>
> - $HH$ : heads on both sides,
> - $TT$ : tails on both sides,
> - $HT$ : one head and one tail.
>
> Assume each coin is equally likely to be chosen.
>
> We are given that after choosing a coin and tossing it, the result is a head. We want
>
> $$
> P(HH \mid \text{head}).
> $$
>
> Use conditional probability:
>
> $$
> P(HH \mid \text{head})=\frac{P(HH \cap \text{head})}{P(\text{head})}.
> $$
>
> Compute the numerator:
>
> - The probability of choosing the $HH$ coin is $\frac{1}{3}$.
> - If $HH$ is chosen, a head occurs with probability $1$.
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(HH \cap \text{head})=\frac{1}{3}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Now compute $P(\text{head})$:
>
> - From $HH$: probability $\frac{1}{3}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{3}$,
> - From $TT$: probability $\frac{1}{3}\cdot 0=0$,
> - From $HT$: probability $\frac{1}{3}\cdot \frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{6}$.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{head})=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(HH \mid \text{head})=\frac{\frac{1}{3}}{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the chance that the chosen coin is the double-headed coin is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
