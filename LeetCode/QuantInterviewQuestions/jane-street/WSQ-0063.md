> [!example] Question 63 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the probability of getting an even number of heads when flipping a fair coin 100 times?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=64)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $100$ fair coin flips. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable with success probability $p=\tfrac12$, the probabilities of an even and an odd number of successes are equal. Since these two events are complementary and exhaustive,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) + P(X \text{ is odd}) = 1,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = P(X \text{ is odd}).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability of getting an even number of heads in $100$ flips is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
