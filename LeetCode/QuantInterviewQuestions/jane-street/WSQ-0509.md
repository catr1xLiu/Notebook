> [!example] Question 509 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You and your opponent play a coin-flipping game. If the first player flips heads, the second player pays him \$30. If the first player flips tails, the coin passes to the second player, who then flips. If the second player flips heads, he wins \$30 from the first player. This process continues. How much should you pay to go first?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=510)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> The coin is passed back and forth, and a tails simply hands the coin to the other player: it is only a head that ends the game, immediately deciding a winner. So the game amounts to flipping a fair coin repeatedly, alternating between the two players starting with the first player, until the first head appears; whoever produces that head wins $30$ dollars from the other.
>
> ### Probability that the first player wins
>
> The first player flips on turns $1,3,5,\dots$ and the second player flips on turns $2,4,6,\dots$. The first player wins exactly when the first head occurs on an odd-numbered flip, that is, when the outcome sequence is a run of tails followed by a head landing on flip $1,3,5,\dots$.
>
> The probability that the very first flip is heads is $\frac12$. The probability that the game reaches flip $3$ as the first head (tails, tails, heads) is $\left(\frac12\right)^3$, and in general the probability the first head lands on flip $2k+1$ is $\left(\frac12\right)^{2k+1}$. Summing over all odd flips,
>
> $$
> P(\text{first player wins})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac12\right)^{2k+1}
> =\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac14}
> =\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}
> =\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Consequently,
>
> $$
> P(\text{second player wins})=1-\frac23=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ### Expected value of going first
>
> Going first, you win $30$ dollars with probability $\frac23$ and pay $30$ dollars with probability $\frac13$, so your expected payoff from the game itself is
>
> $$
> 30\cdot\frac23-30\cdot\frac13
> =20-10
> =10.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Playing first is worth $10$ dollars in expectation, so
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{you should be willing to pay up to } 10 \text{ dollars to go first.}}
> $$
>
> Paying exactly this amount makes the game fair; paying any less still leaves you with a positive expected edge.
