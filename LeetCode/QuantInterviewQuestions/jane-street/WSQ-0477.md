> [!example] Question 477 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

How many coin tosses are required to obtain at least 4 tails with a probability of at least 0.7?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=478)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of tails obtained in $n$ tosses of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want the probability of getting at least $4$ tails to be at least $0.7$:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4) \ge 0.7.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> 1 - P(X \le 3) \ge 0.7
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> P(X \le 3) \le 0.3.
> $$
>
> Now compute $P(X \ge 4)$ for successive values of $n$.
>
> For $n=6$:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=\frac{\binom64+\binom65+\binom66}{2^6}
> =\frac{15+6+1}{64}
> =\frac{22}{64}
> =0.34375.
> $$
>
> For $n=7$:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=\frac{\binom74+\binom75+\binom76+\binom77}{2^7}
> =\frac{35+21+7+1}{128}
> =\frac{64}{128}
> =0.5.
> $$
>
> For $n=8$:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)
> =1-P(X\le 3)
> =1-\frac{\binom80+\binom81+\binom82+\binom83}{2^8}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \binom80+\binom81+\binom82+\binom83
> =1+8+28+56=93.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=1-\frac{93}{256}
> =\frac{163}{256}
> \approx 0.6367.
> $$
>
> For $n=9$:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)
> =1-\frac{\binom90+\binom91+\binom92+\binom93}{2^9}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \binom90+\binom91+\binom92+\binom93
> =1+9+36+84=130.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 4)=1-\frac{130}{512}
> =\frac{382}{512}
> \approx 0.7461.
> $$
>
> This is the first value at least $0.7$.
>
> Therefore, the minimum number of tosses required is
>
> $$
> \boxed{9}.
> $$
