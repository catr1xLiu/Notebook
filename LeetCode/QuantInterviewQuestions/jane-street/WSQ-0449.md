> [!example] Question 449 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

How much should you be willing to pay to play a game where you roll two 6-sided dice (die A and die B), and can then choose to be paid out either (A/B) or (B/A), whichever is higher?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=450)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the outcomes of the two fair six-sided dice be $A$ and $B$, each uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ and independent.
>
> After observing $A$ and $B$, you may choose the larger of
> $$
> \frac{A}{B}
> \quad \text{and} \quad
> \frac{B}{A}.
> $$
> Thus the payoff is
> $$
> X=\max\left(\frac{A}{B},\frac{B}{A}\right)=\frac{\max(A,B)}{\min(A,B)}.
> $$
>
> The fair price to pay is the expected value $E[X]$.
>
> ## Step 1: Count outcomes
>
> There are $36$ equally likely ordered outcomes $(A,B)$.
>
> Split them into two cases:
>
> - If $A=B$, then $X=1$.
> - If $A\ne B$, then for each unordered pair $\{a,b\}$ with $a<b$, both ordered outcomes $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ give payoff
>   $$
>   \frac{b}{a}.
>   $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[X]
> =
> \frac{1}{36}
> \left(
> 6\cdot 1
> +
> 2\sum_{1\le a<b\le 6}\frac{b}{a}
> \right).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the sum
>
> Compute
> $$
> \sum_{1\le a<b\le 6}\frac{b}{a}.
> $$
>
> For each $a$:
>
> - $a=1$:
>   $$
>   \frac21+\frac31+\frac41+\frac51+\frac61=2+3+4+5+6=20
>   $$
> - $a=2$:
>   $$
>   \frac32+\frac42+\frac52+\frac62
>   =
>   \frac32+2+\frac52+3
>   =9
>   $$
> - $a=3$:
>   $$
>   \frac43+\frac53+\frac63
>   =
>   \frac43+\frac53+2
>   =5
>   $$
> - $a=4$:
>   $$
>   \frac54+\frac64
>   =
>   \frac54+\frac32
>   =
>   \frac{11}{4}
>   $$
> - $a=5$:
>   $$
>   \frac65
>   =
>   \frac65
>   $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> \sum_{1\le a<b\le 6}\frac{b}{a}
> =
> 20+9+5+\frac{11}{4}+\frac65
> =
> \frac{759}{20}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Take the expectation
>
> Therefore
> $$
> E[X]
> =
> \frac{1}{36}
> \left(
> 6+2\cdot \frac{759}{20}
> \right)
> =
> \frac{1}{36}
> \left(
> 6+\frac{759}{10}
> \right)
> =
> \frac{1}{36}\cdot \frac{819}{10}
> =
> \frac{91}{40}.
> $$
>
> Thus the fair value of the game is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{91}{40}}
> $$
> which is
> $$
> \boxed{2.275}.
> $$
>
> So you should be willing to pay up to about $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}2.28}$ per play.
