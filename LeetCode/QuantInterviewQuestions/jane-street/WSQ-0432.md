> [!example] Question 432 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You have 3,000 apples in Edinburgh and want to transfer as many apples as possible to London. You have a truck with a maximum capacity of 1,000 apples. London is 1,000 miles away from Edinburgh, and when the truck is carrying apples, it consumes one apple per mile traveled. What is the maximum number of apples you can deliver to London?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=433)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the distance from Edinburgh to London be $1000$ miles. The truck can carry at most $1000$ apples at a time, and whenever it moves while carrying apples, it loses $1$ apple per mile traveled.
>
> The key idea is that when there are more apples than the truck can carry, multiple shuttle trips are required, and this increases the effective loss rate per mile.
>
> ## Step 1: Transporting $3000$ apples initially
>
> Since the truck can carry at most $1000$ apples, moving all $3000$ apples forward requires $3$ full loads.
>
> To advance the stock by $1$ mile:
>
> - $3$ forward trips are needed,
> - after the first $2$ forward trips, the truck must return empty of apples? No: it returns carrying no apples, so no apples are lost on return only if the loss depends on carrying apples. But the problem says whenever the truck is carrying apples, it drops one apple per mile. Thus return trips with no apples cost nothing.
>
> So, to move $3000$ apples forward by $1$ mile, each of the $3$ loaded trips loses $1$ apple, for a total loss of $3$ apples per mile.
>
> Thus while the stock exceeds $2000$ apples, the loss rate is $3$ apples per mile.
>
> We need to reduce the stock from $3000$ to $2000$ apples:
>
> $$
> 3000 - 3x = 2000
> $$
>
> $$
> 3x = 1000
> $$
>
> $$
> x = \frac{1000}{3}
> $$
>
> So after traveling $\frac{1000}{3}$ miles, there are $2000$ apples left.
>
> ## Step 2: Transporting $2000$ apples
>
> Now only $2$ loads are needed.
>
> To move the stock forward by $1$ mile, there are $2$ loaded trips, so the loss rate is $2$ apples per mile.
>
> We continue until the stock is reduced from $2000$ to $1000$ apples:
>
> $$
> 2000 - 2y = 1000
> $$
>
> $$
> 2y = 1000
> $$
>
> $$
> y = 500
> $$
>
> So it takes $500$ more miles to reduce the stock to $1000$ apples.
>
> At this point, the distance already covered is
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3} + 500 = \frac{1000}{3} + \frac{1500}{3} = \frac{2500}{3}
> $$
>
> The remaining distance is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{2500}{3} = \frac{3000 - 2500}{3} = \frac{500}{3}
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Transporting the final $1000$ apples
>
> Now everything fits in one load, so the loss rate is $1$ apple per mile.
>
> Over the remaining $\frac{500}{3}$ miles, the loss is
>
> $$
> \frac{500}{3}
> $$
>
> Therefore the number of apples delivered is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{500}{3} = \frac{3000 - 500}{3} = \frac{2500}{3}
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The maximum number of apples that can be delivered to London is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2500}{3}}
> $$
>
> that is,
>
> $$
> \boxed{833\frac{1}{3}}
> $$
>
> apples.
