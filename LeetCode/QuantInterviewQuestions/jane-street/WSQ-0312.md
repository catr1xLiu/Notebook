> [!example] Question 312 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting an odd number of heads when 100 fair coins are flipped?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=313)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume each of the $100$ coins is fair and is tossed independently. Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable with $p=\tfrac12$, the probabilities of getting an even number of heads and an odd number of heads are equal. Hence,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}) = P(X \text{ is even}).
> $$
>
> Since these two events together exhaust all possibilities,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}) + P(X \text{ is even}) = 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> 2P(X \text{ is odd}) = 1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of getting an odd number of heads in $100$ coin tosses is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> If “odd number of coins” was intended to mean “odd number of heads,” this is the answer.
