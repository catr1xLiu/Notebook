> [!example] Question 435 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A number between 0 and 1 is written on a piece of paper. You and Person X are playing a game. You must choose a number less than the number on the paper, but greater than X's guess. X picks a number at random. What is the lowest number you can choose to maximize your probability of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=436)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the hidden number on the paper be $T$, where $T$ is uniformly distributed on $[0,1]$. Let Person $X$ choose a guess $Y$ uniformly at random on $[0,1]$, independently of $T$.
>
> You choose a fixed number $a \in [0,1]$. You win exactly when your number is above $X$’s guess but below the hidden number, that is,
>
> $$
> Y < a < T.
> $$
>
> We seek the choice of $a$ that maximizes
>
> $$
> P(Y < a < T).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the winning probability
>
> Because $Y$ and $T$ are independent,
>
> $$
> P(Y < a < T) = P(Y < a)\,P(T > a).
> $$
>
> Since both are uniform on $[0,1]$,
>
> $$
> P(Y < a) = a
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> P(T > a) = 1-a.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{win}) = a(1-a).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Maximize the function
>
> Consider
>
> $$
> f(a) = a(1-a) = a - a^2.
> $$
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> f'(a) = 1 - 2a.
> $$
>
> Set $f'(a)=0$:
>
> $$
> 1 - 2a = 0
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> a = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> f''(a) = -2 < 0,
> $$
>
> this critical point gives a maximum.
>
> ## Step 3: Maximum winning probability
>
> At $a=\frac12$,
>
> $$
> P(\text{win}) = \frac12\left(1-\frac12\right) = \frac14.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The number you should choose is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
>
> This maximizes your probability of winning, and the maximum probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac14}.
> $$
