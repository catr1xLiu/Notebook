> [!example] Question 151 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

What is the expected value of the number of heads if you flip 4 coins and, after the initial flips, you can flip over any pair of coins that both show tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=152)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads showing after the initial $4$ coin flips, so the number of tails is $T=4-H$.
>
> The phrase "you can flip over any pair of coins that both show tails" is most naturally read as a repeatable move: as long as at least two tails remain, you may pick two of them and turn them face up, and you may keep doing this. That reading is treated as primary below; the answer under the stricter reading that only a single pair-flip is allowed is given afterward as a variant.
>
> ## Primary interpretation: repeat until fewer than two tails remain
>
> Each application of the move turns two tails into two heads, which removes tails two at a time and never changes the parity of the number of tails remaining. Starting from $T$ tails, you can therefore always continue until either
>
> - $0$ tails remain, if $T$ is even, or
> - exactly $1$ tail remains, if $T$ is odd,
>
> since $1$ is the smallest count from which no further pair can be selected. Consequently the final number of heads $X$ is
>
> $$
> X =
> \begin{cases}
> 4, & T \text{ even (equivalently, } H \text{ even)},\\
> 3, & T \text{ odd (equivalently, } H \text{ odd)}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Since $H\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12)$,
>
> $$
> P(H\text{ even}) = P(H{=}0)+P(H{=}2)+P(H{=}4) = \frac{1+6+1}{16}=\frac{1}{2},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(H\text{ odd}) = P(H{=}1)+P(H{=}3) = \frac{4+4}{16}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[X] = 4\cdot\frac12 + 3\cdot\frac12 = \frac{7}{2}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[X] = \frac{7}{2}}.
> $$
>
> ## Variant: only a single pair-flip is permitted
>
> If instead the rule allows flipping over at most one pair of tails in total, rather than repeating the operation, then a pair is available only when $T\ge2$, i.e. $H\le 2$, and using it converts exactly $2$ tails to heads:
>
> $$
> X =
> \begin{cases}
> H+2, & H=0,1,2,\\
> H, & H=3,4.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> This gives $X=2,3,4,3,4$ for $H=0,1,2,3,4$, so
>
> $$
> E[X] = 2\cdot\frac{1}{16}+3\cdot\frac{4}{16}+4\cdot\frac{6}{16}+3\cdot\frac{4}{16}+4\cdot\frac{1}{16}=\frac{54}{16}=\frac{27}{8}.
> $$
>
> Under this stricter reading, $E[X]=\dfrac{27}{8}$.
>
> ## Answer
>
> Reading the move as repeatable — the natural reading of the problem — gives
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[X]=\frac{7}{2}},
> $$
>
> while restricting to a single pair-flip gives $E[X]=\dfrac{27}{8}$.
