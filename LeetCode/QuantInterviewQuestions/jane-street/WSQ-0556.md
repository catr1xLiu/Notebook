> [!example] Question 556 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A company has a value V uniformly distributed between 0 and 1. You are planning to place a bid B for the company. If B is less than V, your bid loses and you get nothing. If B is greater than or equal to V, you purchase the company at price B, and the company ends up being worth 1.5 × V. What bid B should you place to maximize your expected profit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=557)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the company’s true value be $V \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$, and suppose you bid $B$.
>
> Your profit depends on whether the bid is accepted:
>
> - If $B < V$, the bid fails, so profit is $0$.
> - If $B \ge V$, you buy the company for price $B$, and its value to you becomes $1.5V$, so profit is
>   $$
>   1.5V - B.
>   $$
>
> The goal is to maximize expected profit as a function of $B$.
>
> ## Step 1: Write the expected profit
>
> Since $V$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, its density is $1$ on $[0,1]$. For a given bid $B$, the bid succeeds exactly when $V \le B$.
>
> Thus, for $0 \le B \le 1$, the expected profit is
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{profit}]
> =
> \int_0^B (1.5v - B)\,dv.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
> $$
> \int_0^B (1.5v - B)\,dv
> =
> \left[0.75v^2 - Bv\right]_0^B
> =
> 0.75B^2 - B^2
> =
> -0.25B^2.
> $$
>
> So for $0 \le B \le 1$,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{profit}] = -\frac{1}{4}B^2.
> $$
>
> This is maximized at
> $$
> B = 0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Check other ranges of $B$
>
> ### If $B < 0$
>
> Then $B < V$ for all $V \in [0,1]$, so the bid never succeeds. Expected profit is
> $$
> 0.
> $$
>
> ### If $B > 1$
>
> Then $B \ge V$ for all $V \in [0,1]$, so you always buy. Expected profit is
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[1.5V - B]
> =
> 1.5\mathbb{E}[V] - B
> =
> 1.5 \cdot \frac12 - B
> =
> 0.75 - B < 0.
> $$
>
> So this is worse than bidding $0$.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected profit is never positive, and it is maximized by making the smallest possible bid:
>
> $$
> \boxed{B = 0.}
> $$
>
> The maximum expected profit is
> $$
> \boxed{0.}
> $$
