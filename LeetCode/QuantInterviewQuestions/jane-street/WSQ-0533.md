> [!example] Question 533 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have 27 cubes, each with all faces painted white. You assemble them into a 3x3x3 cube and paint the exterior blue. After breaking it apart, you pick a cube and place it on the table such that the five visible faces are all white. What is the probability that the non-visible face is blue?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=534)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> A $3\times 3\times 3$ cube is assembled from $27$ small cubes, all faces initially white; the exterior of the large cube is then painted blue. After separation, each small cube falls into one of four categories:
>
> - Corner cubes: $8$ of them, each with $3$ blue faces.
> - Edge cubes: $12$ of them, each with $2$ blue faces.
> - Face-center cubes: $6$ of them, each with $1$ blue face.
> - The interior cube: $1$ of it, with $0$ blue faces.
>
> You place a cube on the table so that the five visible faces (the top and four sides) are all white. This means the single hidden face, the one touching the table, is the only face that could possibly be blue.
>
> Corner cubes have $3$ blue faces and edge cubes have $2$ blue faces, so no matter which face is hidden, at least one blue face would still be visible. These two types are therefore ruled out entirely by the observation. Only two possibilities remain:
>
> - the interior cube, with $0$ blue faces, or
> - a face-center cube, with $1$ blue face, provided that its single blue face happens to be the hidden one.
>
> ### Weighting the possibilities correctly
>
> It is not enough to count cubes; we must weight each possibility by how likely it is to produce the observed pattern of five white faces.
>
> Model the experiment as picking one of the $27$ cubes uniformly at random and setting it down with a uniformly random face against the table, so each cube has $6$ equally likely orientations.
>
> - For the interior cube, all six faces are white, so every orientation shows five white faces. This cube contributes the observation with full weight $1$ once it is chosen, and in this case the hidden face is white.
> - For a face-center cube, five white faces are visible only in the one orientation, out of six, where its single blue face lands face-down. This cube contributes the observation with weight $\frac16$ once it is chosen, and in this case the hidden face is blue.
>
> Comparing the total likelihood contributed by each source of the observation:
>
> $$
> \text{interior cube: } 1 \times 1 = 1,
> \qquad
> \text{face-center cubes: } 6 \times \frac16 = 1.
> $$
>
> Both sources contribute equally to the observed pattern, so conditional on seeing five white faces, it is equally likely that the cube came from the interior-cube branch (hidden face white) or from a face-center-cube branch (hidden face blue):
>
> $$
> P(\text{hidden face is blue}\mid\text{five visible faces white})=\frac{1}{1+1}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}
> $$
