> [!example] Question 564 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You are given a die with 100 sides, numbered from 1 to 100. When you roll the die, you can choose to either (a) take the number of dollars equal to the number of dots that show up, or (b) pay \$1 to reroll. You can continue to reroll as many times as you like, but you can only keep the result of your final roll. What is the optimal strategy, and what is the expected value of playing optimally?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=565)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $V$ denote the maximal expected payoff from the start of any decision point, that is, immediately after seeing a roll and deciding whether to stop or pay \(\text{\textdollar}1\) to reroll.
>
> If the current roll is $x \in \{1,2,\dots,100\}$, then:
>
> - stopping yields $x$ dollars;
> - rerolling yields an expected payoff of $V-1$, since you pay \(\text{\textdollar}1\) and then face the same decision problem again.
>
> Thus the optimal rule is:
>
> - **stop** if $x \ge V-1$,
> - **reroll** if $x < V-1$.
>
> So the optimal strategy is a **threshold rule**.
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 1: Determine the threshold
>
> Let
> $$
> C = V-1.
> $$
> Then upon seeing $x$, the optimal payoff is
> $$
> \max(x,C).
> $$
> Since the die is uniform on $\{1,2,\dots,100\}$, the value satisfies
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\sum_{x=1}^{100}\max(x,C).
> $$
>
> Because the outcomes are integers, the stopping rule will be of the form: reroll on $1,2,\dots,k$ and stop on $k+1,\dots,100$, where
> $$
> k < C \le k+1.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\left(\sum_{x=1}^k C + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x\right)
> = \frac{1}{100}\left(kC + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x\right).
> $$
> Using $C=V-1$, this becomes
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{100}\left(k(V-1) + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x\right).
> $$
>
> A simpler approach is to test integer thresholds directly.
>
> Suppose we reroll on $1,\dots,k$ and stop on $k+1,\dots,100$. Then after paying for rerolls, the expected value $V_k$ must satisfy
> $$
> V_k = \frac{1}{100}\left(\sum_{x=1}^k (V_k-1) + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x\right).
> $$
> So
> $$
> 100V_k = k(V_k-1) + \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> (100-k)V_k = \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x - k.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V_k = \frac{\sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x - k}{100-k}.
> $$
>
> Now
> $$
> \sum_{x=k+1}^{100} x
> = \frac{100\cdot 101}{2} - \frac{k(k+1)}{2}
> = 5050 - \frac{k(k+1)}{2}.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> V_k = \frac{5050 - \frac{k(k+1)}{2} - k}{100-k}
> = \frac{5050 - \frac{k(k+3)}{2}}{100-k}.
> $$
>
> For threshold $k$ to be optimal, we need:
> - reroll at $k$, so $k < V_k-1$ or equivalently $k+1 < V_k$;
> - stop at $k+1$, so $k+1 \ge V_k-1$ or equivalently $k+2 \ge V_k$.
>
> So we need
> $$
> k+1 < V_k \le k+2.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 2: Find the correct $k$
>
> Try $k=89$:
> $$
> V_{89} = \frac{5050 - \frac{89\cdot 92}{2}}{11}
> = \frac{5050 - 4094}{11}
> = \frac{956}{11}
> \approx 86.91.
> $$
> This is not in the interval $(90,91]$, so $k=89$ is impossible.
>
> Try $k=88$:
> $$
> V_{88} = \frac{5050 - \frac{88\cdot 91}{2}}{12}
> = \frac{5050 - 4004}{12}
> = \frac{1046}{12}
> = \frac{523}{6}
> \approx 87.17.
> $$
> This is not in $(89,90]$ either.
>
> This indicates the indexing above is slightly awkward. It is cleaner to define the stopping threshold directly.
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 3: Direct threshold formulation
>
> Suppose the rule is: **stop on $t,t+1,\dots,100$ and reroll on $1,\dots,t-1$**.
>
> Let $V$ be the expected value under this rule. Then
> $$
> V
> =
> \frac{1}{100}
> \left(
> \sum_{x=1}^{t-1}(V-1) + \sum_{x=t}^{100} x
> \right).
> $$
> So
> $$
> 100V = (t-1)(V-1) + \sum_{x=t}^{100} x,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> (101-t)V = \sum_{x=t}^{100} x - (t-1).
> $$
> Now
> $$
> \sum_{x=t}^{100} x
> = 5050 - \frac{(t-1)t}{2}.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V
> =
> \frac{5050 - \frac{(t-1)t}{2} - (t-1)}{101-t}
> =
> \frac{5050 - \frac{(t-1)(t+2)}{2}}{101-t}.
> $$
>
> For threshold $t$ to be optimal, we need
> $$
> t-1 < V-1 \le t,
> $$
> which is equivalent to
> $$
> t < V \le t+1.
> $$
>
> Now test values near the answer.
>
> For $t=87$:
> $$
> V
> =
> \frac{5050 - \frac{86\cdot 89}{2}}{14}
> =
> \frac{5050 - 3827}{14}
> =
> \frac{1223}{14}
> \approx 87.36.
> $$
> This satisfies
> $$
> 87 < V \le 88.
> $$
> So $t=87$ is optimal.
>
> Thus the optimal rule is:
>
> - **reroll if the roll is $86$ or less**,
> - **stop if the roll is $87$ or more**.
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 4: Expected value
>
> Under this optimal strategy,
> $$
> V = \frac{1223}{14} \approx 87.357142857.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The optimal strategy is to **keep any roll of $87$ or higher**, and **pay \(\text{\textdollar}1\) to reroll any roll of $86$ or lower**.
>
> The corresponding expected value is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1223}{14} \approx 87.36 \text{ dollars}.}
> $$
