> [!example] Question 207 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

If you roll two dice, what is the probability that the sum is a square number?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=208)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> When two fair six-sided dice are rolled, there are
>
> $$6 \times 6 = 36$$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> The possible sums range from $2$ to $12$. The square numbers in this range are
>
> $$4, 9.$$
>
> Now count the outcomes giving each of these sums.
>
> - Sum $4$:
>   $$(1,3), (2,2), (3,1)$$
>   so there are $3$ outcomes.
>
> - Sum $9$:
>   $$(3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3)$$
>   so there are $4$ outcomes.
>
> Thus the total number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$3+4=7.$$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$\frac{7}{36}.$$
>
> So the required probability is
>
> $$\boxed{\frac{7}{36}}.$$
