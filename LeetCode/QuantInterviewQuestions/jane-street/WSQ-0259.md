> [!example] Question 259 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

Two players toss a fair coin repeatedly. Player A wins if the sequence HHT appears first; player B wins if HTT appears first. What is the probability that player A wins the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=260)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the coin be tossed repeatedly until one of the patterns appears as the **ending** of the sequence:
>
> - $A$ wins if the sequence ends with $HHT$,
> - $B$ wins if the sequence ends with $HTT$.
>
> We compute the probability that $A$ appears before $B$.
>
> ## Step 1: Track relevant partial matches
>
> At any time, only the recent tosses matter. Define states according to the longest suffix that is also a prefix of either target pattern:
>
> - $S$: no relevant suffix,
> - $H$: the current suffix is $H$,
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $HH$,
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $HT$.
>
> Let
>
> - $p_S$ = probability that $A$ eventually wins starting from state $S$,
> - $p_H, p_{HH}, p_{HT}$ similarly.
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ## Step 2: Write recursion equations
>
> From state $S$:
>
> - toss $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $H$,
> - toss $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, staying at $S$.
>
> So
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> p_S=p_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - toss $H$ $\to HH$,
> - toss $T$ $\to HT$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - toss $T$ gives $HHT$, so $A$ wins immediately,
> - toss $H$ leaves suffix $HH$ again.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - toss $T$ gives $HTT$, so $B$ wins immediately,
> - toss $H$ makes the suffix $H$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_H=\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve
>
> Substitute into the equation for $p_H$:
> $$
> p_H=\frac12(1)+\frac12\left(\frac12 p_H\right)
> =\frac12+\frac14 p_H.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> \frac34 p_H=\frac12
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> p_H=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Since $p_S=p_H$, we get
> $$
> p_S=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The probability that $A$ wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
