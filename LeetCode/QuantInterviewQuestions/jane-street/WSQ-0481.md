> [!example] Question 481 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You throw 1,000 fair coins. What is the probability of getting an even number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=482)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $1000$ fair coin tosses. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(1000,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}).
> $$
>
> A standard identity for a binomial random variable gives
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})=\frac{1+(1-2p)^n}{2}.
> $$
>
> Here $n=1000$ and $p=\tfrac12$, so
>
> $$
> 1-2p=1-2\cdot \frac12=0.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})=\frac{1+0^{1000}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of getting an even number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
