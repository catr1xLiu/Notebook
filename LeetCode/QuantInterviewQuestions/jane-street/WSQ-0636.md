> [!example] Question 636 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have five coins, one of which is double-headed. You pick a coin at random without looking and flip it five times. If all five outcomes are heads, what is the probability that the coin you picked is the double-headed one?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=637)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D$ be the event that the chosen coin is the double-headed coin, and let $H$ be the event that the five tosses all result in heads.
>
> There are $5$ coins total, of which $1$ is double-headed and $4$ are ordinary fair coins. Hence
>
> $$
> P(D)=\frac{1}{5}, \qquad P(D^c)=\frac{4}{5}.
> $$
>
> If the chosen coin is double-headed, then five heads occur with certainty:
>
> $$
> P(H \mid D)=1.
> $$
>
> If the chosen coin is a fair coin, then the probability of five heads in five tosses is
>
> $$
> P(H \mid D^c)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5=\frac{1}{32}.
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(D \mid H)=\frac{P(H \mid D)P(D)}{P(H \mid D)P(D)+P(H \mid D^c)P(D^c)}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> P(D \mid H)
> =
> \frac{1 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}{1 \cdot \frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{32}\cdot \frac{4}{5}}
> =
> \frac{\frac{1}{5}}{\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{40}}
> =
> \frac{\frac{1}{5}}{\frac{9}{40}}
> =
> \frac{8}{9}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that the selected coin is the double-headed coin is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{8}{9}}.
> $$
