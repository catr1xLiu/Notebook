> [!example] Question 551 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

The value of a car is uniformly distributed between 0 and 1000. You can sell the car for 1.5 times its actual value, provided you win the car by bidding at least its value. What is the optimal bid amount?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=552)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the car’s true value be $V$, where
>
> $$
> V \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1000).
> $$
>
> You choose a bid $b$. You win the car if and only if your bid is at least its value, that is, if
>
> $$
> V \le b.
> $$
>
> If you win, you can immediately sell the car for $1.5$ times its true value, so your profit is
>
> $$
> 1.5V - b.
> $$
>
> If you lose, your profit is $0$.
>
> ## Expected profit as a function of $b$
>
> For $0 \le b \le 1000$, the expected profit is
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = E\big[(1.5V - b)\mathbf{1}_{\{V \le b\}}\big].
> $$
>
> Since $V$ has density $\frac{1}{1000}$ on $[0,1000]$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \int_0^b (1.5v - b)\frac{1}{1000}\,dv.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = \frac{1}{1000}\left[\frac{1.5}{2}v^2 - bv\right]_0^b
> = \frac{1}{1000}\left(0.75b^2 - b^2\right)
> = -\frac{b^2}{4000}.
> $$
>
> Thus, for $0 \le b \le 1000$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = -\frac{b^2}{4000}.
> $$
>
> This is maximized at
>
> $$
> b = 0.
> $$
>
> ## Check bids above $1000$
>
> If $b > 1000$, you always win, so
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = 1.5E[V] - b.
> $$
>
> Since $E[V] = 500$,
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)] = 1.5(500) - b = 750 - b,
> $$
>
> which is less than or equal to $-250$ for all $b > 1000$, so such bids are worse.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal bid is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> You should not bid at all.
