> [!example] Question 590 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Calculate the sum of the digits of all the numbers from 1 to 1,000,000.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=591)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the sum of the digits of all integers from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$, first compute the sum of the digits of all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$, writing each with exactly $6$ digits by allowing leading zeros.
>
> ## Step 1: Sum of digits from $000000$ to $999999$
>
> In the list of all $6$-digit strings from $000000$ to $999999$:
>
> - Each digit position takes each value $0,1,2,\dots,9$ equally often.
> - There are $10^6$ total strings.
> - For any fixed position, each digit appears $10^5$ times.
>
> The sum of the digits $0$ through $9$ is
>
> $$
> 0+1+2+\cdots+9 = 45.
> $$
>
> So the contribution from one position is
>
> $$
> 45 \cdot 10^5.
> $$
>
> Since there are $6$ positions, the total sum is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 45 \cdot 10^5 = 270 \cdot 10^5 = 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> Thus, the sum of the digits of all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$ is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> Since the number $0$ contributes digit sum $0$, this is also the sum for $1$ to $999{,}999$.
>
> ## Step 2: Include $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The number $1{,}000{,}000$ has digit sum
>
> $$
> 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore the required total is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000 + 1 = 27{,}000{,}001.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{27{,}000{,}001}
> $$
