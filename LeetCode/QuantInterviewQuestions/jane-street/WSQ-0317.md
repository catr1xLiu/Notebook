> [!example] Question 317 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Starting from today's date in DD/MM/YYYY format, what is the next date in the future whose digits are all distinct?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=318)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> # Solution
>
> We seek the next date after $01/05/2026$, in the format $DD/MM/YYYY$, whose eight digits are all distinct.
>
> ## Key structural fact
>
> Every valid month $MM$ has tens digit in $\{0,1\}$: months $01$ through $09$ have tens digit $0$, and months $10,11,12$ have tens digit $1$. Likewise, every valid day $DD$ has tens digit in $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Since all eight digits of the date must be distinct, the year's four digits, together with the month's and day's tens digits, must draw from these overlapping requirements without collision, which makes $\{0,1,2,3\}$ the critical set of digits to track.
>
> ## Ruling out the 2000s
>
> Every year from $2026$ to $2099$ has the form $20XY$, so it always contains the digits $0$ and $2$. Since $0$ is present, every month except $12$ is blocked, because every month other than $12$ has $0$ as one of its two digits. But month $12$ has digits $\{1,2\}$, and $2$ is already used by the year. So no valid month exists for any year in this range: the entire decade is impossible, regardless of whether a given year's own four digits happen to be distinct.
>
> ## Ruling out the 2100s
>
> Every year from $2100$ to $2199$ has the form $21XY$ and so contains the digits $1$ and $2$. Since $1$ is taken, the month's tens digit is forced to be $0$, so the month has the form $0d$. That leaves only the digit $3$ available, among $\{0,1,2,3\}$, for the day's tens digit, forcing the day to be $30$ or $31$. But $30$ needs a units digit of $0$, already used by the month, and $31$ needs a units digit of $1$, already used by the year. Either choice collides, so no valid day exists: this decade is also entirely impossible.
>
> ## Ruling out the 2200s
>
> Every year from $2200$ to $2299$ has the form $22XY$, repeating the digit $2$ within the year itself. No such year has four distinct digits, so this decade is impossible outright.
>
> ## The 2300s
>
> A year $23XY$ contains $2$ and $3$. If either $X$ or $Y$ is $0$ or $1$, then, by the same reasoning as above, the remaining supply of $\{0,1,2,3\}$ is too thin to cover both a month's tens digit and a day's tens digit without collision. So both $X$ and $Y$ must come from $\{4,5,6,7,8,9\}$. The smallest such year is obtained by taking $X=4$ and $Y=5$, the two smallest available digits, giving
>
> $$
> 2345.
> $$
>
> ## Finding the smallest month and day in $2345$
>
> The year $2345$ leaves $\{0,1\}$ free for the month's tens digit. Trying month $01$: its digits are $0,1$, which uses up both remaining members of $\{0,1,2,3\}$, leaving no valid tens digit for the day, so month $01$ must be rejected. Months $02$ through $05$ are also impossible, since their units digits $2,3,4,5$ are exactly the digits already used by the year.
>
> The next candidate is month $06$, with digits $0,6$. This leaves digit $1$ available for the day's tens digit, so the day has the form $1d$, a day between $10$ and $19$. The digits used so far are $2,3,4,5$ from the year and $0,6$ from the month, so the day's units digit must avoid all of $2,3,4,5,0,6,1$, leaving $\{7,8,9\}$. The smallest choice is $7$, giving day $17$, which is a valid day in June.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Assembling the year, month, and day found above, the next date whose eight digits are all distinct is
>
> $$
> \boxed{17/06/2345}
> $$
