> [!example] Question 588 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If the probability of seeing at least one car in an hour is 544/625, what is the probability of seeing no cars in a 15-minute interval?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=589)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be the probability of seeing no cars in a $15$-minute interval.
>
> Assume the four $15$-minute intervals in an hour are independent. Then the probability of seeing no cars in the entire hour is $p^4$.
>
> We are given that the probability of seeing at least one car in an hour is
>
> $$
> \frac{544}{625}.
> $$
>
> So the probability of seeing no cars in an hour is
>
> $$
> 1 - \frac{544}{625} = \frac{81}{625}.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> p^4 = \frac{81}{625}.
> $$
>
> Taking the fourth root,
>
> $$
> p = \left(\frac{81}{625}\right)^{1/4}
> = \frac{3}{5}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of seeing no cars in a $15$-minute interval is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{5}}.
> $$
