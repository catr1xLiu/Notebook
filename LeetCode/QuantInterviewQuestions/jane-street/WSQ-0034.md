> [!example] Question 34 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A bear wants to catch 3 fish from a river, and will leave after catching the 3rd fish. Each time a fish comes, there is a 1/2 chance the bear will catch it. What is the probability that the 5th fish will not be caught?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=35)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_i$ be the event that the $i$th fish is caught, with
>
> $$
> P(X_i)=\frac12, \qquad P(X_i^c)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> The bear stops as soon as he has caught $3$ fish. We want the probability that the $5$th fish is **not** caught.
>
> There are two ways this can happen:
>
> 1. the bear has already caught $3$ fish among the first $4$ fish, so he has already left before the $5$th fish arrives;
> 2. the bear is still there at the $5$th fish, but fails to catch it.
>
> It is simpler to compute this directly by conditioning on how many fish were caught in the first $4$.
>
> ---
>
> Let $K$ be the number of fish caught among the first $4$ fish. Then
>
> $$
> K \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> ### Case 1: $K=3$ or $K=4$
> If $K \ge 3$, then the bear has already reached his quota by or before the $4$th fish, so he has left, and the $5$th fish is certainly not caught.
>
> Thus this contributes
>
> $$
> P(K=3)+P(K=4).
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: $K=0$, $K=1$, or $K=2$
> If at most $2$ fish were caught in the first $4$, the bear has *not yet* reached his quota of $3$, so he is still fishing when the $5$th fish arrives. In every one of these situations ($K=0,1,2$) the $5$th fish is not caught with probability $\frac12$.
>
> Thus this contributes
>
> $$
> P(K\le 2)\cdot \frac12.
> $$
>
> So the desired probability is
>
> $$
> P(K\ge 3)+\frac12 P(K\le 2).
> $$
>
> Now compute the individual terms:
>
> $$
> P(K=0)=\binom{4}{0}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{1}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(K=1)=\binom{4}{1}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{4}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(K=2)=\binom{4}{2}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{6}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(K=3)=\binom{4}{3}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{4}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(K=4)=\binom{4}{4}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(K\ge 3)=P(K=3)+P(K=4)=\frac{4}{16}+\frac{1}{16}=\frac{5}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(K\le 2)=P(K=0)+P(K=1)+P(K=2)=\frac{1}{16}+\frac{4}{16}+\frac{6}{16}=\frac{11}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{5th fish not caught})
> =
> \frac{5}{16}+\frac12\cdot \frac{11}{16}
> =
> \frac{5}{16}+\frac{11}{32}
> =
> \frac{10}{32}+\frac{11}{32}
> =
> \frac{21}{32}.
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{21}{32}}
> $$
