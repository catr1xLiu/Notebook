> [!example] Question 393 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Given two fair dice, what is the probability that both show six if it is observed that at least one die shows a six?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=394)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that both dice show six, and let $B$ be the event that at least one die shows six. We seek the conditional probability
>
> $$
> P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}.
> $$
>
> Since $A$ implies $B$, we have
>
> $$
> A \cap B = A,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A)}{P(B)}.
> $$
>
> Now compute each probability:
>
> - The probability that both dice show six is
>
> $$
> P(A) = \frac{1}{36}.
> $$
>
> - The probability that at least one die shows six is
>
> $$
> P(B) = 1 - P(\text{no six on either die}) = 1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{25}{36} = \frac{11}{36}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(A \mid B) = \frac{\frac{1}{36}}{\frac{11}{36}} = \frac{1}{11}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{11}}.
> $$
