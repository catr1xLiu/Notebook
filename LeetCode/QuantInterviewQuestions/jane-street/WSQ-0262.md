> [!example] Question 262 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected outcome when rolling two 10-sided dice? Please explain why.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=263)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For two fair $10$-sided dice labeled $1$ through $10$, let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be the outcomes.
>
> Each die is uniform on $\{1,2,\dots,10\}$, so the expected value of one die is
>
> $$
> E[X_1] = \frac{1+2+\cdots+10}{10} = \frac{55}{10} = 5.5.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
>
> $$
> E[X_1+X_2] = E[X_1] + E[X_2] = 5.5 + 5.5 = 11.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected sum of two $10$-sided dice is
>
> $$
> \boxed{11}.
> $$
>
> Why this is true: expectation is the long-run average outcome over many rolls. Since each die is symmetric from $1$ to $10$, its average value is the midpoint
>
> $$
> \frac{1+10}{2} = 5.5,
> $$
>
> and adding two such averages gives
>
> $$
> 5.5+5.5=11.
> $$
>
> So the expected outcome is
>
> $$
> \boxed{11}.
> $$
