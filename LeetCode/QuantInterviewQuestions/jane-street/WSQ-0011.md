> [!example] Question 11 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

I toss 10 fair coins. What is the probability of getting an even number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=12)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each coin toss has two equally likely outcomes, so for $10$ fair coins there are
>
> $$
> 2^{10} = 1024
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> An even number of heads can be $0,2,4,6,8,$ or $10$. Thus the number of favorable outcomes is
>
> $$
> \binom{10}{0}+\binom{10}{2}+\binom{10}{4}+\binom{10}{6}+\binom{10}{8}+\binom{10}{10}.
> $$
>
> Using the identity that the sum of the even binomial coefficients equals half of the total sum,
>
> $$
> \sum_{\text{even }k}\binom{10}{k} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{10}\binom{10}{k}
> = \frac{1}{2}(1+1)^{10}
> = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{10}
> = 2^9.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{even number of heads})=\frac{2^9}{2^{10}}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
