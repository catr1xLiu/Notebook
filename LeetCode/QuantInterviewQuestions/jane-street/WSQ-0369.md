> [!example] Question 369 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Given the cards that are already on the table, make a market on the probability that the next card drawn is a face card.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=370)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A market can be made directly from the composition of the unseen deck.
>
> A standard deck has $52$ cards, of which the face cards are
> $$
> J,\ Q,\ K
> $$
> in each of the $4$ suits, so there are
> $$
> 12
> $$
> face cards total.
>
> Suppose the cards already visible on the table contain:
>
> - $t$ total cards, and
> - $f$ face cards among them.
>
> Then among the unseen cards there remain
>
> - $52 - t$ total cards,
> - $12 - f$ face cards.
>
> Therefore, the probability that the next card is a face card is
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\text{next card is face card}) = \frac{12 - f}{52 - t}.
> $$
>
> A fair market on the event “the next card is a face card” is thus centered at
> $$
> p = \frac{12 - f}{52 - t}.
> $$
>
> If one wants decimal odds, use that number as the fair price.  
> If one wants betting odds, then:
>
> - “Yes” is worth
>   $$
>   \frac{12 - f}{52 - t},
>   $$
> - “No” is worth
>   $$
>   1 - \frac{12 - f}{52 - t} = \frac{40 - (t-f)}{52 - t}.
>   $$
>
> For example, if $10$ cards are on the table and $3$ of them are face cards, then
> $$
> p=\frac{12-3}{52-10}=\frac{9}{42}=\frac{3}{14}\approx 0.2143.
> $$
> So the fair market is about $21.43\%$ on “face card.”
>
> Thus the general answer is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{12-f}{52-t}}
> $$
> where $t$ is the number of visible cards and $f$ is the number of visible face cards.
