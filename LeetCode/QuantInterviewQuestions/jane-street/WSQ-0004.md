> [!example] Question 4 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Suppose you are playing Russian roulette. What is the probability that the person who fires first wins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=5)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume a standard two-player version of Russian roulette with a revolver having $6$ chambers and exactly $1$ bullet, the cylinder spun randomly before the first shot, and then the players alternate pulling the trigger without re-spinning.
>
> Let player $A$ fire first, and let “win” mean “survive while the other player is shot.”
>
> There are $6$ equally likely positions for the bullet in the firing order:
>
> - If the bullet is in chamber $1$, player $A$ is shot immediately, so $A$ does not win.
> - If the bullet is in chamber $2$, player $B$ is shot, so $A$ wins.
> - If the bullet is in chamber $3$, player $A$ is shot.
> - If the bullet is in chamber $4$, player $B$ is shot, so $A$ wins.
> - If the bullet is in chamber $5$, player $A$ is shot.
> - If the bullet is in chamber $6$, player $B$ is shot, so $A$ wins.
>
> Thus player $A$ wins exactly when the bullet is in an even-numbered chamber: $2$, $4$, or $6$.
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{first player wins})=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that the person who fires first wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> If instead the rules are different (for example, re-spinning before each trigger pull or a different number of chambers), the answer changes.
