> [!example] Question 345 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of flipping at most two tails when flipping five fair coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=346)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of tails obtained when $5$ fair coins are flipped. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n=5, p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \le 2)=P(X=0)+P(X=1)+P(X=2).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{5}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^5.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> P(X \le 2)
> = \left[\binom{5}{0}+\binom{5}{1}+\binom{5}{2}\right]\left(\tfrac12\right)^5.
> $$
>
> Compute the binomial coefficients:
>
> $$
> \binom{5}{0}=1,\qquad \binom{5}{1}=5,\qquad \binom{5}{2}=10.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X \le 2)=\frac{1+5+10}{32}=\frac{16}{32}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of flipping at most two tails is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
