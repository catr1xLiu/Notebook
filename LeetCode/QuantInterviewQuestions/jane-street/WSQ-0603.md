> [!example] Question 603 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Estimate the mass of the Earth.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=604)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To estimate the Earth’s mass, use Newton’s law of gravitation together with the acceleration due to gravity at the Earth’s surface.
>
> ## 1. Governing relation
>
> At the Earth’s surface, the gravitational force on an object of mass $m$ is
>
> $$
> F = \frac{G M_{\oplus} m}{R_{\oplus}^2},
> $$
>
> where
>
> - $G \approx 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/kg^2}$ is the gravitational constant,
> - $M_{\oplus}$ is the Earth’s mass,
> - $R_{\oplus} \approx 6.37 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{m}$ is the Earth’s radius.
>
> This force is also equal to the object’s weight:
>
> $$
> F = mg,
> $$
>
> with $g \approx 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
>
> Equating the two expressions gives
>
> $$
> mg = \frac{G M_{\oplus} m}{R_{\oplus}^2}.
> $$
>
> Cancel $m$:
>
> $$
> g = \frac{G M_{\oplus}}{R_{\oplus}^2}.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} = \frac{g R_{\oplus}^2}{G}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Substitute numerical values
>
> First compute $R_{\oplus}^2$:
>
> $$
> R_{\oplus}^2 = (6.37 \times 10^6)^2 \approx 4.06 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{m^2}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} = \frac{(9.8)(4.06 \times 10^{13})}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}}.
> $$
>
> Compute the numerator:
>
> $$
> 9.8 \times 4.06 \times 10^{13} \approx 3.98 \times 10^{14}.
> $$
>
> Now divide by $6.67 \times 10^{-11}$:
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} \approx \frac{3.98 \times 10^{14}}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}}
> \approx 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final estimate
>
> $$
> \boxed{M_{\oplus} \approx 6 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}}
> $$
>
> So the Earth’s mass is approximately $6 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}$.
