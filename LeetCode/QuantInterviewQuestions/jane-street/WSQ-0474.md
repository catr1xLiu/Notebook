> [!example] Question 474 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

We randomly select 3 numbers from the set of the first 9 prime numbers, without replacement. What is the probability that the sum of those numbers is even, and why?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=475)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The first $9$ prime numbers are
> $$
> 2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23.
> $$
>
> To determine when the sum of $3$ selected numbers is even, use parity.
>
> ## Step 1: Classify the primes by parity
>
> Among these primes:
>
> - $2$ is the only even prime.
> - The other $8$ primes are odd.
>
> So the set consists of:
>
> - $1$ even number,
> - $8$ odd numbers.
>
> ## Step 2: Determine when the sum of $3$ numbers is even
>
> A sum of $3$ integers is even exactly when the number of odd summands is even.
>
> With $3$ numbers, the possible parity patterns are:
>
> - even $+$ even $+$ even $\to$ even,
> - even $+$ odd $+$ odd $\to$ even,
> - odd $+$ odd $+$ odd $\to$ odd,
> - even $+$ even $+$ odd $\to$ odd.
>
> But there is only one even number available, so it is impossible to choose:
>
> - $3$ evens,
> - or $2$ evens and $1$ odd.
>
> Thus the only way to get an even sum is to choose:
>
> - $1$ even and $2$ odds.
>
> That means the selection must include $2$ and two of the $8$ odd primes.
>
> ## Step 3: Count favorable selections
>
> Choose $2$ odd primes from the $8$ odd primes:
> $$
> \binom{8}{2} = 28.
> $$
>
> So there are $28$ favorable selections.
>
> ## Step 4: Count all possible selections
>
> The total number of ways to choose any $3$ numbers from $9$ is
> $$
> \binom{9}{3} = 84.
> $$
>
> ## Step 5: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{even sum})=\frac{\binom{8}{2}}{\binom{9}{3}}=\frac{28}{84}=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}
> $$
>
> The reason is that among the first $9$ primes, only $2$ is even, so the sum of $3$ chosen primes is even exactly when the selection is $2$ together with two odd primes.
