> [!example] Question 249 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting at least one head in 4 coin tosses? What about in 9 coin tosses?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=250)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For independent tosses of a fair coin, the easiest method is to use the complement.
>
> ## General idea
> The event “at least one head” is the complement of “no heads,” that is, “all tails.”
>
> For $n$ tosses:
> - $P(\text{all tails}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$
> - Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{at least one head}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n
> $$
>
> ## For $4$ tosses
> $$
> P(\text{at least one head in 4 tosses}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4
> $$
> $$
> = 1 - \frac{1}{16} = \frac{15}{16}
> $$
>
> ## For $9$ tosses
> $$
> P(\text{at least one head in 9 tosses}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^9
> $$
> $$
> = 1 - \frac{1}{512} = \frac{511}{512}
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{15}{16}}
> \quad \text{and} \quad
> \boxed{\frac{511}{512}}
> $$
