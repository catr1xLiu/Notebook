> [!example] Question 18 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Person A walks up an escalator at 2 steps/second. Person B walks up the same escalator at 3 steps/second and gets on 5 seconds after Person A, arriving at the top X seconds after Person A. If the escalator moves at 1 step/second, how many steps are visible on the escalator at any given moment?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=19)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the escalator have $N$ visible steps, and let upward motion be positive.
>
> The escalator itself moves upward at $1$ step/second.
>
> - Person A walks at $2$ steps/second relative to the escalator, so A’s speed relative to the ground is
>   $$
>   2+1=3 \text{ steps/second}.
>   $$
> - Person B walks at $3$ steps/second relative to the escalator, so B’s speed relative to the ground is
>   $$
>   3+1=4 \text{ steps/second}.
>   $$
>
> If A takes time $t_A$ to reach the top, then
> $$
> N=3t_A.
> $$
>
> B starts $5$ seconds later, so if B arrives $X$ seconds after A, then B’s travel time is
> $$
> t_B=t_A+X-5.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> N=4t_B=4(t_A+X-5).
> $$
>
> Since both expressions equal $N$,
> $$
> 3t_A=4(t_A+X-5).
> $$
> Solve for $t_A$:
> $$
> 3t_A=4t_A+4X-20,
> $$
> $$
> t_A=20-4X.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> N=3t_A=3(20-4X)=60-12X.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the number of visible steps is
>
> $$
> \boxed{60-12X}.
> $$
>
> Since the exact value of $X$ is unknown, a numerical answer cannot be determined; it depends on $X$.
