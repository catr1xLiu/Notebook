> [!example] Question 42 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Which has a higher probability: (1) Rolling a die twice and getting two sixes, or (2) Rolling a die ten times and never getting a six?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=43)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Compute each probability and compare.
>
> Let a fair die be rolled.
>
> ### 1. Two rolls, both are $6$
>
> Each roll has probability $\frac{1}{6}$ of being a $6$, and the rolls are independent. Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{two 6's in two rolls})=\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2=\frac{1}{36}\approx 0.0278.
> $$
>
> ### 2. Ten rolls, no roll is $6$
>
> Each roll has probability $\frac{5}{6}$ of not being a $6$, and the rolls are independent. Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{no 6 in ten rolls})=\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{10}\approx 0.1615.
> $$
>
> ### Comparison
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{10}\approx 0.1615
> \qquad \text{and} \qquad
> \frac{1}{36}\approx 0.0278.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 0.1615 > 0.0278,
> $$
>
> the higher probability is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Rolling a die ten times and never getting a }6.}
> $$
