> [!example] Question 235 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

You flip a fair coin repeatedly and stop flipping after three heads in a row occur. What is the expected number of flips required?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=236)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E_k$ denote the expected additional number of flips needed to obtain three consecutive heads, given that the current run of consecutive heads has length $k$, where $k \in \{0,1,2,3\}$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - $E_0$ = expected number of flips starting from no current consecutive heads,
> - $E_1$ = expected number of flips given the last flip was a head,
> - $E_2$ = expected number of flips given the last two flips were heads,
> - $E_3 = 0$, since the goal has already been reached.
>
> Assume the coin is fair, so each flip is heads with probability $\tfrac12$ and tails with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the recurrences
>
> From state $0$:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we move to state $1$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we remain at state $0$.
>
> So
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> From state $1$:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we move to state $2$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ a tail occurs and the run resets to $0$.
>
> So
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> From state $2$:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a head and reach state $3$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ a tail occurs and the run resets to $0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_3 + \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> E_3 = 0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0,
> $$
> we get
> $$
> \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 2 + E_1.
> $$
>
> From
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute this into the equation for $E_1$:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0 + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Now use $E_0 = 2 + E_1$:
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + \frac32 + \frac34 E_0
> = \frac72 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_0 - \frac34 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> \frac14 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 14.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of flips needed to obtain three heads in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{14}.
> $$
