> [!example] Question 440 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have 100 light bulbs labeled from 1 to 100, all initially off. You perform 100 rounds of toggling: on the nth round, you toggle every nth bulb. Toggling means turning a bulb on if it's off and off if it's on. After all 100 rounds, which bulbs are on?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=441)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Consider bulb $n$, where $1 \le n \le 100$.
>
> Bulb $n$ is toggled once for each round number that divides $n$. Thus, the total number of times bulb $n$ is toggled equals the number of positive divisors of $n$.
>
> A bulb ends **on** exactly when it is toggled an odd number of times. So we must determine which integers from $1$ to $100$ have an odd number of divisors.
>
> ### Key observation
> Divisors of a number usually come in pairs:
> $$
> d \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{n}{d}.
> $$
> These are distinct unless
> $$
> d = \frac{n}{d},
> $$
> which means
> $$
> d^2 = n.
> $$
>
> Therefore, a number has an odd number of divisors precisely when it is a perfect square.
>
> So the bulbs that remain on are exactly those whose labels are perfect squares between $1$ and $100$:
>
> $$
> 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The bulbs that are on after all $100$ rounds are:
>
> $$
> \boxed{1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100}
> $$
