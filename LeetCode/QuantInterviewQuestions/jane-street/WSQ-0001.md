> [!example] Question 1 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You pick n points independently and uniformly at random on the circumference of a circle. 1. What is the probability P(n) that all n points lie within some semicircle of the circle? 2. Simplify your answer to a closed form in terms of n.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=2)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the $n$ points be chosen independently and uniformly on the circumference of a circle.
>
> We seek the probability $P(n)$ that all $n$ points lie in some semicircle.
>
> ## Step 1: Characterize the event
>
> A set of $n$ points lies in some semicircle if and only if there exists a semicircle whose boundary contains one of the chosen points and whose interior contains all the others.
>
> To see this, imagine rotating a semicircle around the circle until it first contains all the points. At that first moment, one endpoint of the semicircle must coincide with one of the chosen points.
>
> Thus, it suffices to consider semicircles of length $\pi$ whose left endpoint is one of the sampled points.
>
> ## Step 2: Define the relevant events
>
> For each point $i$, let $A_i$ be the event that, starting from point $i$ and moving clockwise, all the other $n-1$ points lie within the next semicircle of angular length $\pi$.
>
> Since the points are independent and uniform, once point $i$ is fixed, each of the other $n-1$ points has probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of falling in that semicircle. Therefore,
>
> $$
> \Pr(A_i) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Show these events are disjoint
>
> If the points are in general position, at most one point can serve as the left endpoint of such a containing semicircle. Hence the events $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ are disjoint, except for boundary cases of probability $0$ (for example, points exactly opposite one another).
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(n) = \Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right)
> = \sum_{i=1}^n \Pr(A_i)
> = n\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}.
> $$
>
> ## Closed form
>
> Thus the probability that all $n$ points lie within some semicircle is
>
> $$
> P(n) = \frac{n}{2^{\,n-1}}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(n)=\frac{n}{2^{\,n-1}}.}
> $$
