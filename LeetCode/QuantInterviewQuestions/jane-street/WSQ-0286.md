> [!example] Question 286 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Interview

What is the weight of each of five melons if their pairwise weights sum to 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 24, 25, and 26?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=287)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> # Solution
>
> Let the five melon weights, in increasing order, be $a \le b \le c \le d \le e$. There are $\binom{5}{2}=10$ pairwise sums, given as
> $$
> 16,17,18,19,20,21,22,24,25,26.
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Find the total weight
>
> Each melon appears in exactly $4$ of the $10$ pairwise sums, so summing all ten given values counts each weight four times:
> $$
> 16+17+18+19+20+21+22+24+25+26 = 208.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> 4(a+b+c+d+e) = 208, \qquad a+b+c+d+e = 52.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Identify the extreme sums
>
> Since $a \le b \le c \le d \le e$, the smallest pairwise sum is the two smallest weights, and the largest pairwise sum is the two largest weights:
> $$
> a+b = 16, \qquad d+e = 26.
> $$
> Because $c$ is excluded from both of these sums,
> $$
> c = 52 - (a+b) - (d+e) = 52 - 16 - 26 = 10.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Identify the second-smallest and second-largest sums
>
> The second-smallest pairwise sum must be $a+c$, and the second-largest must be $c+e$:
> $$
> a+c = 17, \qquad c+e = 25.
> $$
> Since $c=10$,
> $$
> a = 17-10 = 7, \qquad e = 25-10=15.
> $$
> Then from the extreme sums,
> $$
> b = 16-a = 9, \qquad d = 26-e = 11.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Verify all pairwise sums
>
> The five weights are
> $$
> 7,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 15.
> $$
> Their $10$ pairwise sums are
> $$
> 7+9=16,\quad 7+10=17,\quad 7+11=18,\quad 7+15=22,
> $$
> $$
> 9+10=19,\quad 9+11=20,\quad 9+15=24,
> $$
> $$
> 10+11=21,\quad 10+15=25,\quad 11+15=26.
> $$
> Sorted, these are
> $$
> 16,17,18,19,20,21,22,24,25,26,
> $$
> which matches the given list exactly.
>
> ## Answer
>
> The five melons weigh
> $$
> \boxed{7,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 15}.
> $$
