> [!example] Question 62 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the probability of obtaining an even number of heads when flipping 9 coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=63)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $9$ fair coins are tossed. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(9,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want the probability of getting an even number of heads:
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k \text{ even}}}^{9} \binom{9}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^9.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \left(\frac12\right)^9 \left[\binom{9}{0}+\binom{9}{2}+\binom{9}{4}+\binom{9}{6}+\binom{9}{8}\right].
> $$
>
> Using the identity that, for $n \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \sum_{k \text{ even}} \binom{n}{k} = 2^{n-1},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \left(\frac12\right)^9 \cdot 2^8 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of an even number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
