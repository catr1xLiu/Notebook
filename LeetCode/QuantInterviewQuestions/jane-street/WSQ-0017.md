> [!example] Question 17 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability that the sum of two prime numbers between 1 and 20 is even?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=18)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Prime numbers between $1$ and $20$ are
>
> $$
> 2,\,3,\,5,\,7,\,11,\,13,\,17,\,19.
> $$
>
> There are $8$ such primes. We choose two *distinct* primes from this list and ask for the probability that their sum is even.
>
> A sum of two numbers is even precisely when both numbers have the same parity, that is, both are even or both are odd.
>
> Among these $8$ primes:
>
> - only $2$ is even,
> - the other $7$ primes ($3,5,7,11,13,17,19$) are odd.
>
> ## Counting favorable pairs
>
> Since we are choosing two distinct primes from the list, and there is only a single even prime ($2$), it is impossible for both chosen primes to be even. So the only way to get an even sum is for both chosen primes to be odd.
>
> There are $7$ odd primes, so the number of ways to choose $2$ of them is
>
> $$
> \binom{7}{2} = 21.
> $$
>
> This is the total number of favorable pairs.
>
> ## Total number of pairs
>
> There are $8$ primes in total. The number of ways to choose any $2$ distinct primes is
>
> $$
> \binom{8}{2} = 28.
> $$
>
> ## Probability
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{21}{28} = \frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{4}}.
> $$
