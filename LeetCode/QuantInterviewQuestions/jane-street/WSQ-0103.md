> [!example] Question 103 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

Estimate the mass of the Earth.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=104)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To estimate the mass of the Earth, use the balance between gravitational attraction and the centripetal acceleration needed to keep an object on the Earth’s surface in circular motion with the Earth.
>
> The gravitational force on an object of mass $m$ at the Earth’s surface is
>
> $$
> F = \frac{G M_{\oplus} m}{R_{\oplus}^2},
> $$
>
> where
>
> - $G \approx 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/kg^2}$ is the gravitational constant,
> - $M_{\oplus}$ is the Earth’s mass,
> - $R_{\oplus} \approx 6.37 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{m}$ is the Earth’s radius.
>
> This force equals the object’s weight:
>
> $$
> F = mg,
> $$
>
> with $g \approx 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
>
> Set these equal:
>
> $$
> mg = \frac{G M_{\oplus} m}{R_{\oplus}^2}.
> $$
>
> Cancel $m$:
>
> $$
> g = \frac{G M_{\oplus}}{R_{\oplus}^2}.
> $$
>
> Solve for $M_{\oplus}$:
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} = \frac{g R_{\oplus}^2}{G}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the numerical values:
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} = \frac{(9.8)(6.37 \times 10^6)^2}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}}.
> $$
>
> First compute the square:
>
> $$
> (6.37 \times 10^6)^2 \approx 4.06 \times 10^{13}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> 9.8 \times 4.06 \times 10^{13} \approx 3.98 \times 10^{14}.
> $$
>
> Finally,
>
> $$
> M_{\oplus} \approx \frac{3.98 \times 10^{14}}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}}
> \approx 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the Earth’s mass is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{6 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}}.
> $$
