> [!example] Question 274 · Jane Street
> ## Junior Trader Interview

I have a number in mind such that the product of each of its digits is 96, and no digit is 1. What are the largest and smallest numbers I could think of?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=275)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find all possible numbers, factor the required digit product:
>
> $$96 = 2^5 \cdot 3.$$
>
> Since each digit must be a single digit from $2$ to $9$ (there is no digit $1$), we want collections of digits whose product is $96$.
>
> ## Smallest possible number
>
> To make the number as small as possible, we want:
>
> 1. as few digits as possible, and
> 2. among those, the smallest leading digits possible.
>
> So we combine factors into the largest possible digits:
>
> $$96 = 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 8.$$
>
> This gives the $3$ digits $2,6,8$. Arranged in increasing order, the smallest number is
>
> $$268.$$
>
> We check whether a $2$-digit solution is possible. The factor pairs of $96$ are
>
> $$(2,48),\ (3,32),\ (4,24),\ (6,16),\ (8,12),$$
>
> and none gives two single digits. So $3$ digits is the minimum possible, and hence
>
> $$\boxed{268}$$
>
> is the smallest number.
>
> ## Largest possible number
>
> To make the number as large as possible, we want as many digits as possible, because a number with more digits is larger than any number with fewer digits.
>
> Thus we should split $96$ into the smallest allowed digits, namely as many $2$'s as possible and one $3$:
>
> $$96 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3.$$
>
> So the digits are $2,2,2,2,2,3$. To maximize the number, arrange them in descending order:
>
> $$322222.$$
>
> Thus the largest possible number is
>
> $$\boxed{322222}.$$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$\boxed{\text{Smallest number }=268,\qquad \text{Largest number }=322222.}$$
