> [!example] Question 488 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

a) The odds of having pizza on a Saturday are 60%, and on Sunday, 30%. What are the odds of having pizza at least once during the weekend? b) How do the odds change if you are only allowed pizza on one of the two days? c) What if you can only have pizza on Sunday if you had it on Saturday first?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=489)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $S$ denote the event of having pizza on Saturday, and $U$ the event of having pizza on Sunday.
>
> Given:
> $$
> P(S)=0.6, \qquad P(U)=0.3.
> $$
>
> We interpret “at least once during the weekend” as the event $S \cup U$.
>
> ---
>
> ### a) Pizza may occur on either day independently
>
> Assume the Saturday and Sunday events are independent. Then
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=P(S)+P(U)-P(S \cap U).
> $$
>
> By independence,
>
> $$
> P(S \cap U)=P(S)P(U)=0.6 \cdot 0.3=0.18.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=0.6+0.3-0.18=0.72.
> $$
>
> So the probability of having pizza at least once during the weekend is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.72 \text{ or } 72\%}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### b) You are only allowed pizza on one of the two days
>
> Now the events are mutually exclusive, so you cannot have pizza on both Saturday and Sunday. Thus
>
> $$
> P(S \cap U)=0.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=P(S)+P(U)=0.6+0.3=0.9.
> $$
>
> So the probability of having pizza at least once during the weekend is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.90 \text{ or } 90\%}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### c) You can only have pizza on Sunday if you had it on Saturday first
>
> This means that having pizza on Sunday implies having pizza on Saturday. In set notation,
>
> $$
> U \subseteq S.
> $$
>
> Therefore, whenever Sunday occurs, Saturday also occurs, and so
>
> $$
> S \cup U = S.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=P(S)=0.6.
> $$
>
> So the probability of having pizza at least once during the weekend is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.60 \text{ or } 60\%}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answers
>
> $$
> \text{a) } 72\%
> $$
>
> $$
> \text{b) } 90\%
> $$
>
> $$
> \text{c) } 60\%
> $$
