> [!example] Question 390 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

First, throw a die with numbers 1 to 8. After seeing the result, you can either stop or throw a second die with numbers 1 to 12. You win the amount of money equal to the final number shown. What strategy should you use to maximize your expected winnings?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=391)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the outcome of the first die be $X$, where $X \in \{1,2,\dots,8\}$ and each value is equally likely. After observing $X$, you may either
>
> - **stop**, receiving $X$, or
> - **continue** and roll a second die with faces $\{1,2,\dots,12\}$, receiving that outcome.
>
> The problem is to determine the strategy that maximizes expected winnings.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the value of continuing
>
> If you continue, your payoff is the outcome of a fair $12$-sided die, say $Y \in \{1,2,\dots,12\}$.
>
> Its expected value is
>
> $$
> E[Y] = \frac{1+2+\cdots+12}{12}
> = \frac{12 \cdot 13 / 2}{12}
> = \frac{13}{2}
> = 6.5.
> $$
>
> So, if you choose to continue, your expected payoff is always $6.5$, regardless of the first roll.
>
> ## Step 2: Compare stopping with continuing
>
> After seeing the first roll $X=x$:
>
> - If you **stop**, you get $x$ for sure.
> - If you **continue**, your expected gain is $6.5$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - stop if $x > 6.5$,
> - continue if $x < 6.5$,
> - either choice is equivalent if $x = 6.5$, which never occurs since $x$ is an integer.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - for $x=1,2,3,4,5,6$, continue;
> - for $x=7,8$, stop.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the expected value under this strategy
>
> The optimal payoff is
>
> $$
> \max(X, 6.5)
> $$
>
> in expectation, since after seeing $X$ you take the better of stopping at $X$ or continuing for expected value $6.5$.
>
> Because $X$ is uniform on $\{1,\dots,8\}$,
>
> $$
> E[\text{optimal payoff}]
> = \frac{1}{8}\left(6.5+6.5+6.5+6.5+6.5+6.5+7+8\right).
> $$
>
> Now compute:
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 6.5 = 39,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> E[\text{optimal payoff}] = \frac{39+7+8}{8} = \frac{54}{8} = 6.75.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> - **Roll the second die if the first die shows $1,2,3,4,5$, or $6$.**
> - **Stop if the first die shows $7$ or $8$.**
>
> With this strategy, the expected winning is
>
> $$
> 6.75.
> $$
