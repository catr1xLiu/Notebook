> [!example] Question 304 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Intern Interview

An escalator travels upwards at X steps per second. You walk up the escalator at Y steps per second, and the escalator is Z steps tall. How long will it take you to reach the top?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=305)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $t$ be the time, in seconds, required to reach the top.
>
> Since both you and the escalator move upward, your total upward speed relative to the ground is
>
> $$
> X + Y \quad \text{steps/second}.
> $$
>
> The escalator has height
>
> $$
> Z \quad \text{steps}.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> t = \frac{Z}{X+Y}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the time to reach the top is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{Z}{X+Y} \text{ seconds}}.
> $$
