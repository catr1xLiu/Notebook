> [!example] Question 110 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected number of rolls of an n-sided die required so that the cumulative total first exceeds n?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=111)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $T$ be the number of rolls needed for the running total to first exceed $n$, where each roll is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$.
>
> The natural quantity to compute is the expected value $E[T]$.
>
> ## Step 1: Express $E[T]$ using tail probabilities
>
> For any positive-integer-valued random variable,
> $$
> E[T] = \sum_{k\ge 0} P(T > k).
> $$
>
> Here, $T > k$ means that after $k$ rolls, the total is still at most $n$.
>
> Let $X_1,X_2,\dots$ be independent rolls, each uniform on $\{1,\dots,n\}$, and let
> $$
> S_k = X_1+\cdots+X_k.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> P(T > k) = P(S_k \le n).
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E[T] = \sum_{k\ge 0} P(S_k \le n).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Count the sequences with sum at most $n$
>
> For fixed $k\ge 1$, we must count the number of $k$-tuples $(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ with each $x_i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ and
> $$
> x_1+\cdots+x_k \le n.
> $$
>
> Since the sum is at most $n$, the upper bound $x_i \le n$ is automatic. So this is just the number of compositions into $k$ positive parts with total at most $n$.
>
> The number of positive integer solutions to
> $$
> x_1+\cdots+x_k = m
> $$
> is
> $$
> \binom{m-1}{k-1}.
> $$
> Summing over $m=k,k+1,\dots,n$,
> $$
> \#\{(x_1,\dots,x_k): x_i\ge 1,\ x_1+\cdots+x_k\le n\}
> = \sum_{m=k}^n \binom{m-1}{k-1}
> = \binom{n}{k},
> $$
> by the hockey-stick identity.
>
> Since there are $n^k$ total possible outcomes of $k$ rolls,
> $$
> P(S_k \le n) = \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{n^k}.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> P(T>0)=1.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> E[T] = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{n^k}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Final formula
>
> Thus the expected number of rolls needed to exceed a total of $n$ is
> $$
> \boxed{E[T] = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{n^k}}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
> $$
> \boxed{E[T] = 1 + \frac{\binom{n}{1}}{n} + \frac{\binom{n}{2}}{n^2} + \cdots + \frac{\binom{n}{n}}{n^n}}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Asymptotic behavior
>
> As $n\to\infty$,
> $$
> \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{n^k} \to \frac{1}{k!}
> $$
> for each fixed $k$, so
> $$
> E[T] \to \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{1}{k!} = e.
> $$
>
> So for large $n$, the expected number of rolls is approximately
> $$
> \boxed{e \approx 2.71828}.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - **Exact expectation**:
>   $$
>   \boxed{\displaystyle E[T] = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{\binom{n}{k}}{n^k}}
>   $$
> - **For large $n$**:
>   $$
>   \boxed{E[T] \approx e}
>   $$
