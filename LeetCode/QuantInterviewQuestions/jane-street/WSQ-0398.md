> [!example] Question 398 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You flip 4 fair coins and receive x dollars, where x is the square of the number of heads. What are your expected winnings?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=399)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads obtained when $4$ fair coins are flipped. Then
>
> $$
> H \sim \text{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> The winnings are
>
> $$
> X = H^2.
> $$
>
> So the expected winnings are
>
> $$
> E[X] = E[H^2].
> $$
>
> Compute this by summing over all possible values of $H$:
>
> $$
> E[H^2] = \sum_{k=0}^4 k^2 P(H=k).
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> P(H=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^4=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[H^2]
> = \frac{1}{16}\left(0^2\binom{4}{0}+1^2\binom{4}{1}+2^2\binom{4}{2}+3^2\binom{4}{3}+4^2\binom{4}{4}\right).
> $$
>
> Now substitute the binomial coefficients:
>
> $$
> E[H^2]
> = \frac{1}{16}\left(0+1\cdot 4+4\cdot 6+9\cdot 4+16\cdot 1\right).
> $$
>
> $$
> E[H^2]
> = \frac{1}{16}(4+24+36+16)
> = \frac{80}{16}
> = 5.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected winnings are
>
> $$
> \boxed{5 \text{ dollars}}.
> $$
