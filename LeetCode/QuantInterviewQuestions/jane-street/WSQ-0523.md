> [!example] Question 523 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You are waiting for a bus which takes a round trip of 10 minutes to complete (starting again when it finishes one tour). What is your expected waiting time if you arrive at a random time (uniformly from 0 to 10 minutes)? Next, consider that there is an ice cream shop somewhere on the bus route; every time the bus passes it, the driver flips a fair coin and, if heads, spends 10 minutes eating ice cream before continuing the tour. What is your expected waiting time now? How does the answer change if the coin is biased?

> ## 1. NYC to San Francisco: shortest tunnel through the Earth vs. surface distance
>
> Interpret the two cities as points on the Earth’s surface.
>
> - The **shortest path on the surface** is the great-circle arc.
> - The **shortest tunnel through the Earth** is the straight-line chord connecting the two points.
>
> Use approximate coordinates:
>
> - New York City: $(40.71^\circ\text{N},\,74.01^\circ\text{W})$
> - San Francisco: $(37.77^\circ\text{N},\,122.42^\circ\text{W})$
>
> Take Earth’s mean radius as
>
> $$
> R \approx 6371\ \text{km}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 1: Central angle between the cities
>
> Using the spherical law of cosines,
>
> $$
> \cos \theta
> =
> \sin \phi_1 \sin \phi_2
> +
> \cos \phi_1 \cos \phi_2 \cos(\Delta \lambda),
> $$
>
> where
>
> - $\phi_1 = 40.71^\circ$,
> - $\phi_2 = 37.77^\circ$,
> - $\Delta \lambda = 122.42^\circ - 74.01^\circ = 48.41^\circ$.
>
> This gives approximately
>
> $$
> \theta \approx 0.649\ \text{rad}
> \quad
> (\approx 37.2^\circ).
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Surface distance
>
> The great-circle distance is
>
> $$
> s = R\theta \approx 6371 \cdot 0.649 \approx 4130\ \text{km}.
> $$
>
> So the direct surface distance is about
>
> $$
> \boxed{4130\ \text{km}}
> \qquad
> (\boxed{2565\ \text{mi}}).
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Straight tunnel through the Earth
>
> The chord length is
>
> $$
> c = 2R\sin\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right).
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> c \approx 2(6371)\sin(0.649/2)
> \approx 4060\ \text{km}.
> $$
>
> So the shortest tunnel is about
>
> $$
> \boxed{4060\ \text{km}}
> \qquad
> (\boxed{2520\ \text{mi}}).
> $$
>
> ### Step 4: Confidence bound / uncertainty
>
> If one uses city centers, Earth radius approximations, and ignores elevation/route-definition details, a reasonable uncertainty is on the order of a few tens of kilometers. A practical bound is
>
> - surface distance: $\pm 50\ \text{km}$,
> - tunnel distance: $\pm 50\ \text{km}$.
>
> Hence:
>
> $$
> \boxed{4130 \pm 50\ \text{km}}
> \quad\text{(surface)}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \boxed{4060 \pm 50\ \text{km}}
> \quad\text{(straight tunnel)}.
> $$
>

> ## 2. Expected waiting time for a bus with deterministic $10$-minute loop
>
> Let the bus complete one full loop every $10$ minutes and immediately start again. If arrival time is uniform on $[0,10]$, and exactly one bus passes each point once per loop, then the time since the last bus passed is uniform on $[0,10]$, so the time until the next bus is also uniform on $[0,10]$ reflected.
>
> Thus the expected waiting time is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W] = \frac{10}{2} = \boxed{5\ \text{minutes}}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Extension: bus may stop for ice cream
>
> Assume the bus passes the ice cream shop once per loop. Each time it passes, it flips a coin:
>
> - with probability $p$, it stops for $10$ extra minutes;
> - with probability $1-p$, it does not stop.
>
> The fair-coin case is $p=\tfrac12$.
>
> Let the time between two successive visits of the bus to your stop be the random variable $L$.
>
> Without ice cream, one loop takes $10$ minutes. With the possible stop,
>
> $$
> L =
> \begin{cases}
> 10, & \text{with probability } 1-p,\\[4pt]
> 20, & \text{with probability } p.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[L] = 10(1-p)+20p = 10+10p.
> $$
>
> However, if you arrive at a random time in the long-run stationary sense, the expected waiting time is **not** simply $\mathbb{E}[L]/2$, because longer gaps are more likely to contain your arrival. This is the inspection paradox / renewal-theory effect.
>
> For a renewal process with interval length $L$, the expected residual waiting time is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[L^2]}{2\mathbb{E}[L]}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 1: Compute $\mathbb{E}[L^2]$
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[L^2]
> =
> 10^2(1-p)+20^2p
> =
> 100(1-p)+400p
> =
> 100+300p.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Apply the formula
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W]
> =
> \frac{100+300p}{2(10+10p)}
> =
> \frac{100+300p}{20(1+p)}
> =
> 5\,\frac{1+3p}{1+p}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, for a biased coin with probability $p$ of stopping:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[W] = 5\,\frac{1+3p}{1+p}\ \text{minutes}}.
> $$
>
> ### Fair coin case
>
> Set $p=\tfrac12$:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W]
> =
> 5\,\frac{1+3/2}{1+1/2}
> =
> 5\,\frac{5/2}{3/2}
> =
> 5\cdot \frac{5}{3}
> =
> \frac{25}{3}.
> $$
>
> So for a fair coin,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[W] = \frac{25}{3}\ \text{minutes} \approx 8.33\ \text{minutes}}.
> $$
>
> ### Checks
>
> - If $p=0$, then no ice cream stops:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W]=5,
> $$
>
> as expected.
>
> - If $p=1$, every loop has the extra stop, so every interval is $20$ minutes:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W]=10,
> $$
>
> also as expected.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> 1. NYC to San Francisco:
>
> - shortest tunnel through the Earth:
>
> $$
> \boxed{4060 \pm 50\ \text{km}}
> \quad
> (\boxed{2520 \pm 30\ \text{mi}})
> $$
>
> - direct surface great-circle distance:
>
> $$
> \boxed{4130 \pm 50\ \text{km}}
> \quad
> (\boxed{2565 \pm 30\ \text{mi}})
> $$
>
> 2. Bus with fixed $10$-minute loop:
>
> $$
> \boxed{5\ \text{minutes}}
> $$
>
> 3. Bus with ice-cream stop of $10$ minutes, stopping with probability $p$ each loop:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[W] = 5\,\frac{1+3p}{1+p}\ \text{minutes}}
> $$
>
> In particular, for a fair coin $p=\tfrac12$:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{25}{3}\ \text{minutes} \approx 8.33\ \text{minutes}}.
> $$
