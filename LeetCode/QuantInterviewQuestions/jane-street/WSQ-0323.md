> [!example] Question 323 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You are given a deck of cards numbered 1 to 100 and two boxes. For each card, flip a fair coin: place the card in the left box if heads, right box if tails. Find the expected value of the minimum card in the box that contains card 100.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=324)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $M$ denote the minimum card in the box that contains card $100$.
>
> We compute $\mathbb{E}[M]$ by finding the distribution of $M$.
>
> ## Step 1: Characterize the event $M = k$
>
> Fix $k \in \{1,2,\dots,100\}$.
>
> For the minimum in the box containing card $100$ to be exactly $k$:
>
> - card $k$ must be in the same box as card $100$,
> - every card $1,2,\dots,k-1$ must be in the opposite box,
> - cards $k+1,\dots,99$ may be anywhere.
>
> Because each card is independently assigned left/right with probability $\tfrac12$, relative to the box containing card $100$ each other card independently:
> - matches card $100$'s box with probability $\tfrac12$,
> - goes to the opposite box with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> \Pr(M = k) = \left(\frac12\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac12\right) = \left(\frac12\right)^k
> \quad \text{for } k=1,2,\dots,99.
> $$
>
> For $k=100$, this means cards $1,2,\dots,99$ are all in the opposite box, so
> $$
> \Pr(M=100)=\left(\frac12\right)^{99}.
> $$
>
> These probabilities sum correctly:
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^{99}\left(\frac12\right)^k + \left(\frac12\right)^{99}
> = \left(1-\left(\frac12\right)^{99}\right) + \left(\frac12\right)^{99}
> = 1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expectation
>
> So
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \sum_{k=1}^{99} k\left(\frac12\right)^k
> +
> 100\left(\frac12\right)^{99}.
> $$
>
> Use the finite sum formula
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^{n} kx^k
> =
> \frac{x\bigl(1-(n+1)x^n+nx^{n+1}\bigr)}{(1-x)^2}.
> $$
>
> With $x=\tfrac12$ and $n=99$,
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^{99} k\left(\frac12\right)^k
> =
> \frac{\frac12\left(1-100\left(\frac12\right)^{99}+99\left(\frac12\right)^{100}\right)}{\left(\frac12\right)^2}
> =
> 2\left(1-100\left(\frac12\right)^{99}+99\left(\frac12\right)^{100}\right).
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> 99\left(\frac12\right)^{100}
> =
> \frac{99}{2}\left(\frac12\right)^{99},
> $$
> this becomes
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^{99} k\left(\frac12\right)^k
> =
> 2 - 200\left(\frac12\right)^{99} + 99\left(\frac12\right)^{99}
> =
> 2 - 101\left(\frac12\right)^{99}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \left(2 - 101\left(\frac12\right)^{99}\right)
> +
> 100\left(\frac12\right)^{99}
> =
> 2 - \left(\frac12\right)^{99}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[M] = 2 - 2^{-99}}.
> $$
