> [!example] Question 73 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the smallest number whose digits multiply to 10,000?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=74)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make the product of the digits equal to $10{,}000$, first factorize:
>
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 10^4 = 2^4 \cdot 5^4.
> $$
>
> Each digit must be an integer from $1$ to $9$ (no digit can be $0$, since that would make the product $0$).
>
> Only the digit $5$ among $1$ through $9$ carries a factor of $5$, and it carries exactly one such factor. So to produce $5^4$ we need exactly four digits equal to $5$, no more and no fewer: fewer than four would leave a missing factor of $5$, and there is no way to make up a factor of $5$ with any other digit.
>
> The remaining digits (besides the four $5$s) must multiply to
>
> $$
> \frac{10{,}000}{5^4} = 2^4 = 16,
> $$
>
> using digits whose only prime factor is $2$, namely digits from $\{1,2,4,8\}$ (any digit involving another prime, such as $3,6,7,9$, would introduce a prime factor not present in $10{,}000$). Including a digit $1$ never helps, since it adds an extra digit without contributing to the product, making the resulting number longer and hence larger. So we look for the fewest digits from $\{2,4,8\}$ whose product is $16$.
>
> A single digit cannot equal $16$, since the largest digit is $9$. With two digits, the possibilities are
>
> $$
> 2\cdot 8=16, \qquad 4\cdot 4=16,
> $$
>
> and these are the only ways to write $16$ as a product of two digits from $\{2,4,8\}$. No fewer than two extra digits can work, so the minimum total number of digits is
>
> $$
> 4+2=6.
> $$
>
> This leaves exactly two candidate digit multisets for the smallest number:
>
> $$
> \{2,5,5,5,5,8\} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \{4,4,5,5,5,5\}.
> $$
>
> Both have six digits and both have product $10{,}000$:
>
> $$
> 2\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 8 = 16\cdot 625 = 10{,}000, \qquad 4\cdot 4\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5 = 16\cdot 625 = 10{,}000.
> $$
>
> Since both candidates have the same number of digits, the smallest resulting number is found by sorting each multiset in increasing order and comparing:
>
> $$
> 255558 \qquad \text{versus} \qquad 445555.
> $$
>
> Comparing the leading digits, $2<4$, so
>
> $$
> 255558 < 445555.
> $$
>
> Therefore the smallest number whose digits multiply to $10{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{255558}.
> $$
