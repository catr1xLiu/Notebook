> [!example] Question 96 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

Estimate the number of commercial airplanes purchased in the United States each year.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=97)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Estimating how many commercial planes the U.S. purchases each year
>
> A Fermi question is answered by breaking the problem into a few reasonable estimates and combining them.
>
> ### Step 1: Estimate the size of the U.S. commercial fleet
>
> A good starting point is the number of active commercial passenger aircraft operated by U.S. airlines.
>
> A rough estimate is that the U.S. has on the order of a few thousand commercial planes. A reasonable round number is
>
> $$
> N \approx 7{,}000 \text{ to } 8{,}000
> $$
>
> Take
>
> $$
> N \approx 7{,}500
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Estimate how often planes are replaced
>
> Commercial aircraft are expensive long-lived assets. Airlines typically keep them for about
>
> $$
> 20 \text{ to } 25 \text{ years}
> $$
>
> Use an average replacement cycle of
>
> $$
> L \approx 20 \text{ years}
> $$
>
> If the fleet size is roughly stable, then annual purchases just to replace old aircraft are approximately
>
> $$
> \frac{N}{L} \approx \frac{7{,}500}{20} = 375
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Adjust for growth and market fluctuations
>
> The fleet is not perfectly constant. Some years airlines expand, some years they retire planes without replacing them immediately, and some purchases are for growth rather than replacement.
>
> A modest growth adjustment might add roughly
>
> $$
> 50 \text{ to } 100
> $$
>
> planes per year.
>
> So a reasonable total estimate is
>
> $$
> 375 + 75 \approx 450
> $$
>
> ### Step 4: State the Fermi estimate
>
> Thus, a plausible Fermi answer is that the U.S. purchases on the order of
>
> $$
> \boxed{400 \text{ to } 500 \text{ commercial planes per year}}
> $$
>
> A clean interview-style answer would be:
>
> The U.S. probably buys about $500$ commercial planes per year. I’d estimate a commercial fleet of roughly $7{,}500$ aircraft and a replacement cycle of about $20$ years, giving about $375$ annual replacement purchases, then add some amount for growth and fleet modernization to get roughly $400$–$500$ per year.
>
> So the best single-number estimate is
>
> $$
> \boxed{450 \text{ planes per year}}
> $$
