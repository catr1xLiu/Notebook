> [!example] Question 90 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

Suppose there is a game where you flip a fair coin repeatedly until you see two consecutive heads. What is the expected number of coin flips required? Follow-up: What is the expected number of tails observed in this process?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=91)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Assume the coin is fair.
>
> ### 1. Expected number of flips until two consecutive heads
>
> Let
>
> - $E_0$ be the expected additional number of flips starting from the initial state, where no relevant previous head is being carried.
> - $E_1$ be the expected additional number of flips given that the most recent flip was a head, so one more head ends the game.
>
> We now write equations by conditioning on the next flip.
>
> #### State $E_0$
>
> From state $E_0$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $H$, and we move to state $E_1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $T$, and we remain in state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> #### State $E_1$
>
> From state $E_1$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $H$, and the game ends;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $T$, and we return to state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute this into the first equation:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
> So
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0 + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \frac14 E_0 = \frac32,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_0 = 6.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected number of flips is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Expected number of tails
>
> Let
>
> - $T_0$ be the expected number of future tails starting from the initial state;
> - $T_1$ be the expected number of future tails given that the most recent flip was a head.
>
> Again condition on the next flip.
>
> #### State $T_0$
>
> From state $T_0$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $H$, contributing $0$ tails immediately and moving to $T_1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $T$, contributing $1$ tail immediately and remaining in $T_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> T_0 = \frac12 T_1 + \frac12(1 + T_0).
> $$
>
> #### State $T_1$
>
> From state $T_1$:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $H$, and the game ends with no more tails;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the next flip is $T$, contributing $1$ tail and returning to $T_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> T_1 = \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12(1 + T_0).
> $$
>
> Substitute into the first equation:
> $$
> T_0 = \frac12 \cdot \frac12(1 + T_0) + \frac12(1 + T_0)
> = \frac14(1 + T_0) + \frac12(1 + T_0)
> = \frac34(1 + T_0).
> $$
> So
> $$
> T_0 = \frac34 + \frac34 T_0,
> $$
> which gives
> $$
> \frac14 T_0 = \frac34,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> T_0 = 3.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected number of tails is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}.
> $$
>
> ### Final answers
>
> - Expected number of flips until two consecutive heads: $\boxed{6}$.
> - Expected number of tails: $\boxed{3}$.
