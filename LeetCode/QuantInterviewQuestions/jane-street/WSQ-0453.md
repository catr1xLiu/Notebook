> [!example] Question 453 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Imagine I flip 100 coins in a sequence, and you need to guess the sequence in one try. You are allowed to ask one yes/no question. What do you ask to maximize the probability of guessing the sequence?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=454)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the unknown outcome be a binary string $x \in \{H,T\}^{100}$. Before guessing the full sequence, one yes/no question may be asked, and then exactly one sequence must be guessed.
>
> The objective is to maximize the probability of a correct guess.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> A yes/no question partitions the set of all $2^{100}$ possible sequences into two subsets:
>
> - those for which the answer is “yes,”
> - those for which the answer is “no.”
>
> After hearing the answer, one should guess the most likely sequence within the corresponding subset. Since all $2^{100}$ sequences are equally likely, the best one can do in each subset is to choose any one sequence from that subset, yielding success probability
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2^{100}}
> $$
>
> conditional on landing in that chosen sequence.
>
> Thus, if the “yes” subset has size $a$ and the “no” subset has size $2^{100}-a$, then after receiving the answer and making the optimal guess, the total success probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{a}{2^{100}} \cdot \frac{1}{a} \;+\; \frac{2^{100}-a}{2^{100}} \cdot \frac{1}{2^{100}-a}
> \;=\;
> \frac{1}{2^{100}} + \frac{1}{2^{100}}
> \;=\;
> \frac{2}{2^{100}}
> \;=\;
> \frac{1}{2^{99}},
> $$
>
> provided both subsets are nonempty.
>
> So any nontrivial yes/no question can at best double the success probability from
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2^{100}}
> \quad\text{to}\quad
> \frac{1}{2^{99}}.
> $$
>
> ## What question should be asked?
>
> To achieve this bound, ask any question that splits the set of possible sequences into two nonempty classes. The simplest choice is:
>
> $$
> \text{“Is the first coin flip heads?”}
> $$
>
> If the answer is “yes,” guess in advance some fixed sequence beginning with $H$, for example
>
> $$
> HHH\cdots H.
> $$
>
> If the answer is “no,” guess some fixed sequence beginning with $T$, for example
>
> $$
> THH\cdots H.
> $$
>
> There are $2^{99}$ sequences consistent with each answer, and exactly one guessed sequence in each class, so the success probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2^{99}}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> An optimal question is any yes/no question that partitions the $2^{100}$ possible sequences into two nonempty sets; for instance,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{“Is the first flip heads?”}}
> $$
>
> This yields the maximum possible probability of a correct guess:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2^{99}}}.
> $$
