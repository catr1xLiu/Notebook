> [!example] Question 15 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given 3 black and 2 white balls in a bag, you may take out several balls and stop whenever you choose. How should you determine when to stop in order to maximize the expected value of the number of black balls minus the number of white balls (b - w) you draw?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=16)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $B$ denote the number of black balls drawn and $W$ the number of white balls drawn. The objective is to choose a stopping rule that maximizes
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[B-W].
> $$
>
> Since the order of the balls is random, this is an optimal stopping problem on a finite horizon.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. State description
>
> At any stage, suppose there are $b$ black balls and $w$ white balls left in the bag. Let
> $$
> V(b,w)
> $$
> be the maximum expected future reward from that state.
>
> If we stop immediately, the future reward is $0$.
>
> If we continue, then:
>
> - with probability $\dfrac{b}{b+w}$, the next ball is black, giving immediate reward $+1$, and the new state is $(b-1,w)$;
> - with probability $\dfrac{w}{b+w}$, the next ball is white, giving immediate reward $-1$, and the new state is $(b,w-1)$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> V(b,w)=\max\left\{0,\; \frac{b}{b+w}\bigl(1+V(b-1,w)\bigr)+\frac{w}{b+w}\bigl(-1+V(b,w-1)\bigr)\right\}.
> $$
>
> Boundary conditions:
>
> - if $w=0$, every remaining ball is black, so we should take all of them:
>   $$
>   V(b,0)=b;
>   $$
> - if $b=0$, every remaining ball is white, so we stop:
>   $$
>   V(0,w)=0.
>   $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Compute the values for $(3,2)$
>
> We work upward from smaller states.
>
> ### States with one color absent
> $$
> V(0,1)=V(0,2)=0,\qquad V(1,0)=1,\;V(2,0)=2,\;V(3,0)=3.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,1)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac12(1+V(0,1))+\frac12(-1+V(1,0))
> =
> \frac12(1+0)+\frac12(-1+1)=\frac12.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> V(1,1)=\max\{0,\tfrac12\}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,2)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac13(1+V(0,2))+\frac23(-1+V(1,1))
> =
> \frac13(1+0)+\frac23\left(-1+\frac12\right)
> =
> \frac13-\frac13=0.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V(1,2)=0.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,1)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac23(1+V(1,1))+\frac13(-1+V(2,0))
> =
> \frac23\left(1+\frac12\right)+\frac13(-1+2)
> =
> 1+\frac13=\frac43.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> V(2,1)=\frac43.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,2)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac12(1+V(1,2))+\frac12(-1+V(2,1))
> =
> \frac12(1+0)+\frac12\left(-1+\frac43\right)
> =
> \frac12+\frac16=\frac23.
> $$
> So
> $$
> V(2,2)=\frac23.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,1)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac34(1+V(2,1))+\frac14(-1+V(3,0))
> =
> \frac34\left(1+\frac43\right)+\frac14(2)
> =
> \frac74+\frac12=\frac94.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> V(3,1)=\frac94.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,2)$
> Continue value:
> $$
> \frac35(1+V(2,2))+\frac25(-1+V(3,1))
> =
> \frac35\left(1+\frac23\right)+\frac25\left(-1+\frac94\right).
> $$
> Compute:
> $$
> \frac35\cdot\frac53=1,\qquad \frac25\cdot\frac54=\frac12.
> $$
> So the continue value is
> $$
> 1+\frac12=\frac32.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V(3,2)=\frac32.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Optimal stopping rule for $3$ black and $2$ white
>
> At each state, continue exactly when the continuation value is positive.
>
> From the computations:
>
> - $V(1,2)=0$, so at state $(1,2)$ one is indifferent; stopping is optimal;
> - at all other reachable states with at least one black ball left, the continuation value is positive.
>
> Thus the optimal rule is:
>
> - **keep drawing unless the remaining bag contains $1$ black and $2$ white, or contains no black balls;**
> - equivalently, in terms of balls already drawn, **stop exactly when the undrawn balls are in a state where continuing has nonpositive value.**
>
> Starting from $(3,2)$, this means:
>
> - draw at the beginning;
> - after each draw, recompute the remaining numbers $(b,w)$;
> - stop if $(b,w)=(1,2)$ or $b=0$;
> - otherwise continue.
>
> The maximal expected value is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac32}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Generalization
>
> For a bag with $b$ black balls and $w$ white balls, the same dynamic programming argument gives
>
> $$
> V(b,w)=\max\left\{0,\; \frac{b}{b+w}\bigl(1+V(b-1,w)\bigr)+\frac{w}{b+w}\bigl(-1+V(b,w-1)\bigr)\right\},
> $$
> with boundary conditions
> $$
> V(b,0)=b,\qquad V(0,w)=0.
> $$
>
> This recursion completely determines the optimal strategy:
>
> - at state $(b,w)$, **continue** iff
>   $$
>   \frac{b}{b+w}\bigl(1+V(b-1,w)\bigr)+\frac{w}{b+w}\bigl(-1+V(b,w-1)\bigr)>0;
>   $$
> - otherwise **stop**.
>
> So the hard general problem reduces to computing the value function $V(b,w)$ by backward induction.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> For the bag with $3$ black and $2$ white balls, the optimal stopping rule is to continue drawing except when the remaining balls are $(1,2)$ or $(0,w)$. The maximal expected value of $B-W$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac32}.
> $$
>
> In general, if $V(b,w)$ denotes the optimal expected future value when $b$ black and $w$ white balls remain, then
> $$
> V(b,w)=\max\left\{0,\; \frac{b}{b+w}\bigl(1+V(b-1,w)\bigr)+\frac{w}{b+w}\bigl(-1+V(b,w-1)\bigr)\right\},
> $$
> with
> $$
> V(b,0)=b,\qquad V(0,w)=0.
> $$
> The optimal strategy is to continue exactly in those states where the continuation term is positive.
