> [!example] Question 587 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

An apple costs \$0.33. How many apples can you buy with \$1? What is the change if you buy apples with \$5?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=588)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the price of one apple be $\text{\textdollar}0.33$.
>
> The number of apples purchasable with a given amount is the whole-number part of
>
> $$
> \frac{\text{money}}{0.33}.
> $$
>
> ## With $\text{\textdollar}1$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \frac{1.00}{0.33} \approx 3.03.
> $$
>
> So you can buy
>
> $$
> 3
> $$
>
> apples.
>
> The cost of $3$ apples is
>
> $$
> 3 \times 0.33 = 0.99.
> $$
>
> Thus the change from $\text{\textdollar}1$ is
>
> $$
> 1.00 - 0.99 = 0.01.
> $$
>
> ## With $\text{\textdollar}5$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \frac{5.00}{0.33} \approx 15.15.
> $$
>
> So you can buy
>
> $$
> 15
> $$
>
> apples.
>
> The cost of $15$ apples is
>
> $$
> 15 \times 0.33 = 4.95.
> $$
>
> Thus the change from $\text{\textdollar}5$ is
>
> $$
> 5.00 - 4.95 = 0.05.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> With $\text{\textdollar}1$, you can buy $3$ apples and receive $\text{\textdollar}0.01$ in change.
>
> With $\text{\textdollar}5$, you can buy $15$ apples and receive $\text{\textdollar}0.05$ in change.
