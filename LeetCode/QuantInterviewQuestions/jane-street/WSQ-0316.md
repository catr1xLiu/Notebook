> [!example] Question 316 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

In a game where a fair coin is tossed repeatedly, player A wins if the sequence HTT appears first, and player B wins if the sequence TTH appears first. Is this game fair?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=317)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine whether the game is fair, compute the probability that each pattern appears first in an ongoing sequence of fair coin tosses.
>
> Let
>
> - $A =$ pattern $HTT$,
> - $B =$ pattern $TTH$.
>
> We seek
> $$
> P(\text{$A$ appears before $B$})
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> P(\text{$B$ appears before $A$}).
> $$
>
> If these probabilities are equal, the game is fair.
>
> ## State-based analysis
>
> Track the relevant suffix of the current toss sequence, namely the longest suffix that is also a prefix of either target pattern.
>
> The useful states are:
>
> - $S$: start state,
> - $H$: last symbol is $H$,
> - $T$: last symbol is $T$,
> - $HT$: last two symbols are $HT$,
> - $TT$: last two symbols are $TT$.
>
> Let $p_X$ denote the probability that $A=HTT$ occurs before $B=TTH$, given that the current state is $X$.
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ### Equations
>
> From the start state:
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_T.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - tossing $H$ leaves the relevant suffix $H$,
> - tossing $T$ gives state $HT$.
>
> So
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $T$:
>
> - tossing $H$ gives state $H$,
> - tossing $T$ gives state $TT$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_T=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_{TT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - tossing $T$ completes $HTT$, so $A$ wins,
> - tossing $H$ leaves suffix $H$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 \cdot 1.
> $$
>
> From state $TT$:
>
> - tossing $H$ completes $TTH$, so $B$ wins and $A$ loses,
> - tossing $T$ leaves state $TT$.
>
> Therefore
> $$
> p_{TT}=\frac12 \cdot 0+\frac12 p_{TT}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> p_{TT}=\frac12 p_{TT}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> p_{TT}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_{HT},
> $$
> we get
> $$
> p_H=p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> Using
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12 p_H+\frac12,
> $$
> substitute $p_{HT}=p_H$:
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_H+\frac12,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \frac12 p_H=\frac12
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> p_H=1.
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> p_{HT}=1.
> $$
>
> Next,
> $$
> p_T=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_{TT}
> =\frac12 \cdot 1+\frac12 \cdot 0
> =\frac12.
> $$
>
> Finally,
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_T
> =\frac12 \cdot 1+\frac12 \cdot \frac12
> =\frac34.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(\text{$HTT$ occurs before $TTH$})=\frac34,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(\text{$TTH$ occurs before $HTT$})=\frac14.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The game is **not fair**. Pattern $HTT$ wins with probability
>
> $$
> \frac34,
> $$
>
> while pattern $TTH$ wins with probability
>
> $$
> \frac14.
> $$
>
> So player $A$ has a substantial advantage.
