> [!example] Question 324 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Player A rolls three 6-sided dice. Player B rolls one 20-sided die. The player with the highest score wins. Which player would you prefer to be, and why?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=325)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be Player A's total from rolling $3$ fair $6$-sided dice, and let $Y$ be Player B's result from rolling one fair $20$-sided die.
>
> We compare
> $$
> P(X>Y),\qquad P(Y>X),\qquad P(X=Y).
> $$
> Since ties are possible, the better choice is the player with the larger win probability.
>
> ## 1. Distribution of X
>
> The number of ways for three dice to sum to each value from $3$ to $18$ is:
>
> - sum $3$: $1$ way
> - sum $4$: $3$ ways
> - sum $5$: $6$ ways
> - sum $6$: $10$ ways
> - sum $7$: $15$ ways
> - sum $8$: $21$ ways
> - sum $9$: $25$ ways
> - sum $10$: $27$ ways
> - sum $11$: $27$ ways
> - sum $12$: $25$ ways
> - sum $13$: $21$ ways
> - sum $14$: $15$ ways
> - sum $15$: $10$ ways
> - sum $16$: $6$ ways
> - sum $17$: $3$ ways
> - sum $18$: $1$ way
>
> Since there are $6^3=216$ outcomes,
> $$
> P(X=x)=\frac{\#(x)}{216}.
> $$
>
> Also, $Y$ is uniform on $\{1,2,\dots,20\}$, so
> $$
> P(Y=y)=\frac{1}{20}.
> $$
>
> Because $X$ ranges only from $3$ to $18$, Player A can never beat $Y=19$ or $Y=20$.
>
> ## 2. Compute Player A's win probability
>
> Condition on $Y=y$:
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\sum_{y=1}^{20} P(Y=y)\,P(X>y)
> = \frac{1}{20}\sum_{y=1}^{20} P(X>y).
> $$
>
> It is convenient to use cumulative counts for $X$. Writing $\#(X\le y)$ for the number of the $216$ outcomes with sum at most $y$:
>
> - $y=1,2$: $\#(X\le y)=0$
> - $y=3$: $1$
> - $y=4$: $4$
> - $y=5$: $10$
> - $y=6$: $20$
> - $y=7$: $35$
> - $y=8$: $56$
> - $y=9$: $81$
> - $y=10$: $108$
> - $y=11$: $135$
> - $y=12$: $160$
> - $y=13$: $181$
> - $y=14$: $196$
> - $y=15$: $206$
> - $y=16$: $212$
> - $y=17$: $215$
> - $y=18,19,20$: $216$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(X>y)=1-P(X\le y)=\frac{216-\#(X\le y)}{216}.
> $$
>
> So the counts for $X>y$, for $y=1,\dots,20$, are $216,216,215,212,206,196,181,160,135,108,81,56,35,20,10,4,1,0,0,0$.
>
> Their sum is
> $$
> 216+216+215+212+206+196+181+160+135+108+81+56+35+20+10+4+1=2052.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\frac{1}{20}\cdot \frac{2052}{216}
> =\frac{2052}{4320}
> =\frac{19}{40}
> =0.475.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Compute tie probability
>
> A tie can occur only when $Y$ is between $3$ and $18$:
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\sum_{k=3}^{18} P(X=k)P(Y=k)
> =\sum_{k=3}^{18} \frac{\#(k)}{216}\cdot \frac{1}{20}.
> $$
>
> Since the counts sum to $216$,
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\frac{1}{20}\cdot \frac{216}{216}
> =\frac{1}{20}
> =0.05.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Compare win probabilities
>
> Since
> $$
> P(Y>X)=1-P(X>Y)-P(X=Y),
> $$
> we get
> $$
> P(Y>X)=1-\frac{19}{40}-\frac{1}{20}
> =1-\frac{19}{40}-\frac{2}{40}
> =\frac{19}{40}
> =0.475.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\frac{19}{40}=P(Y>X).
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The two players have exactly the same probability of winning:
> $$
> P(\text{A wins})=P(\text{B wins})=\frac{19}{40}=0.475,
> $$
> with tie probability
> $$
> P(\text{tie})=\frac{1}{20}=0.05.
> $$
>
> The game is exactly fair, so there is no preference between the two players — either seat gives the same chance of winning.
