> [!example] Question 637 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You toss four coins in the air with the goal of maximizing the number of heads. After the first toss, you have the option to toss any subset of the coins again. What is the expected number of heads in this game, assuming you play optimally?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=638)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each of the four coins is tossed once, and then you may choose **any subset** of the coins to toss again — none of them, all of them, or anything in between. The goal is to maximize the expected number of heads showing at the end.
>
> ## 1. The decision is separate for each coin
>
> Because the final count of heads is just the sum of four separate $0/1$ indicators (one per coin), and the decision of whether to re-toss a given coin can be made **independently** for each coin once its first result is known, the optimal strategy simply maximizes the expected outcome of each coin on its own.
>
> For a single coin, after seeing its first result there are exactly two options:
>
> - **Keep** the current result.
> - **Re-toss** that one coin.
>
> If the coin already shows heads, keeping it guarantees $1$; re-tossing it gives only a $\tfrac12$ chance of a head, an expected value of $\tfrac12$. Since $1>\tfrac12$, it is always better to keep a head.
>
> If the coin shows tails, keeping it guarantees $0$; re-tossing it gives expected value $\tfrac12$. Since $\tfrac12>0$, it is always better to re-toss a tail.
>
> So the optimal strategy is: after the first toss, keep every coin that already shows heads, and toss again every coin that shows tails. This is exactly a valid choice of "subset to toss again" — namely, the subset currently showing tails.
>
> ## 2. Expected number of heads per coin
>
> Under this strategy, a given coin ends up heads if either:
>
> - it was heads on the first toss, probability $\tfrac12$, or
> - it was tails on the first toss, probability $\tfrac12$, and then heads on the re-toss, probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> So the probability that any one coin finishes heads is
> $$
> \frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac34.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Total expected number of heads
>
> By linearity of expectation, the expected number of heads among the four coins is
> $$
> E[Y]=4\cdot\frac34=3.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of heads, when playing optimally, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}.
> $$
