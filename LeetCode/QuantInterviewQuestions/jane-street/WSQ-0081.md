> [!example] Question 81 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You have 4 coins and toss them all at the same time. You receive as many dollars as there are heads (e.g., 3 dollars for 3 heads). If you are not satisfied with your first result, you may toss again, but then you must accept whatever prize money you get. What is the expected value of your winnings under this strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=82)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads on the first toss of $4$ fair coins, and let $Y$ be the number of heads on the second toss if that second toss is used.
>
> The strategy is to choose a threshold: after observing $X$, either keep $X$ dollars or reject it and take a second toss, whose expected value is fixed.
>
> ## Step 1: Expected value of a fresh toss
>
> For $4$ fair coins, the expected number of heads is
>
> $$
> E[Y] = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2.
> $$
>
> So if the first result is rejected, the expected payoff from rerolling is $2$ dollars.
>
> ## Step 2: Optimal decision rule
>
> After seeing the first result $X$:
>
> - if $X > 2$, it is better to keep it;
> - if $X < 2$, it is better to reroll;
> - if $X = 2$, both choices are equivalent in expectation.
>
> Thus an optimal rule is:
>
> - keep the first toss if it gives $3$ or $4$ heads;
> - reroll if it gives $0$ or $1$ head;
> - for $2$ heads, either choice gives the same expectation.
>
> ## Step 3: Distribution of the first toss
>
> Since $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12)$,
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\frac{1}{16},\quad
> P(X=1)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(X=2)=\frac{6}{16},\quad
> P(X=3)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(X=4)=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Compute the optimal expected payoff
>
> Using the rule “take $\max(X,2)$ in expectation,” the expected value is
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \sum_{k=0}^4 \max(k,2)\,P(X=k).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]
> = 2\left(\frac{1}{16}+\frac{4}{16}+\frac{6}{16}\right) + 3\left(\frac{4}{16}\right) + 4\left(\frac{1}{16}\right).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]
> = 2\cdot \frac{11}{16} + \frac{12}{16} + \frac{4}{16}
> = \frac{22}{16} + \frac{16}{16}
> = \frac{38}{16}
> = \frac{19}{8}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{19}{8}\text{ dollars} = 2.375\text{ dollars}}
> $$
