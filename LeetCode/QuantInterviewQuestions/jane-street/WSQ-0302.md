> [!example] Question 302 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Intern Interview

What is the expected number of rolls of a six-sided die to observe each face at least once? What is the expected number of rolls to observe each face at least twice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=303)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $T_1$ be the number of rolls needed to see all $6$ faces at least once, and let $T_2$ be the number of rolls needed to see all $6$ faces at least twice.
>
> ## 1. Expected rolls to see each face at least once
>
> This is the classical coupon collector problem.
>
> After $k-1$ distinct faces have appeared, the probability that the next roll produces a *new* face is
>
> $$
> \frac{6-(k-1)}{6}=\frac{7-k}{6}.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected number of additional rolls to go from $k-1$ distinct faces to $k$ distinct faces is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{(7-k)/6}=\frac{6}{7-k}.
> $$
>
> Summing for $k=1,2,\dots,6$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T_1]
> =
> \frac{6}{6}+\frac{6}{5}+\frac{6}{4}+\frac{6}{3}+\frac{6}{2}+\frac{6}{1}.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T_1]
> =
> 1+\frac{6}{5}+\frac{3}{2}+2+3+6
> =
> \frac{147}{10}
> =
> 14.7.
> $$
>
> So the expected number of rolls to get each face at least once is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[T_1]=\frac{147}{10}=14.7.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Expected rolls to see each face at least twice
>
> Now we want the expected time until every face has appeared at least twice.
>
> A convenient way is to use the tail-sum formula
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T_2] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \Pr(T_2 > n),
> $$
>
> where $\Pr(T_2 > n)$ is the probability that after $n$ rolls, some face has appeared fewer than $2$ times.
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \Pr(T_2 \le n)
> $$
>
> is the probability that after $n$ rolls, all $6$ faces have count at least $2$.
>
> For fixed $n$, the number of sequences of $n$ die rolls in which each face appears at least twice is
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_6=n\\ x_i\ge 2}}
> \frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!\cdots x_6!}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> \Pr(T_2 \le n)
> =
> \frac{1}{6^n}
> \sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_6=n\\ x_i\ge 2}}
> \frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!\cdots x_6!},
> $$
>
> and so
>
> $$
> \Pr(T_2 > n)
> =
> 1-
> \frac{1}{6^n}
> \sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_6=n\\ x_i\ge 2}}
> \frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!\cdots x_6!}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T_2]
> =
> \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
> \left[
> 1-
> \frac{1}{6^n}
> \sum_{\substack{x_1+\cdots+x_6=n\\ x_i\ge 2}}
> \frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!\cdots x_6!}
> \right].
> $$
>
> Evaluating this sum gives
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T_2] \approx 24.1034.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected number of rolls to get each face at least twice is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[T_2]\approx 24.1034.}
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{14.7}
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \boxed{24.1034\text{ (approximately)}}.
> $$
