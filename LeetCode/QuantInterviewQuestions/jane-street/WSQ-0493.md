> [!example] Question 493 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

How can you create a fair coin using an unfair coin? Is there any method that ensures you do not waste any of your coin flips during the process?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=494)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To obtain a fair coin from an unfair coin, one uses the **von Neumann procedure**.
>
> ## Method
>
> Let the unfair coin land heads with probability $p$ and tails with probability $1-p$, where $p$ may be unknown and need not equal $\tfrac12$.
>
> Flip the coin twice:
>
> - If the result is $HT$, output $H$.
> - If the result is $TH$, output $T$.
> - If the result is $HH$ or $TT$, discard both flips and repeat.
>
> ## Why this is fair
>
> The two relevant outcomes have probabilities
>
> $$
> P(HT)=p(1-p),
> \qquad
> P(TH)=(1-p)p.
> $$
>
> These are equal, so conditioning on getting one of these two outcomes gives
>
> $$
> P(\text{output }H)=P(\text{output }T)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> Thus the output is perfectly fair.
>
> ## Can one avoid wasting flips?
>
> If “not wasting flips” means using every flip in the sequence with no discarded information at all, then the basic von Neumann method does not do that, since $HH$ and $TT$ are ignored.
>
> However, there **are more efficient schemes** that reuse some of the information from rejected patterns. These methods extract fair bits from a biased source with much less waste. A famous example is **Peres’s algorithm**, which recursively reprocesses the discarded cases. Such methods can approach the maximum possible efficiency.
>
> ## Fundamental limitation
>
> Still, in general, one cannot guarantee that **every individual flip** contributes directly to the final fair output in a simple finite procedure, because a single biased flip by itself cannot produce a fair bit. Some redundancy must be resolved by looking at patterns of flips.
>
> So:
>
> - **Yes**, a fair coin can be simulated from an unfair coin.
> - The standard method is the von Neumann pairing rule.
> - **No**, the simplest method wastes some flips.
> - **Yes**, more advanced methods can recycle much of the discarded information, though not in the sense of a trivial no-waste one-step procedure.
