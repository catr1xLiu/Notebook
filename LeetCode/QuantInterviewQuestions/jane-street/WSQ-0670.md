> [!example] Question 670 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

There are 4 players in a game, each rolling a standard die. The player with the largest value and the player with the smallest value form one team; the remaining two players form the other team. The winning team is the one with the larger sum of their dice values, and their payoff is the difference between the team sums. Analyze the expected payoff of the winning team.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=671)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the four dice rolls, arranged in nondecreasing order, be
>
> $$
> X_1 \le X_2 \le X_3 \le X_4.
> $$
>
> Then the two teams are:
>
> - Team $A$: the smallest and largest rolls, with sum $X_1+X_4$
> - Team $B$: the two middle rolls, with sum $X_2+X_3$
>
> The payoff is $\left|(X_1+X_4)-(X_2+X_3)\right|$. We seek the expected payoff.
>
> ## Step 1: Express the payoff in terms of gaps
>
> Since $(X_1+X_4)-(X_2+X_3) = (X_4-X_3)-(X_2-X_1)$, writing the three consecutive gaps as
>
> $$
> a = X_2-X_1,\quad b = X_3-X_2,\quad c = X_4-X_3,
> $$
>
> the payoff is $|c-a|$. In particular, the payoff does not depend on the middle gap $b$ at all.
>
> ## Step 2: Enumerate by sorted type
>
> We work directly with the $6^4=1296$ equally likely ordered outcomes, grouping them by the multiset of values rolled.
>
> ### Case 1: all four dice equal
>
> Type $(x,x,x,x)$. Here $a=c=0$, so the payoff is $0$. There are $6$ such outcomes, contributing $0$.
>
> ### Case 2: three equal, one different
>
> The type has a value $p$ repeated three times and a distinct value $q$. Whether $q$ is smaller or larger than $p$, one gap is $0$ and the other equals $|p-q|$, so the payoff is $|p-q|$. For each ordered choice of $(p,q)$ with $p \ne q$ there are $4$ arrangements.
>
> Summing over the $30$ ordered pairs,
>
> $$
> \sum_{p\ne q}|p-q| = 2\sum_{1\le x<y\le 6}(y-x) = 2(35)=70,
> $$
>
> so the contribution is $4\cdot 70 = 280$.
>
> ### Case 3: two pairs
>
> Type $(x,x,y,y)$ with $x<y$. Sorted, $X_1=X_2=x$ and $X_3=X_4=y$, so $a=0$ and $c=0$. The payoff is $0$ for every outcome of this type, so this case contributes $0$.
>
> ### Case 4: one pair and two distinct singles
>
> Three distinct values $x<y<z$ appear, with one of them doubled. There are $12$ arrangements for each choice of doubled value.
>
> - If the smallest, $x$, is doubled: sorted order $(x,x,y,z)$ gives $a=0$, $c=z-y$, payoff $z-y$.
> - If the largest, $z$, is doubled: sorted order $(x,y,z,z)$ gives $a=y-x$, $c=0$, payoff $y-x$.
> - If the middle value, $y$, is doubled: sorted order $(x,y,y,z)$ gives $a=y-x$, $c=z-y$, payoff $|x+z-2y|$.
>
> Summing over the $20$ triples $1\le x<y<z\le 6$, the relabeling symmetry $(x,y,z)\mapsto(7-z,7-y,7-x)$ gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{x<y<z}(z-y) = \sum_{x<y<z}(y-x) = 35,
> $$
>
> and direct summation gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{x<y<z}|x+z-2y| = 22.
> $$
>
> Hence the total contribution from Case 4 is
>
> $$
> 12(35+22+35) = 12(92) = 1104.
> $$
>
> ### Case 5: all four dice distinct
>
> Type $(w,x,y,z)$ with $w<x<y<z$, payoff $|(z-y)-(x-w)|$, and $4!=24$ arrangements per $4$-set. Summing $|(z-y)-(x-w)|$ over the $15$ increasing $4$-tuples from $\{1,\dots,6\}$ gives $10$, so this case contributes $24\cdot 10 = 240$.
>
> ## Step 3: Combine
>
> The total payoff summed over all $1296$ ordered outcomes is
>
> $$
> 0+280+0+1104+240 = 1624.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{payoff}] = \frac{1624}{1296} = \frac{203}{162}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{203}{162}\approx 1.253}
> $$
