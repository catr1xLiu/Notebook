> [!example] Question 184 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

If you have only 5-cent and 11-cent stamps, what is the smallest amount that cannot be formed using any combination of these stamps?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=185)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine the largest value that **cannot** be formed using only $5$-cent and $11$-cent stamps, consider all amounts of the form
>
> $$
> 5a + 11b
> $$
>
> where $a,b$ are nonnegative integers.
>
> This is a standard two-denomination problem. Since $5$ and $11$ are relatively prime, the largest amount that cannot be expressed is given by the Frobenius formula:
>
> $$
> ab-a-b
> $$
>
> with $a=5$ and $b=11$. Thus,
>
> $$
> 5 \cdot 11 - 5 - 11 = 55 - 16 = 39.
> $$
>
> To confirm, amounts greater than $39$ can all be formed. For example:
>
> - $40 = 8\cdot 5$
> - $41 = 6\cdot 5 + 11$
> - $42 = 4\cdot 5 + 2\cdot 11$
> - $43 = 2\cdot 5 + 3\cdot 11$
> - $44 = 4\cdot 11$
>
> and then adding more $5$-cent stamps gives every larger amount.
>
> Therefore, the smallest-number wording here is intended to mean the largest impossible amount, and the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{39}
> $$
