> [!example] Question 433 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

In Russian Roulette, a six-chamber gun contains two adjacent bullets. You pull the trigger once, and do not get shot. What is the probability that you will get shot on the next trigger pull, without spinning the cylinder?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=434)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider a revolver with $6$ chambers and $2$ bullets in adjacent chambers. The cylinder is spun once initially, so each chamber is equally likely to be under the hammer on the first trigger pull.
>
> You pull the trigger once and survive. The question is: if you pull the trigger again **without respinning**, what is the probability that the next shot fires?
>
> ## Step 1: List the possible bullet configurations
>
> Because the two bullets are adjacent, the cylinder consists of:
>
> - $2$ consecutive loaded chambers,
> - $4$ consecutive empty chambers.
>
> After the first trigger pull, survival means the first chamber was one of the $4$ empty chambers.
>
> ## Step 2: Determine which empty chambers are dangerous on the next pull
>
> Number the chambers around the cylinder. Suppose the bullets are in chambers $1$ and $2$. Then the empty chambers are $3,4,5,6$.
>
> If the first pull lands on:
>
> - chamber $3$, then the next chamber is $4$: empty,
> - chamber $4$, then the next chamber is $5$: empty,
> - chamber $5$, then the next chamber is $6$: empty,
> - chamber $6$, then the next chamber is $1$: loaded.
>
> So among the $4$ possible empty chambers on which you could have survived, only $1$ leads to a bullet on the next pull.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the conditional probability
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(\text{next shot fires} \mid \text{first shot was empty}) = \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
> $$
