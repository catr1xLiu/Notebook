> [!example] Question 656 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

You have a jar containing 3 balls labeled '+1\$' and 2 balls labeled '-1\$'. You draw balls one by one, without replacement, and you can choose to stop at any time. What is the fair price to pay to play this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=657)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_5$ be the values of the balls drawn in order, where each $X_i \in \{+1,-1\}$.  
> There are $3$ balls worth $+1$ and $2$ balls worth $-1$. You may stop after any draw, and your payoff is the sum of the values drawn up to the stopping time.
>
> We seek the value of this optimal stopping game.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. State formulation
>
> At any stage, the relevant information is:
>
> - $p =$ number of $+1$ balls remaining,
> - $m =$ number of $-1$ balls remaining.
>
> Let $V(p,m)$ denote the expected **future** payoff from a state with $p$ positive balls and $m$ negative balls remaining, assuming optimal play from that point onward.
>
> At such a state, you have two choices:
>
> 1. **Stop now**, receiving $0$ future payoff.
> 2. **Continue**, in which case:
>    - with probability $\dfrac{p}{p+m}$, you draw $+1$ and move to state $(p-1,m)$,
>    - with probability $\dfrac{m}{p+m}$, you draw $-1$ and move to state $(p,m-1)$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> V(p,m)=\max\left(0,\ \frac{p}{p+m}\bigl(1+V(p-1,m)\bigr)+\frac{m}{p+m}\bigl(-1+V(p,m-1)\bigr)\right).
> $$
>
> Boundary conditions:
> - $V(0,m)=0$, since if only $-1$ balls remain, you should stop immediately.
> - $V(p,0)=p$, since if only $+1$ balls remain, you should draw all of them.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Compute values recursively
>
> ### State $(1,1)$
> $$
> V(1,1)=\max\left(0,\ \frac12(1+V(0,1))+\frac12(-1+V(1,0))\right).
> $$
> Using $V(0,1)=0$ and $V(1,0)=1$,
> $$
> V(1,1)=\max\left(0,\ \frac12(1)+\frac12(0)\right)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,2)$
> $$
> V(1,2)=\max\left(0,\ \frac13(1+V(0,2))+\frac23(-1+V(1,1))\right).
> $$
> Since $V(0,2)=0$ and $V(1,1)=\frac12$,
> $$
> V(1,2)=\max\left(0,\ \frac13+\frac23\left(-\frac12\right)\right)=\max(0,0)=0.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,1)$
> $$
> V(2,1)=\max\left(0,\ \frac23(1+V(1,1))+\frac13(-1+V(2,0))\right).
> $$
> Using $V(1,1)=\frac12$ and $V(2,0)=2$,
> $$
> V(2,1)=\frac23\left(\frac32\right)+\frac13(1)=1+\frac13=\frac43.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,2)$
> $$
> V(2,2)=\max\left(0,\ \frac12(1+V(1,2))+\frac12(-1+V(2,1))\right).
> $$
> Using $V(1,2)=0$ and $V(2,1)=\frac43$,
> $$
> V(2,2)=\frac12(1)+\frac12\left(\frac13\right)=\frac23.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,1)$
> $$
> V(3,1)=\max\left(0,\ \frac34(1+V(2,1))+\frac14(-1+V(3,0))\right).
> $$
> Using $V(2,1)=\frac43$ and $V(3,0)=3$,
> $$
> V(3,1)=\frac34\left(\frac73\right)+\frac14(2)=\frac74+\frac12=\frac94.
> $$
>
> ### State $(3,2)$
> This is the initial state.
> $$
> V(3,2)=\max\left(0,\ \frac35(1+V(2,2))+\frac25(-1+V(3,1))\right).
> $$
> Using $V(2,2)=\frac23$ and $V(3,1)=\frac94$,
> $$
> V(3,2)=\frac35\left(\frac53\right)+\frac25\left(\frac54\right)=1+\frac12=\frac32.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Interpretation
>
> The value of the game is
> $$
> V(3,2)=\frac32.
> $$
>
> So the maximum fair price to pay is
> $$
> \boxed{\$1.50}.
> $$
>
> This means that if the entry fee is $\text{\textdollar}1.50$, the game is fair under optimal stopping play.
