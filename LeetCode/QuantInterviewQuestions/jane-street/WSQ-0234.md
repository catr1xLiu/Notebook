> [!example] Question 234 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

How can you invert a pyramid of coins by moving only three coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=235)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Arrange the coins in a triangular pyramid shape with $10$ coins:
>
> - Row $1$: $1$ coin  
> - Row $2$: $2$ coins  
> - Row $3$: $3$ coins  
> - Row $4$: $4$ coins  
>
> The goal is to make the triangle point in the opposite direction by moving only $3$ coins.
>
> ## Method
>
> The key idea is to imagine the final inverted triangle placed so that it overlaps the original triangle as much as possible. Then only the coins not lying in the overlap need to be moved.
>
> A $4$-row triangle and its upside-down version can be positioned so that $7$ coins coincide. Since there are $10$ coins total, the remaining
>
> $$
> 10 - 7 = 3
> $$
>
> coins must be moved.
>
> ## Which coins to move
>
> If the original triangle points upward, move the $3$ corner coins:
>
> - the top coin,
> - the bottom-left corner coin,
> - the bottom-right corner coin.
>
> Place these $3$ coins in the positions needed to form the downward-pointing triangle.
>
> After these moves, the figure becomes an inverted pyramid.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> You can invert the coin pyramid by **moving the three corner coins** to the corresponding positions of the upside-down triangle.
