> [!example] Question 52 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

From a standard deck of 52 cards, you randomly pick 26 cards to form a new set. From this set of 26 cards, you pick two cards. You win if both picked cards are of the same color. Is this game preferable to a game where you pick two cards at random (the first two picks) from a deck of 26 cards containing an equal number of black and red cards, and win if both are of the same color? Calculate or compare the probabilities.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=53)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the original deck have $26$ red and $26$ black cards.
>
> We compare two games.
>
> ## Game A
> First choose $26$ cards uniformly at random from the $52$-card deck.  
> Then choose $2$ cards uniformly at random from those $26$.  
> You win if the two chosen cards have the same color.
>
> ## Game B
> Choose $2$ cards uniformly at random from a $26$-card deck containing exactly $13$ red and $13$ black cards.  
> You win if the two chosen cards have the same color.
>
> The question is whether Game A is preferable to Game B.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the winning probability in Game B
>
> In a deck with $13$ red and $13$ black cards, the probability that two cards chosen without replacement have the same color is
>
> $$
> P_B=\frac{\binom{13}{2}+\binom{13}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \binom{13}{2}=78,\qquad \binom{26}{2}=325.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P_B=\frac{78+78}{325}=\frac{156}{325}=\frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P_B=\frac{12}{25}=0.48.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the winning probability in Game A
>
> Let $R$ be the number of red cards among the randomly chosen $26$ cards. Then the number of black cards is $26-R$.
>
> Conditioned on $R=r$, the probability that two cards chosen from this $26$-card set have the same color is
>
> $$
> P(\text{win}\mid R=r)=\frac{\binom{r}{2}+\binom{26-r}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}.
> $$
>
> So the unconditional probability is
>
> $$
> P_A=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\binom{R}{2}+\binom{26-R}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}\right].
> $$
>
> This can be evaluated directly, but there is a much simpler observation:
>
> Choosing $26$ cards from the $52$-card deck and then choosing $2$ from those $26$ is equivalent, as far as the final two cards are concerned, to simply choosing $2$ cards uniformly at random from the original $52$-card deck. Every pair of cards is equally likely to be the final pair.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{\binom{26}{2}+\binom{26}{2}}{\binom{52}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Now
>
> $$
> \binom{26}{2}=325,\qquad \binom{52}{2}=1326,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{325+325}{1326}=\frac{650}{1326}=\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{25}{51}\approx 0.4902.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Compare the two probabilities
>
> We compare
>
> $$
> P_A=\frac{25}{51}
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> P_B=\frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> Compute the difference:
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}-\frac{12}{25}
> =\frac{625-612}{1275}
> =\frac{13}{1275}>0.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}>\frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> So Game A gives a slightly larger chance of winning.
>
> ---
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Yes, the first game is preferable. Its winning probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}\approx 0.4902,
> $$
>
> whereas the second game has winning probability
>
> $$
> \frac{12}{25}=0.48.
> $$
>
> So the first game is better by
>
> $$
> \frac{13}{1275}\approx 0.0102.
> $$
