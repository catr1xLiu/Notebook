> [!example] Question 560 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Four fair coins are flipped. You receive one dollar each time a head appears. What is the expected payoff? If you are told that there are at least two heads among the four coins, what is the expected payoff under this condition?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=561)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $4$ flips of a fair coin. Then the payoff is $X$ dollars, where
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> ## 1. Expected payoff before any extra information
>
> For a binomial random variable,
>
> $$
> E[X] = np = 4 \cdot \tfrac12 = 2.
> $$
>
> So the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Expected payoff given that there are at least two heads
>
> We want
>
> $$
> E[X \mid X \ge 2].
> $$
>
> First compute the relevant probabilities:
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^4=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X=2)=\frac{6}{16}, \qquad P(X=3)=\frac{4}{16}, \qquad P(X=4)=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(X\ge 2)=\frac{6+4+1}{16}=\frac{11}{16}.
> $$
>
> Now use the conditional expectation formula:
>
> $$
> E[X\mid X\ge 2]
> =
> \frac{2P(X=2)+3P(X=3)+4P(X=4)}{P(X\ge 2)}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> E[X\mid X\ge 2]
> =
> \frac{2\cdot \frac{6}{16}+3\cdot \frac{4}{16}+4\cdot \frac{1}{16}}{\frac{11}{16}}
> =
> \frac{\frac{12+12+4}{16}}{\frac{11}{16}}
> =
> \frac{28}{11}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected payoff, given that there are at least two heads, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{28}{11}\text{ dollars} \approx \$2.55}.
> $$
>
> So the answers are:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{28}{11}\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
