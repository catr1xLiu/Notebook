> [!example] Question 468 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

We toss a coin repeatedly until either the sequence HHT appears or HTH appears. What is the probability that HHT appears first? How does this change if the coin is unfair, with probability p of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=469)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $P$ denote the probability that the pattern $\text{HHT}$ appears before $\text{HTH}$.
>
> The key idea is to condition on the current relevant suffix of the toss history. Only the most recent tosses that could still grow into one of the target patterns matter.
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Let
>
> - $q_\emptyset$ = probability that $\text{HHT}$ occurs before $\text{HTH}$ starting from no relevant history,
> - $q_H$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{H}$,
> - $q_{HH}$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{HH}$,
> - $q_{HT}$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{HT}$.
>
> We first solve the fair-coin case, then the general case with $\mathbb P(H)=p$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Part I: Fair coin
>
> Here $\mathbb P(H)=\mathbb P(T)=\tfrac12$.
>
> ### Step 2: Write recursion equations
>
> From the empty state:
>
> - if the next toss is $\text{H}$, we move to state $H$;
> - if the next toss is $\text{T}$, we remain effectively in the empty state.
>
> Thus
> $$
> q_\emptyset=\frac12 q_H+\frac12 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - toss $\text{H}$ $\Rightarrow$ state $HH$,
> - toss $\text{T}$ $\Rightarrow$ state $HT$.
>
> So
> $$
> q_H=\frac12 q_{HH}+\frac12 q_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - toss $\text{T}$ completes $\text{HHT}$, so success probability $1$;
> - toss $\text{H}$ leaves the relevant suffix still $HH$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> q_{HH}=\frac12 q_{HH}+\frac12 \cdot 1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - toss $\text{H}$ completes $\text{HTH}$, so success probability $0$;
> - toss $\text{T}$ produces suffix $\text{T}$, which is irrelevant, so we return to the empty state.
>
> Hence
> $$
> q_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Solve
>
> From
> $$
> q_\emptyset=\frac12 q_H+\frac12 q_\emptyset
> $$
> we get
> $$
> q_\emptyset=q_H.
> $$
>
> From
> $$
> q_{HH}=\frac12 q_{HH}+\frac12,
> $$
> we obtain
> $$
> q_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> From
> $$
> q_{HT}=\frac12 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> Now substitute into the equation for $q_H$:
> $$
> q_H=\frac12 q_{HH}+\frac12 q_{HT}
> =\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\left(\frac12 q_\emptyset\right)
> =\frac12+\frac14 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> Since $q_H=q_\emptyset$, this gives
> $$
> q_\emptyset=\frac12+\frac14 q_\emptyset.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> \frac34 q_\emptyset=\frac12,
> $$
> so
> $$
> q_\emptyset=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Thus, for a fair coin,
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb P(\text{HHT appears before HTH})=\frac23.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Part II: Unfair coin with $\mathbb P(H)=p$
>
> Now let
> $$
> \mathbb P(H)=p,\qquad \mathbb P(T)=1-p.
> $$
>
> Use the same states. Let the corresponding probabilities again be $q_\emptyset,q_H,q_{HH},q_{HT}$.
>
> ### Step 1: Recursions
>
> From the empty state:
> $$
> q_\emptyset=p\,q_H+(1-p)\,q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
> $$
> q_H=p\,q_{HH}+(1-p)\,q_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - an $H$ keeps us in state $HH$;
> - a $T$ completes $\text{HHT}$.
>
> So
> $$
> q_{HH}=p\,q_{HH}+(1-p)\cdot 1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - an $H$ completes $\text{HTH}$, giving probability $0$;
> - a $T$ resets to the empty state.
>
> Thus
> $$
> q_{HT}=p\cdot 0+(1-p)\,q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Solve the equations
>
> From
> $$
> q_\emptyset=p\,q_H+(1-p)\,q_\emptyset,
> $$
> we get
> $$
> p\,q_\emptyset=p\,q_H.
> $$
> Hence, for $p>0$,
> $$
> q_\emptyset=q_H.
> $$
>
> From
> $$
> q_{HH}=p\,q_{HH}+(1-p),
> $$
> we obtain
> $$
> (1-p)q_{HH}=1-p,
> $$
> so for $p<1$,
> $$
> q_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> q_{HT}=(1-p)q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> Substitute into $q_H$:
> $$
> q_H=p\cdot 1+(1-p)\bigl((1-p)q_\emptyset\bigr)
> =p+(1-p)^2 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> Since $q_H=q_\emptyset$,
> $$
> q_\emptyset=p+(1-p)^2 q_\emptyset.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> q_\emptyset\bigl(1-(1-p)^2\bigr)=p.
> $$
>
> Now
> $$
> 1-(1-p)^2=1-(1-2p+p^2)=2p-p^2=p(2-p).
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> q_\emptyset \, p(2-p)=p.
> $$
>
> For $0<p<1$,
> $$
> q_\emptyset=\frac{1}{2-p}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb P(\text{HHT appears before HTH})=\frac{1}{2-p},\qquad 0<p<1.}
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Edge cases
>
> - If $p=0$, the coin always shows $\text{T}$, so neither pattern ever occurs. The probability is not meaningful unless one specifies how to treat this case.
> - If $p=1$, the coin always shows $\text{H}$, so neither pattern ever occurs.
>
> Thus the formula applies for
> $$
> 0<p<1.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> For a fair coin:
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb P(\text{HHT before HTH})=\frac23.}
> $$
>
> For a coin with $\mathbb P(H)=p$:
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb P(\text{HHT before HTH})=\frac{1}{2-p}\qquad (0<p<1).}
> $$
