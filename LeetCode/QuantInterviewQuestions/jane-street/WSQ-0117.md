> [!example] Question 117 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

Suppose you have two urns that are indistinguishable from the outside. One urn contains 3 one-dollar coins and 7 ten-dollar coins. The other urn contains 5 one-dollar coins and 5 ten-dollar coins. You choose an urn at random and draw a coin at random. You find that it is a \$10 coin. Now you have the option to draw again (without replacing the first coin) from either the same urn or the other urn. Should you draw from the same urn or switch to the other urn to maximize the probability of drawing another \$10 coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=118)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let
>
> - $U_A$ be the urn with $3$ one-dollar coins and $7$ ten-dollar coins,
> - $U_B$ be the urn with $5$ one-dollar coins and $5$ ten-dollar coins.
>
> An urn is chosen uniformly at random, so
>
> $$
> P(U_A)=P(U_B)=\frac12.
> $$
>
> You draw one coin and observe that it is a ten-dollar coin. The question is: for the second draw, is the probability of getting a ten-dollar coin larger if you draw again from the same urn, or if you switch to the other urn?
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Update the probability of which urn was chosen
>
> Let $T$ denote the event that the first coin drawn is a ten-dollar coin.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> P(T\mid U_A)=\frac{7}{10}, \qquad P(T\mid U_B)=\frac{5}{10}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> By Bayes' rule,
>
> $$
> P(U_A\mid T)=\frac{P(T\mid U_A)P(U_A)}{P(T)}.
> $$
>
> First compute $P(T)$:
>
> $$
> P(T)=P(T\mid U_A)P(U_A)+P(T\mid U_B)P(U_B)
> =\frac{7}{10}\cdot\frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac12
> =\frac{7}{20}+\frac{5}{20}
> =\frac{12}{20}
> =\frac35.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(U_A\mid T)=\frac{\frac{7}{10}\cdot\frac12}{\frac35}
> =\frac{7/20}{3/5}
> =\frac{7}{12}.
> $$
>
> Similarly,
>
> $$
> P(U_B\mid T)=1-\frac{7}{12}=\frac{5}{12}.
> $$
>
> So after seeing a ten-dollar coin, it is more likely that the chosen urn was $U_A$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Probability of a ten-dollar coin if you draw again from the same urn
>
> If the chosen urn was $U_A$, then after removing one ten-dollar coin it contains $6$ ten-dollar coins out of $9$ total, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{second is }10 \mid U_A, T, \text{same urn})=\frac{6}{9}=\frac23.
> $$
>
> If the chosen urn was $U_B$, then after removing one ten-dollar coin it contains $4$ ten-dollar coins out of $9$ total, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{second is }10 \mid U_B, T, \text{same urn})=\frac{4}{9}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{second is }10 \mid T, \text{same urn})
> = P(U_A\mid T)\cdot\frac23 + P(U_B\mid T)\cdot\frac49.
> $$
>
> Substitute the posterior probabilities:
>
> $$
> = \frac{7}{12}\cdot\frac23+\frac{5}{12}\cdot\frac49
> = \frac{14}{36}+\frac{20}{108}
> = \frac{42}{108}+\frac{20}{108}
> = \frac{62}{108}
> = \frac{31}{54}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{ten-dollar on second draw from same urn})=\frac{31}{54}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Probability of a ten-dollar coin if you switch urns
>
> If the chosen urn was $U_A$, then the other urn is $U_B$, from which the chance of drawing a ten-dollar coin is
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{10}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> If the chosen urn was $U_B$, then the other urn is $U_A$, from which the chance is
>
> $$
> \frac{7}{10}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(\text{second is }10 \mid T, \text{switch})
> = P(U_A\mid T)\cdot\frac12 + P(U_B\mid T)\cdot\frac{7}{10}.
> $$
>
> Substitute:
>
> $$
> = \frac{7}{12}\cdot\frac12 + \frac{5}{12}\cdot\frac{7}{10}
> = \frac{7}{24}+\frac{35}{120}
> = \frac{35}{120}+\frac{35}{120}
> = \frac{70}{120}
> = \frac{7}{12}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{ten-dollar on second draw from other urn})=\frac{7}{12}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 4: Compare
>
> $$
> \frac{7}{12}=\frac{63}{108}, \qquad \frac{31}{54}=\frac{62}{108}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \frac{7}{12}>\frac{31}{54}.
> $$
>
> So the probability of drawing a ten-dollar coin is slightly larger if you **switch urns**.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> You should **switch to the other urn**. The probabilities are
>
> $$
> P(\text{ten-dollar if switch})=\frac{7}{12},
> \qquad
> P(\text{ten-dollar if stay})=\frac{31}{54},
> $$
>
> and since
>
> $$
> \frac{7}{12}>\frac{31}{54},
> $$
>
> switching is better.
