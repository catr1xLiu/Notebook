> [!example] Question 85 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

For which integers b (other than zero) is it possible to find an integer a such that the ratio a/b is contained in the interval \[0.48, 0.52\]?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=86)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> We seek all nonzero integers $b$ for which there exists an integer $a$ satisfying
> $$
> 0.48 \le \frac{a}{b} \le 0.52.
> $$
>
> Rewrite the endpoints as fractions:
> $$
> 0.48=\frac{12}{25}, \qquad 0.52=\frac{13}{25}.
> $$
> Thus we need
> $$
> \frac{12}{25} \le \frac{a}{b} \le \frac{13}{25}.
> $$
>
> The answer depends on the sign of $b$.
>
> ## Case 1: $b>0$
>
> Multiplying by $b$ gives
> $$
> \frac{12b}{25} \le a \le \frac{13b}{25}.
> $$
> So an integer $a$ exists exactly when the interval
> $$
> \left[\frac{12b}{25},\,\frac{13b}{25}\right]
> $$
> contains an integer.
>
> Its length is
> $$
> \frac{13b}{25}-\frac{12b}{25}=\frac{b}{25}.
> $$
> Hence if $b\ge 25$, the interval has length at least $1$, so it must contain an integer.
>
> It remains to check $1\le b\le 24$. In this range the interval has length less than $1$, so at most one integer can lie in it. Testing the possibilities is easiest by comparing with nearby multiples of $25$.
>
> We need some integer $a$ such that
> $$
> 12b \le 25a \le 13b.
> $$
> Since $13b-12b=b$, this asks whether a multiple of $25$ lies in the interval $[12b,13b]$.
>
> For $1\le b\le 24$, the interval length is less than $25$, so at most one multiple of $25$ can occur. Checking the relevant values:
>
> - For $b=2$,
>   $$
>   [12b,13b]=[24,26],
>   $$
>   which contains $25$, giving $a=1$.
>
> - For $b=4$,
>   $$
>   [48,52],
>   $$
>   which contains $50$, giving $a=2$.
>
> - For $b=6$,
>   $$
>   [72,78],
>   $$
>   which contains $75$, giving $a=3$.
>
> Continuing similarly, every even $b$ from $2$ to $24$ works, since
> $$
> a=\frac b2
> $$
> gives
> $$
> \frac ab=\frac12 \in [0.48,0.52].
> $$
>
> Now consider odd $b<25$. Then $a/b$ cannot equal $1/2$, so we ask whether any other integer $a$ works. This would require a multiple of $25$ in $[12b,13b]$. For odd $b<25$, the multiples of $25$ nearest $12.5b$ do not fall in that interval. A direct check shows none work.
>
> Thus for positive $b$, the working values are
> $$
> b \text{ even}, \quad \text{or} \quad b\ge 25.
> $$
>
> ## Case 2: $b<0$
>
> Let $b=-c$ where $c>0$. Then
> $$
> \frac{a}{b}\in[0.48,0.52]
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> \frac{-a}{c}\in[0.48,0.52].
> $$
> So this is equivalent to the positive-denominator case with denominator $c=|b|$. Therefore the same condition applies to $|b|$.
>
> Hence the nonzero integers $b$ that work are exactly those for which either $|b|$ is even or $|b|\ge 25$.
>
> Equivalently, the only nonzero integers $b$ that do **not** work are the odd integers with $|b|<25$:
> $$
> b=\pm1,\pm3,\pm5,\pm7,\pm9,\pm11,\pm13,\pm15,\pm17,\pm19,\pm21,\pm23.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The required integers are precisely all nonzero integers $b$ except the odd integers with $|b|<25$. In other words,
> $$
> \boxed{\text{$b\neq 0$ works iff either $b$ is even or $|b|\ge 25$.}}
> $$
