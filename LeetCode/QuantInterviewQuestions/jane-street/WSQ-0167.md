> [!example] Question 167 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

1) What is 12% of 47? 2) If I pick 2 cards from a shuffled deck (with no jokers), what is the probability that both are queens? 3) i) If I toss 4 fair coins and earn a dollar for every head, what is the expected value of this game? ii) If I can re-toss all 4 coins and must take the value from the second round, what is the expected value now? 4) How confident are you that you got 0, 1, 2, 3, or all questions correct, giving percentages? 5) If two teams play a best 4 out of 7 series, where each team has a 50% chance to win each round, what is the probability that the series lasts all 7 games?

> 0.12 \times 47 = 5.64.

> \boxed{5.64}.

> ## 2) Probability that two cards drawn without replacement are both queens
>
> A standard deck has $52$ cards, of which $4$ are queens.
>
> The probability the first card is a queen is
> $$
> \frac{4}{52}.
> $$
>
> Given that, the probability the second card is also a queen is
> $$
> \frac{3}{51}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(\text{both queens})=\frac{4}{52}\cdot\frac{3}{51}.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> \frac{4}{52}\cdot\frac{3}{51}=\frac{1}{13}\cdot\frac{1}{17}=\frac{1}{221}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{221}\approx 0.452\%}.
> $$
>

> ## 3) Coin-toss game
>
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $4$ fair coin tosses. Since each head pays $\text{\textdollar}1$, the value of the game is the expected number of heads.
>
> ### i) Value without re-tossing
>
> Each coin has expected payoff
> $$
> 0\cdot \frac12 + 1\cdot \frac12 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> For $4$ independent coins,
> $$
> E[H]=4\cdot \frac12=2.
> $$
>
> So the value of the game is
> $$
> \boxed{\$2}.
> $$
>
> ### ii) Value if you may re-toss all $4$ coins, but must take the second result
>
> Let $H_1$ be the number of heads in the first round. You may either keep $H_1$ dollars or discard it and re-toss all $4$ coins.
>
> The second round has expected value
> $$
> E[H_2]=2.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the first round if $H_1 \ge 2$,
> - re-toss if $H_1 < 2$.
>
> So the value is
> $$
> E[\max(H_1,2)].
> $$
>
> Now $H_1 \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12)$, so
> $$
> P(H_1=0)=\frac{1}{16},\quad
> P(H_1=1)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(H_1=2)=\frac{6}{16},\quad
> P(H_1=3)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(H_1=4)=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> E[\max(H_1,2)]
> =2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{16}+\frac{4}{16}+\frac{6}{16}\right)
> +3\cdot\frac{4}{16}
> +4\cdot\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> =2\cdot\frac{11}{16}+\frac{12}{16}+\frac{4}{16}
> =\frac{22+12+4}{16}
> =\frac{38}{16}
> =\frac{19}{8}.
> $$
>
> Hence the new value is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{19}{8}=\$2.375}.
> $$
>

> ## 4) Confidence that $0,1,2,3,$ or all $4$ questions above are correct

>
> Let
> $$
> p=0.999,\qquad q=0.001.
> $$
>

> $$
> P(K=k)=\binom{4}{k}p^k q^{4-k}.

> ## 5) Probability a best-$4$-of-$7$ series goes to the $7$th game
>
> For the series to reach game $7$, after $6$ games each team must have won exactly $3$ games.
>
> Each game is independent, and each team has probability $\frac12$ of winning any game.
>
> The probability that in $6$ games one team wins exactly $3$ is
> $$
> \binom{6}{3}\left(\frac12\right)^6.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(\text{series reaches game }7)=\binom{6}{3}\left(\frac12\right)^6
> =\frac{20}{64}
> =\frac{5}{16}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{16}=31.25\%}.
> $$
>
