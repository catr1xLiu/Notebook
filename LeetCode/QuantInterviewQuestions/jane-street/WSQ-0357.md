> [!example] Question 357 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of drawing two queens from a standard deck of 52 playing cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=358)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume two cards are drawn from a standard $52$-card deck without replacement. There are $4$ queens in the deck.
>
> The probability that both drawn cards are queens is
>
> $$
> \frac{\binom{4}{2}}{\binom{52}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Compute each combination:
>
> $$
> \binom{4}{2} = 6,
> \qquad
> \binom{52}{2} = \frac{52 \cdot 51}{2} = 1326.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{two queens}) = \frac{6}{1326} = \frac{1}{221}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{221}}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, using sequential probability,
>
> $$
> P(\text{first queen and second queen}) = \frac{4}{52}\cdot\frac{3}{51} = \frac{1}{221}.
> $$
