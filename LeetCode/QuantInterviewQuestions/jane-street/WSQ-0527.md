> [!example] Question 527 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You are bidding on a coin with a price uniformly distributed between 0 and 100. If your bid is greater than the price, you acquire the coin and can sell it to your friend at 1.5 times the price. What bid should you make to maximize your expected profit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=528)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the coin’s true price be $p$, and assume, by symmetry of the statement “between $0$ and $100$,” that $p$ is uniformly distributed on $[0,100]$.
>
> If you bid $b$, then:
>
> - you win exactly when $b > p$,
> - if you win, you pay $b$ and immediately sell to your friend for $1.5p$.
>
> Hence your profit is
>
> $$
> \Pi(p;b)=
> \begin{cases}
> 1.5p-b, & p<b,\\
> 0, & p\ge b.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> So the expected profit is
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)]
> =\frac{1}{100}\int_0^b (1.5p-b)\,dp,
> \qquad 0\le b\le 100.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)]
> =\frac{1}{100}\left[0.75p^2-bp\right]_0^b
> =\frac{1}{100}\left(0.75b^2-b^2\right)
> =-\frac{b^2}{400}.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> E[\Pi(b)]\le 0
> $$
>
> for every $b$, with equality only at
>
> $$
> b=0.
> $$
>
> Therefore the bid that maximizes expected profit is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> In other words, under the assumption that the price is uniformly distributed on $[0,100]$, any positive bid gives negative expected profit, so the optimal strategy is not to bid.
