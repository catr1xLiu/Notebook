> [!example] Question 562 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

The value of a box is uniformly distributed between 0 and 100. You place a bet. If your bet is higher than the value, you get the box and can sell it at 1.5 times its value. What is the optimal bet amount?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=563)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the box value be $V \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,100)$, and let the bid be $b$.
>
> If $b > V$, you buy the box for price $b$ and then sell it for $1.5V$.  
> Thus the profit is
>
> $$
> \Pi =
> \begin{cases}
> 1.5V - b, & \text{if } V < b,\\
> 0, & \text{if } V \ge b.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The goal is to choose $b$ to maximize the expected profit.
>
> ## Expected profit as a function of $b$
>
> For $0 \le b \le 100$, the density of $V$ is $\frac{1}{100}$, so
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi]
> = \int_0^b (1.5v - b)\frac{1}{100}\,dv.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi]
> = \frac{1}{100}\left[\int_0^b 1.5v\,dv - \int_0^b b\,dv\right]
> = \frac{1}{100}\left[1.5\cdot \frac{b^2}{2} - b^2\right].
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi]
> = \frac{1}{100}\left(\frac{3}{4}b^2 - b^2\right)
> = -\frac{b^2}{400}.
> $$
>
> So for every $b>0$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi] < 0,
> $$
>
> and at $b=0$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi]=0.
> $$
>
> Therefore, on the interval $[0,100]$, the maximum expected profit occurs at
>
> $$
> b=0.
> $$
>
> ## What if $b>100$?
>
> If $b>100$, then you always buy the box, so
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi] = 1.5\,\mathbb{E}[V] - b.
> $$
>
> Since $\mathbb{E}[V]=50$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi] = 1.5\cdot 50 - b = 75 - b.
> $$
>
> This is maximized at the smallest such $b$, namely $b=100$, giving
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\Pi] = 75 - 100 = -25 < 0.
> $$
>
> So bidding above $100$ is even worse.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal bid is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> Any positive bid gives negative expected profit, so the best strategy is not to bid at all.
