> [!example] Question 386 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Part 1: We are playing a game where we start at 0 and flip a coin until someone wins. Every heads adds +1 to the number and every tails adds -1. If the number reaches -10, the interviewer wins; if it reaches 20, you win. What is the probability that the interviewer will win?

Part 2: I am thinking of a 10-digit number. The first digit is the number of 0s in the number, the second is the number of 1s, and so on. What is the number?

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Probability that the interviewer wins
>
> This is a one-dimensional random walk with absorbing barriers.
>
> Let $X_n$ be the current total after $n$ flips, starting from
>
> $$
> X_0 = 0.
> $$
>
> Each flip changes the total by $+1$ with probability $\tfrac12$ and by $-1$ with probability $\tfrac12$. The game ends when the walk reaches either $-10$ or $20$.
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(\text{hit } -10 \text{ before } 20 \mid X_0=0).
> $$
>
> ### Shift the states
>
> Define
>
> $$
> Y_n = X_n + 10.
> $$
>
> Then the barriers become:
>
> - $X_n=-10 \iff Y_n=0$,
> - $X_n=20 \iff Y_n=30$,
>
> and the starting state is
>
> $$
> Y_0 = 10.
> $$
>
> So this is the classical symmetric gambler’s ruin problem on the states $0,1,\dots,30$, starting from $10$.
>
> For a fair walk, the probability of hitting $0$ before $N$ when starting from $i$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{N-i}{N}.
> $$
>
> Here $N=30$ and $i=10$, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{interviewer wins}) = \frac{30-10}{30} = \frac{20}{30} = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability that the interviewer wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. The 10-digit self-descriptive number
>
> We seek a $10$-digit number $d_0d_1d_2\cdots d_9$ such that for each $k \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$, the digit $d_k$ equals the number of occurrences of the digit $k$ in the whole number.
>
> Thus the digits must satisfy:
>
> - $d_0$ = number of $0$'s,
> - $d_1$ = number of $1$'s,
> - $\dots$,
> - $d_9$ = number of $9$'s.
>
> Also, since there are $10$ digits total,
>
> $$
> d_0 + d_1 + \cdots + d_9 = 10.
> $$
>
> A known candidate is
>
> $$
> 6210001000.
> $$
>
> Check it:
>
> - digit $0$ appears $6$ times,
> - digit $1$ appears $2$ times,
> - digit $2$ appears $1$ time,
> - digit $3$ appears $0$ times,
> - digit $4$ appears $0$ times,
> - digit $5$ appears $0$ times,
> - digit $6$ appears $1$ time,
> - digit $7$ appears $0$ times,
> - digit $8$ appears $0$ times,
> - digit $9$ appears $0$ times.
>
> So the digit counts are exactly
>
> $$
> (6,2,1,0,0,0,1,0,0,0),
> $$
>
> which reproduces the number itself.
>
> Hence the number is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6210001000}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Probability interviewer wins}=\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \boxed{6210001000}.
> $$
