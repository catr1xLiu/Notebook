> [!example] Question 326 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of flipping four heads in a row with a fair coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=327)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume a fair coin, so each flip is independent and
>
> $$
> P(\text{head})=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> To get $4$ heads in a row on $4$ consecutive flips, all four flips must be heads:
>
> $$
> HHHH.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{4 heads in a row})=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{16}}
> $$
>
> which is $0.0625$, or $6.25\%$.
