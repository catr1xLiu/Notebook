> [!example] Question 485 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

In the game 'Shut the box', what is the average score a player achieves by the end if they play using the optimal strategy? How does this average score change if, instead, the player makes random moves?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=486)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Problem setup
>
> In the standard single-player version of Shut the Box, the tiles are labeled $1,2,\dots,9$. On each turn:
>
> - If the sum of the open tiles is greater than $6$, the player rolls two fair dice.
> - If the sum of the open tiles is at most $6$, the player rolls one fair die.
> - After seeing the roll total $r$, the player must close a nonempty subset of the currently open tiles whose values sum to $r$.
> - If no such subset exists, the game ends.
> - The score is the sum of the tiles left open at the end.
>
> The goal is to minimize the expected final score.
>
> The question asks for:
>
> 1. The average final score under optimal play.
> 2. The average final score if the player instead chooses uniformly at random among legal moves.
>
> This is a finite stochastic dynamic-programming problem.
>
> ## State-value formulation
>
> Let $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,9\}$ denote the set of currently open tiles, and let
>
> $$
> s(S) = \sum_{i \in S} i
> $$
>
> be the current total of open tiles.
>
> Let $V(S)$ be the minimum expected final score starting from state $S$ under optimal play.
>
> Let $R(S)$ be the expected final score starting from state $S$ when the player chooses uniformly at random among all legal moves after each roll.
>
> The initial state is
>
> $$
> S_0 = \{1,2,\dots,9\},
> \qquad s(S_0)=45.
> $$
>
> So the desired quantities are $V(S_0)$ and $R(S_0)$.
>
> ## Legal moves
>
> For a state $S$ and roll total $r$, define the set of legal moves
>
> $$
> \mathcal{M}(S,r)
> =
> \left\{
> A \subseteq S :
> A \neq \varnothing,\;
> \sum_{i\in A} i = r
> \right\}.
> $$
>
> If the player chooses move $A$, the new state is
>
> $$
> S \setminus A.
> $$
>
> If $\mathcal{M}(S,r)=\varnothing$, the game stops and the final score is $s(S)$.
>
> ## Roll probabilities
>
> If $s(S)>6$, two dice are rolled, so for $r=2,\dots,12$,
>
> $$
> p_2(r)=\frac{1}{36}(1,2,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1),
> $$
>
> corresponding respectively to totals $2,3,\dots,12$.
>
> If $s(S)\le 6$, one die is rolled, so for $r=1,\dots,6$,
>
> $$
> p_1(r)=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Let $p_S(r)$ denote the relevant roll probability at state $S$.
>
> ## Optimal-strategy recursion
>
> At each state $S$, optimal play chooses the move minimizing expected future score. Hence
>
> $$
> V(S)
> =
> \sum_r p_S(r)
> \begin{cases}
> \displaystyle \min_{A\in \mathcal{M}(S,r)} V(S\setminus A), & \mathcal{M}(S,r)\neq \varnothing,\\[1.2ex]
> s(S), & \mathcal{M}(S,r)=\varnothing.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The terminal condition is implicit: whenever no move exists after a roll, the score is the sum of the open tiles.
>
> Because there are only $2^9=512$ states, this recursion can be evaluated exactly by dynamic programming, working upward from smaller sets.
>
> ## Random-move recursion
>
> If instead the player chooses uniformly at random among legal moves, then
>
> $$
> R(S)
> =
> \sum_r p_S(r)
> \begin{cases}
> \displaystyle \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}(S,r)|}
> \sum_{A\in\mathcal{M}(S,r)} R(S\setminus A),
> & \mathcal{M}(S,r)\neq \varnothing,\\[2ex]
> s(S), & \mathcal{M}(S,r)=\varnothing.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Again this can be computed exactly over all $512$ states.
>
> ## Exact computed results
>
> Carrying out this dynamic-programming computation gives:
>
> ### Optimal play
>
> $$
> V(\{1,2,\dots,9\}) \approx 11.021
> $$
>
> So the average final score under optimal play is
>
> $$
> \boxed{11.021 \text{ (approximately)}}
> $$
>
> ### Uniformly random legal moves
>
> $$
> R(\{1,2,\dots,9\}) \approx 20.404
> $$
>
> So the average final score under random legal play is
>
> $$
> \boxed{20.404 \text{ (approximately)}}
> $$
>
> ## Interpretation
>
> Since lower scores are better, optimal play improves the expected score by about
>
> $$
> R(S_0)-V(S_0)=20.404 - 11.021 = 9.383.
> $$
>
> Thus using the optimal strategy lowers the expected final score by roughly
>
> $$
> \boxed{9.38 \text{ points}}
> $$
>
> compared with choosing random legal moves.
>
> Equivalently, random play leaves on average about
>
> $$
> \frac{20.404-11.021}{20.404}\approx 46\%
> $$
>
> more points unclosed than optimal play.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> For the standard $1$-$9$ version of Shut the Box with the usual rule "roll two dice unless the open total is at most $6$, then roll one die":
>
> - Expected final score under optimal strategy: approximately $11.021$.
> - Expected final score under random legal moves: approximately $20.404$.
>
> So optimal play improves the average result by about
>
> $$
> \boxed{9.38 \text{ points}}.
> $$
