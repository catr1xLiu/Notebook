> [!example] Question 530 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

In the land of 10-digit numbers, what is the probability that the product of these numbers ends with five zeros?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=531)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability that the product of two randomly chosen $10$-digit numbers ends in exactly $5$ zeros, count factors of $10 = 2 \cdot 5$ in the product.
>
> A number ends in exactly $5$ zeros iff its prime factorization contains at least $5$ factors of $2$ and exactly $5$ factors of $5$ beyond what can be paired with $2$’s. Since factors of $2$ are much more common than factors of $5$, the number of trailing zeros is determined by the total number of factors of $5$ in the product, provided there are at least $5$ factors of $2$.
>
> For a random integer, the probability of divisibility by powers of $5$ is:
>
> - $P(v_5(n)\ge 1)=\frac15$
> - $P(v_5(n)\ge 2)=\frac1{25}$
> - $P(v_5(n)\ge 3)=\frac1{125}$, etc.
>
> Thus the distribution of $v_5(n)$ is:
>
> - $P(v_5(n)=0)=1-\frac15=\frac45$
> - $P(v_5(n)=1)=\frac15-\frac1{25}=\frac4{25}$
> - $P(v_5(n)=2)=\frac1{25}-\frac1{125}=\frac4{125}$
> - $P(v_5(n)=3)=\frac4{625}$
> - $P(v_5(n)=4)=\frac4{3125}$
> - $P(v_5(n)=5)=\frac4{15625}$
> - etc.
>
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be the numbers of factors of $5$ in the two random $10$-digit numbers. We need
>
> $$
> X+Y=5.
> $$
>
> So sum over all pairs:
>
> $$
> P(X+Y=5)=\sum_{k=0}^5 P(X=k)P(Y=5-k).
> $$
>
> Using $P(v_5=n)=\frac{4}{5^{n+1}}$ for $n\ge 0$,
>
> $$
> P(X+Y=5)=\sum_{k=0}^5 \frac{4}{5^{k+1}}\cdot \frac{4}{5^{6-k}}
> = \sum_{k=0}^5 \frac{16}{5^7}.
> $$
>
> There are $6$ terms, so
>
> $$
> P(X+Y=5)=6\cdot \frac{16}{5^7}
> = \frac{96}{78125}.
> $$
>
> Now check the factor of $2$ condition. The product will fail to have at least $5$ factors of $2$ only in extremely rare cases. For $10$-digit numbers this can be ignored in the usual interpretation, since almost every such product with $5$ factors of $5$ has at least $5$ factors of $2$.
>
> Therefore the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{96}{78125}}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> \frac{96}{78125}\approx 0.0012288.
> $$
