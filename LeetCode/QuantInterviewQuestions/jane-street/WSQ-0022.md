> [!example] Question 22 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Research Interview

You play a game where you roll two dice. If you get two sixes (6,6), you win \$100. If you get one six and the other die is not a six, you lose \$x. In all other cases, you can choose to roll both dice again. When is it optimal to play the game, in terms of the value of x?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=23)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the game cost $c$ to enter. We determine the fair value of the game, and then the game is worth playing whenever $c$ is less than that value.
>
> ## Step 1: Identify the possible outcomes in one roll
>
> When two fair dice are rolled:
>
> - $(6,6)$ occurs with probability
>   $$
>   \frac{1}{36},
>   $$
>   and pays $100$.
>
> - “One die is $6$ and the other is not $6$” occurs with probability
>   $$
>   \frac{10}{36},
>   $$
>   because there are $5$ outcomes of the form $(6,k)$ with $k \neq 6$, and $5$ of the form $(k,6)$ with $k \neq 6$.
>
> - “Neither of the above” means no die is $6$, which occurs with probability
>   $$
>   \frac{25}{36},
>   $$
>   and then the game is repeated.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up the expected value equation
>
> Let $V$ be the expected payoff of the game before paying any entry fee.
>
> From the first roll,
>
> - with probability $\frac{1}{36}$, payoff is $100$;
> - with probability $\frac{10}{36}$, payoff is $-x$;
> - with probability $\frac{25}{36}$, the game restarts, so the expected value is again $V$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{36}(100)+\frac{10}{36}(-x)+\frac{25}{36}V.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve for $V$
>
> Bring the restart term to the left:
> $$
> V-\frac{25}{36}V=\frac{100}{36}-\frac{10x}{36}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> \frac{11}{36}V=\frac{100-10x}{36}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> V=\frac{100-10x}{11}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Decide when to play
>
> If the game costs $c$ to play, the net expected gain is
> $$
> V-c=\frac{100-10x}{11}-c.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the game is worth playing when
> $$
> c<\frac{100-10x}{11}.
> $$
>
> In particular, if there is no entry fee, one should play exactly when
> $$
> V>0,
> $$
> that is,
> $$
> \frac{100-10x}{11}>0
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> 100-10x>0
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> x<10.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected value of the game is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{100-10x}{11}}.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - if there is **no entry fee**, play when
>   $$
>   \boxed{x<10};
>   $$
> - if the entry fee is $c$, play when
>   $$
>   \boxed{c<\frac{100-10x}{11}}.
>   $$
