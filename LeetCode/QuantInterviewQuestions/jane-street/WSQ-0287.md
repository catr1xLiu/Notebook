> [!example] Question 287 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Interview

How can you simulate the roll of a fair 15-sided die using a standard 6-sided die?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=288)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To simulate a fair $15$-sided die using a fair $6$-sided die, generate enough equally likely outcomes and map them to the numbers $1$ through $15$.
>
> ## Method: use two rolls
>
> Rolling a $6$-sided die twice gives
>
> $$
> 6 \times 6 = 36
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes. Label the two-roll result as an ordered pair $(a,b)$, where each of $a,b$ is in $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.
>
> Convert this pair to a number from $1$ to $36$ by
>
> $$
> N = 6(a-1) + b.
> $$
>
> So each value of $N \in \{1,2,\dots,36\}$ is equally likely.
>
> ## Rejection method
>
> Since $15$ does not divide $36$, use only the first $30$ outcomes, because
>
> $$
> 30 = 15 \times 2.
> $$
>
> - If $N \le 30$, output
>   $$
>   ((N-1) \bmod 15) + 1.
>   $$
>   This gives a uniform result in $\{1,2,\dots,15\}$.
> - If $N > 30$ (that is, $N=31,32,33,34,35,36$), ignore the result and roll twice again.
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Among the accepted outcomes $1$ through $30$, each final value $1$ through $15$ appears exactly twice, so each has probability
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{30} = \frac{1}{15}.
> $$
>
> Thus the simulation is fair.
>
> ## Example mapping
>
> You may map the accepted outcomes as follows:
>
> $$
> 1,16 \to 1,\quad
> 2,17 \to 2,\quad
> \dots,\quad
> 15,30 \to 15.
> $$
>
> Equivalently:
>
> - $1,2,\dots,15$ correspond to $1,2,\dots,15$
> - $16,17,\dots,30$ correspond again to $1,2,\dots,15$
>
> Reject $31$ through $36$ and repeat.
>
> ## Final procedure
>
> 1. Roll the $6$-sided die twice.
> 2. Compute $N = 6(a-1)+b$.
> 3. If $N \le 30$, return $((N-1) \bmod 15)+1$.
> 4. If $N > 30$, roll twice again.
>
> This simulates a fair $15$-sided die.
