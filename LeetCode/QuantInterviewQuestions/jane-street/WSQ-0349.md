> [!example] Question 349 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Given 100 coin flips, what is the probability of getting an even number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=350)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $100$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial expansion,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})
> = \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> A standard identity gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k}
> =
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ odd}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k}
> =
> 2^{n-1}
> \quad \text{for } n \ge 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})
> =
> \frac{2^{99}}{2^{100}}
> =
> \frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability of getting an even number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
