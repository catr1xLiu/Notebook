> [!example] Question 494 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

If we flip a coin 100 times, what is the probability of getting an even number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=495)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $100$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})=\sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Use the binomial identities
>
> $$
> (1+1)^{100}=\sum_{k=0}^{100}\binom{100}{k}=2^{100}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^{100}=\sum_{k=0}^{100}\binom{100}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> The second identity implies that the sum of the even-indexed binomial coefficients equals the sum of the odd-indexed ones. Since their total is $2^{100}$, each sum must be
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{100}\binom{100}{k}=2^{99}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})
> = \frac{2^{99}}{2^{100}}
> = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability of getting an even number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\tfrac12}.
> $$
