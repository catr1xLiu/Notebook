> [!example] Question 300 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

Player 1 chooses a number, then Player 2 chooses a different number. Two dice are tossed, and whoever is closer to the outcome (the sum of the two dice) wins. Would you rather be Player 1 or Player 2? What number would you choose?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=301)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the outcome of the two dice be the **sum** of the faces. Then the possible outcomes are
>
> $$
> 2,3,4,\dots,12
> $$
>
> with probabilities
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{36}(1,2,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1).
> $$
>
> Thus the distribution is symmetric about $7$.
>
> We compare choices by asking: for a chosen number $a$ and an opponent’s number $b\neq a$, which outcomes are closer to $a$ and which are closer to $b$? Since ties in distance can occur, those outcomes contribute equally to both players and do not affect which choice is better.
>
> ## 1. Why Player 1 should choose $7$
>
> The number $7$ is the center of the distribution and also the most likely sum. Intuitively it is the strongest first move. To verify this, compare $7$ with any other choice.
>
> If Player 1 chooses $7$ and Player 2 chooses $b$, then because $b\neq 7$, the set of sums closer to $7$ than to $b$ always carries at least as much probability as the set closer to $b$ than to $7$. This follows from the symmetry and the fact that probability decreases as one moves away from $7$.
>
> It is enough to check the nearest rival choices:
>
> - Against $6$, the midpoint is $6.5$, so $7$ wins on sums $7,8,\dots,12$ and $6$ wins on $2,3,4,5,6$:
>   $$
>   P(7\text{ wins})=\frac{6+5+4+3+2+1}{36}=\frac{21}{36},
>   $$
>   $$
>   P(6\text{ wins})=\frac{1+2+3+4+5}{36}=\frac{15}{36}.
>   $$
> - Against $8$, by symmetry the same comparison holds:
>   $$
>   P(7\text{ wins})=\frac{21}{36}, \qquad P(8\text{ wins})=\frac{15}{36}.
>   $$
>
> Choices farther from $7$ do even worse, since they move away from the high-probability center.
>
> Therefore $7$ is the optimal first choice.
>
> ## 2. Best response for Player 2 if Player 1 chooses $7$
>
> Now suppose Player 1 chooses $7$. Player 2 must choose a different number. The best options are the nearest numbers, $6$ or $8$, since any number farther away loses more central outcomes.
>
> Indeed:
>
> - If Player 2 chooses $6$, then Player 2 wins exactly when the sum is at most $6$:
>   $$
>   P(\text{Player 2 wins})=\frac{1+2+3+4+5}{36}=\frac{15}{36}.
>   $$
> - If Player 2 chooses $5$, then the midpoint is $6$, so Player 2 wins only on sums $2,3,4,5$, while sum $6$ is tied:
>   $$
>   P(\text{Player 2 wins})=\frac{1+2+3+4}{36}=\frac{10}{36},
>   $$
>   which is worse.
> - Similarly, choosing $8$ gives $\frac{15}{36}$, and choices farther away are worse.
>
> So the best response to $7$ is $6$ or $8$.
>
> ## 3. Conclusion
>
> Since Player 1 can choose $7$, and then Player 2’s best possible response is only $6$ or $8$, both of which are inferior to $7$, the advantage lies with Player 1.
>
> - **Be Player 1.**
> - **Choose $7$.**
>
> If forced to be Player 2 against an optimal Player 1, choose either $6$ or $8$.
