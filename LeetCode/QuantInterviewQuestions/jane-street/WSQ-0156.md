> [!example] Question 156 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

A grey cube of size 3x3x3 is painted red on all its edges. Then, it is split into 1x1x1 cubes. One of these small cubes is chosen uniformly at random and placed randomly on a table. What is the probability that no red edges are visible on this small cube?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=157)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the large cube be a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube whose outer edges are painted red, and then cut into $27$ unit cubes.
>
> A small cube is chosen uniformly from these $27$ cubes, and then placed on the table in a uniformly random orientation. We want the probability that **no red edge is visible**.
>
> ## 1. Classify the small cubes by how many red edges they have
>
> Only the cubes touching the large cube’s edges receive red paint.
>
> In a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube:
>
> - **Corner cubes**: $8$ cubes, each has $3$ red edges.
> - **Edge-middle cubes**: each large edge has one middle unit cube, so there are
>   $$
>   12
>   $$
>   such cubes, each with $1$ red edge.
> - **Face-center cubes**: $6$ cubes, with $0$ red edges.
> - **Interior cube**: $1$ cube, with $0$ red edges.
>
> So:
>
> - $8$ cubes have $3$ red edges,
> - $12$ cubes have $1$ red edge,
> - $7$ cubes have $0$ red edges.
>
> ## 2. Visibility condition after throwing onto the table
>
> When the cube lands on the table, exactly:
>
> - $1$ face is on the table,
> - $1$ opposite face is on top,
> - $4$ side faces are visible.
>
> Thus the only edges **not visible** are the $4$ edges of the bottom face. All other $8$ edges are visible.
>
> Therefore, for a chosen small cube to show **no red edges**, all of its red edges must lie on the bottom face.
>
> ## 3. Compute the probability for each cube type
>
> ### Case 1: Cube with $0$ red edges
> This always works.
>
> Probability contribution:
> $$
> \frac{7}{27}\cdot 1
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: Cube with $1$ red edge
> A given edge of a cube is on the bottom face exactly when the bottom face is one of the two faces containing that edge.
>
> Since any of the $6$ faces is equally likely to be the bottom face, the probability the red edge lies on the bottom face is
> $$
> \frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Contribution:
> $$
> \frac{12}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{3}
> $$
>
> ### Case 3: Cube with $3$ red edges
> These are the $3$ edges meeting at one vertex. For all $3$ red edges to be invisible, they would all need to lie on the bottom face.
>
> But a face has only $4$ edges, and among the $3$ edges meeting at a vertex, no single face contains all $3$ of them. In fact, each face through that vertex contains only $2$ of those $3$ edges.
>
> So this is impossible, and the probability is
> $$
> 0.
> $$
>
> Contribution:
> $$
> \frac{8}{27}\cdot 0
> $$
>
> ## 4. Add the contributions
>
> $$
> P=\frac{7}{27}+\frac{12}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{3}
> =\frac{7}{27}+\frac{4}{27}
> =\frac{11}{27}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{27}}
> $$
