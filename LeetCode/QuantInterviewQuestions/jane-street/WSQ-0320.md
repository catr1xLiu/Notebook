> [!example] Question 320 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Suppose you are offered the opportunity to bet on a coin flip resulting in heads, with the odds increasing from 1.5:1 up to 7:1. Explain your reasoning for whether you would bet or not bet at each odds level.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=321)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider a single coin flip, and suppose you may bet on the outcome being heads. The offered odds increase from $1.5:1$ up to $7:1$. The question is when such a bet is favorable and when it is not.
>
> ## 1. Set up the expected value
>
> Assume the coin is fair, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{heads}) = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad P(\text{tails}) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Suppose you stake $1$ unit.
>
> If the odds are $a:1$, then:
>
> - if heads occurs, your profit is $a$ units,
> - if tails occurs, your loss is $1$ unit.
>
> Thus the expected profit is
>
> $$
> E = \frac{1}{2}(a) + \frac{1}{2}(-1)
> = \frac{a-1}{2}.
> $$
>
> This determines whether the bet is worth taking:
>
> - if $E > 0$, the bet is favorable;
> - if $E = 0$, the bet is fair;
> - if $E < 0$, the bet is unfavorable.
>
> ## 2. Find the break-even odds
>
> Set the expected value equal to zero:
>
> $$
> \frac{a-1}{2} = 0.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> a = 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the break-even point is odds of $1:1$.
>
> This means:
>
> - at odds below $1:1$, do not bet;
> - at odds exactly $1:1$, you are indifferent;
> - at odds above $1:1$, you should bet.
>
> Since the offered odds start at $1.5:1$ and rise to $7:1$, every one of these bets is favorable if the coin is fair.
>
> ## 3. Evaluate the offered odds
>
> For several values:
>
> ### At $1.5:1$
>
> $$
> E = \frac{1.5-1}{2} = 0.25.
> $$
>
> Expected profit is $0.25$ units per unit staked, so this is a good bet.
>
> ### At $2:1$
>
> $$
> E = \frac{2-1}{2} = 0.5.
> $$
>
> Expected profit is $0.5$ units per unit staked.
>
> ### At $3:1$
>
> $$
> E = \frac{3-1}{2} = 1.
> $$
>
> Expected profit is $1$ unit per unit staked.
>
> ### At $5:1$
>
> $$
> E = \frac{5-1}{2} = 2.
> $$
>
> Expected profit is $2$ units per unit staked.
>
> ### At $7:1$
>
> $$
> E = \frac{7-1}{2} = 3.
> $$
>
> Expected profit is $3$ units per unit staked.
>
> As the odds increase, the expected value increases linearly, so the bet becomes more attractive.
>
> ## 4. Reasoning about whether to bet
>
> If the coin is truly fair and there are no betting limits, no hidden costs, and no risk constraints, then the rational decision based purely on expected value is:
>
> - bet at $1.5:1$,
> - continue betting at all higher odds,
> - prefer larger stakes as the odds improve, subject to your tolerance for risk.
>
> The reason is simple: for a fair coin, you win half the time. If you are paid more than $1$ unit of profit on a $1$ unit stake when you win, while only losing $1$ unit when you lose, then the average gain is positive.
>
> ## 5. Generalization to a biased coin
>
> If the probability of heads is $p$ rather than $\frac{1}{2}$, then the expected profit from a $1$-unit stake at odds $a:1$ is
>
> $$
> E = pa - (1-p).
> $$
>
> The break-even condition is
>
> $$
> pa - (1-p) = 0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> a = \frac{1-p}{p}.
> $$
>
> Thus you should bet only when
>
> $$
> a > \frac{1-p}{p}.
> $$
>
> For a fair coin, $p = \frac{1}{2}$, giving
>
> $$
> a > \frac{1/2}{1/2} = 1.
> $$
>
> This matches the result above.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> For a fair coin, the expected profit on a $1$-unit bet at odds $a:1$ is
>
> $$
> E = \frac{a-1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So the bet becomes favorable as soon as the odds exceed $1:1$. Since the offered odds range from $1.5:1$ to $7:1$, every such bet is favorable, and the higher the odds, the stronger the case for betting.
