> [!example] Question 368 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

There are four coins. For each heads you get, you receive \$1. After flipping all four coins, you may re-flip one coin of your choice. What is the maximum amount you would pay to play this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=369)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the payoff be the number of heads after the four initial flips and, if desired, one re-flip of a single coin. The maximum fair price to pay is the expected value of the game under optimal play.
>
> ## Step 1: Value of the initial four flips
>
> If no re-flip were allowed, the expected number of heads from four fair coins would be
>
> $$
> 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2.
> $$
>
> The option to re-flip can only improve this expected value.
>
> ## Step 2: Optimal re-flip strategy
>
> After seeing the initial four flips, suppose there are $k$ heads and $4-k$ tails.
>
> A head is already worth $\text{\textdollar}1$, so re-flipping a head would replace a sure $\text{\textdollar}1$ by an expected value of
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}(1) + \frac{1}{2}(0) = \frac{1}{2},
> $$
>
> which is worse.
>
> A tail is currently worth $\text{\textdollar}0$, and re-flipping it gives expected value
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}(1) + \frac{1}{2}(0) = \frac{1}{2},
> $$
>
> which is better.
>
> Therefore, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - if there is at least one tail, re-flip one tail;
> - if all four are heads, do not re-flip.
>
> ## Step 3: Expected gain from the re-flip option
>
> The only time the option is useless is when all four coins are heads, which occurs with probability
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4 = \frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> So with probability
>
> $$
> 1 - \frac{1}{16} = \frac{15}{16},
> $$
>
> there is at least one tail, and re-flipping one tail adds an expected gain of
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected extra value from the option is
>
> $$
> \frac{15}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{32}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Total expected value
>
> Thus the expected payoff under optimal play is
>
> $$
> 2 + \frac{15}{32} = \frac{64}{32} + \frac{15}{32} = \frac{79}{32}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the maximum fair amount to pay is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{79}{32}}
> $$
>
> dollars, i.e.
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2.46875}.
> $$
