> [!example] Question 592 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You repeatedly flip a fair coin until either the sequence HHT or HTT appears. If you can choose which combination to bet on (HHT or HTT), which should you pick and why? Calculate the probability of winning for each choice.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=593)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> We compare the two target patterns
>
> - $A = \text{HHT}$
> - $B = \text{HTT}$
>
> when a fair coin is tossed repeatedly until one of these two patterns appears first. We seek the probability that each pattern appears before the other.
>
> ### Step 1: Relevant states
>
> Because both target patterns have length $3$, it is enough to track the current suffix of recent tosses that could still grow into one of the targets. The relevant states are:
>
> - $S$: start state (no useful suffix),
> - $H$: the current relevant suffix is $\text{H}$,
> - $HH$: the current relevant suffix is $\text{HH}$,
> - $HT$: the current relevant suffix is $\text{HT}$.
>
> Let
>
> - $p_S$ be the probability that $\text{HHT}$ occurs before $\text{HTT}$ starting from $S$,
> - $p_H, p_{HH}, p_{HT}$ defined similarly.
>
> We then write recursive equations.
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 2: Recurrence relations
>
> From the start state $S$:
>
> - Tossing $\text{H}$ leads to state $H$,
> - Tossing $\text{T}$ leaves no useful suffix, so we remain in $S$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_S = \frac12 p_H + \frac12 p_S.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> p_S = p_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Tossing $\text{H}$ gives suffix $\text{HH}$,
> - Tossing $\text{T}$ gives suffix $\text{HT}$.
>
> So
> $$
> p_H = \frac12 p_{HH} + \frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Tossing $\text{T}$ completes $\text{HHT}$, so this is a win for pattern $A$,
> - Tossing $\text{H}$ leaves suffix $\text{HH}$ again.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HH} = \frac12 \cdot 1 + \frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_{HH} = 1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - Tossing $\text{T}$ completes $\text{HTT}$, so pattern $A$ loses,
> - Tossing $\text{H}$ gives suffix $\text{H}$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HT} = \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 p_H = \frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 3: Solve
>
> Using
> $$
> p_H = \frac12 p_{HH} + \frac12 p_{HT},
> $$
> with $p_{HH}=1$ and $p_{HT}=\frac12 p_H$, we get
> $$
> p_H = \frac12 \cdot 1 + \frac12 \cdot \frac12 p_H
> = \frac12 + \frac14 p_H.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> \frac34 p_H = \frac12,
> $$
> so
> $$
> p_H = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Since $p_S = p_H$,
> $$
> p_S = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> P(\text{HHT appears before HTT}) = \frac{2}{3},
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> P(\text{HTT appears before HHT}) = 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The better strategy is to choose $\text{HHT}$, because it appears before $\text{HTT}$ with probability
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
>
> The probabilities are
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{HHT wins}) = \frac{2}{3}}, \qquad
> \boxed{P(\text{HTT wins}) = \frac{1}{3}}.
> $$
>
> So $\text{HHT}$ is twice as likely to occur first as $\text{HTT}$.
