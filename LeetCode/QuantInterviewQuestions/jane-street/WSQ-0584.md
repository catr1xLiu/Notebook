> [!example] Question 584 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Consider the numbers from 1 to 1,000,000. How many times does the digit '2' appear in all of these numbers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=585)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Count the occurrences of the digit $2$ in all integers from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$.
>
> ## Step 1: Count from $0$ to $999{,}999$
>
> Write all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$ as $6$-digit strings, allowing leading zeros.  
> There are $1{,}000{,}000$ such strings.
>
> For each of the $6$ digit positions:
>
> - each digit $0,1,2,\dots,9$ appears equally often,
> - so each digit appears
>   $$
>   \frac{1{,}000{,}000}{10} = 100{,}000
>   $$
>   times in each position.
>
> Thus, the digit $2$ appears
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 100{,}000 = 600{,}000
> $$
>
> times among all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$.
>
> ## Step 2: Adjust to the range $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The number $0$ contributes no digit $2$, so the count from $1$ to $999{,}999$ is still $600{,}000$.
>
> Now include $1{,}000{,}000$. Its digits are $1,0,0,0,0,0,0$, so it contains no digit $2$.
>
> Therefore the total number of digit $2$'s from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> 600{,}000.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{600{,}000}
> $$
