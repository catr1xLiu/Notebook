> [!example] Question 660 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

How would you bet in a best-of-seven series so that you win 100 dollars if the team you support wins the series, and lose 100 dollars otherwise?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=661)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the probability that your team wins the best-of-$7$ series be reflected in the market odds game by game. To guarantee a net result of $+\text{\textdollar}100$ if your team wins the series and $-\text{\textdollar}100$ if it loses, choose stakes recursively so that after each game your remaining position still has the same desired payoff structure.
>
> ## Step 1: Define the target payoff
>
> You want your final net profit to be
>
> - $+\text{\textdollar}100$ if your team wins the series,
> - $-\text{\textdollar}100$ if your team loses the series.
>
> At any point in the series, suppose your team still needs $a$ more wins and the opponent needs $b$ more wins. Let $V(a,b)$ denote the value of your position at that state. Then the terminal conditions are
>
> $$
> V(0,b)=100,\qquad V(a,0)=-100.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Fair-value recursion
>
> If each game is an even-money proposition, then from state $(a,b)$ the next game moves to either
>
> - $(a-1,b)$ if your team wins,
> - $(a,b-1)$ if your team loses.
>
> Under fair betting, the current value is the average of the two continuation values:
>
> $$
> V(a,b)=\frac{V(a-1,b)+V(a,b-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> This determines all intermediate values.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the values backward
>
> For a best-of-$7$ series, the initial state is $(4,4)$.
>
> Using the recursion:
>
> $$
> V(1,1)=\frac{100+(-100)}{2}=0.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(1,2)=\frac{100+0}{2}=50,\qquad
> V(2,1)=\frac{0+(-100)}{2}=-50.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(1,3)=\frac{100+50}{2}=75,\qquad
> V(3,1)=\frac{-50+(-100)}{2}=-75.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(2,2)=\frac{50+(-50)}{2}=0.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(1,4)=\frac{100+75}{2}=87.5,\qquad
> V(4,1)=\frac{-75+(-100)}{2}=-87.5.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(2,3)=\frac{75+0}{2}=37.5,\qquad
> V(3,2)=\frac{0+(-75)}{2}=-37.5.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(3,3)=\frac{37.5+(-37.5)}{2}=0.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(2,4)=\frac{87.5+37.5}{2}=62.5,\qquad
> V(4,2)=\frac{-37.5+(-87.5)}{2}=-62.5.
> $$
>
> $$
> V(3,4)=\frac{62.5+0}{2}=31.25,\qquad
> V(4,3)=\frac{0+(-62.5)}{2}=-31.25.
> $$
>
> Finally,
>
> $$
> V(4,4)=\frac{31.25+(-31.25)}{2}=0.
> $$
>
> So initially the fair value of the desired contract is $\text{\textdollar}0$.
>
> ## Step 4: Determine the bet size each game
>
> At state $(a,b)$, let the stake on the next game be $s(a,b)$, positive if you bet on your team, negative if you bet against it. Since the game is even money,
>
> - if your team wins the next game, your position changes by $+s(a,b)$,
> - if it loses, your position changes by $-s(a,b)$.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> V(a-1,b)=V(a,b)+s(a,b),
> $$
>
> $$
> V(a,b-1)=V(a,b)-s(a,b).
> $$
>
> Subtracting gives
>
> $$
> s(a,b)=\frac{V(a-1,b)-V(a,b-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Now compute the stakes.
>
> ### Initial game: state $(4,4)$
>
> $$
> s(4,4)=\frac{V(3,4)-V(4,3)}{2}
> =\frac{31.25-(-31.25)}{2}=31.25.
> $$
>
> So bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$ on your team in Game $1$.
>
> ## Step 5: Continue according to the series score
>
> After each game, recompute from the new state.
>
> ### If the series is tied $0$-$0$ initially:
> - Bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$ on Game $1$.
>
> ### If after one game your team leads $1$-$0$:
> state $(3,4)$, so
>
> $$
> s(3,4)=\frac{V(2,4)-V(3,3)}{2}
> =\frac{62.5-0}{2}=31.25.
> $$
>
> Bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$ on Game $2$.
>
> ### If after one game your team trails $0$-$1$:
> state $(4,3)$, so
>
> $$
> s(4,3)=\frac{V(3,3)-V(4,2)}{2}
> =\frac{0-(-62.5)}{2}=31.25.
> $$
>
> Again bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$ on Game $2$.
>
> Continuing similarly, the required stake depends only on how many wins remain. The full pattern is:
>
> - When one side needs $4$ wins and the other also needs $4$: bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$.
> - When the state is $(3,4)$ or $(4,3)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}31.25$.
> - When the state is $(2,4)$ or $(4,2)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}25$.
> - When the state is $(3,3)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}37.50$.
> - When the state is $(1,4)$ or $(4,1)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}12.50$.
> - When the state is $(2,3)$ or $(3,2)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}37.50$.
> - When the state is $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}25$.
> - When the state is $(2,2)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}50$.
> - When the state is $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}50$.
> - When the state is $(1,1)$: bet $\text{\textdollar}100$.
>
> In every case, the bet is placed **on your team to win the next game**.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A betting strategy that guarantees
>
> $$
> +\$100 \text{ if your team wins the series,}
> \qquad
> -\$100 \text{ if it loses}
> $$
>
> is to bet on your team each game according to the current series state, with stakes determined backward from the terminal payoffs. In particular, for an initially tied best-of-$7$ series, the first bet is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$31.25}
> $$
>
> on your team, and after each game you update the next stake using
>
> $$
> s(a,b)=\frac{V(a-1,b)-V(a,b-1)}{2},
> \qquad
> V(0,b)=100,\quad V(a,0)=-100,\quad
> V(a,b)=\frac{V(a-1,b)+V(a,b-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> This produces a self-financing strategy with the required final payoff.
