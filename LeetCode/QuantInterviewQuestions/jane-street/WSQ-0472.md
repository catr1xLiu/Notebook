> [!example] Question 472 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You may throw a six-sided die and, if you are dissatisfied with the result, you may re-roll it once. How should you decide when to keep the initial roll and when to re-roll in order to maximize the expected value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=473)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the result of the first roll be $X \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. After seeing $X$, you may either keep it or discard it and take a second roll $Y$, which must then be accepted.
>
> The problem is to choose a rule that maximizes the expected final value.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the value of re-rolling
>
> If you reject the first roll, your final outcome is the second roll $Y$, which is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[Y] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So re-rolling is worth an expected value of $3.5$.
>
> ## Step 2: Optimal decision after observing the first roll
>
> After seeing a first-roll value $x$, you should compare:
>
> - keeping it, which gives value $x$;
> - re-rolling, which gives expected value $3.5$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - if $x > 3.5$, keep $x$;
> - if $x < 3.5$, re-roll;
> - if $x = 3.5$, either choice is equivalent.
>
> Since $x$ must be an integer, this means:
>
> - re-roll on $1,2,3$;
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> This is the optimal strategy.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the maximal expected value
>
> Under this strategy, the final payoff is
>
> - $3.5$ when the first roll is $1,2,3$ (because you re-roll),
> - $4,5,6$ when the first roll is $4,5,6$.
>
> Therefore the expected value is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}\left(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6\right)
> = \frac{1}{6}(10.5+15)
> = \frac{25.5}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\max(X, \text{reroll value in expectation})]
> = \frac{1}{6}(3+3+3)\cdot \frac{3.5}{3} + \frac{4+5+6}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> To maximize the expected value, **re-roll if the first result is $1$, $2$, or $3$, and keep it if it is $4$, $5$, or $6$**.
>
> The maximum expected value is
>
> $$
> 4.25 = \frac{17}{4}.
> $$
