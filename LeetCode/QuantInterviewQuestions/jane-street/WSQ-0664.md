> [!example] Question 664 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting an odd number of heads when flipping 200 fair coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=665)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume each coin is fair and independent. Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained in $200$ flips. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(200,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want the probability that the number of heads is odd:
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable with $p=\tfrac12$, the probabilities of getting an even number and an odd number are equal. Since these two cases exhaust all possibilities,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd}) = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
