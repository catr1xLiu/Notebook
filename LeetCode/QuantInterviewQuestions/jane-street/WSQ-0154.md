> [!example] Question 154 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

You have a shuffled deck of 26 red and 26 black cards. You play a game by repeatedly looking at the top card and either discarding it or ending the game. At the end, if the color of the next card matches the top card, you win; otherwise, you lose. What is the optimal strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=155)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V(r,b)$ denote the maximum probability of winning when $r$ red and $b$ black cards remain (including the currently visible top card), under optimal play.
>
> ## 1. Boundary values
>
> If only a single card remains in total, there is no further card to compare it to, so no win is possible from that point:
>
> $$
> V(1,0)=V(0,1)=0.
> $$
>
> If instead at least two cards remain and all remaining cards share one color — say $r=0$ and $b\ge 2$, so the visible top card is black — then stopping immediately guarantees a match, since the next card is certainly black as well:
>
> $$
> V(0,b)=1\ (b\ge 2), \qquad V(r,0)=1\ (r\ge 2).
> $$
>
> ## 2. Dynamic programming formulation
>
> Suppose $r,b\ge 1$ and the top card is red. There are two possible actions:
>
> - **Stop now.** Then you win exactly when the next card is also red. Among the remaining $r+b-1$ unseen cards, $r-1$ are red, so the win probability is $\frac{r-1}{r+b-1}$.
> - **Discard the top red card.** Then the game continues from state $(r-1,b)$, with success probability $V(r-1,b)$.
>
> Hence, upon seeing a red top card, the optimal value is
>
> $$
> V_R(r,b)=\max\!\left\{\frac{r-1}{r+b-1},\,V(r-1,b)\right\}.
> $$
>
> Similarly, if the top card is black,
>
> $$
> V_B(r,b)=\max\!\left\{\frac{b-1}{r+b-1},\,V(r,b-1)\right\}.
> $$
>
> Before revealing the top card, with $r$ red and $b$ black cards remaining, the top card is red with probability $\frac{r}{r+b}$ and black with probability $\frac{b}{r+b}$, so
>
> $$
> V(r,b)=\frac{r}{r+b}V_R(r,b)+\frac{b}{r+b}V_B(r,b).
> $$
>
> ## 3. Solving the recursion
>
> We claim that for all $r+b\ge 2$,
>
> $$
> V(r,b) = 1-\frac{rb}{(r+b)(r+b-1)}.
> $$
>
> This immediately matches the boundary values above: whenever $r=0$ or $b=0$ (with $r+b\ge 2$), the fraction vanishes and $V=1$.
>
> To confirm this is the correct optimal value, first check that the formula is consistent with always discarding whenever the recursion is applied in "pure discard" form, i.e. that it satisfies
>
> $$
> (r+b)V(r,b) = r\,V(r-1,b)+b\,V(r,b-1),
> $$
>
> which one can verify by direct substitution.
>
> Next, confirm that discarding really is the better action whenever it should be. For a red top card, compare discarding to stopping:
>
> $$
> V(r-1,b)-\frac{r-1}{r+b-1} = \frac{b(b-1)}{(r+b-1)(r+b-2)}.
> $$
>
> This quantity is exactly $0$ when $b=1$, and strictly positive whenever $b\ge 2$. By the symmetric computation for a black top card, discarding is strictly better than stopping whenever the color *other than* the one currently showing has at least $2$ cards remaining, and the two actions are exactly tied when the opposite color has only $1$ card left.
>
> ## 4. Optimal strategy
>
> Consequently, the optimal rule is:
>
> - **Discard** the current top card as long as the color other than the one showing still has at least $2$ cards remaining.
> - **Stop** once the color other than the one showing has $0$ or $1$ cards left (at that point stopping is at least as good as discarding, and strictly better once it has $0$ left).
>
> Equivalently: keep discarding as long as both colors still have at least $2$ cards remaining; stop the instant you would otherwise be looking at a top card whose opposite color is down to at most $1$.
>
> ## 5. Initial state and value of the game
>
> Initially $r=b=26$. Applying the formula from Section 3,
>
> $$
> V(26,26) = 1-\frac{26\cdot 26}{52\cdot 51} = 1-\frac{676}{2652} = 1-\frac{13}{51} = \frac{38}{51}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> **Keep discarding cards as long as both remaining colors still have at least $2$ cards left; stop the moment the color opposite your current top card has only $0$ or $1$ cards remaining.**
>
> With this strategy, the probability of winning starting from a full shuffled deck is
>
> $$
> \boxed{V(26,26)=\frac{38}{51}\approx 0.745}.
> $$
