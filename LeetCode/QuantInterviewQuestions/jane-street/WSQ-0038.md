> [!example] Question 38 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You flip a fair coin repeatedly. What is the expected number of flips required to see the sequence 'HHT' for the first time?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=39)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of flips needed to obtain the pattern $\text{HHT}$ for the first time.
>
> ## State-based setup
>
> Track the longest suffix of the observed sequence that is also a prefix of $\text{HHT}$.
>
> Define:
>
> - $E_0$: expected additional flips starting with no matched prefix,
> - $E_1$: expected additional flips starting with matched prefix $\text{H}$,
> - $E_2$: expected additional flips starting with matched prefix $\text{HH}$.
>
> The target state $\text{HHT}$ is absorbing, with expected additional time $0$.
>
> ## Recurrence equations
>
> ### From state $E_0$
> With one flip:
>
> - flip $\text{H}$ with probability $\frac12$, moving to state $E_1$,
> - flip $\text{T}$ with probability $\frac12$, staying in state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### From state $E_1$
> With one flip:
>
> - flip $\text{H}$ with probability $\frac12$, moving to state $E_2$,
> - flip $\text{T}$ with probability $\frac12$, since $\text{HT}$ has no suffix that is a prefix of $\text{HHT}$, return to state $E_0$.
>
> So
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### From state $E_2$
> With one flip:
>
> - flip $\text{T}$ with probability $\frac12$, obtaining $\text{HHT}$ and finishing,
> - flip $\text{H}$ with probability $\frac12$, the suffix is still $\text{HH}$, so remain in state $E_2$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 E_2.
> $$
>
> ## Solve the system
>
> From the last equation,
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_2
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> \frac12 E_2 = 1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_2 = 2.
> $$
>
> From the first equation,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 2 + E_1.
> $$
>
> Substitute $E_2 = 2$ into the equation for $E_1$:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 \cdot 2 + \frac12 E_0
> = 2 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Now use $E_0 = 2 + E_1$:
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + \left(2 + \frac12 E_0\right)
> = 4 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \frac12 E_0 = 4
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 8.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of flips needed to get $\text{HHT}$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{8}.
> $$
