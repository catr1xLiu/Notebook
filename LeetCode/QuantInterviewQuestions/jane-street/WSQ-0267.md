> [!example] Question 267 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected value of a die roll? Additionally, create a market (bid-ask spread) based on your expectation.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=268)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For a fair six-sided die with outcomes $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each occurring with probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$, the expected value is
>
> $$
> E[X] = \sum_{k=1}^{6} k \cdot \frac{1}{6}
> = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6}
> = \frac{21}{6}
> = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected value of a fair die roll is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> To “make a market” on the die outcome, a natural fair price for a contract that pays the rolled number is its expectation, so the fair market price is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> A simple symmetric market around this fair value is:
>
> - **Bid:** $3.45$
> - **Ask:** $3.55$
>
> This means one is willing to buy the payoff contract at $3.45$ and sell it at $3.55$, with midpoint
>
> $$
> \frac{3.45+3.55}{2} = 3.50.
> $$
>
> So the fair value is $3.5$, and an example market is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.45 \; / \; 3.55}.
> $$
