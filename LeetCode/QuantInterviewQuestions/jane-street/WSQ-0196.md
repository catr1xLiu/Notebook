> [!example] Question 196 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

What is the probability that exactly half of the coins show heads when n fair coins are flipped?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=197)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $n$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> The probability of getting exactly half heads means
>
> $$
> X = \frac{n}{2}.
> $$
>
> This is possible only when $n$ is even.
>
> For a binomial random variable,
>
> $$
> \Pr(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^k\left(\tfrac12\right)^{n-k}
> =\binom{n}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> Substituting $k=\frac{n}{2}$ gives, for even $n$,
>
> $$
> \Pr\!\left(X=\frac{n}{2}\right)
> =
> \binom{n}{n/2}\left(\tfrac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> If $n$ is odd, this probability is $0$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \Pr(\text{exactly half of the flips are heads})=
> \begin{cases}
> \displaystyle \binom{n}{n/2}\,2^{-n}, & \text{if } n \text{ is even},\\[1em]
> 0, & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> For large even $n$, this can be approximated using Stirling’s formula:
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{n/2}2^{-n} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}}.
> $$
>
> So the exact answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \Pr(\text{half heads in } n \text{ flips})=
> \begin{cases}
> \displaystyle \binom{n}{n/2}2^{-n}, & n \text{ even},\\[1em]
> 0, & n \text{ odd}.
> \end{cases}}
> $$
