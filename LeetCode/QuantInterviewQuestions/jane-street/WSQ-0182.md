> [!example] Question 182 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

If I spin a roulette wheel, roll a die, and pick a card from a 52-card deck, what is the probability that all three show the same number? How confident are you in your answer?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=183)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the roulette wheel is the standard American wheel with numbers $1$ through $36$ plus $0$ and $00$, the die is a standard six-sided die with outcomes $1$ through $6$, and the card’s value is its rank with Ace counted as $1$, so the possible card values are $1,2,\dots,10,11,12,13$.
>
> To have all three results be the same number, that common number must be one that can appear on all three devices:
>
> - the die can show only $1$ through $6$,
> - the card can have value only $1$ through $13$,
> - the roulette wheel has $1$ through $36$ (but not $00$ as a number).
>
> Thus the only possible common values are
> $$
> 1,2,3,4,5,6.
> $$
>
> For any fixed number $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$:
>
> - $P(\text{roulette}=k)=\frac{1}{38}$ on an American wheel,
> - $P(\text{die}=k)=\frac{1}{6}$,
> - $P(\text{card}=k)=\frac{4}{52}=\frac{1}{13}$.
>
> Since these are independent,
> $$
> P(\text{all three equal }k)=\frac{1}{38}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}.
> $$
>
> There are $6$ possible common values, so
> $$
> P(\text{all three are the same number})
> =6\cdot \frac{1}{38}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}
> =\frac{1}{38\cdot 13}
> =\frac{1}{494}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{494}\approx 0.002024 \approx 0.2024\%}.
> $$
>
> Regarding confidence: under the standard interpretation above, the answer is exact, so confidence is essentially complete. The only ambiguity is the roulette convention. If instead the wheel is European, with $37$ slots ($0$ through $36$), then the probability becomes
> $$
> 6\cdot \frac{1}{37}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{13}
> =\frac{1}{481}.
> $$
>
> So the answer is:
>
> - **American roulette:** $\boxed{\frac{1}{494}}$,
> - **European roulette:** $\boxed{\frac{1}{481}}$.
>
> I am very confident in this result, provided the roulette type is specified.
