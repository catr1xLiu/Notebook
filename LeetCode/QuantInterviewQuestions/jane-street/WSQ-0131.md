> [!example] Question 131 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader - Intern Interview

Two players play a game of coin toss with one coin. One wins if the sequence HTH occurs first, the other if HHT occurs first (H = heads, T = tails). Is the game fair? If not, who has the advantage?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=132)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let player $A$ win when the pattern $HTH$ appears first, and player $B$ win when the pattern $HHT$ appears first. We determine which pattern is more likely to occur first in an infinite sequence of fair coin tosses.
>
> ## Method
>
> Track the relevant recent tosses, since only the last few symbols matter for whether one of the target patterns can be completed.
>
> We use states corresponding to the longest suffix of the current toss history that is also a prefix of either target pattern.
>
> The target patterns are:
>
> - $HTH$
> - $HHT$
>
> Their relevant prefixes are:
> - $\varnothing$
> - $H$
> - $HT$
> - $HH$
>
> Let $p_S$ denote the probability that $HTH$ occurs before $HHT$, given current state $S$.
>
> We want $p_{\varnothing}$.
>
> ## State equations
>
> ### State $\varnothing$
>
> If no relevant partial pattern is present, then:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\frac12$, moving to state $H$.
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\frac12$, remaining in state $\varnothing$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{\varnothing}=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_{\varnothing}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> p_{\varnothing}=p_H.
> $$
>
> ### State $H$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\frac12$, giving suffix $HT$, so move to state $HT$.
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\frac12$, giving suffix $HH$, so move to state $HH$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> ### State $HT$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\frac12$, forming $HTH$, so player $A$ wins.
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\frac12$, resulting in suffix with no useful prefix, so move to $\varnothing$.
>
> Therefore
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_{\varnothing}.
> $$
>
> ### State $HH$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\frac12$, forming $HHT$, so player $B$ wins.
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\frac12$, the last two tosses are still $HH$, so remain in state $HH$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_{HH},
> $$
> which gives
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Solve the system
>
> Since $p_{\varnothing}=p_H$, we have
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p_{\varnothing}.
> $$
>
> Now substitute into
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
> Using $p_{HH}=0$,
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
> Since $p_H=p_{\varnothing}$,
> $$
> p_{\varnothing}=\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p_{\varnothing}\right).
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_{\varnothing}=\frac14+\frac14 p_{\varnothing}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \frac34 p_{\varnothing}=\frac14,
> $$
> and therefore
> $$
> p_{\varnothing}=\frac13.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(HTH \text{ occurs before } HHT)=\frac13,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(HHT \text{ occurs before } HTH)=\frac23.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The game is **not fair**. The pattern $HHT$ has the advantage: it appears before $HTH$ with probability $\frac23$, while $HTH$ appears first with probability $\frac13$.
