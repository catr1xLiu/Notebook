> [!example] Question 348 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You roll a fair six-sided die. If you roll an even number, for every \$2 you bet, you win \$3. If you roll an odd number, for every \$4 you bet, you win \$6. What is your expected return, and how should you bet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=349)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the stake be $x$ dollars, and consider a fair die with
> $$
> P(\text{even})=\frac12,\qquad P(\text{odd})=\frac12.
> $$
>
> ## 1. Two readings of "you win"
>
> Betting odds of the form "for every $\text{\textdollar}2$ you bet, you win $\text{\textdollar}3$" can be read two ways:
>
> - Profit reading (standard): the quoted amount is the net profit paid on top of your returned stake. Betting $\text{\textdollar}2$ and winning nets you $\text{\textdollar}3$ profit (your $\text{\textdollar}2$ stake is also returned), while losing forfeits the stake.
> - Total-return reading: the quoted amount is the entire amount handed back, stake included, so winning $\text{\textdollar}3$ on a $\text{\textdollar}2$ bet nets only $\text{\textdollar}1$ profit.
>
> The betting decision depends on which is meant; the profit reading is the standard convention for odds phrased this way, so it is treated as primary below.
>
> ## 2. Profit reading
>
> Suppose $x$ dollars are staked on the outcome "even." If even occurs, the profit is
> $$
> \frac{3}{2}x,
> $$
> and the stake is also returned, for a net gain of $\frac32x$. If odd occurs, the stake is lost, a net loss of $x$.
>
> The expected net gain per dollar staked on even is therefore
> $$
> \frac12\cdot\frac32+\frac12\cdot(-1)=\frac34-\frac12=\frac14.
> $$
>
> Likewise, staking $x$ dollars on "odd," a win pays profit
> $$
> \frac{6}{4}x=\frac32x,
> $$
> with the stake returned, and a loss forfeits the stake. The expected net gain per dollar is again $\frac14$.
>
> So under the profit reading, both bets have expected net gain $\frac14$ dollar per dollar staked — a $25\%$ positive edge, the same on either side.
>
> ## 3. Total-return reading
>
> If instead the quoted amounts are total returns (stake included), staking $x$ on either side returns $\frac32x$ if that side occurs and $0$ otherwise. The expected return per dollar is
> $$
> \frac12\cdot\frac32+\frac12\cdot 0=\frac34,
> $$
> so the expected net gain per dollar is
> $$
> \frac34-1=-\frac14.
> $$
>
> Under this reading, both bets lose $25\%$ in expectation, and neither should be played.
>
> ## 4. Which bet to make
>
> Since both readings give the same edge on the even bet and the odd bet, the split between the two never matters — only whether to bet, and how much.
>
> - Under the standard (profit) reading, every dollar staked has expected net gain $\frac14=0.25$, a positive-EV wager. Since the edge per dollar is constant and does not diminish with size, you should bet as much as you are permitted to, on either side or any mix of the two.
> - Under the total-return reading, every dollar staked has expected net loss $\frac14=0.25$, a negative-EV wager, so the best choice would be not to bet at all.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Reading the payoffs the standard way — "win $\text{\textdollar}3$" means a $\text{\textdollar}3$ profit in addition to the returned stake — the expected return is $+25\%$ per dollar bet (an expected gain of $\text{\textdollar}0.25$ per dollar wagered) on either the even bet or the odd bet, so you should bet the maximum amount allowed. If instead "win" is read as the total amount returned, inclusive of the stake, the expected return is $-25\%$ per dollar, and the correct play would be not to bet at all.
