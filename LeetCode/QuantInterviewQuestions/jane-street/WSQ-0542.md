> [!example] Question 542 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have a 3x3x3 cube painted yellow on the outside, which is then split into 1x1x1 cubes. You randomly pick one of these cubes and observe that the side facing you is painted. Without knowing if any other sides are painted, what is the probability that this cube is a corner cube?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=543)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube, which is divided into $27$ unit cubes.
>
> We are told that the visible face of the chosen small cube is painted. Thus, the chosen cube is known to have at least one painted face. We want the conditional probability that such a cube is a corner cube.
>
> ## Step 1: Classify the painted cubes
>
> Among the $27$ unit cubes:
>
> - **Corner cubes**: $8$, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - **Edge cubes (non-corner)**: $12$, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - **Face-center cubes**: $6$, each with $1$ painted face.
> - **Interior cube**: $1$, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> Since we observe a painted face, the interior cube is impossible. So the relevant cubes are the $8+12+6=26$ cubes on the surface.
>
> ## Step 2: Interpret the condition correctly
>
> The phrase “you notice that the side you are looking at is painted” means that the observed face is a painted face. A cube with more painted faces is more likely to produce this observation, because there are more painted sides that could be the one seen.
>
> Thus, we count **painted faces**, not just cubes.
>
> Total number of painted unit faces on the outside of the large cube is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 3^2 = 54.
> $$
>
> Now count how many of these painted faces belong to corner cubes:
>
> - Each corner cube has $3$ painted faces.
> - There are $8$ corner cubes.
>
> So corner cubes contribute
>
> $$
> 8 \cdot 3 = 24
> $$
>
> painted faces.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the conditional probability
>
> Given that the observed face is painted, the probability that it belongs to a corner cube is
>
> $$
> \frac{24}{54}=\frac{4}{9}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{9}}
> $$
