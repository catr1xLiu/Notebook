> [!example] Question 3 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You are rolling a die. Each time you roll, you can either stop the game and receive payment equal to the face-up value, or choose to roll again. You may roll up to three times in total. What is the expected gain from playing this game optimally?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=4)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V_n$ denote the maximum expected payoff when there are $n$ rolls remaining and, after seeing the current roll, one may either stop and take that amount or continue.
>
> The problem is solved by backward induction.
>
> ## Step 1: One roll remaining
>
> If there is only one roll left, then after seeing the die result $x$ you must take it, since no future rolls remain. Thus
>
> $$
> V_1 = \mathbb{E}[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Two rolls remaining
>
> With two rolls remaining, after seeing the first result $x$, you should stop if $x \ge V_1 = 3.5$, and continue otherwise.
>
> Since $x$ is an integer, this means:
>
> - stop on $4,5,6$,
> - continue on $1,2,3$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(3\cdot V_1 + 4 + 5 + 6\bigr)
> = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(3\cdot 3.5 + 15\bigr)
> = \frac{25.5}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Three rolls remaining
>
> Now with three rolls remaining, after seeing the first result $x$, compare $x$ with $V_2 = 4.25$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - stop on $5,6$,
> - continue on $1,2,3,4$.
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(4\cdot V_2 + 5 + 6\bigr)
> = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(4\cdot 4.25 + 11\bigr)
> = \frac{28}{6}
> = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal expected gain is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{14}{3}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.667}.
> $$
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> - on the first roll, stop only if you get $5$ or $6$;
> - if you continue to the second roll, stop if you get $4,5,$ or $6$;
> - on the last roll, take whatever appears.
