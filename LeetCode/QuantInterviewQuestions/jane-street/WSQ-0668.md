> [!example] Question 668 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

If there's a 20% chance of rain on Saturday and a 30% chance of rain on Sunday, what is the probability it rains at least once this weekend?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=669)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the rain events on Saturday and Sunday are independent.
>
> Let
> - $S$ = event that it rains on Saturday, with $P(S)=0.20$,
> - $U$ = event that it rains on Sunday, with $P(U)=0.30$.
>
> We want the probability that it rains at least once during the weekend:
> $$
> P(S \cup U).
> $$
>
> Use the complement:
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=1-P(\text{no rain on both days}).
> $$
>
> The probability of no rain on Saturday is
> $$
> 1-0.20=0.80,
> $$
> and the probability of no rain on Sunday is
> $$
> 1-0.30=0.70.
> $$
>
> By independence,
> $$
> P(\text{no rain on both days})=0.80 \cdot 0.70=0.56.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(S \cup U)=1-0.56=0.44.
> $$
>
> So the probability that it rains this weekend is
> $$
> \boxed{44\%}.
> $$
>
> If independence is not assumed, the exact probability cannot be determined from the given information alone.
