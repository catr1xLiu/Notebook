> [!example] Question 658 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

Suppose there is a fair 4-sided die (numbered 1-4) and a fair 6-sided die (numbered 1-6). One die is chosen at random and rolled, resulting in a 2. What is the probability that the die rolled was the 4-sided die?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=659)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D_4$ be the event that the $4$-sided die was chosen, and $D_6$ the event that the $6$-sided die was chosen. Since the die is picked at random,
>
> $$
> P(D_4)=P(D_6)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Let $R_2$ be the event that a $2$ is rolled. Then
>
> $$
> P(R_2 \mid D_4)=\frac{1}{4}, \qquad P(R_2 \mid D_6)=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(D_4 \mid R_2).
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(D_4 \mid R_2)
> =
> \frac{P(R_2 \mid D_4)P(D_4)}{P(R_2)}.
> $$
>
> Now compute $P(R_2)$ using the law of total probability:
>
> $$
> P(R_2)
> =
> P(R_2 \mid D_4)P(D_4)+P(R_2 \mid D_6)P(D_6)
> =
> \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(R_2)
> =
> \frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{12}
> =
> \frac{3}{24}+\frac{2}{24}
> =
> \frac{5}{24}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(D_4 \mid R_2)
> =
> \frac{\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{5}{24}}
> =
> \frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{24}{5}
> =
> \frac{3}{5}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability that the die rolled was the $4$-sided die, given that a $2$ was rolled, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{5}}.
> $$
