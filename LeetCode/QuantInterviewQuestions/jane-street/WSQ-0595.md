> [!example] Question 595 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

How many ways are there to partition 20 into prime numbers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=596)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To count the partitions of $20$ into prime numbers, we seek the number of unordered representations of $20$ as a sum of primes.
>
> The primes not exceeding $20$ are
> $$
> 2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19.
> $$
>
> Thus we must count the number of solutions in nonnegative integers to
> $$
> 2a+3b+5c+7d+11e+13f+17g+19h=20,
> $$
> where order does not matter.
>
> A systematic count is easiest by considering the largest part.
>
> ## 1. Partitions using $19$, $17$, or $13$
>
> - $19$ leaves $1$, impossible.
> - $17$ leaves $3$, and $3$ is itself prime, so the only partition of the remainder is $3$ alone, giving
>   $$
>   17+3.
>   $$
> - $13$ leaves $7$, and $7$ can be partitioned into primes in three ways:
>   $$
>   7,\qquad 5+2,\qquad 3+2+2.
>   $$
>   giving
>   $$
>   13+7,\qquad 13+5+2,\qquad 13+3+2+2.
>   $$
>
> So these contribute $1+3=4$ partitions.
>
> ## 2. Partitions using $11$
>
> If $11$ is used, the remainder is $9$, which must be partitioned into primes not exceeding $11$:
>
> - $9=7+2$
> - $9=5+2+2$
> - $9=3+3+3$
> - $9=3+2+2+2$
>
> Hence:
> $$
> 11+7+2,\qquad 11+5+2+2,\qquad 11+3+3+3,\qquad 11+3+2+2+2.
> $$
>
> This contributes $4$ more.
>
> ## 3. Partitions using $7$ but no part greater than $7$
>
> We partition $20$ using $7,5,3,2$.
>
> Consider the number of $7$'s.
>
> ### Two $7$'s
> Remainder:
> $$
> 20-14=6.
> $$
> Prime partitions of $6$:
> $$
> 3+3,\qquad 2+2+2.
> $$
> So:
> $$
> 7+7+3+3,\qquad 7+7+2+2+2.
> $$
>
> ### One $7$
> Remainder:
> $$
> 20-7=13.
> $$
> Partitions of $13$ using $5,3,2$:
>
> - $5+5+3$
> - $5+3+3+2$
> - $5+2+2+2+2$
> - $3+3+3+2+2$
> - $3+2+2+2+2+2$
>
> Thus:
> $$
> 7+5+5+3,
> $$
> $$
> 7+5+3+3+2,
> $$
> $$
> 7+5+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 7+3+3+3+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 7+3+2+2+2+2+2.
> $$
>
> ### No $7$
> The remaining case partitions $20$ using only $5,3,2$; this is handled next.
>
> ## 4. Partitions using only $5,3,2$
>
> Solve
> $$
> 5c+3b+2a=20.
> $$
>
> Consider $c$.
>
> ### $c=4$
> Remainder $0$:
> $$
> 5+5+5+5.
> $$
>
> ### $c=3$
> Remainder $5$. With $c$ fixed at $3$, the remainder $5$ must be built from $3$'s and $2$'s, and the only way is
> $$
> 3+2,
> $$
> giving
> $$
> 5+5+5+3+2.
> $$
>
> ### $c=2$
> Remainder $10$. Solve
> $$
> 3b+2a=10.
> $$
> Possibilities:
> - $b=0$, $a=5$
> - $b=2$, $a=2$
>
> Hence:
> $$
> 5+5+2+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 5+5+3+3+2+2.
> $$
>
> ### $c=1$
> Remainder $15$. Solve
> $$
> 3b+2a=15.
> $$
> Possibilities:
> - $b=1$, $a=6$
> - $b=3$, $a=3$
> - $b=5$, $a=0$
>
> Hence:
> $$
> 5+3+2+2+2+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 5+3+3+3+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 5+3+3+3+3+3.
> $$
>
> ### $c=0$
> Remainder $20$. Solve
> $$
> 3b+2a=20.
> $$
> Possibilities:
> - $b=0$, $a=10$
> - $b=2$, $a=7$
> - $b=4$, $a=4$
> - $b=6$, $a=1$
>
> Hence:
> $$
> 2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 3+3+2+2+2+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 3+3+3+3+2+2+2+2,
> $$
> $$
> 3+3+3+3+3+3+2.
> $$
>
> So the partitions using only $5,3,2$ contribute
> $$
> 1+1+2+3+4=11.
> $$
>
> ## 5. Total
>
> Adding all contributions:
>
> - using $17$ or $13$: $4$
> - using $11$: $4$
> - using $7$ but no larger prime: $2+5=7$
> - using only $5,3,2$: $11$
>
> Therefore the total number of partitions is
> $$
> 4+4+7+11=26.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{26}
> $$
