> [!example] Question 519 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

In a best-of-7, first-to-4 competition, what is the probability that a game 7 is played?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=520)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the two teams are equally matched, so each game is won independently by either team with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> A game $7$ is played exactly when, after $6$ games, the series is tied $3$–$3$. Thus we need the probability that in the first $6$ games, each team wins exactly $3$ times.
>
> The number of ways to choose which $3$ of the $6$ games are won by one team is
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{3} = 20.
> $$
>
> Each particular sequence of $6$ game outcomes has probability
>
> $$
> \left(\tfrac12\right)^6 = \frac{1}{64}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{game 7 is played}) = \binom{6}{3}\left(\tfrac12\right)^6
> = 20 \cdot \frac{1}{64}
> = \frac{20}{64}
> = \frac{5}{16}.
> $$
>
> So the odds that a game $7$ is played are
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{16}}
> $$
>
> which is
>
> $$
> \boxed{31.25\%}.
> $$
>
> If one prefers odds in the form “in favor,” this is
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{16} : \frac{11}{16} = 5:11.
> $$
