> [!example] Question 285 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Interview

If you flip a fair coin, what is the probability of getting a head? What is the probability of getting a certain number of heads if you flip two coins? How does this generalize for n coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=286)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the coin is fair, so each flip has probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of landing heads and $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of landing tails.
>
> ## One coin
>
> For a single flip, the probability of getting a head is
>
> $$
> P(\text{head}) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Two coins
>
> With two independent flips, the possible outcomes are
>
> $$
> \{HH, HT, TH, TT\}.
> $$
>
> Each outcome has probability $\tfrac{1}{4}$.
>
> - Probability of exactly one head:
>
> $$
> P(\text{exactly one head}) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> - Probability of at least one head:
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one head}) = 1 - P(\text{no heads}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> - Probability of two heads:
>
> $$
> P(\text{two heads}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## $n$ coins
>
> For $n$ independent fair coin flips:
>
> - Probability of all heads:
>
> $$
> P(\text{all heads}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.
> $$
>
> - Probability of no heads:
>
> $$
> P(\text{no heads}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.
> $$
>
> - Probability of at least one head:
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one head}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.
> $$
>
> - Probability of exactly $k$ heads:
>
> There are $\binom{n}{k}$ ways to choose which $k$ of the $n$ flips are heads, and each such outcome has probability $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$. Hence
>
> $$
> P(\text{exactly } k \text{ heads}) = \binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n,
> \qquad k = 0,1,\dots,n.
> $$
>
> Thus the number of heads in $n$ fair flips has the binomial distribution:
>
> $$
> X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, \tfrac{1}{2}).
> $$
>
> In particular, for one coin the probability of a head is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> For two coins, the probability of at least one head is
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{4},
> $$
>
> and in general for $n$ coins it is
>
> $$
> 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.
> $$
