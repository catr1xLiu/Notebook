> [!example] Question 376 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

I flip four coins and give you a dollar for each head. After all four coins are flipped, you may choose to re-flip one coin that landed tails. What is the expected value of this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=377)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads among the $4$ fair coin flips.
>
> Without the extra option, the payoff is simply $H$ dollars, so
> $$
> E[H] = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2.
> $$
>
> Now incorporate the option to flip one tail after all four coins have been flipped.
>
> ## Optimal strategy
> If there is at least one tail among the $4$ coins, flipping a tail is beneficial: with probability $\frac{1}{2}$ it becomes a head, increasing the payoff by $1$, and with probability $\frac{1}{2}$ it remains a tail, increasing the payoff by $0$. Thus the expected gain from using the option is
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}
> $$
> whenever at least one tail exists.
>
> If all $4$ coins are already heads, there is no tail to flip, so the extra expected gain is $0$.
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = E[H] + \frac{1}{2}\,P(\text{at least one tail}).
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> P(\text{at least one tail}) = 1 - P(\text{all heads}) = 1 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4 = 1 - \frac{1}{16} = \frac{15}{16},
> $$
> we get
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = 2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot \frac{15}{16}
> = 2 + \frac{15}{32}
> = \frac{79}{32}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{79}{32}\text{ dollars}}
> $$
