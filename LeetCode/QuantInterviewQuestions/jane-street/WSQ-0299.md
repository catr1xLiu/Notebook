> [!example] Question 299 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

I am thinking of a number that does not contain the digit 1. The product of its digits is 96. What is the smallest possible number? What is the largest?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=300)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> # Solution
>
> Let the digits of the number be chosen from $\{0,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9\}$, since the digit $1$ is forbidden.
>
> Because the product of the digits is $96$, no digit can be $0$; otherwise the product would be $0$. Thus every digit must come from
> $$
> 2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9.
> $$
>
> Now factor
> $$
> 96 = 2^5 \cdot 3.
> $$
> Since $5$ and $7$ do not divide $96$, and $9=3^2$ would require two factors of $3$ where only one is available, the only usable digits are
> $$
> 2,3,4,6,8.
> $$
>
> We seek the number with the fewest digits (for the smallest value) and the number with the most digits (for the largest value), since fewer digits generally makes a number smaller and more digits generally makes it larger.
>
> ## Smallest possible number
>
> First check whether two digits suffice. No pair of digits from $2$ through $9$ multiplies to $96$, since the partner needed exceeds $9$ in every case, for example $96/8=12$ and $96/6=16$. So two digits are impossible, and at least three digits are required.
>
> With three digits, the single factor $3$ in $96=2^5\cdot 3$ must appear in exactly one digit, either as a $3$ or as part of a $6$, and the remaining two digits must be powers of $2$ chosen from $\{2,4,8\}$ multiplying to whatever is left. This gives three valid digit multisets:
>
> - $3\times 4\times 8 = 96$
> - $2\times 6\times 8 = 96$
> - $4\times 4\times 6 = 96$
>
> To get the smallest possible number, compare the leading digit available from each multiset when sorted in increasing order: $3,\ 2,\ 4$ respectively. Since $2$ is the smallest leading digit, the multiset $\{2,6,8\}$ gives the smallest number. Arranging its digits in increasing order:
> $$
> 268.
> $$
>
> ## Largest possible number
>
> To maximize the number, use as many digits as possible. Since every digit must be at least $2$, and
> $$
> 96 = 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 3,
> $$
> splitting $96$ into five $2$'s and one $3$ gives $6$ digits, which is the maximum possible, since replacing any $2$ with a larger digit would only reduce the digit count.
>
> Arranging these six digits in decreasing order gives the largest possible number:
> $$
> 322222.
> $$
>
> ## Answer
>
> The smallest possible number is
> $$
> 268,
> $$
> and the largest possible number is
> $$
> 322222.
> $$
