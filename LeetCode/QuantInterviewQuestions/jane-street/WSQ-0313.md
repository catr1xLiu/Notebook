> [!example] Question 313 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Develop a strategy for the Colonel Blotto game with 10 battlefields valued from 1 to 10 and 100 soldiers to allocate.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=314)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> In a Colonel Blotto game, the objective is to allocate a fixed number of soldiers across several battlefields so as to maximize the total value of the battlefields won. Here there are $10$ battlefields with values
>
> $$
> 1,2,3,\dots,10
> $$
>
> and a total of $100$ soldiers.
>
> Since no information is given about the opponent’s strategy, the natural goal is to design a **balanced mixed strategy** rather than a single deterministic allocation. A fixed allocation is easy to exploit; a randomized allocation is much more robust.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Guiding principle
>
> Let the battlefield values be
>
> $$
> v_i=i, \qquad i=1,\dots,10.
> $$
>
> The total value is
>
> $$
> V=\sum_{i=1}^{10} i=55.
> $$
>
> A standard heuristic in Blotto is to allocate soldiers **proportionally to battlefield value**, since higher-value battlefields are worth fighting harder for. Thus the expected allocation to battlefield $i$ should be
>
> $$
> E[X_i]=100\cdot \frac{v_i}{55}.
> $$
>
> So the baseline expected numbers are:
>
> $$
> E[X_1]=\frac{100}{55}\approx 1.82,
> \quad
> E[X_2]=\frac{200}{55}\approx 3.64,
> \quad
> E[X_3]=\frac{300}{55}\approx 5.45,
> $$
>
> $$
> E[X_4]\approx 7.27,
> \quad
> E[X_5]\approx 9.09,
> \quad
> E[X_6]\approx 10.91,
> $$
>
> $$
> E[X_7]\approx 12.73,
> \quad
> E[X_8]\approx 14.55,
> \quad
> E[X_9]\approx 16.36,
> \quad
> E[X_{10}]\approx 18.18.
> $$
>
> These sum to $100$.
>
> A deterministic allocation close to these expectations is
>
> $$
> (2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18),
> $$
>
> since
>
> $$
> 2+4+5+7+9+11+13+15+16+18=100.
> $$
>
> This is a good **base allocation**.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Why a pure strategy is not enough
>
> If the same allocation is used every time, an opponent can counter it by slightly outbidding the key battlefields and conceding the rest. Therefore the strategy should be **mixed**: randomize around the proportional baseline.
>
> The idea is:
>
> - put **more soldiers on high-value battlefields on average**,
> - but vary the exact allocation from game to game,
> - while always keeping the total equal to $100$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. A practical mixed strategy
>
> Start from the base allocation
>
> $$
> (2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18).
> $$
>
> Then randomize by shifting a small number of soldiers among nearby battlefields, especially among the high-value ones.
>
> For example, choose one of the following allocations uniformly at random:
>
> $$
> A_1=(2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18),
> $$
>
> $$
> A_2=(1,3,5,7,9,11,14,15,17,18),
> $$
>
> $$
> A_3=(2,4,4,7,9,10,13,16,17,18),
> $$
>
> $$
> A_4=(2,3,5,6,9,11,13,16,17,18),
> $$
>
> $$
> A_5=(1,4,5,7,8,11,13,15,18,18).
> $$
>
> Each of these sums to $100$, and each keeps the emphasis on larger battlefields.
>
> This produces a strategy with the following properties:
>
> 1. **Proportional emphasis:** battlefields with values $8,9,10$ receive the largest forces.
> 2. **Unpredictability:** the opponent cannot target one fixed allocation.
> 3. **Resource efficiency:** low-value battlefields are not overfunded.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Strategic interpretation
>
> This strategy reflects the fact that winning battlefield $10$ is worth as much as winning battlefields $1$ through $4$ combined, since
>
> $$
> 10=1+2+3+4.
> $$
>
> So it is usually inefficient to commit many soldiers to the smallest battlefields unless doing so is part of a random bluff. The larger-valued battlefields should receive the bulk of the force.
>
> A useful way to think about the $100$ soldiers is:
>
> - battlefields $1$ through $4$: light coverage,
> - battlefields $5$ through $7$: moderate coverage,
> - battlefields $8$ through $10$: heavy coverage.
>
> Indeed, under the baseline allocation,
>
> $$
> X_1+X_2+X_3+X_4 = 2+4+5+7 = 18,
> $$
>
> $$
> X_5+X_6+X_7 = 9+11+13 = 33,
> $$
>
> $$
> X_8+X_9+X_{10} = 15+16+18 = 49.
> $$
>
> So nearly half the army goes to the top three battlefields, which is reasonable because their total value is
>
> $$
> 8+9+10=27,
> $$
>
> almost half of the total value $55$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Recommended strategy
>
> A strong practical strategy is therefore:
>
> 1. **Use value-proportional allocation as the center:**
>    $$
>    (2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18).
>    $$
> 2. **Randomize around it** by moving $1$ to $3$ soldiers between battlefields while preserving the total of $100$.
> 3. **Keep most variation among battlefields $6$ through $10$**, since these are the most consequential.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A sound strategy is to allocate soldiers approximately in proportion to battlefield values. Since the values sum to $55$, assign battlefield $i$ about
>
> $$
> 100\cdot \frac{i}{55}
> $$
>
> soldiers on average. This gives the practical baseline allocation
>
> $$
> (2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18).
> $$
>
> Because Colonel Blotto rewards unpredictability, this should be used as the center of a **mixed strategy**: randomize slightly around this vector while always keeping the total at $100$. For example, rotate among allocations such as
>
> $$
> (2,4,5,7,9,11,13,15,16,18),\;
> (1,3,5,7,9,11,14,15,17,18),\;
> (2,4,4,7,9,10,13,16,17,18),
> $$
>
> and similar nearby vectors summing to $100$.
>
> Thus the recommended strategy is: **weight soldiers toward the higher-value battlefields in proportion to value, and randomize the exact allocation from game to game.**
