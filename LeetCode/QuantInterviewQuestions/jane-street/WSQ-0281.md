> [!example] Question 281 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trading Intern Interview

You have a drawer with an infinite supply of socks in a 1:1 ratio of two colors. What is the expected number of draws needed to get a matching pair?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=282)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two sock colors be, for example, black and white, with each draw equally likely to be either color. We seek the expected number of draws until the first matching pair appears.
>
> ## Step 1: Observe the process
>
> - After the first draw, no pair is possible.
> - On the second draw:
>   - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, it matches the first sock, so we have a pair in $2$ draws;
>   - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, it is the other color, so after $2$ draws we have one black and one white.
>
> But if the first two draws are different, then the third draw must match one of them, since there are only two colors. Hence in this case the process ends on draw $3$.
>
> So the number of draws $N$ satisfies
> $$
> P(N=2)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad P(N=3)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expectation
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E[N] = 2\cdot \frac{1}{2} + 3\cdot \frac{1}{2}
> = 1 + \frac{3}{2}
> = \frac{5}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{2}}
> $$
>
> So the expected number of draws needed to get a pair is $\boxed{2.5}$.
