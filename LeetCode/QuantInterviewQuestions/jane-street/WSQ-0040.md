> [!example] Question 40 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Research Interview

How many ways are there to shuffle a deck of cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=41)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A standard deck has $52$ distinct cards. A shuffle can produce any permutation of these $52$ cards, so the number of possible shuffles is the number of permutations of $52$ distinct objects:
>
> $$
> 52! = 52 \cdot 51 \cdot 50 \cdots 2 \cdot 1.
> $$
>
> Thus, the number of ways to shuffle a deck of cards is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52!}
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> 52! = 80,\!658,\!175,\!170,\!943,\!878,\!571,\!660,\!636,\!856,\!403,\!766,\!975,\!289,\!505,\!440,\!883,\!277,\!824,\!000,\!000,\!000.
> $$
>
> So there are
>
> $$
> \boxed{80,\!658,\!175,\!170,\!943,\!878,\!571,\!660,\!636,\!856,\!403,\!766,\!975,\!289,\!505,\!440,\!883,\!277,\!824,\!000,\!000,\!000}
> $$
>
> possible shuffles of a standard deck.
