> [!example] Question 172 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

A cube that is white on the inside is painted blue on the outside and then cut into thirds along each dimension, resulting in 27 smaller cubes. Blindfolded, I randomly pick one of the smaller cubes and toss it so it lands with one side down. I observe that all of the visible sides are white. What is the probability that the side facing down is blue?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=173)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Paint the outside of the large cube blue, then cut it into a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid of $27$ small cubes. Each small cube has some number of blue faces:
>
> - $8$ corner cubes: $3$ blue faces each.
> - $12$ edge cubes: $2$ blue faces each.
> - $6$ face-center cubes: $1$ blue face each.
> - $1$ interior cube: $0$ blue faces.
>
> Let $E$ be the event that, after the cube lands, all visible faces are white. Since one face is down, this means the other $5$ faces are white.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(\text{down face is blue} \mid E).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Determine which cubes can produce event $E$
>
> If all $5$ visible faces are white, then the cube can have at most one blue face total, and that blue face must be the one on the bottom.
>
> Thus the only possible cubes are:
>
> - the interior cube, with $0$ blue faces;
> - the $6$ face-center cubes, with $1$ blue face.
>
> Corner and edge cubes are impossible, since they have at least $2$ blue faces, so at least one blue face would remain visible.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute probabilities of producing $E$
>
> A small cube is chosen uniformly from the $27$ cubes, and its landing orientation is equally likely among its $6$ faces.
>
> ### Interior cube
> There is $1$ such cube. It always has all faces white, so event $E$ always occurs.
>
> Contribution:
> $$
> \frac{1}{27} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> ### Face-center cube
> There are $6$ such cubes. Each has exactly $1$ blue face. For event $E$ to occur, that blue face must land face down, which happens with probability
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
> So the total contribution is
> $$
> 6 \cdot \frac{1}{27} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> P(E) = \frac{1}{27} + \frac{1}{27} = \frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the joint probability that the down face is blue and $E$ occurs
>
> This can happen only for a face-center cube, with its blue face down. As above, that probability is
> $$
> \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> P(\text{down face is blue} \mid E)
> =
> \frac{P(\text{down face is blue and } E)}{P(E)}
> =
> \frac{\frac{1}{27}}{\frac{2}{27}}
> =
> \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> $$
