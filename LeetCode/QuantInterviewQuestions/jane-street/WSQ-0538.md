> [!example] Question 538 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If I roll a die multiple times and can keep the value of each roll, what strategy should I use to maximize my total without exceeding 6, given that if my sum reaches 7 or more I go bust?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=539)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Model the game as follows. On each roll of a fair six-sided die, the rolled value is added to a running total. You may stop after any roll and keep your current total. If your total ever becomes at least $7$, you bust and receive $0$.
>
> The objective is to choose when to stop so as to maximize the expected payoff.
>
> ## Step 1: Define the value function
>
> Let $V(s)$ be the maximum expected payoff when your current total is $s$, where $s \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.
>
> If you stop immediately, your payoff is $s$.
>
> If you continue, then after one more roll $X \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, each with probability $\frac16$, your new total is $s+X$:
>
> - if $s+X \le 6$, you may continue optimally and receive $V(s+X)$;
> - if $s+X \ge 7$, you bust and receive $0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> V(s)=\max\left(s,\ \frac16\sum_{x=1}^6 W(s+x)\right),
> $$
> where
> $$
> W(t)=
> \begin{cases}
> V(t), & t\le 6,\\
> 0, & t\ge 7.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> V(6)=6,
> $$
> since rolling again certainly causes a bust.
>
> ## Step 2: Work backward
>
> ### State $s=5$
>
> If you stop, you get $5$.
>
> If you continue, then every roll causes $5+x \ge 6$, and only $x=1$ gives total $6$; all other outcomes bust. Hence
> $$
> \text{continue value}=\frac16 V(6)=\frac16\cdot 6=1.
> $$
> So
> $$
> V(5)=\max(5,1)=5.
> $$
>
> ### State $s=4$
>
> If you continue:
> - roll $1 \to 5$,
> - roll $2 \to 6$,
> - rolls $3,4,5,6$ bust.
>
> So
> $$
> \text{continue value}=\frac16\bigl(V(5)+V(6)\bigr)=\frac16(5+6)=\frac{11}{6}.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> V(4)=\max\left(4,\frac{11}{6}\right)=4.
> $$
>
> ### State $s=3$
>
> If you continue:
> - roll $1 \to 4$,
> - roll $2 \to 5$,
> - roll $3 \to 6$,
> - rolls $4,5,6$ bust.
>
> Hence
> $$
> \text{continue value}=\frac16\bigl(V(4)+V(5)+V(6)\bigr)
> =\frac16(4+5+6)=\frac{15}{6}=2.5.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V(3)=\max(3,2.5)=3.
> $$
>
> ### State $s=2$
>
> If you continue:
> - roll $1 \to 3$,
> - roll $2 \to 4$,
> - roll $3 \to 5$,
> - roll $4 \to 6$,
> - rolls $5,6$ bust.
>
> So
> $$
> \text{continue value}=\frac16\bigl(V(3)+V(4)+V(5)+V(6)\bigr)
> =\frac16(3+4+5+6)=3.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> V(2)=\max(2,3)=3.
> $$
> So at total $2$, continuing is better.
>
> ### State $s=1$
>
> If you continue:
> - roll $1 \to 2$,
> - roll $2 \to 3$,
> - roll $3 \to 4$,
> - roll $4 \to 5$,
> - roll $5 \to 6$,
> - roll $6 \to 7$ bust.
>
> Hence
> $$
> \text{continue value}=\frac16\bigl(V(2)+V(3)+V(4)+V(5)+V(6)\bigr)
> =\frac16(3+3+4+5+6)=\frac{21}{6}=3.5.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> V(1)=\max(1,3.5)=3.5.
> $$
>
> ### State $s=0$
>
> Initially,
> $$
> V(0)=\frac16\bigl(V(1)+V(2)+V(3)+V(4)+V(5)+V(6)\bigr).
> $$
> So
> $$
> V(0)=\frac16(3.5+3+3+4+5+6)=\frac{24.5}{6}=\frac{49}{12}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Determine the optimal stopping rule
>
> From the comparisons above:
>
> - At totals $3,4,5,6$, stopping is better.
> - At total $2$, continuing is better.
> - At total $1$, continuing is better.
> - At total $0$, you must roll.
>
> Thus the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Keep rolling until your total is at least } 3,\text{ then stop.}
> $$
>
> Equivalently:
>
> - if your total is $1$ or $2$, roll again;
> - if your total is $3$, $4$, $5$, or $6$, stop and take it.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is to **continue rolling while your total is $0$, $1$, or $2$, and stop once your total reaches $3$, $4$, $5$, or $6$**.
>
> Under this strategy, the expected value at the start of the game is
>
> $$
> V(0)=\frac{49}{12}\approx 4.083.
> $$
