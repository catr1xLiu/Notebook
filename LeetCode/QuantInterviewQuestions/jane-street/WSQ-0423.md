> [!example] Question 423 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You have two ropes, each of which takes 1 hour to burn from end to end, but they burn at non-uniform rates along their lengths. How can you use them to measure exactly 45 minutes?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=424)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Use the two ropes so that changing burn speed does not matter.
>
> ## Strategy
> If a rope is lit from **both ends**, it burns out in **half** its total burn time, regardless of uneven density.  
> Since each rope normally takes $60$ minutes to burn from one end, burning from both ends makes it last $30$ minutes.
>
> ## Steps
> 1. At time $t=0$, light:
>    - **Rope 1** at **both ends**,
>    - **Rope 2** at **one end**.
>
> 2. Rope 1 will finish burning in $30$ minutes.
>
> 3. At the moment Rope 1 burns out, light the **other end** of Rope 2.
>
>    By then, Rope 2 has already been burning for $30$ minutes from one end, but because the rope burns unevenly, the amount left is unknown. However, the remaining portion would have taken exactly $30$ more minutes to burn if left from one end alone.
>
> 4. Now that Rope 2 is burning from **both ends**, the remaining time is cut in half, so it burns out in $15$ minutes.
>
> ## Total time measured
> $$30 + 15 = 45 \text{ minutes}$$
>
> ## Final answer
> Light one rope from both ends and the other rope from one end. When the first rope finishes, light the second end of the other rope. When that second rope finishes burning, exactly $45$ minutes have elapsed.
