> [!example] Question 221 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

What is the probability of getting an even number of heads after tossing a fair coin n times?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=222)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained in $n$ tosses of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> To evaluate this sum, use the binomial expansions
>
> $$
> (1+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}=2^n
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> Write the second identity as
>
> $$
> \sum_{k\text{ even}} \binom{n}{k}-\sum_{k\text{ odd}} \binom{n}{k}=0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \sum_{k\text{ even}} \binom{n}{k}=\sum_{k\text{ odd}} \binom{n}{k}.
> $$
>
> Since the two sums together equal $2^n$, each must be
>
> $$
> \sum_{k\text{ even}} \binom{n}{k}=2^{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})=\left(\tfrac12\right)^n 2^{n-1}=\tfrac12.
> $$
>
> Hence, for any $n \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{an even number of heads in } n \text{ tosses})=\tfrac12.}
> $$
>
> If $n=0$, there are $0$ heads, which is even, so the probability is $1$.
