> [!example] Question 397 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Suppose there are n people on a bus. At each of three stops, 2/3 of the people on the bus get off and 7 people get on. What is the minimum number of people that were initially on the bus before the first stop, given that the number of people on the bus remains an integer after each stop?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=398)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of people on the bus be $n$.
>
> At each stop:
>
> - $\frac{2}{3}$ of the people get off,
> - so $\frac{1}{3}$ remain,
> - then $7$ people get on.
>
> If the number of people after each stop must remain an integer, then at each stage the number of people **before** the stop must be divisible by $3$.
>
> Let the numbers of people just before the first, second, and third stops be:
>
> $$
> n,\quad n_1,\quad n_2
> $$
>
> and after the third stop be $n_3$.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> n_1 = \frac{n}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> $$
> n_2 = \frac{n_1}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> $$
> n_3 = \frac{n_2}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> We seek the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $n$, $n_1$, and $n_2$ are all divisible by $3$ before the corresponding stop.
>
> Work backward.
>
> Since $n_2$ must be divisible by $3$,
>
> $$
> n_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}
> $$
>
> But
>
> $$
> n_2 = \frac{n_1}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{3} + 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}
> $$
>
> Since $7 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$,
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{3} \equiv -1 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{3} \equiv 2 \pmod{3}
> $$
>
> so the smallest possible value is
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{3} = 2
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> n_1 = 6
> $$
>
> Now use
>
> $$
> n_1 = \frac{n}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> to get
>
> $$
> 6 = \frac{n}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> which gives
>
> $$
> \frac{n}{3} = -1
> $$
>
> impossible. So take the next value:
>
> $$
> \frac{n_1}{3} = 5
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> n_1 = 15
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> 15 = \frac{n}{3} + 7
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \frac{n}{3} = 8
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> n = 24
> $$
>
> Check:
>
> - First stop: from $24$, $\frac{2}{3}\cdot 24 = 16$ get off, leaving $8$; then $7$ get on, so $15$ remain.
> - Second stop: from $15$, $\frac{2}{3}\cdot 15 = 10$ get off, leaving $5$; then $7$ get on, so $12$ remain.
> - Third stop: from $12$, $\frac{2}{3}\cdot 12 = 8$ get off, leaving $4$; then $7$ get on, so $11$ remain.
>
> All counts are integers.
>
> Therefore, the minimum initial number of people on the bus is
>
> $$
> \boxed{24}
> $$
