> [!example] Question 297 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

It rains 30% of the time on Saturday and 40% of the time on Sunday. How often does it rain on the weekend?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=298)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $B$ be the event that it rains on Sunday. We are given
>
> $$
> P(A)=0.30,\qquad P(B)=0.40.
> $$
>
> The phrase “it rains on the weekend” means that it rains on at least one of the two days, that is, the event $A\cup B$.
>
> Using the addition rule,
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B).
> $$
>
> To compute this exactly, $P(A\cap B)$ must be known. Since no information is given about whether rain on Saturday and Sunday are related, the exact probability cannot be determined from the data provided alone.
>
> If one assumes the two days are independent, then
>
> $$
> P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)=0.30\cdot 0.40=0.12.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=0.30+0.40-0.12=0.58.
> $$
>
> Therefore, under the independence assumption, it rains on the weekend with probability
>
> $$
> \boxed{58\%}.
> $$
>
> Without assuming independence, the answer cannot be determined exactly.
