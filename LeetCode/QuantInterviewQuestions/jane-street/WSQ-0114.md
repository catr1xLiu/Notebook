> [!example] Question 114 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader - Intern Interview

What is the expected value of the number of heads when tossing a fair coin five times? Please explain your reasoning.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=115)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained in $5$ fair coin tosses.
>
> Each toss has probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of being a head, so
> $$
> X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n=5, p=\tfrac{1}{2}).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable, the expected value is
> $$
> E[X] = np.
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E[X] = 5 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5.
> $$
>
> ### Why?
>
> Define indicator variables $X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4, X_5$, where
> $$
> X_i =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if toss } i \text{ is a head},\\
> 0, & \text{if toss } i \text{ is a tail}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> X = X_1 + X_2 + X_3 + X_4 + X_5.
> $$
>
> Since each toss is fair,
> $$
> E[X_i] = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 0 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> E[X] = E[X_1 + X_2 + X_3 + X_4 + X_5]
> = E[X_1] + E[X_2] + E[X_3] + E[X_4] + E[X_5]
> = 5 \cdot \frac{1}{2}
> = 2.5.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected number of heads in $5$ fair coin tosses is
> $$
> \boxed{2.5}.
> $$
