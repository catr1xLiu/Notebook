> [!example] Question 445 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A coin has a probability of 0.8 of landing heads. What is the expected number of coin tosses needed to observe two consecutive heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=446)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of tosses needed to obtain $2$ consecutive heads for the first time, where each toss is a head with probability $p=0.8$ and a tail with probability $q=0.2$.
>
> Use states based on the current run of consecutive heads:
>
> - State $S_0$: currently have $0$ consecutive heads.
> - State $S_1$: currently have $1$ consecutive head.
> - State $S_2$: have achieved $2$ consecutive heads (absorbing state).
>
> Let
>
> - $E_0$ be the expected remaining number of tosses starting from $S_0$,
> - $E_1$ be the expected remaining number of tosses starting from $S_1$.
>
> We want $E_0$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up equations
>
> From state $S_0$:
>
> - One toss is used.
> - With probability $p$, the result is head, moving to $S_1$.
> - With probability $q$, the result is tail, staying in $S_0$.
>
> So,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + pE_1 + qE_0.
> $$
>
> From state $S_1$:
>
> - One toss is used.
> - With probability $p$, the next toss is head, so we reach $S_2$ and stop.
> - With probability $q$, the next toss is tail, so we return to $S_0$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + p\cdot 0 + qE_0 = 1 + qE_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> Substitute $p=0.8$ and $q=0.2$:
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + 0.8E_1 + 0.2E_0,
> $$
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + 0.2E_0.
> $$
>
> From the first equation,
> $$
> 0.8E_0 = 1 + 0.8E_1,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_0 = \frac{1}{0.8} + E_1 = 1.25 + E_1.
> $$
>
> Now substitute $E_1 = 1 + 0.2E_0$:
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1.25 + 1 + 0.2E_0 = 2.25 + 0.2E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> 0.8E_0 = 2.25,
> $$
> and therefore
> $$
> E_0 = \frac{2.25}{0.8} = 2.8125.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of tosses needed to see $2$ consecutive heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2.8125}.
> $$
