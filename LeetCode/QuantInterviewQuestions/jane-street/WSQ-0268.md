> [!example] Question 268 · Jane Street
> ## Junior Trader Interview

You flip a fair coin an unlimited number of times. If the sequence HHT appears before HTT, you win. What is the probability that you win?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=269)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be the probability that the pattern $\text{HHT}$ appears before $\text{HTT}$ in an infinite sequence of fair coin flips.
>
> The key idea is to track the relevant suffix of the flips seen so far. Only prefixes of the target patterns matter.
>
> ## States
>
> Define the following states according to the current suffix:
>
> - $S$: no relevant partial match
> - $H$: the current suffix is $\text{H}$
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $\text{HH}$
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $\text{HT}$
>
> Let the corresponding winning probabilities be
>
> $$
> x_S,\; x_H,\; x_{HH},\; x_{HT}.
> $$
>
> We want $x_S$.
>
> ## Transition equations
>
> From state $S$:
>
> - Flip $\text{H}$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $H$
> - Flip $\text{T}$ with probability $\tfrac12$, staying in $S$
>
> So
>
> $$
> x_S=\frac12 x_H+\frac12 x_S.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> x_S=x_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Flip $\text{H}$, giving suffix $\text{HH}$
> - Flip $\text{T}$, giving suffix $\text{HT}$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> x_H=\frac12 x_{HH}+\frac12 x_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Flip $\text{T}$, and $\text{HHT}$ occurs, so you win
> - Flip $\text{H}$, and the suffix remains $\text{HH}$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> x_{HH}=\frac12 \cdot 1+\frac12 x_{HH}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> x_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - Flip $\text{T}$, and $\text{HTT}$ occurs first, so you lose
> - Flip $\text{H}$, and the new suffix is $\text{H}$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> x_{HT}=\frac12 \cdot 0+\frac12 x_H=\frac12 x_H.
> $$
>
> ## Solve
>
> Substitute into the equation for $x_H$:
>
> $$
> x_H=\frac12 x_{HH}+\frac12 x_{HT}
> =\frac12 \cdot 1+\frac12 \cdot \frac12 x_H
> =\frac12+\frac14 x_H.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> x_H-\frac14 x_H=\frac12
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \frac34 x_H=\frac12,
> \qquad
> x_H=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Since $x_S=x_H$, the desired probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
