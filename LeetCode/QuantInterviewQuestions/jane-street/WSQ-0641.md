> [!example] Question 641 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

I roll a single die. If I am not satisfied with the number, I can choose to reroll once (for a maximum of two rolls). What is my expected value, assuming I play optimally?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=642)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V$ denote the maximum expected value achievable when using the option to reroll once.
>
> On the second roll, there is no further choice, so the expected value is simply the mean of a fair die:
>
> $$
> E[\text{second roll}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, after seeing the first roll, the rational strategy is:
>
> - keep the first roll if it is at least $3.5$,
> - reroll if it is less than $3.5$.
>
> Since die outcomes are integers, this means:
>
> - reroll on $1,2,3$,
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> Now compute the expected value under this strategy. With probability $\frac{3}{6}$, the first roll is $1,2,3$, and then the expected value is $3.5$. With probability $\frac{1}{6}$ each, the first roll is $4,5,6$, which are kept.
>
> So
>
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V = \frac{10.5 + 15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected value when playing rationally is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
