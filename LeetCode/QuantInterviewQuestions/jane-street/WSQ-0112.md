> [!example] Question 112 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

I sample p uniformly from \[0,1\] and flip a coin 100 times. The coin lands heads with probability p in each flip. Before each flip, you are allowed to guess which side it will land on. For each correct guess, you gain \$1; for each incorrect guess, you lose \$1. What would your strategy be, and would you pay \$20 to play this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=113)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$ be chosen once, and then, conditional on $p$, the $100$ coin flips are independent with
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \mid p)=p, \qquad \Pr(T \mid p)=1-p.
> $$
>
> Before each flip, a guess must be made. A correct guess yields $+1$ dollar and an incorrect guess yields $-1$ dollar. The goal is to maximize expected total profit.
>
> ## 1. Optimal strategy
>
> After observing previous flips, the distribution of $p$ is updated. Suppose that after some number of flips there have been $h$ heads and $t$ tails. Since the prior on $p$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, the posterior distribution is
>
> $$
> p \mid (h,t) \sim \mathrm{Beta}(h+1,t+1).
> $$
>
> Hence the posterior predictive probability that the next flip is heads is
>
> $$
> \Pr(H_{\text{next}} \mid h,t)=\mathbb{E}[p \mid h,t]
> = \frac{h+1}{h+t+2}.
> $$
>
> Similarly,
>
> $$
> \Pr(T_{\text{next}} \mid h,t)=\frac{t+1}{h+t+2}.
> $$
>
> To maximize expected gain on the next flip, one should guess the more likely outcome. Therefore:
>
> - guess **heads** if $h>t$,
> - guess **tails** if $t>h$,
> - if $h=t$, either guess is equally good.
>
> So the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{At each stage, guess the majority side among the previous flips.}
> $$
>
> Initially, before any flips, $h=t=0$, so either guess is equivalent on the first flip.
>
> ## 2. Expected gain on a given flip
>
> If after $n=h+t$ observed flips the counts are $(h,t)$, then by guessing the more likely side, the probability of being correct is
>
> $$
> \max\left(\frac{h+1}{n+2},\frac{t+1}{n+2}\right).
> $$
>
> Therefore the conditional expected gain on the next flip is
>
> $$
> \left(+1\right)\Pr(\text{correct}\mid h,t)+\left(-1\right)\Pr(\text{wrong}\mid h,t)
> =2\max\left(\frac{h+1}{n+2},\frac{t+1}{n+2}\right)-1.
> $$
>
> Since $h+t=n$, this simplifies to
>
> $$
> \frac{|h-t|}{n+2}.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected gain on flip $n+1$ is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{|H_n-T_n|}{n+2}\right],
> $$
>
> where $(H_n,T_n)$ are the numbers of heads and tails among the first $n$ flips.
>
> ## 3. Distribution of the number of heads
>
> Because $p$ is uniform and then flips are generated conditionally on $p$, the unconditional distribution of the number of heads in the first $n$ flips is beta-binomial:
>
> $$
> \Pr(H_n=k)=\binom{n}{k}\int_0^1 p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\,dp.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \int_0^1 p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\,dp=\frac{k!(n-k)!}{(n+1)!},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> \Pr(H_n=k)=\frac{1}{n+1}, \qquad k=0,1,\dots,n.
> $$
>
> So $H_n$ is uniformly distributed on $\{0,1,\dots,n\}$.
>
> Since $T_n=n-H_n$,
>
> $$
> |H_n-T_n|=|2H_n-n|.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected gain on flip $n+1$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{n+2}\cdot \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n |2k-n|.
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> S_n=\sum_{k=0}^n |2k-n|.
> $$
>
> Then the expected total gain over $100$ flips is
>
> $$
> \sum_{n=0}^{99} \frac{S_n}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
> $$
>
> For $n=2m$ even,
>
> $$
> S_{2m}=2\sum_{j=1}^m 2j=2m(m+1).
> $$
>
> For $n=2m+1$ odd,
>
> $$
> S_{2m+1}=2\sum_{j=0}^m (2j+1)=2(m+1)^2.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - if $n=2m$,
>
> $$
> \frac{S_n}{(n+1)(n+2)}
> =\frac{2m(m+1)}{(2m+1)(2m+2)}
> =\frac{m}{2m+1};
> $$
>
> - if $n=2m+1$,
>
> $$
> \frac{S_n}{(n+1)(n+2)}
> =\frac{2(m+1)^2}{(2m+2)(2m+3)}
> =\frac{m+1}{2m+3}.
> $$
>
> Thus the total expected gain is
>
> $$
> \sum_{m=0}^{49}\frac{m}{2m+1}+\sum_{m=0}^{49}\frac{m+1}{2m+3}.
> $$
>
> Writing the first sum with index shift $j=m+1$ gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{m=0}^{49}\frac{m}{2m+1}
> =
> \sum_{j=1}^{50}\frac{j-1}{2j-1},
> $$
>
> and the second is
>
> $$
> \sum_{j=1}^{50}\frac{j}{2j+1}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> \sum_{n=0}^{99} \frac{S_n}{(n+1)(n+2)} \approx 47.557.
> $$
>
> So the optimal expected profit is about
>
> $$
> \text{\textdollar}47.56.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Decision about paying twenty dollars
>
> If the entry fee is $\text{\textdollar}20$, then the expected net gain is
>
> $$
> 47.557 - 20 \approx 27.557.
> $$
>
> This is positive, so one should pay $\text{\textdollar}20$ to play.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Before each flip, guess the side that has appeared more often so far;}
> $$
>
> if the counts are tied, either guess is equally good.
>
> Under this strategy, the expected total profit from the $100$ guesses is approximately
>
> $$
> \text{\textdollar}47.56.
> $$
>
> Therefore, paying $\text{\textdollar}20$ to play has expected net value approximately
>
> $$
> \text{\textdollar}27.56,
> $$
>
> so **yes, it is worth paying $\text{\textdollar}20$ to play**.
