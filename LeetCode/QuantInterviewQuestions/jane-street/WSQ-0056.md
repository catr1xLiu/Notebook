> [!example] Question 56 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A clock falls from the wall and breaks into three pieces, each of which has the same sum of the numbers printed on it. What are the three pieces?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=57)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A standard clock has the numbers $1$ through $12$, whose total is
>
> $$
> 1+2+\cdots+12=\frac{12\cdot 13}{2}=78.
> $$
>
> If the clock breaks into $3$ pieces with the same sum of numbers, then each piece must have sum
>
> $$
> \frac{78}{3}=26.
> $$
>
> ## Why the pieces need not be consecutive arcs
>
> If a clock face broke along cracks that all pass through the center, like slicing a pie, each piece would necessarily be a single wedge containing a run of consecutive numbers. But a clock face can just as easily break along straight cracks that do **not** pass through the center. In particular, consider two **parallel** straight cracks cutting across the disk, one nearer the top and one nearer the bottom. These two parallel cracks divide the face into three regions:
>
> - a small cap above the upper crack,
> - a small cap below the lower crack,
> - a wide band in between.
>
> The two caps each touch the rim in a single connected arc, so each one carries a set of consecutive numbers. The middle band, however, is a single connected physical piece that touches the rim in **two** separate arcs, one on each side of the band. So the band can carry two disjoint groups of consecutive numbers while still being one solid fragment of the clock face.
>
> ## Constructing the break
>
> Place the two parallel cracks as follows:
>
> - the upper crack cuts off the top cap containing $11,12,1,2$;
> - the lower crack cuts off the bottom cap containing $5,6,7,8$;
> - the middle band is everything left over, namely $3,4$ on one side and $9,10$ on the other side, joined into a single band-shaped piece.
>
> ## Checking the sums
>
> Each of the three pieces sums to $26$:
>
> $$
> 11+12+1+2=26,
> $$
>
> $$
> 3+4+9+10=26,
> $$
>
> $$
> 5+6+7+8=26.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The clock breaks into three pieces along two parallel cracks:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\{11,12,1,2\},\ \{3,4,9,10\},\ \{5,6,7,8\}},
> $$
>
> each summing to $26$. The middle piece, $\{3,4,9,10\}$, is a single band-shaped fragment that touches the rim in two separate arcs rather than one; that is why its numbers are not consecutive around the clock face even though the piece itself is a single connected region.
