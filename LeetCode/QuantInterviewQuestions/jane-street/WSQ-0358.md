> [!example] Question 358 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A cube painted with red paint is divided into 27 smaller cubes (3×3×3). If one small cube is randomly selected and tossed, what is the probability that at least one of the sides that are visible has paint on it?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=359)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Divide the painted cube into $27$ small cubes arranged as a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid. A small cube is chosen uniformly at random and then tossed, so each of its $6$ faces is equally likely to end up on the bottom. Hence the number of painted faces visible after the toss depends only on how many painted faces the chosen small cube has.
>
> We classify the small cubes by position:
>
> - **Corner cubes:** $8$ cubes, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - **Edge cubes (not corners):** $12$ cubes, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - **Face-center cubes:** $6$ cubes, each with $1$ painted face.
> - **Interior cube:** $1$ cube, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> For a cube with $k$ painted faces, after tossing it, at least one painted face is visible unless all $k$ painted faces are hidden on the bottom. Since only one face is hidden, this is impossible when $k \ge 2$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - If the chosen cube has $3$ painted faces, the probability is $1$.
> - If it has $2$ painted faces, the probability is $1$.
> - If it has $1$ painted face, the probability is $\frac{5}{6}$, since the painted face is hidden only when it lands on the bottom.
> - If it has $0$ painted faces, the probability is $0$.
>
> Now average over all $27$ cubes:
>
> $$
> P=\frac{8}{27}(1)+\frac{12}{27}(1)+\frac{6}{27}\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)+\frac{1}{27}(0).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> P=\frac{8+12}{27}+\frac{5}{27}
> =\frac{25}{27}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{25}{27}}.
> $$
