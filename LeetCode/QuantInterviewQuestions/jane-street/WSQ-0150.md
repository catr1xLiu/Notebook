> [!example] Question 150 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

I'm flipping three coins. If all three are the same (all heads or all tails), then I will receive \$10 and may finish the game. If not, I may choose to flip any number of the coins again. What is the expected gain from playing this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=151)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Model the game as a decision problem: after each set of three coin flips, you may either stop or choose some subset of coins to reflip in order to maximize the expected eventual payoff.
>
> The payoff is:
>
> - $10$ dollars if the three coins are all the same, i.e. either $HHH$ or $TTT$,
> - $0$ otherwise, unless you continue and reflip some coins.
>
> Thus the problem is to determine the optimal strategy and its expected value.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Classify the possible states
>
> After any round of flips, only the pattern of agreement matters.
>
> There are two essentially different states:
>
> 1. **Winning state:** all three coins are equal.
>    - Probability on a fresh flip:  
>      $$
>      P(HHH \text{ or } TTT)=\frac{2}{8}=\frac14.
>      $$
>    - In this state you should stop and take $10$ dollars.
>
> 2. **Non-winning state:** exactly two coins are the same and one is different.
>    - This always happens when the three are not all equal.
>    - Examples: $HHT$, $HTH$, $THH$, $TTH$, $THT$, $HTT$.
>
> So after any non-winning result, the state is always “two alike, one different.”
>
> Let $V$ denote the optimal expected payoff from this non-winning state.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Determine the best action from the non-winning state
>
> Suppose the current pattern is, for example, $HHT$. The same analysis applies to any permutation.
>
> Possible choices:
>
> ### Option A: Reflip the odd coin only
>
> Reflip the $T$.
>
> - With probability $\frac12$, it becomes $H$, giving $HHH$ and payoff $10$.
> - With probability $\frac12$, it remains $T$, so the state is again non-winning, with value $V$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> \text{Value of this action}=\frac12\cdot 10+\frac12\cdot V = 5+\frac12 V.
> $$
>
> ### Option B: Reflip one of the matching coins
>
> For instance, reflip one of the $H$'s.
>
> - With probability $\frac12$, it stays $H$, so the pattern remains $HHT$, again value $V$.
> - With probability $\frac12$, it becomes $T$, giving $THT$, still non-winning, again value $V$.
>
> So this yields
> $$
> V.
> $$
>
> This is no improvement at all.
>
> ### Option C: Reflip two coins
>
> There are two subcases.
>
> #### Reflip the two matching coins
>
> From $HHT$, reflip the two $H$'s.
>
> The four equally likely outcomes are:
>
> - $HH$ $\Rightarrow HHT$ : non-winning,
> - $HT$ $\Rightarrow HTT$ : non-winning,
> - $TH$ $\Rightarrow THT$ : non-winning,
> - $TT$ $\Rightarrow TTT$ : winning.
>
> So the value is
> $$
> \frac14\cdot 10+\frac34\cdot V = \frac52+\frac34 V.
> $$
>
> #### Reflip one matching coin and the odd coin
>
> Again from $HHT$, reflip one $H$ and the $T$.
>
> The outcomes are:
>
> - $HH \Rightarrow HHH$ : winning,
> - $HT \Rightarrow HHT$ : non-winning,
> - $TH \Rightarrow TTH$ : non-winning,
> - $TT \Rightarrow TTT$ : winning.
>
> Thus with probability $\frac12$ you win immediately, and with probability $\frac12$ you return to the non-winning state. Hence the value is
> $$
> \frac12\cdot 10+\frac12\cdot V = 5+\frac12 V.
> $$
>
> ### Option D: Reflip all three coins
>
> This is just a fresh start. The expected value from a fresh start, call it $E$, will be computed later. For comparison, this cannot be better than using the best continuation rule from the current state; in any case we can solve directly without needing it.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Solve for the optimal value $V$
>
> Among the actions above, the best is to reflip either:
>
> - the odd coin only, or
> - one matching coin together with the odd coin.
>
> Both give
> $$
> 5+\frac12 V.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimality equation is
> $$
> V = 5+\frac12 V.
> $$
> Solving,
> $$
> \frac12 V = 5,
> \qquad
> V=10.
> $$
>
> So from any non-winning state, the optimal expected payoff is also $10$ dollars.
>
> This is intuitively clear: by repeatedly reflipping the odd coin, eventually it must match the other two with probability $1$, so you will eventually win $10$ dollars for sure.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 4: Compute the expected payoff from the start
>
> At the initial flip:
>
> - with probability $\frac14$, all three coins are equal and you receive $10$ immediately;
> - with probability $\frac34$, you are in the non-winning state, whose value is $V=10$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E=\frac14\cdot 10+\frac34\cdot 10 = 10.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected gain is
>
> $$
> \boxed{10\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Optimal strategy
>
> After any non-winning result, keep the two matching coins fixed and reflip the odd coin until it matches them. This guarantees eventual success with probability $1$, so the payoff is $10$ dollars with certainty.
>
> Thus the expected gain is
>
> $$
> \boxed{10\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
