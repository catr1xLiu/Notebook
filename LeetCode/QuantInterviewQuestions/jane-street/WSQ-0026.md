> [!example] Question 26 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A weather report says there is a 30% chance of rain on Saturday and a 30% chance of rain on Sunday. What can you say about the probability that it rains on at least one of the days?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=27)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it rains on Saturday and $B$ the event that it rains on Sunday. We are given
>
> $$
> P(A)=0.3, \qquad P(B)=0.3.
> $$
>
> The probability that it rains on at least one of the two days is
>
> $$
> P(A \cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A \cap B)=0.6-P(A \cap B).
> $$
>
> Since probabilities satisfy
>
> $$
> 0 \le P(A \cap B) \le 0.3,
> $$
>
> it follows that
>
> $$
> 0.6-0.3 \le P(A \cup B) \le 0.6,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> 0.3 \le P(\text{rain on at least one day}) \le 0.6.
> $$
>
> Thus, without additional information about the dependence between the two days, the probability of rain on at least one of the days could be anywhere between $\boxed{0.3 \text{ and } 0.6}$.
>
> In particular:
>
> - if the events are mutually exclusive, then
>   $$
>   P(A \cup B)=0.6;
>   $$
> - if one event always occurs whenever the other occurs, then
>   $$
>   P(A \cup B)=0.3;
>   $$
> - if the two days are independent, then
>   $$
>   P(A \cup B)=1-(0.7)(0.7)=0.51.
>   $$
