> [!example] Question 470 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

How many coin tosses are expected before getting 5 heads in a row?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=471)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E_k$ denote the expected number of additional tosses needed to obtain $5$ consecutive heads, given that the current run of consecutive heads has length $k$, where $k=0,1,2,3,4,5$. Clearly,
>
> $$
> E_5 = 0,
> $$
>
> since once $5$ heads in a row have occurred, no more tosses are needed.
>
> For $k=0,1,2,3,4$, after one toss:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, a head occurs and the run increases to length $k+1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, a tail occurs and the run resets to $0$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E_k = 1 + \frac12 E_{k+1} + \frac12 E_0, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> We now solve these equations backward.
>
> For $k=4$:
>
> $$
> E_4 = 1 + \frac12 E_5 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> For $k=3$:
>
> $$
> E_3 = 1 + \frac12 E_4 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12\left(1+\frac12 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> For $k=2$:
>
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_3 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12\left(\frac32+\frac34 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac74 + \frac78 E_0.
> $$
>
> For $k=1$:
>
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12\left(\frac74+\frac78 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac{15}{8} + \frac{15}{16} E_0.
> $$
>
> For $k=0$:
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute $E_1$:
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12\left(\frac{15}{8} + \frac{15}{16}E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{15}{16} + \frac{15}{32}E_0 + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac{31}{16} + \frac{31}{32}E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> E_0 - \frac{31}{32}E_0 = \frac{31}{16},
> $$
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{32}E_0 = \frac{31}{16},
> $$
>
> and therefore
>
> $$
> E_0 = 62.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected number of tosses required to get $5$ heads in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{62}.
> $$
