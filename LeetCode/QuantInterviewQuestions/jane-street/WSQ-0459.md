> [!example] Question 459 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the expected number of tries needed to get 3 consecutive heads in repeated fair coin flips?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=460)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E_k$ denote the expected additional number of flips needed to obtain $3$ consecutive heads, given that the current run of consecutive heads has length $k$, where $k \in \{0,1,2,3\}$. Clearly, $E_3 = 0$.
>
> We seek $E_0$.
>
> Assume the coin is fair, so each flip is heads with probability $\tfrac12$ and tails with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the recurrence relations
>
> ### State $0$
> If currently there are $0$ consecutive heads:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a head and move to state $1$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a tail and remain in state $0$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### State $1$
> If currently there is $1$ consecutive head:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a head and move to state $2$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a tail and return to state $0$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### State $2$
> If currently there are $2$ consecutive heads:
>
> - one flip is used,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a head and finish, reaching state $3$,
> - with probability $\tfrac12$ we get a tail and return to state $0$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_3 + \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> From the first equation,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 2 + E_1.
> $$
>
> From the third equation,
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute this into the equation for $E_1$:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0 + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Now use $E_0 = 2 + E_1$:
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + \frac32 + \frac34 E_0
> = \frac72 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> E_0 - \frac34 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \frac14 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 14.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of flips needed to get $3$ consecutive heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{14}.
> $$
