> [!example] Question 166 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Suppose you are standing at one-third the length of a bridge when you hear a train coming from behind. You have just enough time to run back and get off the bridge before the train reaches you, and you also have just enough time if you run forward. What is the relative speed between you and the train?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=167)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the bridge have length $L$, and let your position be at $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of the bridge length from the nearer end behind you. Thus:
>
> - distance to the back end: $\dfrac{L}{3}$,
> - distance to the front end: $\dfrac{2L}{3}$.
>
> Let your running speed be $v$, and let the train speed be $u$.
>
> Assume the train is behind you and is at distance $d$ from your position when you first hear it.
>
> ## Condition 1: running backward
> If you run backward, you must cover $\dfrac{L}{3}$ before the train reaches you. Since you have *just enough time*, the times are equal:
>
> $$
> \frac{L/3}{v}=\frac{d}{u}.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> d=\frac{uL}{3v}.
> $$
>
> ## Condition 2: running forward
> If instead you run forward, you must cover $\dfrac{2L}{3}$ to reach the other end. In this case, the train must travel the initial gap $d$ plus the distance you run, namely $\dfrac{2L}{3}$. Again the times are equal:
>
> $$
> \frac{2L/3}{v}=\frac{d+2L/3}{u}.
> $$
>
> Substitute $d=\dfrac{uL}{3v}$:
>
> $$
> \frac{2L}{3v}=\frac{\frac{uL}{3v}+\frac{2L}{3}}{u}.
> $$
>
> Multiply by $u$:
>
> $$
> \frac{2Lu}{3v}=\frac{uL}{3v}+\frac{2L}{3}.
> $$
>
> Multiply through by $\dfrac{3v}{L}$:
>
> $$
> 2u=u+2v.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> u=2v.
> $$
>
> Therefore the train’s speed is twice your speed, so the relative speed between you and the train is
>
> $$
> u-v=2v-v=v.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
> The train moves at twice your speed, and the relative speed between you and the train is equal to **your running speed**. Equivalently, the ratio of train speed to your speed is
>
> $$
> 2:1.
> $$
