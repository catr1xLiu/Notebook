> [!example] Question 529 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

N points lie on a circle. You draw lines connecting all the points to each other. These lines divide the circle into a number of regions. How many regions are created? Assume the points are positioned so as to give the maximum number of regions for that N.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=530)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $R_n$ denote the maximum number of regions determined inside a circle by drawing all chords joining $n$ points on the circle, under the assumption that no three chords intersect at a single interior point.
>
> ## Counting the regions
>
> Draw the chords one at a time. Each new chord increases the number of regions by the number of pieces into which it is cut, because each piece splits one existing region into two.
>
> So if a chord is cut into $k+1$ pieces by $k$ interior intersection points, then it creates $k+1$ new regions.
>
> Thus the total number of regions is
>
> $$
> R_n = 1 + (\text{number of chords}) + (\text{number of interior intersections of chords}).
> $$
>
> Indeed:
>
> - Start with $1$ region.
> - Each chord contributes $1$ new region if it has no intersection.
> - Each interior intersection on a chord adds one more piece, hence one more region.
>
> Therefore it remains to count:
>
> 1. the number of chords,
> 2. the number of interior intersections.
>
> ## Number of chords
>
> Every pair of points determines a chord, so the number of chords is
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Number of interior intersections
>
> An interior intersection is determined by choosing $4$ points on the circle: if $A,B,C,D$ are chosen in circular order, then the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect once inside the circle.
>
> Conversely, every interior intersection comes from exactly one set of $4$ points.
>
> Hence the number of interior intersections is
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Formula
>
> Substituting into the region count gives
>
> $$
> R_n = 1 + \binom{n}{2} + \binom{n}{4}.
> $$
>
> So the maximum number of regions formed by connecting all $n$ points on a circle is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 + \binom{n}{2} + \binom{n}{4}}.
> $$
>
> ## Expanded form
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}, \qquad
> \binom{n}{4} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{24},
> $$
>
> we may also write
>
> $$
> R_n = 1 + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{24}.
> $$
>
> Thus the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{R_n = 1 + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{24}}.
> $$
