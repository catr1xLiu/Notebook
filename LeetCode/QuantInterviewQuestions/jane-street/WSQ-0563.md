> [!example] Question 563 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the largest positive integer that cannot be expressed as a sum of non-negative multiples of 7 and 11?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=564)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the largest integer that cannot be expressed in the form
>
> $$
> 7a + 11b
> $$
>
> where $a,b$ are nonnegative integers, use the Frobenius coin formula.
>
> For two relatively prime positive integers $m$ and $n$, the largest integer that cannot be written as
>
> $$
> mx + ny
> $$
>
> with $x,y \ge 0$ integers is
>
> $$
> mn - m - n.
> $$
>
> Here, $m=7$ and $n=11$. Since
>
> $$
> \gcd(7,11)=1,
> $$
>
> the formula applies. Thus the largest unattainable number is
>
> $$
> 7\cdot 11 - 7 - 11 = 77 - 18 = 59.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the largest number that cannot be formed using $7$ and $11$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{59}.
> $$
