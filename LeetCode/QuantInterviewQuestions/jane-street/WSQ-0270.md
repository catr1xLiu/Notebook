> [!example] Question 270 · Jane Street
> ## Junior Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting an even number of heads in seven coin tosses?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=271)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $7$ tosses of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \text{Binomial}(7,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = P(X=0)+P(X=2)+P(X=4)+P(X=6).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{7}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^7.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})
> =
> \left[\binom{7}{0}+\binom{7}{2}+\binom{7}{4}+\binom{7}{6}\right]\left(\tfrac12\right)^7.
> $$
>
> Compute the coefficients:
>
> $$
> \binom{7}{0}=1,\quad \binom{7}{2}=21,\quad \binom{7}{4}=35,\quad \binom{7}{6}=7.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even})
> =
> \frac{1+21+35+7}{2^7}
> =
> \frac{64}{128}
> =
> \frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of getting an even number of heads in $7$ coin tosses is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
