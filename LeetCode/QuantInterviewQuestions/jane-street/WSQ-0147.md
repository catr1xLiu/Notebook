> [!example] Question 147 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Given two dice, where it is known that the first die shows a 6, what is the probability that both dice show a 6?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=148)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two dice be fair and independent.
>
> Given that the first die shows $6$, the only uncertainty is the result of the second die. For both dice to show $6$, the second die must also show $6$.
>
> Since a fair die has probability
>
> $$
> P(\text{second die is }6)=\frac{1}{6},
> $$
>
> it follows that
>
> $$
> P(\text{both show }6 \mid \text{first shows }6)=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}.
> $$
