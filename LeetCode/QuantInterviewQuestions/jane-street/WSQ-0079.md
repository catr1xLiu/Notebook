> [!example] Question 79 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

What is the sum of all the digits needed to form the numbers from 1 to 1,000,000?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=80)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the sum of all digits appearing in the integers from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$, first compute the sum of digits from $0$ to $999{,}999$, then add the contribution of $1{,}000{,}000$.
>
> ## 1. Sum of digits from $0$ to $999{,}999$
>
> Write each number with exactly $6$ digits, allowing leading zeros. Thus we consider all strings from $000000$ to $999999$.
>
> There are $10^6$ such strings. In each of the $6$ digit positions:
>
> - each digit $0,1,2,\dots,9$ appears equally often,
> - namely $10^5$ times.
>
> So the total contribution from one position is
>
> $$
> (0+1+2+\cdots+9)\cdot 10^5 = 45\cdot 10^5.
> $$
>
> Since there are $6$ positions, the total sum of all digits is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 45 \cdot 10^5 = 270 \cdot 10^5 = 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> This is the sum of digits of all numbers from $000000$ to $999999$, which is the same as from $0$ to $999{,}999$.
>
> ## 2. Adjust to the desired range
>
> The number $0$ contributes digit sum $0$, so the sum from $1$ to $999{,}999$ is still
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> Now include $1{,}000{,}000$, whose digit sum is
>
> $$
> 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore the total sum is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000 + 1 = 27{,}000{,}001.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{27{,}000{,}001}
> $$
