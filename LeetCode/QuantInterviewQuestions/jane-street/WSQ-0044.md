> [!example] Question 44 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You have two decks of cards: a 52-card deck (26 black, 26 red) and a 26-card deck (13 black, 13 red). You randomly draw two cards and win if both are the same color. Which deck would you prefer? What if the 26-card deck was randomly drawn from the 52-card deck? Which deck would you prefer then?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=45)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> We compare the probability of drawing two cards of the same color.
>
> Let “success” mean that the two drawn cards are both red or both black.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Comparing a full $52$-card deck with a balanced $26$-card deck
>
> ### Deck of $52$ cards
>
> There are $26$ red and $26$ black cards.
>
> The probability that two cards drawn without replacement have the same color is
>
> $$
> P_{52}
> = \frac{\binom{26}{2}+\binom{26}{2}}{\binom{52}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> P_{52}
> = \frac{2\binom{26}{2}}{\binom{52}{2}}
> = \frac{2 \cdot \frac{26\cdot 25}{2}}{\frac{52\cdot 51}{2}}
> = \frac{26\cdot 25}{26\cdot 51}
> = \frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Deck of $26$ cards
>
> There are $13$ red and $13$ black cards.
>
> Similarly,
>
> $$
> P_{26}
> = \frac{\binom{13}{2}+\binom{13}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}
> = \frac{2\binom{13}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> P_{26}
> = \frac{2 \cdot \frac{13\cdot 12}{2}}{\frac{26\cdot 25}{2}}
> = \frac{13\cdot 12}{13\cdot 25}
> = \frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> Now compare:
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51} - \frac{12}{25}
> = \frac{625 - 612}{1275}
> = \frac{13}{1275} > 0.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P_{52} > P_{26}.
> $$
>
> Thus, with a truly balanced $26$-card deck, the better choice is the **$52$-card deck**.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. If the $26$-card deck is formed by randomly selecting $26$ cards from the $52$-card deck
>
> Now the $26$-card deck is no longer guaranteed to contain exactly $13$ red and $13$ black. Its composition is random.
>
> Let $R$ be the number of red cards in this random $26$-card deck. Then the number of black cards is $26-R$.
>
> Conditional on $R=r$, the probability of drawing two cards of the same color from this deck is
>
> $$
> P(\text{same} \mid R=r)
> = \frac{\binom{r}{2}+\binom{26-r}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}}.
> $$
>
> We want the average of this over the randomness of the deck.
>
> A simpler approach is to view the experiment this way:
>
> - First choose a random $26$-card subset from the $52$-card deck.
> - Then choose $2$ cards from that subset.
>
> By symmetry, the final pair of cards is just a uniformly random pair from the original $52$-card deck. Therefore the probability that the two cards have the same color is exactly the same as in the full $52$-card deck:
>
> $$
> P(\text{same from random }26\text{-card subdeck}) = \frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> Hence in this case the two options are **equally good**.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - If the $26$-card deck has exactly $13$ red and $13$ black cards, prefer the **$52$-card deck**, since
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51} > \frac{12}{25}.
> $$
>
> - If the $26$-card deck is itself chosen uniformly at random from the $52$-card deck, then the two choices are **equally good**, and each gives probability
>
> $$
> \frac{25}{51}.
> $$
