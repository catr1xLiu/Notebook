> [!example] Question 264 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

You are playing a game in which four fair coins are flipped, and the amount of money you receive in dollars equals the number of heads that appear. If you do not like the outcome of the first four flips, you may re-flip all four coins, but you must accept the result of the second set. Determine the fair value for the game.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=265)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads on the first four fair coins, and let $Y$ be the number of heads on the second four fair coins if a re-flip is used.
>
> A fair value for the game is the expected amount won when one plays optimally.
>
> ## 1. Distribution of one set of four flips
>
> Since each coin is fair, the number of heads in four flips has distribution
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16},
> \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> E[Y]=4\cdot \frac12=2.
> $$
>
> Thus, if the first result is rejected and the coins are re-flipped, the expected payoff from the second outcome is $2$ dollars.
>
> ## 2. Optimal strategy
>
> After seeing $X$, one should keep the first outcome if it is at least as good as the expected value of re-flipping, and re-flip otherwise.
>
> So compare $X$ with $2$:
>
> - if $X=0$ or $X=1$, re-flip;
> - if $X=2,3,4$, keep the first outcome.
>
> Hence the payoff under the optimal strategy is
>
> $$
> \max(X,2).
> $$
>
> Therefore the fair value is
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)].
> $$
>
> ## 3. Compute the expected value
>
> Using the distribution of $X$,
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)]
> =
> 2\cdot P(X=0)+2\cdot P(X=1)+2\cdot P(X=2)+3\cdot P(X=3)+4\cdot P(X=4).
> $$
>
> Now substitute the probabilities:
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\frac{1}{16},\quad
> P(X=1)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(X=2)=\frac{6}{16},\quad
> P(X=3)=\frac{4}{16},\quad
> P(X=4)=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> E[\max(X,2)]
> =
> 2\cdot \frac{1}{16}
> +2\cdot \frac{4}{16}
> +2\cdot \frac{6}{16}
> +3\cdot \frac{4}{16}
> +4\cdot \frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> This is
>
> $$
> \frac{2+8+12+12+4}{16}
> =
> \frac{38}{16}
> =
> \frac{19}{8}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The fair value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{19}{8}\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
