> [!example] Question 77 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

A dealer runs a card game where there is a 20% chance of winning \$10 and an 80% chance of losing everything. How much should the dealer charge to make the game worthwhile? (Calculate the fair expected value.)

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=78)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make the game fair, set the price equal to the expected value of the payout.
>
> The payout is:
>
> - $\text{\textdollar}10$ with probability $0.2$
> - $\text{\textdollar}0$ with probability $0.8$
>
> So the expected payout is
>
> $$
> E[X] = 0.2 \cdot 10 + 0.8 \cdot 0 = 2.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the dealer should charge **$\text{\textdollar}2$** for a fair game.
>
> If the dealer wants profit, the charge should be **more than $\text{\textdollar}2$**; if the player wants it to be worthwhile, they should pay **at most $\text{\textdollar}2$**.
