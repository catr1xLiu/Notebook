> [!example] Question 265 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Intern Interview

In Russian Roulette, there is a revolver with 4 blanks and 2 bullets loaded consecutively in adjacent chambers. If the person before you fires a blank (the gun did not discharge), should you take the next shot as-is, or spin the cylinder before shooting? Justify your answer with probabilities.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=266)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the revolver have $6$ chambers containing $4$ blanks and $2$ bullets, arranged so that the $4$ blanks are consecutive and the $2$ bullets are consecutive.
>
> You observe that the previous person fired a **blank**, and now the gun is passed to you. The question is whether your chance of survival is better if you:
>
> 1. **do not spin** and pull the trigger on the next chamber, or  
> 2. **spin** the cylinder randomly before firing.
>
> ## Step 1: Survival probability if you spin
>
> If you spin, every chamber is equally likely to be under the hammer. Since $4$ of the $6$ chambers are blanks,
>
> $$
> P(\text{survive by spinning}) = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Survival probability if you do not spin
>
> Now use the information that the previous chamber was a blank.
>
> Label the chambers around the cylinder. Since the $4$ blanks are consecutive, they form a block:
>
> $$
> B,B,B,B,L,L
> $$
>
> where $B$ denotes a blank and $L$ a live round.
>
> You know the just-fired chamber was one of the $4$ blank chambers. Among these $4$ blank positions:
>
> - If the fired blank was the first, second, or third blank in the block, then the next chamber is also a blank.
> - If the fired blank was the fourth blank in the block, then the next chamber is a live round.
>
> Thus, conditional on having just seen a blank, the next chamber is blank in $3$ of the $4$ possible blank positions.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{survive without spinning} \mid \text{previous was blank}) = \frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compare
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{4} = 0.75
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.667.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{4} > \frac{2}{3},
> $$
>
> your survival probability is higher if you **do not spin**.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> You should **take another shot without spinning**. Your survival chance is
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{4}
> $$
>
> instead of
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3}
> $$
>
> if you spin.
