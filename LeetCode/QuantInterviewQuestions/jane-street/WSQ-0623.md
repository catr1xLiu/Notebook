> [!example] Question 623 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have 100 green marbles and 100 red marbles and can divide them however you like between two bags, with at least one marble in each bag. A player, who does not know the distribution, randomly chooses one of the bags and draws a single marble. If the marble is green, the player wins \$100; if red, they win nothing. What is the fair price for this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=624)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let bag $1$ contain $g_1$ green marbles and $r_1$ red marbles, and let bag $2$ contain $g_2$ green marbles and $r_2$ red marbles. Then
>
> $$
> g_1+g_2=100,\qquad r_1+r_2=100,
> $$
>
> with each bag containing at least one marble.
>
> You are given a bag uniformly at random, so each bag is chosen with probability $\tfrac12$. Then a marble is drawn uniformly from the chosen bag.
>
> The probability of drawing a green marble is therefore
>
> $$
> P(\text{green})=\frac12\cdot \frac{g_1}{g_1+r_1}+\frac12\cdot \frac{g_2}{g_2+r_2}.
> $$
>
> Since the payoff is $\text{\textdollar}100$ for green and $\text{\textdollar}0$ for red, the fair price is
>
> $$
> 100\cdot P(\text{green}).
> $$
>
> So the problem is to maximize $P(\text{green})$ over all possible divisions.
>
> ## Optimal division
>
> A standard maximizing strategy is:
>
> - put $1$ green marble alone in one bag;
> - put the remaining $99$ green marbles and all $100$ red marbles in the other bag.
>
> Then
>
> - from the first bag, the probability of green is $1$;
> - from the second bag, the probability of green is $\frac{99}{199}$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(\text{green})
> =
> \frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\cdot \frac{99}{199}
> =
> \frac12+\frac{99}{398}
> =
> \frac{298}{398}
> =
> \frac{149}{199}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fair price is
>
> $$
> 100\cdot \frac{149}{199}
> =
> \frac{14900}{199}
> \approx 74.87.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The maximum probability of winning is
>
> $$
> \frac{149}{199}\approx 0.7487,
> $$
>
> so the fair price of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$74.87\text{ (approximately)}}.
> $$
