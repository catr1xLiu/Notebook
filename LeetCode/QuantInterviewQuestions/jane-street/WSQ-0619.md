> [!example] Question 619 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

In an infinite sequence of coin flips, what is the probability that HHT appears before THH?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=620)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be the probability that the pattern $\text{HHT}$ appears before $\text{THH}$ in an infinite sequence of fair coin flips.
>
> The key idea is to track the relevant suffix of the sequence already seen, namely the longest suffix that is also a prefix of either target pattern.
>
> The target patterns are:
>
> - $A = \text{HHT}$
> - $B = \text{THH}$
>
> We define states according to the current relevant suffix:
>
> - $S$: no relevant suffix
> - $H$
> - $HH$
> - $T$
> - $TH$
>
> Let the corresponding probabilities of eventually getting $A$ before $B$ be
>
> $$
> x_S,\; x_H,\; x_{HH},\; x_T,\; x_{TH}.
> $$
>
> We want $x_S$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the recursion
>
> From state $S$:
>
> - Flip $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $H$
> - Flip $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $T$
>
> So
>
> $$
> x_S = \frac12 x_H + \frac12 x_T.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Flip $H$ $\to$ state $HH$
> - Flip $T$ $\to$ state $T$ (since the suffix $\text{HT}$ has no useful prefix, but the final $T$ is relevant)
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> x_H = \frac12 x_{HH} + \frac12 x_T.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Flip $T$ completes $\text{HHT}$, so this is a win for $A$
> - Flip $H$ leaves suffix $HH$ again
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> x_{HH} = \frac12 \cdot 1 + \frac12 x_{HH}.
> $$
>
> From state $T$:
>
> - Flip $H$ $\to$ state $TH$
> - Flip $T$ $\to$ state $T$
>
> So
>
> $$
> x_T = \frac12 x_{TH} + \frac12 x_T.
> $$
>
> From state $TH$:
>
> - Flip $H$ completes $\text{THH}$, so this is a loss for $A$
> - Flip $T$ leaves suffix $T$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> x_{TH} = \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 x_T = \frac12 x_T.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> First, from
>
> $$
> x_{HH} = \frac12 + \frac12 x_{HH},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> x_{HH} = 1.
> $$
>
> Next, from
>
> $$
> x_T = \frac12 x_{TH} + \frac12 x_T,
> $$
>
> we have
>
> $$
> \frac12 x_T = \frac12 x_{TH} \quad \Rightarrow \quad x_T = x_{TH}.
> $$
>
> But also
>
> $$
> x_{TH} = \frac12 x_T.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> x_T = \frac12 x_T,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> x_T = 0,
> $$
>
> and hence
>
> $$
> x_{TH} = 0.
> $$
>
> Now
>
> $$
> x_H = \frac12 x_{HH} + \frac12 x_T = \frac12 \cdot 1 + \frac12 \cdot 0 = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Finally,
>
> $$
> x_S = \frac12 x_H + \frac12 x_T = \frac12 \cdot \frac12 + \frac12 \cdot 0 = \frac14.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The probability that $\text{HHT}$ occurs before $\text{THH}$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac14}.
> $$
