> [!example] Question 546 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting an even number of heads when flipping 39 fair coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=547)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> When $39$ fair coins are flipped, each outcome is equally likely, and the total number of possible outcomes is
>
> $$
> 2^{39}.
> $$
>
> An even number of heads means obtaining $0,2,4,\dots,38$ heads. The number of outcomes with exactly $k$ heads is
>
> $$
> \binom{39}{k}.
> $$
>
> Thus the number of outcomes with an even number of heads is
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k \text{ even}}}^{39} \binom{39}{k}.
> $$
>
> Use the binomial identities
>
> $$
> (1+1)^{39}=\sum_{k=0}^{39}\binom{39}{k}=2^{39},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^{39}=\sum_{k=0}^{39}\binom{39}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> The second identity implies that the sum of the even-indexed binomial coefficients equals the sum of the odd-indexed ones. Since together they add to $2^{39}$, each sum must be
>
> $$
> 2^{38}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the desired probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{2^{38}}{2^{39}}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> \[
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> \]
