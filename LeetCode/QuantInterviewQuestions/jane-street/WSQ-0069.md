> [!example] Question 69 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

1. What is the angle between the hour and minute hands at 11:40? 2. Four fair coins are tossed. (a) What is the probability of getting at least 2 heads? (b) Given that there are at least 2 heads, what is the expected number of heads? 3. You roll two fair dice, one with 6 sides and one with 10 sides. You may guess the sum x of the two dice. If you guess correctly, you win \$x; otherwise, you win nothing. Which number should you guess?

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Angle between the hour and minute hands at $11{:}40$
>
> At $11{:}40$:
>
> - The minute hand is at
>   $$
>   40 \times 6^\circ = 240^\circ
>   $$
>   from $12$.
>
> - The hour hand is at
>   $$
>   11 \times 30^\circ + 40 \times 0.5^\circ = 330^\circ + 20^\circ = 350^\circ.
>   $$
>
> Thus the difference is
> $$
> |350^\circ - 240^\circ| = 110^\circ.
> $$
>
> The smaller angle is therefore
> $$
> 110^\circ.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Four fair coins
>
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $4$ fair coin tosses. Then
> $$
> H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12),
> $$
> so
> $$
> P(H=k)=\binom{4}{k}\frac{1}{16}, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> ### (a) Probability of at least $2$ heads
>
> $$
> P(H\ge 2)=P(H=2)+P(H=3)+P(H=4).
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> P(H=2)=\frac{\binom{4}{2}}{16}=\frac{6}{16},\qquad
> P(H=3)=\frac{\binom{4}{3}}{16}=\frac{4}{16},\qquad
> P(H=4)=\frac{\binom{4}{4}}{16}=\frac{1}{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> P(H\ge 2)=\frac{6+4+1}{16}=\frac{11}{16}.
> $$
>
> ### (b) Given there are at least $2$ heads, expectation of the number of heads
>
> We want
> $$
> E[H\mid H\ge 2].
> $$
>
> By definition,
> $$
> E[H\mid H\ge 2]
> =\frac{2P(H=2)+3P(H=3)+4P(H=4)}{P(H\ge 2)}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
> $$
> E[H\mid H\ge 2]
> =
> \frac{2\cdot \frac{6}{16}+3\cdot \frac{4}{16}+4\cdot \frac{1}{16}}{\frac{11}{16}}
> =
> \frac{\frac{12+12+4}{16}}{\frac{11}{16}}
> =
> \frac{28}{11}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[H\mid H\ge 2]=\frac{28}{11}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Guessing the sum of a fair $6$-sided die and a fair $10$-sided die
>
> Let the dice be:
>
> - $D_6 \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$,
> - $D_{10} \in \{1,2,\dots,10\}$.
>
> There are
> $$
> 6\cdot 10=60
> $$
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> If you guess sum $x$, your expected payoff is
> $$
> x\cdot P(D_6+D_{10}=x).
> $$
>
> Thus we must maximize
> $$
> x\cdot P(S=x),
> $$
> where
> $$
> S=D_6+D_{10}.
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> P(S=x)=\frac{N(x)}{60},
> $$
> where $N(x)$ is the number of pairs $(a,b)$ with
> $$
> a+b=x,\qquad 1\le a\le 6,\quad 1\le b\le 10,
> $$
> it suffices to maximize
> $$
> x\,N(x).
> $$
>
> ### Count the number of ways to obtain each sum
>
> The possible sums are from $2$ to $16$.
>
> For $x=2,3,4,5,6,7$, the counts increase:
> $$
> N(x)=x-1.
> $$
>
> For $x=8,9,10,11$, the count stays at $6$:
> $$
> N(x)=6.
> $$
>
> For $x=12,13,14,15,16$, the counts decrease:
> $$
> N(x)=17-x.
> $$
>
> Now compute $xN(x)$:
>
> - For $x=2,3,4,5,6,7$:
>   $$
>   2\cdot 1=2,\;
>   3\cdot 2=6,\;
>   4\cdot 3=12,\;
>   5\cdot 4=20,\;
>   6\cdot 5=30,\;
>   7\cdot 6=42.
>   $$
>
> - For $x=8,9,10,11$:
>   $$
>   8\cdot 6=48,\;
>   9\cdot 6=54,\;
>   10\cdot 6=60,\;
>   11\cdot 6=66.
>   $$
>
> - For $x=12,13,14,15,16$:
>   $$
>   12\cdot 5=60,\;
>   13\cdot 4=52,\;
>   14\cdot 3=42,\;
>   15\cdot 2=30,\;
>   16\cdot 1=16.
>   $$
>
> The maximum value is
> $$
> 66
> $$
> at
> $$
> x=11.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimal guess is
> $$
> 11.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> 1. The angle is
>    $$
>    110^\circ.
>    $$
>
> 2. For four fair coins:
>    - $P(\text{at least }2\text{ heads})=\dfrac{11}{16}$.
>    - $E[\#\text{ heads}\mid \#\text{ heads}\ge 2]=\dfrac{28}{11}$.
>
> 3. The best sum to guess is
>    $$
>    11.
>    $$
