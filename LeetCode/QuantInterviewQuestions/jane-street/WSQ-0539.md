> [!example] Question 539 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Today is Monday. Ten years from now, on this same date, what day of the week will it be? Explain your reasoning and state your confidence level.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=540)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A calendar date advances by:
>
> - $1$ weekday in a common year,
> - $2$ weekdays in a leap year.
>
> Over $10$ years, the weekday shift depends on how many leap years occur in that interval. Thus the total shift is
>
> $$
> 10 + L \pmod{7},
> $$
>
> where $L$ is the number of leap years in the next $10$ years.
>
> Since $L$ can vary depending on the starting date and whether the interval includes $2$ or $3$ leap days, the answer is **not uniquely determined** from “Monday” alone. In practice:
>
> - if $L=2$, the shift is $12 \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$, giving **Saturday**;
> - if $L=3$, the shift is $13 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$, giving **Sunday**.
>
> So **$10$ years from now on this date will be either Saturday or Sunday, depending on the exact date and the leap years crossed**.
>
> **Confidence:** low for a single weekday answer, because the exact calendar date was not specified. The information given is insufficient to determine one unique day.
