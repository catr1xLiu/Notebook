> [!example] Question 271 · Jane Street
> ## Junior Trader Interview

A train is approaching a bridge, and you are standing at the 1/4 position along the length of the bridge. If running in either direction allows you to escape just in time, what is the ratio of your speed to the speed of the train?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=272)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the length of the bridge be $L$, your speed be $v$, and the train's speed be $u$. Let $d$ be the train's distance from the near end of the bridge (the end you are closer to) at the moment you must decide which way to run.
>
> You are standing at the $\tfrac14$ point of the bridge, so your distances to the two ends are:
>
> - toward the nearer end: $\dfrac{L}{4}$
> - toward the farther end: $\dfrac{3L}{4}$
>
> Since running to **either end** saves you only just in time, in each case your travel time must exactly equal the train's travel time to that same end.
>
> ### Running toward the nearer end
>
> You must cover $L/4$, while the train must cover the distance $d$ remaining to that end. For the two to arrive together,
> $$
> \frac{L/4}{v}=\frac{d}{u}.
> $$
>
> ### Running toward the farther end
>
> You must cover $3L/4$, while the train must cover $d$ to reach the near end of the bridge and then the full length $L$ to cross it, a total of $d+L$. For the two to arrive together,
> $$
> \frac{3L/4}{v}=\frac{d+L}{u}.
> $$
>
> ### Solving the two equations
>
> From the first equation,
> $$
> d=\frac{uL}{4v}.
> $$
> Substituting into the second equation,
> $$
> \frac{3L}{4v}=\frac{1}{u}\left(\frac{uL}{4v}+L\right)=\frac{L}{4v}+\frac{L}{u}.
> $$
> Subtracting $\dfrac{L}{4v}$ from both sides,
> $$
> \frac{3L}{4v}-\frac{L}{4v}=\frac{L}{u}
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \frac{2L}{4v}=\frac{L}{u}
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \frac{L}{2v}=\frac{L}{u}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> u=2v.
> $$
>
> The distance $d$ cancels out entirely, so the result holds regardless of exactly where the train is when you spot it — only the ratio of speeds is pinned down.
>
> Thus the required speed ratio is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2:1}
> $$
>
> That is, the **train's speed : your speed** is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2:1}
> $$
>
> equivalently, your speed : train's speed is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1:2}
> $$
