> [!example] Question 497 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

An asymmetrical 12-sided die has a 40% chance of rolling a 12, and the remaining faces are equally likely. Two people each choose a number between 1 and 12. The person whose chosen number is closer to the result of the die roll wins. Which number should you choose to maximize your chances of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=498)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the die outcome be $X \in \{1,2,\dots,12\}$ with
>
> - $P(X=12)=0.4$,
> - and the remaining probability $0.6$ spread equally over $1,2,\dots,11$.
>
> Thus, for each $k=1,\dots,11$,
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{0.6}{11}.
> $$
>
> Two players each choose a number, and the one whose choice is closer to $X$ wins. To determine the best single number to choose, one should select a number minimizing the expected absolute distance to the outcome. Equivalently, one should choose a **median** of the distribution of $X$.
>
> ## Step 1: Use the median criterion
>
> A number $m$ is a median if
> $$
> P(X \le m)\ge \frac12
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> P(X \ge m)\ge \frac12.
> $$
>
> Compute the cumulative probabilities.
>
> For $k=1,\dots,11$,
> $$
> P(X\le k)=k\cdot \frac{0.6}{11}.
> $$
>
> In particular,
> $$
> P(X\le 9)=9\cdot \frac{0.6}{11}=\frac{5.4}{11}\approx 0.4909,
> $$
> while
> $$
> P(X\le 10)=10\cdot \frac{0.6}{11}=\frac{6}{11}\approx 0.5455.
> $$
>
> So the cumulative probability first reaches $\frac12$ at $10$. Hence the median is
>
> $$
> m=10.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Interpretation for the game
>
> If you choose $10$, then outcomes $1$ through $10$ are at or below your choice with total probability exceeding $\frac12$, while the heavy mass at $12$ pulls the distribution to the high end. This makes $10$ the point that best balances the distribution in terms of closeness.
>
> Therefore, the optimal choice is
>
> $$
> \boxed{10}.
> $$
>
> If ties are possible and broken fairly, this conclusion remains the same.
