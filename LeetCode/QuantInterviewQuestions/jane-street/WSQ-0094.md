> [!example] Question 94 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You have a Rubik's Cube, and you paint its exterior red before breaking it up into 27 smaller cubes. You pick one of the smaller cubes at random and roll it. What is the probability that all the faces landing upward are unpainted?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=95)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube is painted on the outside and then cut into $27$ small cubes. One small cube is chosen uniformly at random, and then it is rolled so that each face is equally likely to end up on top.
>
> We want the probability that the face showing on top is **unpainted**.
>
> ## Step 1: Classify the small cubes
>
> In a painted $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube, the small cubes fall into four types:
>
> - **Corner cubes**: $8$ cubes, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - **Edge cubes**: $12$ cubes, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - **Face-center cubes**: $6$ cubes, each with $1$ painted face.
> - **Interior cube**: $1$ cube, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> ## Step 2: Probability of landing unpainted for each type
>
> When a cube is rolled, each of its $6$ faces is equally likely to be on top.
>
> - Corner cube: $3$ unpainted faces, so probability $= \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
> - Edge cube: $4$ unpainted faces, so probability $= \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
> - Face-center cube: $5$ unpainted faces, so probability $= \dfrac{5}{6}$.
> - Interior cube: $6$ unpainted faces, so probability $= 1$.
>
> ## Step 3: Average over all $27$ cubes
>
> Thus the desired probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{27}\left(8 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 12 \cdot \frac{2}{3} + 6 \cdot \frac{5}{6} + 1 \cdot 1\right).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> 8 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 4, \qquad
> 12 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 8, \qquad
> 6 \cdot \frac{5}{6} = 5.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{27}(4+8+5+1)=\frac{18}{27}=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
