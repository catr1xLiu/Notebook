> [!example] Question 567 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Given a black box that generates random numbers uniformly from 0 to 1, with independent draws and the probability of drawing any exact number being zero, design an algorithm to simulate a fair coin flip (with exactly 50% probability of heads or tails).

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=568)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1, X_2$ be two independent outputs of the black box, each taking values in $[0,1]$. The distribution may be arbitrary, but assume:
>
> - the outputs are independent, and
> - for every fixed $x \in [0,1]$, $\Pr(X_i = x) = 0$.
>
> The second condition implies that ties occur with probability $0$:
> $$
> \Pr(X_1 = X_2) = 0.
> $$
>
> ## Construction
>
> Define the coin flip as follows:
>
> - output **Heads** if $X_1 < X_2$,
> - output **Tails** if $X_1 > X_2$.
>
> Since $\Pr(X_1 = X_2)=0$, this rule produces an output with probability $1$.
>
> ## Why this is fair
>
> Because $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent and identically distributed, the pair $(X_1, X_2)$ has the same distribution as $(X_2, X_1)$. Therefore,
> $$
> \Pr(X_1 < X_2) = \Pr(X_2 < X_1).
> $$
> But the events $\{X_1 < X_2\}$ and $\{X_2 < X_1\}$ are disjoint and, since ties have probability $0$, their probabilities sum to $1$:
> $$
> \Pr(X_1 < X_2) + \Pr(X_2 < X_1) = 1.
> $$
> Hence each must equal $\tfrac12$:
> $$
> \Pr(X_1 < X_2) = \Pr(X_1 > X_2) = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Thus the procedure yields an exact fair coin.
>
> ## Final procedure
>
> 1. Draw two independent samples $X_1, X_2$ from the black box.
> 2. If $X_1 < X_2$, return **Heads**.
> 3. If $X_1 > X_2$, return **Tails**.
>
> This simulates a coin flip with exactly
> $$
> \Pr(\text{Heads}) = \Pr(\text{Tails}) = \frac12.
> $$
