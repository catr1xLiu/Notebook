> [!example] Question 378 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What day of the week will it be exactly 10 years from today?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=379)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine the day of the week $10$ years from today, use the current date.
>
> Today is **March 25, 2026**, which is a **Wednesday**.
>
> Ten years later is **March 25, 2036**. Count the weekday shift year by year:
>
> - A common year shifts the weekday by $1$ day.
> - A leap year shifts it by $2$ days whenever the elapsed span includes February $29$ — that is, the shift from year $Y$ to year $Y+1$ is $2$ days precisely when $Y+1$ is a leap year, since February $29$ of $Y+1$ falls inside the window from March $25$ of $Y$ to March $25$ of $Y+1$.
>
> From $2026$ to $2036$, the leap years are $2028$, $2032$, and $2036$, so the $+2$ transitions are the ones ending on those years:
>
> - $2026 \to 2027$: $+1$
> - $2027 \to 2028$: $+2$ since $2028$ is a leap year and its February $29$ falls before March $25$, $2028$
> - $2028 \to 2029$: $+1$
> - $2029 \to 2030$: $+1$
> - $2030 \to 2031$: $+1$
> - $2031 \to 2032$: $+2$ since $2032$ is a leap year
> - $2032 \to 2033$: $+1$
> - $2033 \to 2034$: $+1$
> - $2034 \to 2035$: $+1$
> - $2035 \to 2036$: $+2$ since $2036$ is a leap year
>
> Total shift:
> $$
> 1+2+1+1+1+2+1+1+1+2 = 13.
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> 13 \equiv 6 \pmod{7},
> $$
> the weekday moves forward $6$ days from Wednesday:
>
> - Wednesday $\to$ Thursday $\to$ Friday $\to$ Saturday $\to$ Sunday $\to$ Monday $\to$ **Tuesday**
>
> Therefore, **10 years from today will be Tuesday**.
