> [!example] Question 351 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If you throw a fair die with 6 faces repeatedly, what is the expected number of throws until you get two consecutive sixes?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=352)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of throws needed to obtain two consecutive $6$’s.
>
> A convenient way to solve this is to use states.
>
> - **State $A$**: no previous throw was a $6$.
> - **State $B$**: the previous throw was a single $6$.
> - **Goal**: reach two consecutive $6$’s.
>
> Let
>
> - $E_A$ = expected additional throws starting from state $A$,
> - $E_B$ = expected additional throws starting from state $B$.
>
> We want $E_A$, since initially no throw has occurred.
>
> ## Step 1: Equation for $E_A$
>
> From state $A$:
>
> - with probability $\frac{1}{6}$, the next throw is $6$, so we move to state $B$;
> - with probability $\frac{5}{6}$, the next throw is not $6$, so we remain in state $A$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_A = 1 + \frac{1}{6}E_B + \frac{5}{6}E_A.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Equation for $E_B$
>
> From state $B$:
>
> - with probability $\frac{1}{6}$, the next throw is again $6$, and we are done;
> - with probability $\frac{5}{6}$, the next throw is not $6$, so we return to state $A$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_B = 1 + \frac{1}{6}\cdot 0 + \frac{5}{6}E_A
> = 1 + \frac{5}{6}E_A.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> E_A = 1 + \frac{1}{6}E_B + \frac{5}{6}E_A,
> $$
> subtract $\frac{5}{6}E_A$ from both sides:
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}E_A = 1 + \frac{1}{6}E_B.
> $$
> Multiply by $6$:
> $$
> E_A = 6 + E_B.
> $$
>
> Now substitute $E_B = 1 + \frac{5}{6}E_A$:
> $$
> E_A = 6 + 1 + \frac{5}{6}E_A.
> $$
> So
> $$
> E_A = 7 + \frac{5}{6}E_A.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> E_A - \frac{5}{6}E_A = 7,
> $$
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}E_A = 7,
> $$
> and thus
> $$
> E_A = 42.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of throws until two consecutive $6$’s appear is
>
> $$
> \boxed{42}.
> $$
