> [!example] Question 559 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If you could ask one true or false question to all Americans and wanted to minimize the number of people who answer correctly, what would you ask?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=560)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Ask:
>
> **“Will your answer to this question be wrong?”**
>
> Let $p$ be the fraction of people who answer “True.”
>
> - If someone answers **“True,”** then the statement says their answer will be wrong.  
>   So a “True” answer is correct exactly when it is wrong, which is impossible. Thus everyone who answers “True” gets it **wrong**.
>
> - If someone answers **“False,”** then they are claiming the statement “your answer will be wrong” is false, meaning their answer will **not** be wrong.  
>   Since they answered “False,” this is consistent, so everyone who answers “False” gets it **right**.
>
> Therefore, exactly the people who answer “False” get the question right. The number correct is the number of “False” answers.
>
> To minimize the number correct, one would want to maximize the number of “True” answers. In the ideal case, if everyone answers “True,” then **nobody** gets it right.
>
> So the best question is:
>
> $$\text{“Will your answer to this question be wrong?”}$$
>
> and the minimum possible number of correct answers is
>
> $$0.$$
