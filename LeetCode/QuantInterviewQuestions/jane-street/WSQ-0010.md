> [!example] Question 10 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given the probability of rain on Saturday and Sunday, find the probability that it is sunny for the whole weekend.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=11)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $R_S$ denote the event that it rains on Saturday, and $R_U$ the event that it rains on Sunday.  
> Then the event that it is sunny for the whole weekend is the complement of “it rains on at least one of the two days.”
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(\text{sunny whole weekend})=1-P(R_S\cup R_U).
> $$
>
> Using the addition rule,
> $$
> P(R_S\cup R_U)=P(R_S)+P(R_U)-P(R_S\cap R_U).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{sunny whole weekend})
> =1-\bigl(P(R_S)+P(R_U)-P(R_S\cap R_U)\bigr).
> $$
>
> So the required probability is
> $$
> \boxed{1-P(R_S)-P(R_U)+P(R_S\cap R_U)}.
> $$
>
> If rain on Saturday and Sunday are independent, then
> $$
> P(R_S\cap R_U)=P(R_S)P(R_U),
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> P(\text{sunny whole weekend})
> =\bigl(1-P(R_S)\bigr)\bigl(1-P(R_U)\bigr).
> $$
>
> Therefore, under independence,
> $$
> \boxed{(1-P(\text{rain Saturday}))(1-P(\text{rain Sunday}))}.
> $$
