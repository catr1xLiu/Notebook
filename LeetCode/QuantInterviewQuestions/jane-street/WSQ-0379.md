> [!example] Question 379 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

You roll a fair six-sided die and receive a payoff equal to the number shown. a) What is the expected payoff? b) If you are given the option to re-roll after seeing the result, under what conditions should you choose to re-roll? What is your expected payoff with this re-roll option?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=380)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the payoff from one fair die roll be the number shown, so the possible payoffs are $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each with probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$.
>
> ### (a) Expected payoff from one roll
>
> The expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### (b) Optimal re-roll rule and new expected payoff
>
> Suppose after seeing the first roll, you may either keep it or re-roll once. If you re-roll, the new outcome replaces the first one.
>
> The key idea is to compare the current payoff to the expected payoff from a fresh roll.
>
> A fresh roll has expected value
>
> $$
> 3.5.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - if the current roll is less than $3.5$, it is better to re-roll;
> - if the current roll is greater than $3.5$, it is better to keep it.
>
> Since the die shows only integers, this means:
>
> - re-roll on $1,2,3$;
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> So the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Re-roll if the first roll is }1,2,\text{ or }3.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Expected payoff under this strategy
>
> Compute the expected payoff:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac{3}{6}$, the first roll is $1,2,$ or $3$, and then you re-roll, giving expected payoff $3.5$;
> - with probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$ each, the first roll is $4,5,$ or $6$, and you keep it.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \frac{3}{6}\cdot 3.5 + \frac{4+5+6}{6}.
> $$
>
> Now simplify:
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 3.5 + \frac{15}{6}
> = 1.75 + 2.5
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected payoff with the re-roll option is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{(a)\ 3.5}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{(b)\ \text{Re-roll on }1,2,3;\ \text{expected payoff }=4.25}
> $$
