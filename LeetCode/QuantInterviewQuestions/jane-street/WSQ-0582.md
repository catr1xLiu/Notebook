> [!example] Question 582 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

In a best-of-7 game series between two teams, A and B, you get even odds on both teams for each game. Determine how much you should bet on each game so that you win \$100 if Team A wins the series and lose \$100 if Team B wins the series. Specifically, how much should you bet on the first game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=583)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $V(i,j)$ denote the amount of money you want to have after the current state of the series, when team $A$ has won $i$ games and team $B$ has won $j$ games, where the goal is:
>
> - $V(i,j)=100$ if $A$ has already won the series,
> - $V(i,j)=-100$ if $B$ has already won the series.
>
> Since this is a best-of-$7$ series, the series ends when either team reaches $4$ wins. Thus,
>
> $$
> V(i,j)=100 \quad \text{if } i=4,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> V(i,j)=-100 \quad \text{if } j=4.
> $$
>
> At any nonterminal state $(i,j)$, one more game is played. Since each game is offered at even odds, if at state $(i,j)$ you bet an amount $x$ on team $A$:
>
> - if $A$ wins the next game, your wealth changes by $+x$ and the new state is $(i+1,j)$;
> - if $B$ wins the next game, your wealth changes by $-x$ and the new state is $(i,j+1)$.
>
> To replicate the desired final payoff, the wealth at state $(i,j)$ must satisfy
>
> $$
> V(i,j)+x=V(i+1,j),
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> V(i,j)-x=V(i,j+1).
> $$
>
> Adding these equations gives the recursion
>
> $$
> 2V(i,j)=V(i+1,j)+V(i,j+1),
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> V(i,j)=\frac{V(i+1,j)+V(i,j+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus $V(i,j)$ is the average of the two successor values.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the value at the start
>
> Because each future game is a fair $50$-$50$ event, $V(0,0)$ is just $100$ times
>
> $$
> \bigl(\Pr(A\text{ wins series})-\Pr(B\text{ wins series})\bigr).
> $$
>
> In a fair best-of-$7$ series,
>
> $$
> \Pr(A\text{ wins series})=\Pr(B\text{ wins series})=\frac12,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> V(0,0)=100\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)=0.
> $$
>
> Thus you should begin with wealth $0$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the first-game hedge
>
> Let $x_1$ be the amount to bet on team $A$ in game $1$.
>
> After game $1$:
>
> - if $A$ wins, the state is $(1,0)$ and wealth must be $V(1,0)$;
> - if $B$ wins, the state is $(0,1)$ and wealth must be $V(0,1)$.
>
> From the replication equations,
>
> $$
> x_1=\frac{V(1,0)-V(0,1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> So we need $V(1,0)$ and $V(0,1)$.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute $V(1,0)$ and $V(0,1)$
>
> From state $(1,0)$, team $A$ needs $3$ more wins while team $B$ needs $4$ more wins. It is easiest to count the ways $A$ can get its $3$ remaining wins before $B$ gets $4$ losses:
>
> $$
> \Pr_{(1,0)}(A\text{ wins})
> =
> \frac{1}{2^3}
> +\frac{3}{2^4}
> +\frac{6}{2^5}
> +\frac{10}{2^6}
> =
> \frac{8+12+12+10}{64}
> =
> \frac{42}{64}
> =
> \frac{21}{32}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V(1,0)=100\left(\frac{21}{32}-\frac{11}{32}\right)
> =100\cdot \frac{10}{32}
> =100\cdot \frac{5}{16}
> =31.25.
> $$
>
> By symmetry,
>
> $$
> V(0,1)=-31.25.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: First-game bet
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> x_1=\frac{31.25-(-31.25)}{2}=31.25.
> $$
>
> So you should bet
>
> $$
> \boxed{31.25\text{ dollars on team } A \text{ in game 1}.}
> $$
>
> If $A$ wins game $1$, your wealth becomes $+31.25$ dollars; if $B$ wins, your wealth becomes $-31.25$ dollars. Continuing similarly in later games will ensure that the final outcome is:
>
> - $+100$ dollars if $A$ wins the series,
> - $-100$ dollars if $B$ wins the series.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The amount to bet on the first game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{31.25\text{ dollars on team } A.}
> $$
>
> More generally, at any state $(i,j)$, bet on team $A$ the amount
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{V(i+1,j)-V(i,j+1)}{2}},
> $$
>
> where $V(i,j)$ is defined recursively by
>
> $$
> V(i,j)=\frac{V(i+1,j)+V(i,j+1)}{2},
> $$
>
> with terminal values
>
> $$
> V(4,j)=100,\qquad V(i,4)=-100.
> $$
>
> This dynamic strategy replicates the desired series payoff exactly.
