> [!example] Question 126 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

A bag contains three visually indistinguishable coins: one with a 10% chance, one with a 30% chance, and one with a 60% chance of landing heads. You randomly select a coin and flip it, and it lands heads. What is the probability that if you flip the same coin again, it will land heads? Explain your reasoning.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=127)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the three coins be:
>
> - $C_1$: $P(H \mid C_1)=0.1$
> - $C_2$: $P(H \mid C_2)=0.3$
> - $C_3$: $P(H \mid C_3)=0.6$
>
> Since the coin is chosen uniformly at random,
>
> $$
> P(C_1)=P(C_2)=P(C_3)=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> We are told that the first flip is heads. We want
>
> $$
> P(\text{heads on second flip} \mid \text{heads on first flip}).
> $$
>
> Because the same coin is reflipped, this probability is the posterior mean of the coin’s head probability after observing one head.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the probability of observing heads on the first flip
>
> By the law of total probability,
>
> $$
> P(H)=\frac{1}{3}(0.1+0.3+0.6)=\frac{1.0}{3}=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Update the probability of which coin was chosen
>
> Using Bayes’ rule,
>
> $$
> P(C_i \mid H)=\frac{P(H \mid C_i)P(C_i)}{P(H)}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(C_1 \mid H)=\frac{0.1 \cdot \frac{1}{3}}{\frac{1}{3}}=0.1,
> $$
>
> $$
> P(C_2 \mid H)=\frac{0.3 \cdot \frac{1}{3}}{\frac{1}{3}}=0.3,
> $$
>
> $$
> P(C_3 \mid H)=\frac{0.6 \cdot \frac{1}{3}}{\frac{1}{3}}=0.6.
> $$
>
> So after seeing a head, the probabilities that the chosen coin is $C_1$, $C_2$, or $C_3$ are $0.1$, $0.3$, and $0.6$, respectively.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the probability of heads on the second flip
>
> Condition on which coin was chosen:
>
> $$
> P(H_2 \mid H_1)
> = \sum_{i=1}^3 P(H_2 \mid C_i, H_1)\,P(C_i \mid H_1).
> $$
>
> Given the coin, flips are independent, so
>
> $$
> P(H_2 \mid C_i, H_1)=P(H \mid C_i).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(H_2 \mid H_1)
> = (0.1)(0.1)+(0.3)(0.3)+(0.6)(0.6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> 0.1^2+0.3^2+0.6^2=0.01+0.09+0.36=0.46.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.46}
> $$
>
> The reason is that observing a head makes it more likely that you selected a coin with a larger probability of heads. After updating for that information, the chance of heads on the next flip is the weighted average of the three head probabilities using the posterior weights, which gives $0.46$.
