> [!example] Question 347 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Given two bowling balls of the same density, if one weighs X kg and has a diameter of 10 inches, and the other has a diameter of 16 inches, how much does the larger ball weigh?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=348)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Since the two bowling balls have the same density, their weights are proportional to their volumes. For spheres, volume is proportional to the cube of the diameter:
>
> $$
> V \propto d^3
> $$
>
> Let the weight of the $10$-inch ball be $X$ kg. Then the weight of the $16$-inch ball, say $W$, satisfies
>
> $$
> \frac{W}{X}=\left(\frac{16}{10}\right)^3
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{16}{10}\right)^3=\left(\frac{8}{5}\right)^3=\frac{512}{125}=4.096
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> W=4.096X
> $$
>
> Therefore, the $16$-inch bowling ball weighs
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.096X\text{ kg}}
> $$
