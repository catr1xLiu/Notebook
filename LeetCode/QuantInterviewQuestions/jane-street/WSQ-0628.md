> [!example] Question 628 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the expected number of coin flips required to simulate a fair 6-sided die?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=629)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To simulate a fair $6$-sided die using fair coin flips, one standard method is:
>
> 1. Use $3$ coin flips to generate a number from $0$ to $7$.
> 2. If the outcome is one of $0,1,2,3,4,5$, accept it.
> 3. If the outcome is $6$ or $7$, reject and repeat.
>
> Thus each round uses exactly $3$ flips.
>
> ### Step 1: Probability of success in one round
> There are $8$ equally likely outcomes from $3$ flips, and $6$ are acceptable, so
>
> $$
> p = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Expected number of rounds
> The number of rounds until success is geometric with success probability $p = \frac{3}{4}$, so the expected number of rounds is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{p} = \frac{4}{3}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Expected number of flips
> Since each round uses $3$ flips, the expected total number of flips is
>
> $$
> 3 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 4.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected number of coin flips is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4}.
> $$
>
> If one asks for the minimum possible expected number, more sophisticated schemes can do better than $4$, but for the usual rejection-sampling method with $3$ flips at a time, the expectation is $\boxed{4}$.
