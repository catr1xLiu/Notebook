> [!example] Question 333 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A fair coin is flipped repeatedly. What is the expected number of flips required to obtain three consecutive heads (HHH)? What is the expected value of the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=334)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E_k$ denote the expected additional number of flips needed to obtain the pattern $HHH$, given that the current run of consecutive heads has length $k$, where $k=0,1,2,3$. Clearly,
>
> $$
> E_3=0.
> $$
>
> We now write equations for $E_0,E_1,E_2$.
>
> - From state $0$ (no current consecutive heads), one flip is made:
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a head occurs and we move to state $1$;
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a tail occurs and remain in state $0$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> - From state $1$:
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a head occurs and we move to state $2$;
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a tail occurs and the run is broken, so we return to state $0$.
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12 E_2 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> - From state $2$:
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a head occurs and we reach $HHH$, so the process ends;
>   - with probability $\tfrac12$, a tail occurs and we return to state $0$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_3 + \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Now solve the system.
>
> From the first equation,
>
> $$
> E_0 - \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 2 + E_1.
> $$
>
> From the third equation,
>
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute into the second equation:
>
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right) + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0 + \frac12 E_0
> = \frac32 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> Now use $E_0 = 2 + E_1$:
>
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + \frac32 + \frac34 E_0
> = \frac72 + \frac34 E_0.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> E_0 - \frac34 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> \frac14 E_0 = \frac72
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_0 = 14.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected number of flips to get $HHH$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{14}.
> $$
>
> If the “value of the game” means the fair entry fee for a game that pays \(\text{\textdollar}1\) when $HHH$ first appears and costs one unit per flip, then the expected cost is the expected number of flips, so the fair value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{14}.
> $$
>
> In general, the game’s fair value equals the expected waiting time, namely
>
> $$
> \boxed{14}.
> $$
