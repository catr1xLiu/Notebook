> [!example] Question 310 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Can you generate a number from 1 to 10 uniformly at random using two dice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=311)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To generate a number uniformly from $1$ to $10$ using two fair six-sided dice, use **rejection sampling**.
>
> ## Step 1: Create a uniform outcome space
> Roll two dice and interpret the result as an ordered pair. There are
>
> $$
> 6 \times 6 = 36
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> ## Step 2: Map outcomes to numbers
> Use only the first $30$ outcomes, since $30$ is divisible by $10$:
>
> $$
> 30 = 10 \times 3.
> $$
>
> For example, label the $36$ ordered outcomes in any fixed order, such as
>
> $$
> (1,1),(1,2),\dots,(1,6),(2,1),\dots,(6,6),
> $$
>
> and assign:
>
> - outcomes $1$–$3$ to number $1$,
> - outcomes $4$–$6$ to number $2$,
> - $\dots$
> - outcomes $28$–$30$ to number $10$.
>
> The remaining $6$ outcomes are discarded.
>
> ## Step 3: Re-roll if necessary
> If one of the last $6$ outcomes occurs, ignore it and roll both dice again.
>
> ## Why this is uniform
> Each accepted outcome has probability
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{36},
> $$
>
> and each number from $1$ to $10$ is assigned exactly $3$ accepted outcomes. Thus, conditional on acceptance, each number has probability
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{30} = \frac{1}{10}.
> $$
>
> So the output is uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,10\}$.
>
> ## Example of an explicit rule
> Number the ordered outcomes by
>
> $$
> n = 6(d_1-1) + d_2,
> $$
>
> where $d_1,d_2 \in \{1,\dots,6\}$ are the two dice. Then:
>
> - if $n \le 30$, output
>
> $$
> \left\lfloor \frac{n-1}{3} \right\rfloor + 1;
> $$
>
> - if $n > 30$, roll again.
>
> This produces a uniform random integer from $1$ to $10$.
