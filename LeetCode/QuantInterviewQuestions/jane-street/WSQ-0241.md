> [!example] Question 241 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

Two players each have a die. I have a 20-sided die numbered 1-20, and the other player has a 30-sided die numbered 1-30. Both players roll their respective die. If my number is greater, the other player pays me the value of my die roll in dollars. If the other player's number is greater, I pay them the value of their die roll in dollars. If we roll the same number, I pay the other player that number in dollars. What is the expected value of my winnings or losses for a single round of this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=242)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be your die roll and $Y$ be the other player’s die roll.
>
> Then
>
> - $X \sim \text{Uniform}\{1,2,\dots,20\}$,
> - $Y \sim \text{Uniform}\{1,2,\dots,30\}$,
>
> and the rolls are independent.
>
> Define your payoff $W$ by the rules:
>
> - if $X>Y$, you win $X$ dollars, so $W=X$;
> - if $X<Y$, you lose $Y$ dollars, so $W=-Y$;
> - if $X=Y$, you pay the other player that number of dollars, so $W=-X$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> W=
> \begin{cases}
> X, & X>Y,\\
> -Y, & X<Y,\\
> -X, & X=Y.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> We compute the expected value
> $$
> E[W]=\frac{1}{20\cdot 30}\sum_{x=1}^{20}\sum_{y=1}^{30} w(x,y).
> $$
>
> Since there are $600$ equally likely outcomes, it is convenient to sum by conditioning on your roll $x$.
>
> For a fixed $x\in\{1,\dots,20\}$:
>
> - there are $x-1$ values of $y$ with $y<x$, each giving payoff $x$;
> - there is $1$ value $y=x$, giving payoff $-x$;
> - there are $30-x$ values of $y$ with $y>x$, and for these the payoff is $-y$.
>
> So the total payoff contribution for fixed $x$ is
> $$
> (x-1)x + (-x) + \sum_{y=x+1}^{30} (-y).
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> E[W]
> =
> \frac{1}{600}
> \sum_{x=1}^{20}
> \left(
> x(x-1)-x-\sum_{y=x+1}^{30} y
> \right).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> x(x-1)-x = x^2-2x.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> \sum_{y=x+1}^{30} y
> =
> \sum_{y=1}^{30} y - \sum_{y=1}^{x} y
> =
> 465 - \frac{x(x+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fixed-$x$ contribution is
> $$
> x^2-2x - \left(465-\frac{x(x+1)}{2}\right)
> =
> \frac{3x^2-3x-930}{2}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[W]
> =
> \frac{1}{600}\sum_{x=1}^{20}\frac{3x^2-3x-930}{2}
> =
> \frac{1}{1200}
> \left(
> 3\sum_{x=1}^{20}x^2
> -
> 3\sum_{x=1}^{20}x
> -
> 930\cdot 20
> \right).
> $$
>
> Now use
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{20}x = \frac{20\cdot 21}{2}=210,
> $$
> and
> $$
> \sum_{x=1}^{20}x^2 = \frac{20\cdot 21\cdot 41}{6}=2870.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> E[W]
> =
> \frac{1}{1200}\left(3\cdot 2870 - 3\cdot 210 - 18600\right)
> =
> \frac{1}{1200}(8610-630-18600)
> =
> \frac{-10620}{1200}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> E[W] = -8.85.
> $$
>
> So your expected result is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{-\$8.85}
> $$
>
> That is, on average, you **lose $8.85$ per game**.
