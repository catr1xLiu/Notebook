> [!example] Question 356 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What day of the week will it be exactly one year from today?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=357)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let today be the current date, and let “exactly one year from now” mean the same calendar date in the next year.
>
> A common year has $365$ days, and
> $$
> 365 \equiv 1 \pmod{7},
> $$
> so the day of the week advances by $1$ day after one common year.
>
> A leap year has $366$ days, and
> $$
> 366 \equiv 2 \pmod{7},
> $$
> so if the interval includes February $29$, the day of the week advances by $2$ days instead.
>
> Today is $2026\text{-}05\text{-}01$, and one year from now is $2027\text{-}05\text{-}01$. The year between these dates does not include February $29$, since $2027$ is not a leap year and the leap day in $2028$ has not occurred yet. Thus the shift is by $1$ day.
>
> Since $2026\text{-}05\text{-}01$ is a Friday, one year later will be:
>
> $$
> \text{Saturday}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, exactly one year from now, it will be **Saturday**.
