> [!example] Question 179 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

Consider a game where you roll a fair six-sided die. What is the expected value of a single roll? What is the expected value if you can roll twice and either keep the result of the first roll, or discard it and are forced to keep the second roll? How does this extend to three rolls or an infinite number of rolls?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=180)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ denote the outcome of a fair six-sided die, and suppose your payoff is the number shown.
>
> ## 1. Expected value of the first roll
>
> Since each outcome $1,2,3,4,5,6$ is equally likely,
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So the expected value of a single roll is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Expected value if you may choose to roll a second time
>
> Now suppose you roll once, observe the result, and then decide whether to keep it or discard it and take a second roll. Since the second roll has expected value $3.5$, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the first roll if it is greater than $3.5$,
> - reroll if it is less than $3.5$.
>
> Thus:
> - reroll on $1,2,3$,
> - keep on $4,5,6$.
>
> Let $V_2$ be the expected value under this optimal strategy. Then
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(3.5 + 3.5 + 3.5 + 4 + 5 + 6\bigr).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{10.5 + 15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Expected value if you can roll up to three times
>
> Now suppose you may roll up to three times total, deciding after each of the first two rolls whether to keep the result or roll again.
>
> Let $V_2 = 4.25$ be the expected value when there are two rolls remaining. Then on the first roll, you should compare the observed value to $4.25$:
>
> - if the first roll is less than $4.25$, continue;
> - if it is greater than $4.25$, keep it.
>
> Since the die outcomes are integers, this means:
> - reroll on $1,2,3,4$,
> - keep on $5,6$.
>
> Let $V_3$ be the expected value with up to three rolls. Then
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(4.25 + 4.25 + 4.25 + 4.25 + 5 + 6\bigr).
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{17 + 11}{6} = \frac{28}{6} = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - One roll: $$\boxed{3.5}$$
> - Up to two rolls, choosing whether to reroll: $$\boxed{4.25}$$
> - Up to three rolls, choosing optimally each time: $$\boxed{\frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}$$
