> [!example] Question 653 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

If copying, pasting, and typing a letter each take one second, what is the fastest way to reach at least 200 letters written?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=654)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the current number of letters on the screen be $n$.
>
> At any moment, there are three possible actions, each taking $1$ second:
>
> - type one letter: $n \mapsto n+1$,
> - copy all: this does not change $n$,
> - paste: after a copy, each paste adds the copied amount.
>
> The goal is to reach at least $200$ letters as quickly as possible.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> If one has already typed $k$ letters, then performing
>
> - one copy, and
> - $m$ pastes
>
> takes $m+1$ seconds and produces
> $$
> k(1+m)
> $$
> letters.
>
> So a plan consists of:
>
> 1. typing some initial number $k$ of letters, costing $k$ seconds;
> 2. performing several rounds of “copy all + some number of pastes.”
>
> Suppose the numbers of pastes in the successive rounds are $p_1,p_2,\dots,p_r$.  
> Each round multiplies the current amount by $p_i+1$, and costs $p_i+1$ seconds.
>
> Thus the total time is
> $$
> T = k + \sum_{i=1}^r (p_i+1),
> $$
> and the final number of letters is
> $$
> N = k\prod_{i=1}^r (p_i+1).
> $$
>
> We want $N \ge 200$ with $T$ as small as possible.
>
> ---
>
> ## Key optimization idea
>
> For a fixed total sum, a product is maximized by using factors near $3$.  
> Thus the fastest method should use multipliers mostly equal to $3$, with possibly one factor $2$ or $4$.
>
> So we seek a factorization of at least $200$ whose factor sum is as small as possible, where one factor is the initial typed amount $k$, and the others are the multipliers from copy-paste rounds.
>
> Try products built from $3$'s:
>
> $$
> 3^4 = 81,\qquad 3^5 = 243.
> $$
>
> Since $243 \ge 200$, one candidate is
>
> - type $3$ letters,
> - then do $4$ rounds of “copy all, paste twice,” each multiplying by $3$.
>
> This gives total time
> $$
> 3 + 4\cdot 3 = 15.
> $$
>
> Indeed, the letter counts go as follows:
> $$
> 3 \to 9 \to 27 \to 81 \to 243.
> $$
>
> More explicitly:
>
> - type $3$ letters: $3$ seconds,
> - copy, paste, paste: $3$ more seconds, total $9$ letters,
> - copy, paste, paste: $3$ more seconds, total $27$ letters,
> - copy, paste, paste: $3$ more seconds, total $81$ letters,
> - copy, paste, paste: $3$ more seconds, total $243$ letters.
>
> Total:
> $$
> 3+3+3+3+3=15 \text{ seconds}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Why $15$ seconds is minimal
>
> We show that $14$ seconds cannot suffice.
>
> If the total time is at most $14$, then in any plan the sum of the factors used is at most $14$.  
> To maximize the resulting product for a given sum, one should use as many $3$'s as possible. Hence the maximum achievable product with factor-sum $14$ is
> $$
> 3^4\cdot 2 = 162,
> $$
> since
> $$
> 14 = 3+3+3+3+2
> $$
> and this gives the largest product.
>
> But
> $$
> 162 < 200.
> $$
>
> So reaching $200$ letters in $14$ seconds is impossible.
>
> Therefore the minimum time is $15$ seconds.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The fastest method is:
>
> - type $3$ letters,
> - then repeat $4$ times: **copy all, paste, paste**.
>
> This reaches
> $$
> 3 \cdot 3^4 = 243
> $$
> letters in
>
> $$
> 3 + 4\cdot 3 = 15
> $$
>
> seconds.
>
> So the minimum time is
>
> $$
> \boxed{15 \text{ seconds}}.
> $$
