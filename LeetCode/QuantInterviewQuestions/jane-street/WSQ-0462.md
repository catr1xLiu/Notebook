> [!example] Question 462 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Two symmetric dice each have two sides painted red, two sides painted black, one side painted yellow, and one side painted white. When this pair of dice is rolled, what is the probability that both dice land with the same color face up?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=463)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each die has the following color distribution on its $6$ faces:
>
> - $2$ red
> - $2$ black
> - $1$ yellow
> - $1$ white
>
> Since the dice are symmetric, each face is equally likely to land face up. Thus, for one die,
>
> $$
> P(\text{red})=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}, \quad
> P(\text{black})=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}, \quad
> P(\text{yellow})=\frac{1}{6}, \quad
> P(\text{white})=\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> The probability that both dice show the same color is the sum of the probabilities that both are red, both are black, both are yellow, or both are white:
>
> $$
> P(\text{same color})
> = P(RR)+P(BB)+P(YY)+P(WW).
> $$
>
> Because the dice are rolled independently,
>
> $$
> P(RR)=\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2=\frac{1}{9}, \qquad
> P(BB)=\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2=\frac{1}{9},
> $$
>
> $$
> P(YY)=\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2=\frac{1}{36}, \qquad
> P(WW)=\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2=\frac{1}{36}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{same color})
> = \frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{36}+\frac{1}{36}
> = \frac{4}{36}+\frac{4}{36}+\frac{1}{36}+\frac{1}{36}
> = \frac{10}{36}
> = \frac{5}{18}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{18}}.
> $$
