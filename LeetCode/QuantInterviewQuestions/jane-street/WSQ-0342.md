> [!example] Question 342 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

The chance of rain on Saturday is 50%, and the chance of rain on Sunday is 60%. What is the probability that it will rain on at least one day during the weekend?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=343)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $B$ be the event that it rains on Sunday.
>
> We are given
>
> $$
> P(A)=0.5, \qquad P(B)=0.6.
> $$
>
> The probability that it rains on the weekend means the probability that it rains on at least one of the two days:
>
> $$
> P(A \cup B).
> $$
>
> Using the addition rule,
>
> $$
> P(A \cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A \cap B).
> $$
>
> To find a numerical value, the relationship between the two days must be known. If we assume the rain chances on Saturday and Sunday are independent, then
>
> $$
> P(A \cap B)=P(A)P(B)=0.5 \cdot 0.6=0.3.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(A \cup B)=0.5+0.6-0.3=0.8.
> $$
>
> So, assuming independence, the chance of rain on the weekend is
>
> $$
> \boxed{80\%}.
> $$
>
> Without the independence assumption, the exact probability cannot be determined from the given information alone.
