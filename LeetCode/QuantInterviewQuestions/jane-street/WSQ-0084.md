> [!example] Question 84 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You are going camping over the weekend. There is a 50% chance of rain on Saturday and a 60% chance on Sunday. What is the probability that you will not experience rain? (You are not told whether the days are independent, so consider this in your answer.) Then, after giving your answer, assume the probabilities are not independent and are positively correlated. Will the chance of having a 'dry' weekend increase or decrease?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=85)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $S$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $U$ be the event that it rains on Sunday.
>
> We are given
>
> $$
> P(S) = 0.5, \qquad P(U) = 0.6.
> $$
>
> A “dry” weekend means no rain on either day, so the desired probability is
>
> $$
> P(S^c \cap U^c).
> $$
>
> Using complements,
>
> $$
> P(S^c \cap U^c) = 1 - P(S \cup U).
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U) = P(S) + P(U) - P(S \cap U).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(S^c \cap U^c) = 1 - \bigl(0.5 + 0.6 - P(S \cap U)\bigr)
> = P(S \cap U) - 0.1.
> $$
>
> Since independence is **not** given, $P(S \cap U)$ is unknown, so the probability of a dry weekend cannot be determined uniquely from the information provided.
>
> However, it can be bounded. Because
>
> $$
> \max\{0,\, P(S)+P(U)-1\} \le P(S \cap U) \le \min\{P(S),P(U)\},
> $$
>
> we have
>
> $$
> \max\{0,\, 0.5+0.6-1\} \le P(S \cap U) \le \min\{0.5,0.6\},
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> 0.1 \le P(S \cap U) \le 0.5.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> 0 \le P(S^c \cap U^c) = P(S \cap U)-0.1 \le 0.4.
> $$
>
> So the probability of no rain all weekend could be anywhere between
>
> $$
> 0 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.4.
> $$
>
> If one additionally assumes independence, then
>
> $$
> P(S^c \cap U^c) = P(S^c)P(U^c) = (0.5)(0.4) = 0.2.
> $$
>
> Thus, under independence, the probability of a dry weekend is
>
> $$
> 0.20.
> $$
>
> Now suppose the two days are **positively correlated** and not independent. Positive correlation means rain on one day makes rain on the other day more likely, so
>
> $$
> P(S \cap U) > P(S)P(U) = 0.3.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> P(S^c \cap U^c) = P(S \cap U) - 0.1,
> $$
>
> a larger value of $P(S \cap U)$ makes $P(S^c \cap U^c)$ larger as well. Therefore, with positive correlation, the chance of a dry weekend **increases** relative to the independent case.
>
> In summary:
>
> - Without assuming independence, the exact probability cannot be determined.
> - It lies in the interval
>
> $$
> [0,\;0.4].
> $$
>
> - If the days are independent, it equals
>
> $$
> 0.2.
> $$
>
> - If they are positively correlated, the probability of a dry weekend **increases** above $0.2$.
