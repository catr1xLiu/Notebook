> [!example] Question 604 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If it is 12 o'clock right now, when was the last time the hour and minute hands of the clock were aligned?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=605)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the most recent time before $12{:}00$ when the hour and minute hands were aligned, compare their angular speeds.
>
> - The minute hand moves at $6^\circ$ per minute.
> - The hour hand moves at $0.5^\circ$ per minute.
>
> Let $t$ be the number of minutes before $12{:}00$ when they were last aligned. Then at that moment:
>
> - the minute hand was $6t^\circ$ behind $12$,
> - the hour hand was $0.5t^\circ$ behind $12$.
>
> For the hands to coincide, the minute hand must have gained exactly $360^\circ$ on the hour hand over one full overlap cycle just before $12{:}00$. Thus,
>
> $$
> (6 - 0.5)t = 360
> $$
>
> $$
> 5.5t = 360
> $$
>
> $$
> t = \frac{360}{5.5} = \frac{720}{11} = 65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}
> $$
>
> So the last alignment occurred $65\frac{5}{11}$ minutes before $12{:}00$, that is:
>
> $$
> 12{:}00 - 65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes} = 10{:}54\frac{6}{11}
> $$
>
> Since $\frac{6}{11}$ minute equals $\frac{360}{11}$ seconds $= 32\frac{8}{11}$ seconds, the time was
>
> $$
> \boxed{10{:}54{:}32\frac{8}{11}}
> $$
>
> approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{10{:}54{:}32.73}
> $$
