> [!example] Question 86 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

With one die, suppose in a round you earn the amount of dollars equal to the value that appears on the upward face of the die. After your first roll, you have the option to cancel the first roll and roll again, taking the second roll as your final value. What should your optimal strategy be?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=87)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the outcome of the first roll be $x \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.
>
> The decision is whether to **keep** $x$ or **reroll** and accept the second roll, whose value is then final.
>
> ## Step 1: Compute the value of rerolling
>
> If you reroll, the second roll is equally likely to be any of $1,2,3,4,5,6$, so its expected value is
>
> $$
> E[\text{reroll}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, rerolling is worth an expected payoff of $3.5$ dollars.
>
> ## Step 2: Compare keeping $x$ with rerolling
>
> - If $x > 3.5$, keeping the first roll is better.
> - If $x < 3.5$, rerolling is better.
> - Since no die face equals $3.5$, there is no tie case.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - For $x = 1,2,3$, reroll.
> - For $x = 4,5,6$, keep.
>
> ## Step 3: Expected value under the optimal strategy
>
> Under this strategy, the payoff is:
>
> - $3.5$ when the first roll is $1,2,3$ (because you reroll),
> - $4,5,6$ when the first roll is $4,5,6$.
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> E = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> E = \frac{10.5 + 15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is to **reroll if the first roll is $1$, $2$, or $3$, and keep it if it is $4$, $5$, or $6$**.
>
> With this strategy, the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> 4.25 \text{ dollars}.
> $$
