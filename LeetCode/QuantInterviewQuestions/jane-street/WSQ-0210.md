> [!example] Question 210 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

You have 100 blank cards and can write a single positive integer on each card. After assigning numbers, the interviewer shuffles the deck and guesses the top card. If the interviewer guesses correctly, they earn the amount written on the card. What numbers should you write on the cards to minimize the expected return of the interviewer?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=211)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the numbers written on the $100$ cards be $a_1,a_2,\dots,a_{100}$, where each $a_i$ is a positive integer.
>
> The interviewer sees the multiset of card values, then the deck is shuffled uniformly. He chooses a value to guess for the top card. If his guess is correct and the top card has value $x$, then his payoff is $\text{\textdollar}x$; otherwise his payoff is $0$.
>
> We seek the multiset $\{a_1,\dots,a_{100}\}$ that minimizes his expected payoff.
>
> ## 1. Expected payoff for a given multiset
>
> Suppose a value $v$ appears on exactly $m_v$ cards. If the interviewer guesses $v$, then:
>
> - the probability the top card is $v$ is $\dfrac{m_v}{100}$,
> - if correct, he earns $v$.
>
> So his expected payoff from guessing $v$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{m_v}{100}\,v.
> $$
>
> Since he knows the deck composition, he will choose the value $v$ maximizing this quantity. Hence his optimal expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{100}\max_v (m_v v).
> $$
>
> Therefore, the problem reduces to:
>
> Choose positive integers on $100$ cards so as to minimize
> $$
> \max_v (m_v v),
> $$
> where $m_v$ is the number of cards labeled $v$.
>
> ## 2. Reformulation
>
> Let
>
> $$
> M=\max_v (m_v v).
> $$
>
> We want to make $M$ as small as possible.
>
> If a value $v$ is used at all, then $m_v v \le M$, so
>
> $$
> m_v \le \left\lfloor \frac{M}{v}\right\rfloor.
> $$
>
> Thus, for a given $M$, the total number of cards we can have is at most
>
> $$
> \sum_{v=1}^{M} \left\lfloor \frac{M}{v}\right\rfloor,
> $$
>
> because no value greater than $M$ can appear even once: if $v>M$, then $1\cdot v>M$.
>
> So a necessary and sufficient condition for being able to fill all $100$ cards with $\max_v(m_vv)\le M$ is
>
> $$
> \sum_{v=1}^{M} \left\lfloor \frac{M}{v}\right\rfloor \ge 100.
> $$
>
> We now find the smallest such $M$.
>
> ## 3. Find the minimal feasible $M$
>
> Compute:
>
> ### For $M=27$
>
> $$
> \sum_{v=1}^{27}\left\lfloor \frac{27}{v}\right\rfloor
> =
> 27+13+9+6+5+4+3+3+3+2+2+2+2+1+\cdots+1.
> $$
>
> There are $14$ terms from $v=14$ to $v=27$, each equal to $1$, so
>
> $$
> 27+13+9+6+5+4+3+3+3+2+2+2+2+14 = 97.
> $$
>
> Thus $M=27$ is not enough.
>
> ### For $M=28$
>
> $$
> \sum_{v=1}^{28}\left\lfloor \frac{28}{v}\right\rfloor
> =
> 28+14+9+7+5+4+4+3+3+2+2+2+2+2+1+\cdots+1.
> $$
>
> There are $14$ terms from $v=15$ to $v=28$, each equal to $1$, so
>
> $$
> 28+14+9+7+5+4+4+3+3+2+2+2+2+2+14 = 101.
> $$
>
> Thus $M=28$ is feasible, and since $M=27$ is not, the minimum is
>
> $$
> M_{\min}=28.
> $$
>
> Therefore the smallest possible expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{28}{100}=\$0.28.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Construct an optimal deck
>
> We need $100$ cards with $m_v v \le 28$ for every used value $v$.
>
> Take the maximum allowable multiplicities for small values until reaching $100$ cards:
>
> $$
> m_1=28,\quad
> m_2=14,\quad
> m_3=9,\quad
> m_4=7,\quad
> m_5=5,\quad
> m_6=4,\quad
> m_7=4,\quad
> m_8=3,\quad
> m_9=3,\quad
> m_{10}=2,\quad
> m_{11}=2,\quad
> m_{12}=2,\quad
> m_{13}=2,\quad
> m_{14}=2.
> $$
>
> These account for
>
> $$
> 28+14+9+7+5+4+4+3+3+2+2+2+2+2=87
> $$
>
> cards. We need $13$ more cards. Assign one card each with values
>
> $$
> 15,16,17,\dots,27.
> $$
>
> Now the total is
>
> $$
> 87+13=100.
> $$
>
> For every value $v$ used, we have $m_v v \le 28$, so the interviewer’s best possible expected payoff is exactly
>
> $$
> \frac{28}{100}.
> $$
>
> ## 5. Final answer
>
> An optimal choice is to make the multiplicity of each integer $v$ as large as possible subject to $m_v v \le 28$. One explicit optimal deck is:
>
> - $28$ cards labeled $1$,
> - $14$ cards labeled $2$,
> - $9$ cards labeled $3$,
> - $7$ cards labeled $4$,
> - $5$ cards labeled $5$,
> - $4$ cards labeled $6$,
> - $4$ cards labeled $7$,
> - $3$ cards labeled $8$,
> - $3$ cards labeled $9$,
> - $2$ cards each labeled $10,11,12,13,14$,
> - $1$ card each labeled $15,16,\dots,27$.
>
> For this deck, the interviewer’s maximum expected return is minimized and equals
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$0.28}.
> $$
>
> Any arrangement with $\max_v(m_vv)=28$ is optimal.
