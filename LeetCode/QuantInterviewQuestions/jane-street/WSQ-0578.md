> [!example] Question 578 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have a lottery ticket with 10 slots. Behind each slot is an independently and uniformly distributed number between 0 and 1. Your payout is the maximum of these ten numbers. How much should you be willing to pay for the lottery ticket (i.e., what is the expected value of your payout)?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=579)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1,\dots,X_{10}$ be the numbers behind the $10$ slots, where each $X_i$ is independently and uniformly distributed on $[0,1]$. The payout is
>
> $$
> M=\max\{X_1,\dots,X_{10}\}.
> $$
>
> A fair price for the ticket is the expected payout, $E[M]$.
>
> ## Step 1: Distribution of the maximum
>
> For $0 \le x \le 1$,
>
> $$
> P(M \le x)=P(X_1 \le x,\dots,X_{10}\le x).
> $$
>
> By independence,
>
> $$
> P(M \le x)=P(X_1 \le x)^{10}=x^{10}.
> $$
>
> Thus the cumulative distribution function of $M$ is
>
> $$
> F_M(x)=x^{10}, \qquad 0 \le x \le 1.
> $$
>
> Differentiating gives the density:
>
> $$
> f_M(x)=10x^9, \qquad 0 \le x \le 1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expected value
>
> Now
>
> $$
> E[M]=\int_0^1 x f_M(x)\,dx
> =\int_0^1 x \cdot 10x^9\,dx
> =10\int_0^1 x^{10}\,dx.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[M]=10\left[\frac{x^{11}}{11}\right]_0^1=\frac{10}{11}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The fair amount to pay for the lottery ticket is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{10}{11} \approx 0.9091}.
> $$
>
> So one should be prepared to pay about $0.91$ units of currency.
