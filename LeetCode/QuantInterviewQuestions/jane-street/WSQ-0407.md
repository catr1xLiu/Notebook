> [!example] Question 407 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A bus pulls up to a stop. Three-quarters of the people get off and seven people get on. The bus goes to two more stops with the same results. What is the smallest number of people that could have initially been on the bus?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=408)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial number of people on the bus be $x$.
>
> At each stop, $\frac{3}{4}$ of the people get off, so $\frac{1}{4}$ remain, and then $7$ more get on.
>
> Thus after one stop, the number of people is
>
> $$
> \frac{x}{4} + 7.
> $$
>
> After the second stop, the number is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{4} + 7\right) + 7
> = \frac{x}{16} + \frac{7}{4} + 7
> = \frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}.
> $$
>
> After the third stop, the number is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x}{16} + \frac{35}{4}\right) + 7
> = \frac{x}{64} + \frac{35}{16} + 7
> = \frac{x}{64} + \frac{147}{16}.
> $$
>
> Since the number of people must be a whole number after each stop, work backward.
>
> Let the number of people just before the third stop be $n_2$. Then after $\frac{3}{4}$ get off, $\frac{n_2}{4}$ must be an integer, so $n_2$ must be divisible by $4$. The same is true before each stop.
>
> Work backward from the third stop. Let the number of people after the third stop be $m$.
>
> Then the number before the third stop is
>
> $$
> 4(m-7).
> $$
>
> Similarly, before the second stop it is
>
> $$
> 4\bigl(4(m-7)-7\bigr),
> $$
>
> and before the first stop it is
>
> $$
> 4\left(4\bigl(4(m-7)-7\bigr)-7\right).
> $$
>
> To minimize the initial number, minimize $m$ subject to all counts being nonnegative integers.
>
> The smallest possible number after the third stop is $7$, since just before that stop there could be $0$ people left after $\frac{3}{4}$ get off, and then $7$ get on.
>
> So set
>
> $$
> m=7.
> $$
>
> Then:
>
> - Before the third stop:
>
> $$
> 4(7-7)=0.
> $$
>
> - Before the second stop:
>
> $$
> 4(0-7)=-28,
> $$
>
> which is impossible.
>
> So try the next value of $m$ that makes the previous count nonnegative. We need
>
> $$
> 4(m-7)\ge 7,
> $$
>
> so $m-7\ge 2$, hence $m\ge 9$.
>
> Try $m=9$:
>
> - Before the third stop:
>
> $$
> 4(9-7)=8.
> $$
>
> - Before the second stop:
>
> $$
> 4(8-7)=4.
> $$
>
> - Before the first stop:
>
> $$
> 4(4-7)=-12,
> $$
>
> impossible.
>
> Next, require
>
> $$
> 4(4(m-7)-7)\ge 7.
> $$
>
> Compute step by step with the smallest valid values:
>
> Try $m=10$:
>
> $$
> 4(10-7)=12,\qquad 4(12-7)=20,\qquad 4(20-7)=52.
> $$
>
> Check forward:
>
> - Start with $52$.
> - First stop: $\frac{1}{4}\cdot 52 = 13$ remain, then $13+7=20$.
> - Second stop: $\frac{1}{4}\cdot 20 = 5$ remain, then $5+7=12$.
> - Third stop: $\frac{1}{4}\cdot 12 = 3$ remain, then $3+7=10$.
>
> All numbers are whole numbers, so this works.
>
> Therefore, the smallest possible initial number of people on the bus is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52}.
> $$
