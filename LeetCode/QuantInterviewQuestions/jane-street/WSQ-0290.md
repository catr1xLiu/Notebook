> [!example] Question 290 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Interview

Two teams, A and B, played football twice in independent matches. In match 1, the average number of shots by team A is greater than that of team B. In match 2, the average number of shots by team A is also greater than that of team B. Can we conclude that, when combining data from both matches, the average number of shots by team A is still greater than that of team B? If so, why? If not, provide an example.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=291)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> No. The statement is **not** always true.
>
> This is an instance of a weighted-average effect: even if team $A$ has a higher shooting average than team $B$ in each match separately, the overall average across both matches can reverse if the numbers of shots/attempts in the two matches are very different for the two teams.
>
> ## Example
>
> Interpret “average shoot” as “goals per shot attempt” (or any success rate).
>
> ### Match 1
> - Team $A$: $9$ goals out of $10$ shots, so average $= \frac{9}{10} = 0.9$
> - Team $B$: $80$ goals out of $100$ shots, so average $= \frac{80}{100} = 0.8$
>
> Thus, in match $1$,
> $$
> \frac{9}{10} > \frac{80}{100}.
> $$
>
> ### Match 2
> - Team $A$: $1$ goal out of $10$ shots, so average $= \frac{1}{10} = 0.1$
> - Team $B$: $0$ goals out of $1$ shot, so average $= \frac{0}{1} = 0$
>
> Thus, in match $2$,
> $$
> \frac{1}{10} > \frac{0}{1}.
> $$
>
> So $A$ has the higher average in each match separately.
>
> ## Combined over both matches
>
> Now combine the two matches.
>
> - Team $A$: total goals $= 9+1=10$, total shots $=10+10=20$, so overall average is
> $$
> \frac{10}{20} = 0.5.
> $$
>
> - Team $B$: total goals $=80+0=80$, total shots $=100+1=101$, so overall average is
> $$
> \frac{80}{101} \approx 0.792.
> $$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> \frac{10}{20} < \frac{80}{101}.
> $$
>
> So overall, team $B$ has the higher average, even though team $A$ had the higher average in each match.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> One **cannot** conclude that team $A$’s overall average over the two matches is greater than team $B$’s overall average. A counterexample exists, as above. This phenomenon is known as **Simpson’s paradox**.
