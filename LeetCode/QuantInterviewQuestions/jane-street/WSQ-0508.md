> [!example] Question 508 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Given two bowling balls of the same density, if one weighs X kg and has a diameter of 10 inches, and the other has a diameter of 16 inches, how much does the second ball weigh?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=509)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Since the two bowling balls have the same density, their weights are proportional to their volumes. A bowling ball is a sphere, and the volume of a sphere is proportional to the cube of its diameter.
>
> Thus, if the 10-inch ball weighs $X$ kg, then the 16-inch ball weighs
>
> $$
> X\left(\frac{16}{10}\right)^3
> $$
>
> Compute the ratio:
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{16}{10}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{8}{5}\right)^3 = \frac{512}{125} = 4.096
> $$
>
> Therefore, the weight of the 16-inch bowling ball is
>
> $$
> 4.096X \text{ kg}
> $$
>
> So the other ball weighs:
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.096X \text{ kg}}
> $$
