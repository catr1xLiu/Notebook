> [!example] Question 629 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You toss 4 coins. For each head that appears, the player receives \$1,000. The player is then given a second chance to play the game again. What is the optimal strategy for the game, and what is the expected monetary value of the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=630)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the payoff from one play of the game. Since $4$ fair coins are tossed and each head is worth $\text{\textdollar}1000$, if the number of heads is $H$, then
>
> $$
> X = 1000H,
> \qquad H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> Thus the possible payoffs are
>
> $$
> 0,\ 1000,\ 2000,\ 3000,\ 4000,
> $$
>
> with probabilities
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\frac1{16},\quad
> P(X=1000)=\frac4{16},\quad
> P(X=2000)=\frac6{16},\quad
> P(X=3000)=\frac4{16},\quad
> P(X=4000)=\frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Expected value of one play
>
> The expected number of heads in $4$ fair tosses is
>
> $$
> E[H]=4\cdot \frac12=2.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[X]=1000\,E[H]=1000\cdot 2=2000.
> $$
>
> So a single play is worth
>
> $$
> \$2000.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Optimal strategy with a second chance
>
> You may play once, observe the result, and then decide whether to keep it or use the second chance to replay. Let the second play be independent and have the same expected value $\text{\textdollar}2000$.
>
> The optimal rule is:
>
> - keep the first payoff if it is at least the expected value of a new play;
> - otherwise, reject it and replay.
>
> Since the second play has expected value $\text{\textdollar}2000$, the rule becomes:
>
> - keep if the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}2000$, $\text{\textdollar}3000$, or $\text{\textdollar}4000$;
> - replay if the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}0$ or $\text{\textdollar}1000$.
>
> Thus the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{Accept if } X \ge 2000,\qquad \text{reroll if } X < 2000.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Expected value under the optimal strategy
>
> Let $V$ be the expected value when using this strategy. Then
>
> $$
> V
> = 0\cdot P(X=0)\text{ is not kept,}
> $$
>
> so more directly, condition on the first result:
>
> - with probability $\frac{1+4}{16}=\frac5{16}$, the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}0$ or $\text{\textdollar}1000$, and you replay, giving expected value $\text{\textdollar}2000$;
> - with probability $\frac6{16}$, the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}2000$, and you keep it;
> - with probability $\frac4{16}$, the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}3000$, and you keep it;
> - with probability $\frac1{16}$, the first payoff is $\text{\textdollar}4000$, and you keep it.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> V
> = \frac5{16}(2000)+\frac6{16}(2000)+\frac4{16}(3000)+\frac1{16}(4000).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> V
> = \frac{10000+12000+12000+4000}{16}
> = \frac{38000}{16}
> = 2375.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The optimal strategy is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Replay if the first payoff is } \$0 \text{ or } \$1000;\ \text{otherwise keep it.}}
> $$
>
> The expected value of the game under this strategy is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2375}.
> $$
>
> For comparison, without the second chance the expectation is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$2000}.
> $$
