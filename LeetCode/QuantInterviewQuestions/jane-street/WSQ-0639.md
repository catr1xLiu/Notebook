> [!example] Question 639 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You are sitting in front of a roulette table with a 6-sided die and a standard deck of playing cards. What is the probability that you play all three games and receive the same number in all three?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=640)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To receive the same number in all three games, the outcomes from the roulette wheel, the $6$-sided die, and the card draw must all match.
>
> Assume the roulette table is the standard American roulette wheel, which has numbers $1$ through $36$ (plus $0$ and $00$), and the deck is standard with cards ranked $A,2,3,\dots,10,J,Q,K$. Since the die can only show numbers $1$ through $6$, the only possible common values are $1,2,3,4,5,6$.
>
> For any fixed number $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$:
>
> - Probability the die shows $k$ is $\frac{1}{6}$.
> - Probability the roulette spin lands on $k$ is $\frac{1}{38}$.
> - Probability a card of rank $k$ is drawn is $\frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}$.
>
> Thus, for a particular $k$,
> $$
> P(\text{all three are }k)=\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{38}\cdot\frac{1}{13}.
> $$
>
> There are $6$ possible matching numbers, so
> $$
> P(\text{same number in all three})=6\left(\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{38}\cdot\frac{1}{13}\right)
> =\frac{1}{38\cdot 13}
> =\frac{1}{494}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{494}}.
> $$
