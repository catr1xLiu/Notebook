> [!example] Question 36 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the largest number such that the product of its digits is 32?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=37)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To maximize a number subject to a fixed product of its digits, the digits should be chosen so that:
>
> 1. their product is $32$, and  
> 2. the resulting number is as large as possible.
>
> Since the product of the digits is $32$, factor:
> $$
> 32 = 2^5.
> $$
>
> Thus the digits must multiply to $32$. Possible digit decompositions include:
> - $8 \cdot 4 = 32$, giving digits $8,4$ and number $84$,
> - $8 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32$, giving the largest arrangement $822$,
> - $4 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 32$, giving $442$,
> - $4 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32$, giving $4222$,
> - $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32$, giving $22222$.
>
> A number with more digits is always larger than any number with fewer digits, provided the first digit is nonzero. Therefore among all such numbers, the one with the most digits is largest.
>
> Using five digits,
> $$
> 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32,
> $$
> which gives the number
> $$
> 22222.
> $$
>
> So the largest possible number is
> $$
> \boxed{22222}.
> $$
