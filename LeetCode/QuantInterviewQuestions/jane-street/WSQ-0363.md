> [!example] Question 363 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A car has a uniformly distributed value between 0 and 1000. If you bid less than this value, you get nothing but keep your bid. If you bid more than this value, you can sell the car for 1.5 times the value. What should you bid? The surface of a 3x3 block is painted. The block is split into 27 cubes and one is dropped on the floor. What is the probability that no visible face is painted? If no visible face is painted, what is the probability that it is the center cube?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=364)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Optimal bid for the car
>
> Let the car’s true value be $V$, where
>
> $$
> V \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1000).
> $$
>
> Suppose you bid $b$.
>
> - If $b < V$, you do not buy the car, so your payoff is $0$.
> - If $b > V$, you buy the car for $b$ and can sell it for $1.5V$, so your payoff is
>
> $$
> 1.5V - b.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected payoff from bidding $b$ is
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}] = \int_0^b (1.5v - b)\frac{1}{1000}\,dv,
> $$
>
> since you only buy when $V < b$.
>
> Compute the integral:
>
> $$
> E[\text{payoff}]
> = \frac{1}{1000}\int_0^b (1.5v - b)\,dv
> = \frac{1}{1000}\left[0.75v^2 - bv\right]_0^b
> = \frac{1}{1000}\left(0.75b^2 - b^2\right)
> = -\frac{b^2}{4000}.
> $$
>
> This is nonpositive for every $b$, and is maximized when
>
> $$
> b = 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimal strategy is to bid
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>

> ## 2. Painted $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube
>
> A large $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube has all of its outer surface painted, and is then cut into $27$ small cubes.
>
> When one small cube is dropped on the floor, one face is hidden and the other $5$ faces are visible.
>
> We want:
>
> 1. The probability that **no visible face is painted**.
> 2. Given that no visible face is painted, the probability that the cube is the center cube.
>
> ### Step 1: Classify the small cubes
>
> In a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube:
>
> - $8$ corner cubes have $3$ painted faces.
> - $12$ edge-center cubes have $2$ painted faces.
> - $6$ face-center cubes have $1$ painted face.
> - $1$ center cube has $0$ painted faces.
>
> ### Step 2: When can no visible face be painted?
>
> For no visible face to be painted, every painted face must be on the bottom face touching the floor.
>
> - A corner cube has $3$ painted faces, so at least $2$ painted faces remain visible no matter how it lands. Impossible.
> - An edge cube has $2$ painted faces, so at least $1$ painted face remains visible. Impossible.
> - A face-center cube has $1$ painted face. This works only if that painted face is on the bottom. Since the cube has $6$ possible bottom faces, this occurs with probability
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> - The center cube has no painted faces, so it always satisfies the condition.
>
> ### Step 3: Total probability
>
> Assume each of the $27$ cubes is equally likely to be the one dropped, and each orientation is equally likely.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{no visible face painted})
> = \frac{6}{27}\cdot\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{27}\cdot 1.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \frac{6}{27}\cdot\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{27},
> \qquad
> \frac{1}{27}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(\text{no visible face painted}) = \frac{1}{27} + \frac{1}{27} = \frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 4: Conditional probability that it is the center cube
>
> Let $A$ be the event that no visible face is painted, and let $C$ be the event that the cube is the center cube.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> P(C \mid A) = \frac{P(C \cap A)}{P(A)}.
> $$
>
> Since the center cube always has no painted faces visible,
>
> $$
> P(C \cap A) = P(C) = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(C \mid A) = \frac{1/27}{2/27} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Optimal bid } = 0}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{no visible face is painted}) = \frac{2}{27}}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{center cube} \mid \text{no visible face painted}) = \frac{1}{2}}
> $$
