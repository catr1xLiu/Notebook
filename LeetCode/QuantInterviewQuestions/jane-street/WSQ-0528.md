> [!example] Question 528 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You flip four coins. At least two coins show tails. What is the probability that exactly three of them are tails? Solve this mentally without writing anything down.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=529)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event “at least two tails” and $B$ the event “exactly three tails.” We want the conditional probability
>
> $$
> P(B \mid A)=\frac{P(B)}{P(A)},
> $$
>
> since $B \subseteq A$.
>
> For four fair coin flips, the total number of outcomes is $2^4=16$.
>
> - Exactly three tails: choose which one flip is heads, so there are $\binom{4}{1}=4$ outcomes.
> - At least two tails: this means $2$, $3$, or $4$ tails, giving
>   $$
>   \binom{4}{2}+\binom{4}{3}+\binom{4}{4}=6+4+1=11
>   $$
>   outcomes.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(B \mid A)=\frac{4}{11}.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{11}}.
> $$
