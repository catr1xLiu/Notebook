> [!example] Question 198 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

You have 5 buckets and infinitely many balls. You earn \$1 each time you throw a ball into an empty bucket and lose \$1 if you throw the ball into a bucket that already contains at least one ball. What is your optimal exit strategy, and what is the corresponding expected return?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=199)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> At any moment, the state of the game is determined by the number $k$ of nonempty buckets, where $k \in \{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$.
>
> - If there are $k$ nonempty buckets, then:
>   - with probability $\dfrac{5-k}{5}$, the next ball lands in an empty bucket and you gain $\text{\textdollar}1$;
>   - with probability $\dfrac{k}{5}$, it lands in a nonempty bucket and you lose $\text{\textdollar}1$.
>
> Thus the expected gain from **one more throw** when $k$ buckets are already occupied is
>
> $$
> \frac{5-k}{5}(1) + \frac{k}{5}(-1)
> = \frac{5-2k}{5}.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - if $k=0$, expected gain is $1$;
> - if $k=1$, expected gain is $\dfrac{3}{5}$;
> - if $k=2$, expected gain is $\dfrac{1}{5}$;
> - if $k=3$, expected gain is $-\dfrac{1}{5}$;
> - if $k=4$, expected gain is $-\dfrac{3}{5}$;
> - if $k=5$, expected gain is $-1$.
>
> Therefore, once $k \ge 3$, continuing has negative expected value for the next throw, and there is no reason to continue further. Hence the optimal strategy is:
>
> **Stop as soon as $3$ buckets are occupied.**
>
> Equivalently, keep throwing until the third distinct bucket is hit, then quit.
>
> ---
>
> ## Expected return under this strategy
>
> Let us compute the expected total gain.
>
> To go from $k$ occupied buckets to $k+1$ occupied buckets, we keep throwing until we hit a new bucket.
>
> When $k$ buckets are occupied:
>
> - each throw hits a new bucket with probability $\dfrac{5-k}{5}$;
> - hence the number of throws needed to get a new bucket is geometric with mean
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{(5-k)/5} = \frac{5}{5-k}.
> $$
>
> Among these throws:
>
> - exactly one throw is successful and gives $+\text{\textdollar}1$;
> - all previous throws are failures and give $-\text{\textdollar}1$.
>
> So if $N_k$ is the number of throws needed to go from $k$ occupied buckets to $k+1$, the net gain during this stage is
>
> $$
> 1 - (N_k - 1) = 2 - N_k.
> $$
>
> Taking expectation,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{gain from stage }k]
> = 2 - \mathbb{E}[N_k]
> = 2 - \frac{5}{5-k}.
> $$
>
> Now sum for $k=0,1,2$, since we stop once $3$ buckets are occupied.
>
> ### Stage $0 \to 1$
>
> $$
> 2 - \frac{5}{5} = 2 - 1 = 1.
> $$
>
> ### Stage $1 \to 2$
>
> $$
> 2 - \frac{5}{4} = \frac{3}{4}.
> $$
>
> ### Stage $2 \to 3$
>
> $$
> 2 - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the expected total return is
>
> $$
> 1 + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{3}
> = \frac{12}{12} + \frac{9}{12} + \frac{4}{12}
> = \frac{25}{12}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal exit strategy is to **continue until exactly $3$ buckets are nonempty, then stop**.
>
> The corresponding expected return is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{25}{12}}.
> $$
>
> So the maximum expected profit is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$\,\frac{25}{12}}.
> $$
