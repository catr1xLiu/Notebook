> [!example] Question 305 · Jane Street
> ## Analyst Interview

What is the smallest positive integer whose digits multiply to 108?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=306)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the number have digits whose product is $108$. We seek the **smallest** such positive integer.
>
> ## Step 1: Factorize $108$
>
> $$
> 108 = 2^2 \cdot 3^3
> $$
>
> We want to express $108$ as a product of decimal digits from $1$ to $9$, using digits that make the resulting number as small as possible.
>
> ## Step 2: Use as few digits as possible
>
> To minimize the number itself, it is best first to minimize the number of digits. Combine prime factors into the largest possible digits:
>
> $$
> 108 = 9 \cdot 6 \cdot 2
> $$
>
> since
>
> $$
> 9 = 3^2, \quad 6 = 2 \cdot 3, \quad 2 = 2
> $$
>
> Thus the digits can be $9, 6, 2$.
>
> A two-digit representation is impossible, because no two digits from $1$ to $9$ multiply to $108$.
>
> ## Step 3: Arrange digits in increasing order
>
> For a fixed set of digits, the smallest number is obtained by arranging them in ascending order:
>
> $$
> 2, 6, 9
> $$
>
> Hence the smallest number is
>
> $$
> 269
> $$
>
> ## Verification
>
> $$
> 2 \times 6 \times 9 = 108
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest number whose digits have product $108$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{269}
> $$
