> [!example] Question 419 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the sum of the digits of all the numbers from 1 to 1,000,000? This is different from the sum of the numbers. For instance, the sum of the numbers from 1 to 10 is 55, whereas the sum of the digits is 46.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=420)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the sum of the digits of all integers from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$, it is convenient to first consider all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$ written with exactly $6$ digits, allowing leading zeros.
>
> ## Step 1: Sum of digits from $000000$ to $999999$
>
> In the list of all $6$-digit strings from $000000$ to $999999$:
>
> - Each of the $6$ digit positions is equally likely to contain any digit from $0$ to $9$.
> - In any fixed position, each digit $0,1,2,\dots,9$ appears exactly $10^5$ times.
>
> Thus, the sum contributed by one position is
>
> $$
> (0+1+2+\cdots+9)\cdot 10^5 = 45 \cdot 10^5.
> $$
>
> Since there are $6$ positions, the total sum of all digits is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 45 \cdot 10^5 = 270 \cdot 10^5 = 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> So the sum of the digits of all numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$ is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000.
> $$
>
> Because the number $0$ contributes digit sum $0$, this is also the sum of the digits of all numbers from $1$ to $999{,}999$.
>
> ## Step 2: Add the number $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The number $1{,}000{,}000$ has digit sum
>
> $$
> 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the total sum of the digits from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> 27{,}000{,}000 + 1 = 27{,}000{,}001.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{27{,}000{,}001}
> $$
