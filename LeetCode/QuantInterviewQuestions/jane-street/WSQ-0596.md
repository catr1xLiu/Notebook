> [!example] Question 596 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Given an unlimited supply of coins of denominations 3, 8, and 20, what is the smallest positive integer that cannot be formed as a non-negative integer combination of these numbers?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=597)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> This is the classical **largest unattainable value** problem for fixed positive integers, often called the **Frobenius coin problem**.
>
> ## Step 1: Check whether all sufficiently large numbers are obtainable
>
> A necessary condition is that the greatest common divisor be $1$:
> $$
> \gcd(3,8,20)=1.
> $$
> Since this is true, all sufficiently large integers can be expressed in the form
> $$
> 3a+8b+20c
> $$
> with $a,b,c \ge 0$ integers.
>
> We seek the **largest** integer that cannot be written in this form.
>
> ## Step 2: Use the fact that $20$ is redundant for large values
>
> Because $20$ itself can be formed from $3$ and $8$:
> $$
> 20=4\cdot 3+1\cdot 8=12+8,
> $$
> any number obtainable using $20$ is also obtainable using only $3$ and $8$. Thus the set of numbers representable by $\{3,8,20\}$ is exactly the same as the set representable by $\{3,8\}$.
>
> So the problem reduces to finding the largest integer not of the form
> $$
> 3a+8b, \qquad a,b\ge 0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Apply the two-number Frobenius formula
>
> For two coprime positive integers $m$ and $n$, the largest integer not representable as
> $$
> mx+ny \qquad (x,y\ge 0)
> $$
> is
> $$
> mn-m-n.
> $$
>
> Here $m=3$ and $n=8$, so the largest unattainable integer is
> $$
> 3\cdot 8-3-8=24-11=13.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Verify
>
> Indeed,
> $$
> 13
> $$
> cannot be written as $3a+8b$:
>
> - If $b=0$, then $13$ is not divisible by $3$.
> - If $b=1$, then $13-8=5$, not divisible by $3$.
> - If $b\ge 2$, then $8b>13$.
>
> So $13$ is impossible.
>
> Also, every integer greater than $13$ is obtainable:
> $$
> 14=8+3+3, \quad
> 15=3\cdot 5, \quad
> 16=8+8, \quad
> 17=8+3+3+3,
> $$
> and adding $3$ repeatedly gives all larger integers.
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The smallest threshold after which every number is obtainable is $14$, so the **largest number that cannot be obtained** using $3$, $8$, and $20$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{13}.
> $$
>
> If the question is interpreted as “the smallest positive integer that cannot be obtained,” that would instead be
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}.
> $$
>
> But in the usual Frobenius-problem sense, the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{13}.
> $$
