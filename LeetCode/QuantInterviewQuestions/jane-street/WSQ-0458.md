> [!example] Question 458 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Eight quants from different banks want to know the average salary of the group, but each prefers not to disclose their own salary to the others. Can you design a strategy for the group to calculate the average salary without revealing individual salaries?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=459)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> A simple secure strategy is to use **random offsets that cancel out in the total**.
>
> Let the eight salaries be
> $$
> s_1, s_2, \dots, s_8.
> $$
> They want to compute
> $$
> \frac{s_1+s_2+\cdots+s_8}{8}
> $$
> without revealing any individual $s_i$.
>
> ### Step 1: Arrange the quants in a circle
> Label them $1,2,\dots,8$ around a table.
>
> ### Step 2: Each quant chooses a secret random number
> Quant $i$ chooses a private random number $r_i$, known only to that quant.
>
> ### Step 3: Secret sharing of random numbers
> Each quant $i$ secretly sends $r_i$ to quant $i+1$ (with quant $8$ sending to quant $1$).
>
> So each person knows:
> - their own salary $s_i$,
> - their own random number $r_i$,
> - the random number $r_{i-1}$ received from the previous person.
>
> ### Step 4: Each quant announces a masked value
> Quant $i$ publicly announces
> $$
> a_i = s_i + r_i - r_{i-1},
> $$
> where indices are taken modulo $8$.
>
> ### Step 5: Add the announced values
> Now sum all the public announcements:
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^8 a_i
> = \sum_{i=1}^8 (s_i + r_i - r_{i-1})
> = \sum_{i=1}^8 s_i + \sum_{i=1}^8 r_i - \sum_{i=1}^8 r_{i-1}.
> $$
> But
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^8 r_i = \sum_{i=1}^8 r_{i-1},
> $$
> since these are the same eight numbers just indexed differently. Hence the random terms cancel, leaving
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^8 a_i = \sum_{i=1}^8 s_i.
> $$
>
> Therefore the group learns the total salary, and hence the average salary is
> $$
> \frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^8 a_i.
> $$
>
> ## Why this preserves privacy
> Each announced number $a_i$ is disguised by the random terms $r_i-r_{i-1}$. Without knowing both random numbers, no one can recover $s_i$. Thus no individual salary is disclosed, while the group still obtains the exact average.
>
> ## Final answer
> Have each quant privately pass a random number to the next person in a circle, then publicly announce
> $$
> s_i + r_i - r_{i-1}.
> $$
> When these eight announced values are summed, the random numbers cancel, yielding the total salary. Dividing by $8$ gives the average salary, without revealing any individual salary.
