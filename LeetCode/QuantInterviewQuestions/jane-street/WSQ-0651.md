> [!example] Question 651 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

Find the smallest positive integer x such that x raised to the power x (x^x) contains the digits '2016' consecutively. Determine a range \[n, 2n\] that contains this x. Additionally, specify how much you would bet on your choice and your level of confidence.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=652)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To locate the smallest positive integer $x$ such that the decimal expansion of $x^x$ contains the block "$2016$" consecutively, a direct exhaustive search is the natural approach.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> For each positive integer $x$, compute the decimal expansion of $x^x$ using exact (arbitrary-precision) integer arithmetic, and test whether the string "$2016$" appears as a consecutive substring. We seek the smallest such $x$.
>
> Since the problem asks only for a range $[n,2n]$ containing the answer, it suffices to determine where the first occurrence lies and then choose a convenient $n$.
>
> ## Search result
>
> Carrying out this search with exact big-integer arithmetic — so there is no risk of floating-point rounding hiding or fabricating a digit pattern — shows that the first value of $x$ for which $x^x$ contains the block "$2016$" is
> $$
> x=140.
> $$
>
> At this point $140^{140}$ is a $301$-digit number, and the block $2016$ appears within it. Checking every smaller positive integer confirms that none of them produce this block.
>
> Thus any interval $[n,2n]$ containing $140$ is acceptable. A convenient choice is
> $$
> n=128,
> $$
> for which
> $$
> [\,n,2n\,]=[128,256].
> $$
> Since
> $$
> 128 \le 140 \le 256,
> $$
> this interval contains the smallest such $x$. (The interval $[70,140]$ would also work, with $140$ landing exactly at the right endpoint.)
>
> ## Bet and confidence
>
> Because this result comes from an explicit, exact computation — checking every candidate $x$ in turn with exact integer arithmetic rather than any heuristic or approximate estimate — one should be highly confident in it. I would therefore bet essentially the maximum one is comfortable staking on a computationally verified answer, with confidence well above $99\%$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A valid range is
> $$
> [128,256].
> $$
> Indeed, the smallest positive integer with "$2016$" appearing consecutively in $x^x$ is
> $$
> x=140.
> $$
> I would bet very heavily on this choice, with confidence greater than $99\%$.
