> [!example] Question 343 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If I flip a fair coin five times, what is the probability of getting at least two heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=344)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $5$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(5, \tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2).
> $$
>
> Use the complement:
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=1-P(X=0)-P(X=1).
> $$
>
> Compute each term:
>
> $$
> P(X=0)=\binom{5}{0}\left(\tfrac12\right)^5=\frac{1}{32},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(X=1)=\binom{5}{1}\left(\tfrac12\right)^5=\frac{5}{32}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 2)=1-\frac{1}{32}-\frac{5}{32}
> =1-\frac{6}{32}
> =\frac{26}{32}
> =\frac{13}{16}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of getting at least $2$ heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{13}{16}}.
> $$
