> [!example] Question 372 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of drawing two kings from a standard deck of cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=373)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume two cards are drawn **without replacement** from a standard $52$-card deck.
>
> There are $4$ kings in the deck.
>
> The probability that the first card is a king is
>
> $$
> \frac{4}{52}.
> $$
>
> Given that the first card is a king, there are now $3$ kings left among $51$ remaining cards, so the probability that the second card is also a king is
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{51}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of drawing two kings is
>
> $$
> \frac{4}{52}\cdot\frac{3}{51}
> = \frac{12}{2652}
> = \frac{1}{221}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of drawing two kings is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{221}}.
> $$
