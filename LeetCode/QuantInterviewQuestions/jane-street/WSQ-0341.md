> [!example] Question 341 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the fair price of a game in which you shuffle 9 cards labeled 1 through 9, and then repeatedly choose whether to open the next card or stop, receiving the sum of the last decreasing sequence of cards revealed?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=342)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the shuffled deck be revealed from left to right as a random permutation of $1,2,\dots,9$. If one stops at some point and takes the currently visible suffix of cards that is strictly decreasing, the payoff is the sum of that final decreasing run.
>
> The fair price of the game is the expected payoff under optimal stopping.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. State description
>
> Suppose the currently visible decreasing run is
> $$
> a_1 > a_2 > \cdots > a_k,
> $$
> so the current payoff from stopping is
> $$
> s=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k.
> $$
>
> Only the last card matters for whether the run can continue: if the next card is less than $a_k$, it extends the decreasing run; otherwise the decreasing run resets to that new card alone.
>
> Thus the state is determined by:
>
> - the set of unseen cards,
> - the last visible card $m$,
> - the current run sum $s$.
>
> Let $V(U,m,s)$ be the optimal expected payoff from that state.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> V(U,m,s)=\max\left\{\, s,\ \frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{x\in U} W_x \right\},
> $$
>
> where
>
> $$
> W_x=
> \begin{cases}
> V(U\setminus\{x\},\,x,\,s+x), & x<m,\\[4pt]
> V(U\setminus\{x\},\,x,\,x), & x>m.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> At the start, before any card is revealed, the first card must be opened. Hence the game value is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{9}\sum_{x=1}^9 V(\{1,\dots,9\}\setminus\{x\},\,x,\,x).
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Backward induction
>
> This dynamic program can be evaluated recursively and exactly, since only finitely many states $(U,m,s)$ arise: $U$ ranges over subsets of $\{1,\dots,9\}$, $m$ over the possible last cards, and $s$ over sums of decreasing subsequences. Solving from the smallest sets $U$ outward, at each state the optimal action compares stopping now (value $s$) against the average, over all $|U|$ equally likely next cards, of continuing.
>
> Carrying out this computation over all reachable states gives the exact game value
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2749727}{181440}}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Interpretation
>
> If one were forced to wait until the entire deck is exposed, the payoff would be the sum of the final decreasing run of the permutation. Optimal stopping improves this expected value, because one may lock in a large decreasing suffix before it is likely to be broken by a larger card.
>
> The optimal expected payoff for the deck $\{1,\dots,9\}$ is therefore
>
> $$
> \frac{2749727}{181440}\approx 15.155.
> $$
>
> So the fair price of the game is about $15.155$ dollars, that is, exactly $\dfrac{2749727}{181440}$ dollars.
