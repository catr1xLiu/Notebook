> [!example] Question 171 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

You roll a six-sided die and receive the amount you roll in cash. How much should you pay to play this game? What if you do not like your first roll and are allowed to roll exactly once more (but you must take what you get the last time)? What if you can roll up to three times, always keeping the last roll? What if you can roll infinitely many times?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=172)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The fair price of the game is its expected payoff.
>
> ## 1. One roll
>
> Let $X$ be the result of one fair die roll. Then
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So the fair price is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$3.50}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Up to two rolls, with the option to reroll once
>
> After seeing the first roll, you should keep it or reroll depending on which choice gives larger expected value.
>
> If you reroll, the expected value of the second roll is
>
> $$
> E[X] = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus, after the first roll, the optimal strategy is:
>
> - keep the first roll if it is at least $4$,
> - reroll if it is $1,2,$ or $3$.
>
> So the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> This equals
>
> $$
> \frac{10.5+15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Hence the fair price is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$4.25}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Up to three rolls
>
> Work backward.
>
> On the third roll, you must keep the result, so its expected value is
>
> $$
> V_1 = 3.5.
> $$
>
> With two rolls remaining, as above, the value is
>
> $$
> V_2 = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Now suppose you are on the first of the three allowed rolls. After seeing the roll, you may keep it or continue, in which case your expected future value is $V_2 = 4.25$.
>
> Therefore you should:
>
> - keep $5$ or $6$,
> - reroll $1,2,3,4$.
>
> So the expected payoff is
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{1}{6}(4.25+4.25+4.25+4.25+5+6).
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{17+11}{6} = \frac{28}{6} = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> Hence the fair price is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$ \frac{14}{3} \approx \$4.67}.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Infinitely many times
>
> Let $V$ be the value of the game when you may reroll indefinitely.
>
> After each roll, you may either keep the outcome or reroll and still have value $V$. Thus the optimal strategy is to keep any roll at least $V$, and reroll any roll below $V$.
>
> Since the die only shows integers from $1$ to $6$, the threshold must lie between two consecutive integers. The natural candidate is to keep $5$ and $6$, and reroll $1,2,3,4$.
>
> If that is the optimal strategy, then
>
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{6}(V+V+V+V+5+6).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 6V = 4V + 11,
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> 2V = 11,
> \qquad
> V = 5.5.
> $$
>
> This is consistent, since $4 < 5.5 < 5$, is false; so this threshold cannot occur.
>
> Try instead keeping only $6$ and rerolling $1,2,3,4,5$. Then
>
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{6}(V+V+V+V+V+6),
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> 6V = 5V + 6,
> \qquad
> V = 6.
> $$
>
> This is consistent: you should keep a roll if it is at least $6$, so only $6$ is kept.
>
> Therefore the game value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$6.00}.
> $$
>
> Indeed, with unlimited rerolls, you can wait until a $6$ appears, which happens with probability $1$, so the payoff is $6$.
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - One roll: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}3.50}$
> - Up to two rolls: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}4.25}$
> - Up to three rolls: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}14/3 \approx \text{\textdollar}4.67}$
> - Infinitely many rolls: $\boxed{\text{\textdollar}6.00}$
