> [!example] Question 185 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

a) What is the expected value of a die?
b) Suppose you play a game where you receive a dollar amount equivalent to the number of dots that show up on a die. You roll once; if you don't like the result, you may reroll, but you must keep the second roll. What is the fair value of this game?
c) Same as (b), but now you may reroll up to twice.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=186)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## a) Expected value of a fair die
>
> Let $X$ be the outcome of a fair six-sided die, so $X \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ with
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{1}{6}, \qquad k=1,2,3,4,5,6.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> E[X]=\sum_{k=1}^6 k \cdot \frac{1}{6}
> = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6}
> = \frac{21}{6}
> = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## b) One optional reroll
>
> Let the first roll be $X_1$. After seeing $X_1$, you may either keep it or reroll once, obtaining $X_2$, which must be kept.
>
> The key is to compare the first-roll value to the expected value of the reroll. Since
>
> $$
> E[X_2]=3.5,
> $$
>
> it is optimal to:
>
> - keep $X_1$ if $X_1 \ge 4$,
> - reroll if $X_1 \le 3$.
>
> So the payoff $Y$ is
>
> $$
> Y=
> \begin{cases}
> X_1, & \text{if } X_1=4,5,6,\\
> X_2, & \text{if } X_1=1,2,3.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Now compute $E[Y]$ by conditioning on the first roll:
>
> $$
> E[Y]
> = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> E[Y]
> = \frac{10.5+15}{6}
> = \frac{25.5}{6}
> = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fair value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## c) Two optional rerolls
>
> Now you may roll up to three times total: after the first roll, you may reroll; after the second roll, you may reroll again; but the third roll must be kept.
>
> This is solved by backward induction.
>
> ### Step 1: Value with one reroll remaining
>
> From part b), if you are at a stage where you may either keep your current roll or reroll once, the value of that situation is
>
> $$
> V_1=4.25.
> $$
>
> So whenever you have one reroll left, you should keep the current roll if it is at least $5$, because:
>
> - keeping $4$ gives $4$, but rerolling is worth $4.25$,
> - keeping $5$ or $6$ is better than rerolling.
>
> Thus with one reroll remaining, the optimal rule is:
>
> - reroll on $1,2,3,4$,
> - keep on $5,6$.
>
> ### Step 2: Initial decision with two rerolls available
>
> At the first roll, if you reject it, you move to a position worth $V_1=4.25$ in expectation. Therefore you should:
>
> - keep the first roll if it is greater than $4.25$, that is, if it is $5$ or $6$,
> - reroll if it is $1,2,3,4$.
>
> So the expected value $V_2$ is
>
> $$
> V_2
> = \frac{1}{6}(4.25+4.25+4.25+4.25+5+6).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V_2
> = \frac{17+11}{6}
> = \frac{28}{6}
> = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fair value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{14}{3}} \approx \boxed{4.667}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{a) } 3.5}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{b) } 4.25}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{c) } \frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}
> $$
