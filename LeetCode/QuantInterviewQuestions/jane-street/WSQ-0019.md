> [!example] Question 19 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

If I throw two regular dice and tell you that one of them is a six, what is the probability that both dice are sixes?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=20)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the ordered outcome of the two dice be one of the $36$ equally likely pairs
> $$
> (1,1),(1,2),\dots,(6,6).
> $$
>
> We are told that **at least one die is a six**. The outcomes consistent with this information are:
> $$
> (6,1),(6,2),(6,3),(6,4),(6,5),(6,6),(1,6),(2,6),(3,6),(4,6),(5,6).
> $$
> There are $11$ such outcomes.
>
> Among these, the outcome with **two sixes** is only
> $$
> (6,6).
> $$
>
> Therefore, the conditional probability is
> $$
> \frac{1}{11}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that both dice are sixes is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{11}}.
> $$
