> [!example] Question 226 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

What is the expected number of heads when tossing 6 coins, given that the number of heads is greater than 2?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=227)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $6$ fair coins are tossed. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(6,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want the conditional expectation
>
> $$
> E[X \mid X>2].
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Compute $P(X>2)$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{6}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^6=\frac{\binom{6}{k}}{64},
> $$
>
> we have
>
> $$
> P(X>2)=\sum_{k=3}^{6} P(X=k)
> = \frac{\binom{6}{3}+\binom{6}{4}+\binom{6}{5}+\binom{6}{6}}{64}.
> $$
>
> Using the binomial coefficients,
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{3}=20,\quad \binom{6}{4}=15,\quad \binom{6}{5}=6,\quad \binom{6}{6}=1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(X>2)=\frac{20+15+6+1}{64}=\frac{42}{64}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute $E[X \mid X>2]$
>
> By definition,
>
> $$
> E[X \mid X>2]
> = \frac{\sum_{k=3}^{6} k\,P(X=k)}{P(X>2)}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \sum_{k=3}^{6} k\,P(X=k)
> = \frac{3\binom{6}{3}+4\binom{6}{4}+5\binom{6}{5}+6\binom{6}{6}}{64}.
> $$
>
> Substitute values:
>
> $$
> 3\cdot 20 + 4\cdot 15 + 5\cdot 6 + 6\cdot 1
> = 60+60+30+6=156.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> E[X \mid X>2]
> = \frac{156/64}{42/64}
> = \frac{156}{42}
> = \frac{26}{7}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> E[X \mid X>2]=\frac{26}{7}.
> $$
>
> So the expected number of heads, given that there are more than $2$ heads, is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{26}{7}}.
> $$
