> [!example] Question 236 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

How many handshakes occur if every person in a room shakes hands with every other person exactly once?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=237)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the number of handshakes if everyone in a room shakes hands with everybody else exactly once, suppose there are $n$ people in the room.
>
> Each handshake is determined by choosing a pair of people. Thus the total number of handshakes is the number of unordered pairs that can be formed from $n$ people:
>
> $$
> \binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Another way to see this is:
>
> - Person $1$ shakes hands with $n-1$ others.
> - Person $2$ then has $n-2$ new handshakes left.
> - Continuing this way gives
>
> $$
> (n-1) + (n-2) + \cdots + 2 + 1.
> $$
>
> This sum equals
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the number of handshakes is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}.
> $$
