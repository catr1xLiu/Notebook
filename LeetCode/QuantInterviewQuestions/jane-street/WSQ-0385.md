> [!example] Question 385 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

If there are N points on a circle and you draw a chord between each pair of points, how many regions is the circle subdivided into?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=386)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the $N$ points are distinct and positioned so that no three chords intersect at a single interior point. Under this general-position assumption, each new chord intersects existing chords only at distinct interior points.
>
> ## Step 1: Count the regions created incrementally
>
> Start with the circle itself, which is one region.
>
> Each time a new chord is drawn, if it crosses $k$ existing chords, then it is divided into $k+1$ segments, and each segment splits one existing region into two. Thus, that chord increases the number of regions by $k+1$.
>
> So the total number of regions equals
>
> $$
> 1 + \sum (\text{number of intersections on each chord} + 1).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Count contributions from chords
>
> There are
>
> $$
> \binom{N}{2}
> $$
>
> chords in total, since each chord is determined by a pair of points.
>
> Thus the total contribution of the $+1$ terms is
>
> $$
> \binom{N}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Count interior intersections
>
> An interior intersection is determined by choosing $4$ of the $N$ points: any set of $4$ points forms a quadrilateral, whose two diagonals intersect once inside the circle.
>
> Hence the number of interior intersection points is
>
> $$
> \binom{N}{4}.
> $$
>
> Each such intersection is counted exactly once in the total crossing count relevant to the incremental construction.
>
> ## Step 4: Combine the counts
>
> Therefore the number of regions is
>
> $$
> 1 + \binom{N}{2} + \binom{N}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The circle is subdivided into
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 + \binom{N}{2} + \binom{N}{4}}
> $$
>
> regions, provided no three chords meet at a single interior point.
