> [!example] Question 671 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

In a group of 10 people standing in a circle, how many handshakes occur if each person shakes hands with every other person exactly once?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=672)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Arrange the $10$ people as vertices of a circle. A handshake corresponds to choosing a pair of distinct people.
>
> Each person must shake hands with every other person exactly once, so the total number of handshakes is the number of unordered pairs of people:
>
> $$
> \binom{10}{2}=\frac{10\cdot 9}{2}=45.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, each of the $10$ people shakes hands with $9$ others, giving $10\cdot 9=90$ handshakes if counted person-by-person, but this counts each handshake twice, so:
>
> $$
> \frac{90}{2}=45.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the number of handshakes is
>
> $$
> \boxed{45}.
> $$
