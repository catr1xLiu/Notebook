> [!example] Question 195 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

If you roll a fair six-sided die twice, what is the probability that the sum of the two rolls is 10?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=196)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Interpret “getting $10$” as obtaining a total of $10$ from the two rolls of a fair six-sided die.
>
> There are $6 \times 6 = 36$ equally likely outcomes when a die is rolled twice.
>
> The outcomes whose sum is $10$ are:
> $$
> (4,6),\ (5,5),\ (6,4)
> $$
> So there are $3$ favorable outcomes.
>
> Hence the probability is
> $$
> \frac{3}{36}=\frac{1}{12}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of getting $10$ is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{12}}.
> $$
