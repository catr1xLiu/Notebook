> [!example] Question 106 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You have a truck that can carry up to 1,000 apples and must transport 3,000 apples from your farm to a market 1,000 miles away. The truck has a hole that causes it to irrecoverably drop 1 apple per mile traveled. You may drop apples off in secure boxes along the road to pick them up later, but the truck can still hold only 1,000 apples at a time. What strategy maximizes the number of apples delivered to market?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=107)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> The key idea is that when many apples remain to be moved, the truck must shuttle back and forth, so the effective loss rate is larger than $1$ apple per mile. We therefore transport the apples in stages.
>
> Let the truck capacity be $1000$, and initially there are $3000$ apples to move.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Effective loss rate with $3000$ apples
>
> As long as more than $2000$ apples remain at some location, all $3$ truckloads are needed. To move the entire stock forward by a small distance $dx$:
>
> - $3$ loaded trips forward are required,
> - but only $2$ return trips are required, since after the last forward trip there is no need to return.
>
> Thus over distance $dx$, the truck travels a total of
>
> $$
> 3dx + 2dx = 5dx
> $$
>
> and since it loses $1$ apple per mile traveled, the apples lost are
>
> $$
> 5dx.
> $$
>
> So while transporting $3000$ apples, the effective loss rate is
>
> $$
> 5 \text{ apples per mile}.
> $$
>
> We should continue this stage until only $2000$ apples remain, because then only $2$ loads are needed.
>
> To reduce from $3000$ apples to $2000$ apples, we may lose
>
> $$
> 3000 - 2000 = 1000 \text{ apples}.
> $$
>
> At $5$ apples per mile, this can happen over
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{5} = 200 \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> So after moving the cache forward $200$ miles, we can have $2000$ apples there.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Effective loss rate with $2000$ apples
>
> Now only $2$ truckloads are needed. To move the entire stock forward by $dx$:
>
> - $2$ loaded trips forward,
> - $1$ return trip.
>
> So the truck travels
>
> $$
> 2dx + 1dx = 3dx,
> $$
>
> hence the effective loss rate is
>
> $$
> 3 \text{ apples per mile}.
> $$
>
> We continue until only $1000$ apples remain, because then everything fits in one load.
>
> To reduce from $2000$ apples to $1000$ apples, we may lose
>
> $$
> 2000 - 1000 = 1000 \text{ apples}.
> $$
>
> At $3$ apples per mile, this takes
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3} = 333\tfrac{1}{3} \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> So this second stage extends from mile $200$ to mile
>
> $$
> 200 + 333\tfrac{1}{3} = 533\tfrac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> At that point, there are $1000$ apples remaining.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Final stage with $1000$ apples
>
> Now all remaining apples fit in one load, so we simply drive straight to market.
>
> Distance remaining:
>
> $$
> 1000 - 533\tfrac{1}{3} = 466\tfrac{2}{3} \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> Since the truck loses $1$ apple per mile, the apples lost in this final stage are
>
> $$
> 466\tfrac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the number delivered is
>
> $$
> 1000 - 466\tfrac{2}{3} = 533\tfrac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Optimal plan
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> 1. Use shuttling trips to move all apples to a cache $200$ miles from the farm, ending with $2000$ apples there.
> 2. Then shuttle those apples to a cache $533\tfrac{1}{3}$ miles from the farm, ending with $1000$ apples there.
> 3. Finally, drive the remaining $1000$ apples directly to market.
>
> Thus the maximum number of apples that can reach the market is
>
> $$
> \boxed{533\tfrac{1}{3}}.
> $$
>
> So, in principle, the market receives exactly
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1600}{3}}
> $$
>
> apples.
