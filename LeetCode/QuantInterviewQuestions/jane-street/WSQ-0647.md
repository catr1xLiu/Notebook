> [!example] Question 647 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Assistant Interview

You are trying to get to Orlando, which is 800 miles away. You have 2,500 apples and a truck that can hold 1,000 apples at a time. You have unlimited gas, can take as many trips as you like, and can store apples anywhere along the road to pick up later. However, for every mile you drive, one apple falls out of the truck and is lost forever. What is the maximum number of apples you can deliver to Orlando? Solve this mentally, without paper or pencil.

> ## Solution
>
> This is a transport-optimization problem: because the truck holds at most $1000$ apples, whenever more than $1000$ apples remain to be moved forward, multiple shuttle trips are required, and each mile traveled consumes apples.
>
> The key idea is to move the apples in **stages**, reducing the number of required trips whenever the remaining stock drops below a multiple of the truck capacity.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Cost per mile when moving a given stock
>
> Suppose there are $N$ apples at some point, all of which we want to advance a little farther.
>
> - If $2000 < N \le 3000$, then since the truck holds $1000$, this requires **3 loads forward**.
> - To support those forward trips, the truck must return after the first two deliveries, so the truck travels:
>   - $3$ forward trips,
>   - $2$ return trips.
>
> Thus each mile of progress costs
>
> $$
> 3 + 2 = 5
> $$
>
> apples.
>
> Similarly:
>
> - If $1000 < N \le 2000$, each mile costs
>   $$
>   2 + 1 = 3
>   $$
>   apples.
> - If $N \le 1000$, only one one-way trip is needed, so each mile costs
>   $$
>   1
>   $$
>   apple.
>
> So the strategy is:
>
> 1. Move the apples forward until only $2000$ remain.
> 2. Then move until only $1000$ remain.
> 3. Then carry the remaining apples to Orlando.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. First stage: reduce from $2500$ apples to $2000$
>
> In this stage, cost is $5$ apples per mile.
>
> To lose $2500 - 2000 = 500$ apples at a rate of $5$ apples per mile, the distance is
>
> $$
> \frac{500}{5} = 100 \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> So after moving everything optimally for $100$ miles, there are $2000$ apples gathered there.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Second stage: reduce from $2000$ apples to $1000$
>
> Now the cost is $3$ apples per mile.
>
> To lose $2000 - 1000 = 1000$ apples at a rate of $3$ apples per mile, the distance is
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3} = 333\tfrac{1}{3} \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> So after another $333\tfrac{1}{3}$ miles, there are $1000$ apples remaining.
>
> Total distance covered so far is
>
> $$
> 100 + 333\tfrac{1}{3} = 433\tfrac{1}{3} \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Final stage: carry $1000$ apples the rest of the way
>
> The remaining distance to Orlando is
>
> $$
> 800 - 433\tfrac{1}{3} = 366\tfrac{2}{3} \text{ miles}.
> $$
>
> Now only one load remains, so the cost is $1$ apple per mile. Hence apples lost on the final trip are
>
> $$
> 366\tfrac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the number delivered is
>
> $$
> 1000 - 366\tfrac{2}{3} = 633\tfrac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{633\tfrac{1}{3}\text{ apples}}
> $$
>
> can be transported to Orlando.
