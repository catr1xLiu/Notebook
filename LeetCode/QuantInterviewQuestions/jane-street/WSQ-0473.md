> [!example] Question 473 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting an even number of heads when flipping n coins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=474)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained.
>
> For $9$ fair coins, the probability of getting exactly $k$ heads is
>
> $$
> P(X = k) = \binom{9}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^9.
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{9} \binom{9}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^9.
> $$
>
> Using the binomial identity
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k} = 2^{n-1},
> $$
>
> for $n \ge 1$, we get
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \frac{2^{8}}{2^{9}} = \frac12.
> $$
>
> Thus, for $9$ fair coins,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
>
> Now consider $n$ fair coins. If $X \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n,\tfrac12)$, then
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^n.
> $$
>
> Again using
>
> $$
> \sum_{\substack{k=0 \\ k\text{ even}}}^{n} \binom{n}{k} = 2^{n-1},
> $$
>
> we obtain
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is even}) = \frac{2^{n-1}}{2^n} = \frac12,
> $$
>
> for every $n \ge 1$.
>
> Therefore, for $n$ fair coins,
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{even number of heads}) = \frac12}.
> $$
>
> If $n=0$, there are $0$ heads, which is even, so the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}.
> $$
