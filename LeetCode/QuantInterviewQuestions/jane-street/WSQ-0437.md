> [!example] Question 437 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You are offered a game where you flip a fair coin. Each time it comes up heads, you win \$1 and can choose to continue playing or stop. Each time it comes up tails, you lose \$1. When is the optimal time to stop?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=438)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $S_n$ denote your cumulative winnings after $n$ flips. Since each head adds $1$ and each tail subtracts $1$, the process $\{S_n\}$ is a simple symmetric random walk with increments
> $$
> X_i=
> \begin{cases}
> +1, & \text{with probability } \frac12,\\
> -1, & \text{with probability } \frac12.
> \end{cases}
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X_i]=\frac12(1)+\frac12(-1)=0,
> $$
> so the game is fair at every step.
>
> The question is whether there is any stopping rule that gives a positive expected gain. Let $\tau$ be any stopping time representing the time at which you decide to stop, and suppose first that $\tau$ is bounded. Then
> $$
> S_\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau} X_i.
> $$
> By the optional stopping principle for a fair random walk,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[S_\tau]=\mathbb{E}[S_0]=0.
> $$
> So no bounded stopping rule improves the expected payoff.
>
> More generally, for any reasonable stopping rule for which optional stopping applies, the same conclusion holds:
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[S_\tau]=0.
> $$
> Hence there is no strategy based only on past flips that gives a positive expected value. In particular, “stop after the first head,” “stop when ahead by $k$ dollars,” or “play until some pattern appears” do not change the expected gain.
>
> Therefore, from the standpoint of maximizing expected winnings, you are indifferent among all such stopping rules: the expected payoff is always $0$. If forced to give a stopping point, the natural answer is to stop immediately, since continuing does not improve expected value.
>
> ## Final answer
> There is no advantageous stopping time: for a fair coin game, the expected gain under any valid stopping rule is
> $$
> 0.
> $$
> So, in expectation, you should be willing to stop immediately; continuing does not increase your expected winnings.
