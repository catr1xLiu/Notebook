> [!example] Question 176 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

How many times does the digit '1' appear in all the numbers from 1 to 1,000,000 inclusive?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=177)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To count how many times the digit $1$ appears in all integers from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ inclusive, count contributions by digit position.
>
> ## Step 1: Count from $0$ to $999{,}999$
>
> Consider all $6$-digit strings from $000000$ to $999999$. There are $1{,}000{,}000$ such strings.
>
> In each of the $6$ positions, each digit $0,1,2,\dots,9$ appears equally often. Thus, in any fixed position, the digit $1$ appears
>
> $$
> \frac{1{,}000{,}000}{10} = 100{,}000
> $$
>
> times.
>
> Since there are $6$ positions, the total number of $1$'s appearing from $000000$ to $999999$ is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 100{,}000 = 600{,}000.
> $$
>
> Leading zeros do not affect the count of digit $1$, so this is also the number of $1$'s appearing in the usual representations of the numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$.
>
> ## Step 2: Include $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The number $1{,}000{,}000$ contains exactly one digit $1$.
>
> So the total from $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> 600{,}000 + 1 = 600{,}001.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{600001}
> $$
