> [!example] Question 646 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Construct a 2-point wide market for the probability that a randomly selected integer between 1 and 100 does not contain the digit 7.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=647)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make a $2$-point wide market, first compute the probability exactly.
>
> A randomly selected integer from $1$ to $100$ is equally likely. Count how many do **not** contain the digit $7$.
>
> From $00$ to $99$, think in two-digit form. Each digit can be any of $10$ possibilities except $7$, so there are $9$ choices for each digit. Hence the number of two-digit strings with no $7$ is
>
> $$
> 9 \times 9 = 81.
> $$
>
> These correspond to the numbers $00$ through $99$ with no digit $7$. Excluding $00$ leaves $80$ valid numbers from $1$ to $99$.
>
> Now consider $100$, which also does not contain a $7$, so the total count is
>
> $$
> 80 + 1 = 81.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{81}{100} = 0.81 = 81\%.
> $$
>
> A $2$-point wide market centered at $81\%$ is:
>
> $$
> 80\% \text{ bid at } 82\% \text{ offered}.
> $$
>
> So a suitable $2$-point market is **$80$–$82$**.
