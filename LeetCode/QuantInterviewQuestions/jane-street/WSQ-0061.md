> [!example] Question 61 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A tosses n+1 coins. B tosses n coins. B wins if he has at least as many heads as A. What is the probability that B wins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=62)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ toss $n+1$ fair coins and $B$ toss $n$ fair coins. We seek
>
> $$
> P(\text{$B$ has at least as many heads as $A$}).
> $$
>
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained by $A$ in her first $n$ tosses, and let $Y$ be the number of heads obtained by $B$ in his $n$ tosses. Also let $Z$ be the result of $A$'s extra coin, where
>
> $$
> Z=
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if the extra coin is a head},\\
> 0, & \text{if the extra coin is a tail}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then the total number of heads for $A$ is
>
> $$
> X+Z,
> $$
>
> while the number of heads for $B$ is
>
> $$
> Y.
> $$
>
> Thus $B$ wins precisely when
>
> $$
> Y \ge X+Z.
> $$
>
> Now $X$ and $Y$ are independent and identically distributed as $\operatorname{Bin}(n,\tfrac12)$. Hence, by symmetry,
>
> $$
> P(Y>X)=P(X>Y).
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> P(X=Y)+P(Y>X)+P(X>Y)=1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(Y>X)=\frac{1-P(X=Y)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Condition on $Z$:
>
> - If $Z=0$, then $B$ wins when $Y\ge X$.
> - If $Z=1$, then $B$ wins when $Y\ge X+1$, i.e. when $Y>X$.
>
> Since $P(Z=0)=P(Z=1)=\tfrac12$,
>
> $$
> P(B\text{ wins})
> =\frac12 P(Y\ge X)+\frac12 P(Y>X).
> $$
>
> But
>
> $$
> P(Y\ge X)=P(Y>X)+P(X=Y).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(B\text{ wins})
> =\frac12\bigl(P(Y>X)+P(X=Y)\bigr)+\frac12 P(Y>X)
> = P(Y>X)+\frac12 P(X=Y).
> $$
>
> Substitute $P(Y>X)=\dfrac{1-P(X=Y)}{2}$:
>
> $$
> P(B\text{ wins})
> =\frac{1-P(X=Y)}{2}+\frac12 P(X=Y)
> =\frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence the probability that $B$ wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
