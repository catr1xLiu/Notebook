> [!example] Question 549 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

If you have \$20 in \$1 and \$5 denominations, how many bills do you expect to have?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=550)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of bills when a total of $\text{\textdollar}20$ is made up of $\text{\textdollar}1$ and $\text{\textdollar}5$ bills, assuming each valid combination is equally likely.
>
> A valid combination has the form
> $$
> x+5y=20,
> $$
> where $x$ is the number of $\text{\textdollar}1$ bills and $y$ is the number of $\text{\textdollar}5$ bills, with $x,y \ge 0$ integers.
>
> Solve for all possibilities:
> - If $y=0$, then $x=20$, so total bills $=20$.
> - If $y=1$, then $x=15$, so total bills $=16$.
> - If $y=2$, then $x=10$, so total bills $=12$.
> - If $y=3$, then $x=5$, so total bills $=8$.
> - If $y=4$, then $x=0$, so total bills $=4$.
>
> Thus the possible numbers of bills are
> $$
> 20,\,16,\,12,\,8,\,4.
> $$
>
> Since there are $5$ equally likely combinations, the expected number of bills is
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{20+16+12+8+4}{5}
> =\frac{60}{5}
> =12.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected number of bills is
> $$
> \boxed{12}.
> $$
