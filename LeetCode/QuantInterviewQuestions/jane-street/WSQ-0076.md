> [!example] Question 76 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

Estimate the total mass of all oceans on Earth and provide a 90% confidence interval for your estimate.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=77)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To estimate the total mass of Earth’s oceans, use
>
> $$
> \text{mass} = \text{volume} \times \text{density}.
> $$
>
> ### 1. Estimate ocean volume
> A standard geophysical estimate for total ocean volume is about
>
> $$
> V \approx 1.33 \times 10^9 \ \text{km}^3.
> $$
>
> Convert to cubic meters:
>
> $$
> 1 \ \text{km}^3 = 10^9 \ \text{m}^3,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> V \approx 1.33 \times 10^9 \times 10^9 = 1.33 \times 10^{18} \ \text{m}^3.
> $$
>
> ### 2. Estimate average seawater density
> Average seawater density is approximately
>
> $$
> \rho \approx 1025 \ \text{kg/m}^3.
> $$
>
> ### 3. Compute mass
> Thus,
>
> $$
> M = \rho V \approx 1025 \times 1.33 \times 10^{18}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> M \approx 1.36 \times 10^{21} \ \text{kg}.
> $$
>
> ### 4. 90% confidence interval
> The main uncertainty comes from average ocean depth, total ocean area, and variation in seawater density. For a rough second-round estimate, a $\pm 10\%$ range is reasonable, giving a 90% confidence interval of approximately
>
> $$
> M \in \left[1.2 \times 10^{21},\ 1.5 \times 10^{21}\right] \ \text{kg}.
> $$
>
> ### Final answer
> The total mass of all Earth’s oceans is approximately
>
> $$
> 1.4 \times 10^{21} \ \text{kg},
> $$
>
> with a rough $90\%$ confidence interval of
>
> $$
> \left(1.2 \text{ to } 1.5\right)\times 10^{21} \ \text{kg}.
> $$
