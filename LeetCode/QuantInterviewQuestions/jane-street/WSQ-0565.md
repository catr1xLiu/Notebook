> [!example] Question 565 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Given two dice—one 10-sided and one 6-sided—you must guess the roll of each die. If you guess under or exactly, there is no penalty. You receive the total amount shown on the dice if your guess matches the rolls exactly. What is the expected value of your winnings? What guessing strategy would maximize your expected payout?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=566)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the $10$-sided die show $X \in \{1,2,\dots,10\}$ and the $6$-sided die show $Y \in \{1,2,\dots,6\}$. Assume both dice are fair and independent.
>
> You choose guesses $a$ for the $10$-sided die and $b$ for the $6$-sided die.
>
> The rule is:
>
> - if you guess under or exactly right, you receive the amount on the die;
> - if you guess over, you receive nothing.
>
> Thus the payoff from the first die is
> $$
> X \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{a \le X\}},
> $$
> and from the second die is
> $$
> Y \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{b \le Y\}}.
> $$
>
> So the total expected payoff is the sum of the two separate expected payoffs, and each die can be optimized independently.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Expected payoff from the $10$-sided die
>
> If you guess $a$, then you are paid when $X \ge a$. Hence
> $$
> E_{10}(a)=\frac{1}{10}\sum_{x=a}^{10} x.
> $$
>
> Using the formula for an arithmetic sum,
> $$
> \sum_{x=a}^{10} x = \frac{(a+10)(11-a)}{2}.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> E_{10}(a)=\frac{1}{10}\cdot \frac{(a+10)(11-a)}{2}.
> $$
>
> To maximize this, it is enough to observe that increasing $a$ removes positive terms from the sum. Indeed,
> $$
> E_{10}(a+1)=E_{10}(a)-\frac{a}{10},
> $$
> so $E_{10}(a+1)<E_{10}(a)$ for every $a \ge 1$.
>
> Hence the maximum occurs at the smallest possible guess:
> $$
> a=1.
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> E_{10}(1)=\frac{1}{10}\sum_{x=1}^{10}x
> =\frac{1}{10}\cdot \frac{10\cdot 11}{2}
> =\frac{55}{10}
> =5.5.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Expected payoff from the $6$-sided die
>
> Similarly, if you guess $b$, then
> $$
> E_6(b)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{y=b}^{6} y.
> $$
>
> Again, this decreases as $b$ increases, so the maximum is at
> $$
> b=1.
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> E_6(1)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{y=1}^{6} y
> =\frac{1}{6}\cdot \frac{6\cdot 7}{2}
> =\frac{21}{6}
> =3.5.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Total expected value
>
> The total expected payoff is
> $$
> E(a,b)=E_{10}(a)+E_6(b).
> $$
>
> Thus the maximum expected value is
> $$
> E(1,1)=5.5+3.5=9.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected value is maximized by guessing
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 \text{ on the } 10\text{-sided die, and } 1 \text{ on the } 6\text{-sided die}.}
> $$
>
> The corresponding expected payout is
>
> $$
> \boxed{9}.
> $$
>
> So the bet that maximizes your payout is to guess the minimum on each die, and the maximum expected value is $\boxed{9}$.
