> [!example] Question 662 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

Two players take turns flipping a fair coin. The first player starts and has already obtained one head. They continue flipping until one player gets three heads in total. What is the probability that the first player wins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=663)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the first player and $B$ the second player. Each turn consists of one fair coin flip, and the players alternate turns. The winner is the first player to accumulate $3$ heads.
>
> We are given that $A$ goes first and that $A$’s first flip is a head. Thus, after this known first flip, the state is:
>
> - $A$ has $1$ head,
> - $B$ has $0$ heads,
> - it is now $B$’s turn.
>
> We seek the probability that $A$ eventually wins from this state.
>
> ## Step 1: Define the state probabilities
>
> Let $P(a,b,\text{turn})$ denote the probability that $A$ eventually wins when:
>
> - $A$ currently has $a$ heads,
> - $B$ currently has $b$ heads,
> - and it is the indicated player’s turn.
>
> We need
> $$
> P(1,0,B).
> $$
>
> The terminal conditions are:
>
> - If $a=3$, then $A$ has already won, so
> $$
> P(3,b,\text{turn})=1.
> $$
> - If $b=3$, then $B$ has already won, so
> $$
> P(a,3,\text{turn})=0.
> $$
>
> Since the coin is fair, on each turn the current player gets a head with probability $\tfrac12$ and a tail with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - If it is $A$’s turn,
> $$
> P(a,b,A)=\frac12 P(a+1,b,B)+\frac12 P(a,b,B).
> $$
>
> - If it is $B$’s turn,
> $$
> P(a,b,B)=\frac12 P(a,b+1,A)+\frac12 P(a,b,A).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute needed values
>
> We work backward from states near completion.
>
> ### State $(2,2,A)$
> If $A$ has $2$ heads, $B$ has $2$ heads, and it is $A$’s turn, then:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, $A$ gets a head and wins immediately;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, $A$ gets a tail, and the state becomes $(2,2,B)$.
>
> So
> $$
> P(2,2,A)=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 P(2,2,B).
> $$
>
> Similarly,
> $$
> P(2,2,B)=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 P(2,2,A)=\frac12 P(2,2,A).
> $$
>
> Substitute:
> $$
> P(2,2,A)=\frac12+\frac12\cdot \frac12 P(2,2,A)
> =\frac12+\frac14 P(2,2,A).
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \frac34 P(2,2,A)=\frac12
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(2,2,A)=\frac23.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> P(2,2,B)=\frac12\cdot \frac23=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,1,B)$
> $$
> P(2,1,B)=\frac12 P(2,2,A)+\frac12 P(2,1,A).
> $$
> Also
> $$
> P(2,1,A)=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 P(2,1,B)
> =\frac12+\frac12 P(2,1,B).
> $$
> Substitute:
> $$
> P(2,1,B)=\frac12\cdot \frac23+\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 P(2,1,B)\right)
> =\frac13+\frac14+\frac14 P(2,1,B)
> =\frac7{12}+\frac14 P(2,1,B).
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> \frac34 P(2,1,B)=\frac7{12}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(2,1,B)=\frac79.
> $$
>
> ### State $(2,0,B)$
> $$
> P(2,0,B)=\frac12 P(2,1,A)+\frac12 P(2,0,A).
> $$
> Now
> $$
> P(2,1,A)=\frac12+\frac12\cdot \frac79=\frac89,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(2,0,A)=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 P(2,0,B)=\frac12+\frac12 P(2,0,B).
> $$
> So
> $$
> P(2,0,B)=\frac12\cdot \frac89+\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 P(2,0,B)\right)
> =\frac49+\frac14+\frac14 P(2,0,B)
> =\frac{25}{36}+\frac14 P(2,0,B).
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> \frac34 P(2,0,B)=\frac{25}{36}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(2,0,B)=\frac{25}{27}.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,2,B)$
> $$
> P(1,2,B)=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 P(1,2,A)=\frac12 P(1,2,A).
> $$
> Also
> $$
> P(1,2,A)=\frac12 P(2,2,B)+\frac12 P(1,2,B)
> =\frac12\cdot \frac13+\frac12 P(1,2,B)
> =\frac16+\frac12 P(1,2,B).
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> P(1,2,B)=\frac12\left(\frac16+\frac12 P(1,2,B)\right)
> =\frac1{12}+\frac14 P(1,2,B).
> $$
> So
> $$
> \frac34 P(1,2,B)=\frac1{12}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(1,2,B)=\frac19.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,1,B)$
> $$
> P(1,1,B)=\frac12 P(1,2,A)+\frac12 P(1,1,A).
> $$
> Now
> $$
> P(1,2,A)=\frac16+\frac12\cdot \frac19=\frac29,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(1,1,A)=\frac12 P(2,1,B)+\frac12 P(1,1,B)
> =\frac12\cdot \frac79+\frac12 P(1,1,B)
> =\frac7{18}+\frac12 P(1,1,B).
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> P(1,1,B)=\frac12\cdot \frac29+\frac12\left(\frac7{18}+\frac12 P(1,1,B)\right)
> =\frac19+\frac7{36}+\frac14 P(1,1,B)
> =\frac{11}{36}+\frac14 P(1,1,B).
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> \frac34 P(1,1,B)=\frac{11}{36}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(1,1,B)=\frac{11}{27}.
> $$
>
> ### State $(1,0,B)$
> Finally,
> $$
> P(1,0,B)=\frac12 P(1,1,A)+\frac12 P(1,0,A).
> $$
> Now
> $$
> P(1,1,A)=\frac7{18}+\frac12\cdot \frac{11}{27}
> =\frac7{18}+\frac{11}{54}
> =\frac{16}{27},
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(1,0,A)=\frac12 P(2,0,B)+\frac12 P(1,0,B)
> =\frac12\cdot \frac{25}{27}+\frac12 P(1,0,B)
> =\frac{25}{54}+\frac12 P(1,0,B).
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> P(1,0,B)=\frac12\cdot \frac{16}{27}+\frac12\left(\frac{25}{54}+\frac12 P(1,0,B)\right)
> =\frac8{27}+\frac{25}{108}+\frac14 P(1,0,B)
> =\frac{57}{108}+\frac14 P(1,0,B)
> =\frac{19}{36}+\frac14 P(1,0,B).
> $$
> So
> $$
> \frac34 P(1,0,B)=\frac{19}{36}
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> P(1,0,B)=\frac{19}{27}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Given that the first player goes first and that their first flip is a head, the probability that the first player eventually wins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{19}{27}}.
> $$
