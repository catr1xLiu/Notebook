> [!example] Question 545 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the smallest number whose digits have a product of 10,000?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=546)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To make the product of the digits equal to $10{,}000$, first factorize:
>
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 10^4 = 2^4 \cdot 5^4.
> $$
>
> Each digit must be an integer from $1$ to $9$ (no digit can be $0$, since that would make the product $0$), and since only the digit $5$ contributes a factor of $5$, and no digit contributes more than one factor of $5$, exactly four of the digits must equal $5$, supplying $5^4$.
>
> The remaining digits must multiply to
>
> $$
> 2^4 = 16,
> $$
>
> using digits from $\{1,2,4,8\}$ (the only digits built purely from factors of $2$). Since no single digit equals $16$, at least two extra digits are needed, and two digits suffice in more than one way:
>
> $$
> 16 = 4\times 4,
> \qquad
> 16 = 2\times 8.
> $$
>
> Both give exactly two extra digits, so both give a total of $4+2=6$ digits, which is the minimum possible (four digits are already forced by the factors of $5$, and $16$ cannot be reached with fewer than two more digits). This yields two candidate digit multisets:
>
> $$
> \{4,4,5,5,5,5\}
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \{2,5,5,5,5,8\}.
> $$
>
> ### Comparing the two candidates
>
> For a fixed multiset of digits with no zeros, the smallest number they can form is obtained by arranging them in increasing order. Doing this for each multiset:
>
> $$
> \{4,4,5,5,5,5\} \;\longrightarrow\; 445555,
> $$
>
> $$
> \{2,5,5,5,5,8\} \;\longrightarrow\; 255558.
> $$
>
> Comparing these two six-digit numbers, $255558$ has a smaller leading digit ($2$ versus $4$), so
>
> $$
> 255558 < 445555.
> $$
>
> No other six-digit multiset achieves the product $10{,}000$: the four $5$'s are forced, and the only pairs of digits from $1$ to $9$ multiplying to $16$ are $4\times 4$ and $2\times 8$, both already considered. Any representation using more digits (for example, three digits multiplying to $16$) would require seven digits or more, giving a larger number than any six-digit candidate.
>
> Therefore, the smallest number whose digits multiply to $10{,}000$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{255558}.
> $$
