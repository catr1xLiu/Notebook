> [!example] Question 6 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You have 3,000 apples in Edinburgh and want to transfer as many as possible to London. You have a truck with a maximum capacity of 1,000 apples. London is 1,000 miles away from Edinburgh. For every mile the truck drives while carrying apples, it consumes (or drops) one apple. What is the maximum number of apples you can deliver to London?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=7)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> This is a transport optimization problem. The truck can carry at most $1000$ apples, and for every mile traveled while carrying apples, it consumes $1$ apple. Since there are initially $3000$ apples, multiple trips are required at the beginning.
>
> The key idea is:
>
> - When there are more apples remaining than can be carried in one trip, the truck must shuttle back and forth.
> - In each stage, we reduce the stock of apples until the number remaining is small enough to require fewer trips.
>
> Let the destination be $1000$ miles away.
>
> ---
>
> ## Stage 1: Move from $3000$ apples down to $2000$ apples
>
> With $3000$ apples and truck capacity $1000$, transporting apples forward requires:
>
> - $3$ forward trips to carry all apples,
> - and $2$ return trips to bring the truck back for the next load.
>
> Thus, for each mile advanced, the apples consumed are
>
> $$
> 3 + 2 = 5 \text{ apples per mile.}
> $$
>
> To reduce the stock from $3000$ apples to $2000$ apples, we must spend
>
> $$
> 3000 - 2000 = 1000 \text{ apples.}
> $$
>
> At a rate of $5$ apples per mile, this allows progress of
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{5} = 200 \text{ miles.}
> $$
>
> So after $200$ miles, there are $2000$ apples left.
>
> ---
>
> ## Stage 2: Move from $2000$ apples down to $1000$ apples
>
> Now only $2$ loads are needed. For each mile advanced:
>
> - $2$ forward trips,
> - $1$ return trip.
>
> So the cost is
>
> $$
> 2 + 1 = 3 \text{ apples per mile.}
> $$
>
> To reduce from $2000$ apples to $1000$ apples requires spending
>
> $$
> 2000 - 1000 = 1000 \text{ apples.}
> $$
>
> At $3$ apples per mile, this allows progress of
>
> $$
> \frac{1000}{3} \text{ miles.}
> $$
>
> So after this stage, the total distance covered is
>
> $$
> 200 + \frac{1000}{3} = \frac{1600}{3} \text{ miles.}
> $$
>
> There are now $1000$ apples left.
>
> ---
>
> ## Stage 3: Carry the final $1000$ apples to London
>
> Once only $1000$ apples remain, a single trip suffices, so the cost is
>
> $$
> 1 \text{ apple per mile.}
> $$
>
> The remaining distance is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{1600}{3}
> = \frac{3000 - 1600}{3}
> = \frac{1400}{3} \text{ miles.}
> $$
>
> Therefore the apples consumed on the final trip are
>
> $$
> \frac{1400}{3}.
> $$
>
> So the number of apples delivered is
>
> $$
> 1000 - \frac{1400}{3}
> = \frac{3000 - 1400}{3}
> = \frac{1600}{3}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1600}{3}}
> $$
>
> So the maximum number of apples that can be delivered is
>
> $$
> \boxed{533\tfrac{1}{3}}
> $$
>
> apples.
