> [!example] Question 505 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

It is 10:45 in London. What is the angle, in degrees, between the hour and minute hands of a clock at this time?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=506)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> At $10{:}45$, the minute hand is at
>
> $$
> 45 \times 6^\circ = 270^\circ
> $$
>
> from the $12$ o'clock position, since each minute corresponds to $6^\circ$.
>
> The hour hand is at
>
> $$
> 10 \times 30^\circ + 45 \times 0.5^\circ = 300^\circ + 22.5^\circ = 322.5^\circ
> $$
>
> from the $12$ o'clock position, since each hour corresponds to $30^\circ$ and each minute moves the hour hand by an additional $0.5^\circ$.
>
> The difference between the two positions is
>
> $$
> |322.5^\circ - 270^\circ| = 52.5^\circ.
> $$
>
> Since this is already less than $180^\circ$, it is the smaller angle between the hands, so no further adjustment is needed.
>
> Therefore, the angle between the two hands is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52.5^\circ}.
> $$
