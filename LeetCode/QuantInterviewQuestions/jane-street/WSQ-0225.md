> [!example] Question 225 · Jane Street
> ## Internship Interview

You have 100 white balls and your opponent has 100 black balls. Each of you may put any number of your balls into a common pot. A third party randomly draws one ball from the pot. Whoever's ball is drawn wins an amount of money equal to the number of balls they have left over. If you know your opponent will put in 99 balls, how many balls should you put in to maximize your expected winnings?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=226)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $x$ be the number of white balls you put into the common pot. Your opponent puts in $99$ black balls.
>
> If a white ball is drawn, you win an amount equal to the number of white balls you have left, namely
> $$
> 100 - x.
> $$
>
> The common pot contains
> $$
> x + 99
> $$
> balls in total, of which $x$ are white. Thus the probability that a white ball is drawn is
> $$
> \frac{x}{x+99}.
> $$
>
> Therefore your expected payoff is
> $$
> E(x)=\frac{x}{x+99}(100-x).
> $$
>
> We maximize this over integers $x$ with $0 \le x \le 100$.
>
> First simplify:
> $$
> E(x)=\frac{x(100-x)}{x+99}.
> $$
>
> Treat $x$ as a continuous variable and differentiate:
> $$
> E'(x)=\frac{(100-2x)(x+99)-\bigl(100x-x^2\bigr)}{(x+99)^2}.
> $$
>
> Simplifying the numerator:
> $$
> (100-2x)(x+99)-(100x-x^2)
> =100x+9900-2x^2-198x-100x+x^2
> =9900-198x-x^2.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E'(x)=\frac{9900-198x-x^2}{(x+99)^2}.
> $$
>
> Set the numerator equal to $0$:
> $$
> 9900-198x-x^2=0,
> $$
> or
> $$
> x^2+198x-9900=0.
> $$
>
> Using the quadratic formula,
> $$
> x=\frac{-198\pm\sqrt{198^2+4\cdot 9900}}{2}
> =\frac{-198\pm\sqrt{39204+39600}}{2}
> =\frac{-198\pm\sqrt{78804}}{2}.
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> \sqrt{78804}=6\sqrt{2189}\approx 280.72,
> $$
> the positive root is
> $$
> x\approx \frac{-198+280.72}{2}\approx 41.36.
> $$
>
> Hence the maximizing integer should be near $41$ or $42$.
>
> Check both:
> $$
> E(41)=\frac{41\cdot 59}{140}=\frac{2419}{140}\approx 17.279,
> $$
> $$
> E(42)=\frac{42\cdot 58}{141}=\frac{2436}{141}\approx 17.277.
> $$
>
> Thus $E(41)>E(42)$, so the optimal choice is
>
> $$
> \boxed{41}.
> $$
>
> You should put $41$ balls into the pot.
