> [!example] Question 59 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

In a game, I throw one die four times, trying to get at least one 6. You throw two dice 24 times, trying to get at least one double six (both dice show 6 at the same time). Who has a greater probability of reaching their goal?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=60)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two events be:
>
> - $A$: getting at least one $6$ in $4$ throws of one die.
> - $B$: getting at least one double-$6$ in $24$ throws of two dice.
>
> We compare $P(A)$ and $P(B)$.
>
> ## 1. Probability for one die thrown $4$ times
>
> On a single throw of one die, the probability of **not** getting a $6$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{6}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of no $6$ in $4$ independent throws is
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(A)=1-\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4=\frac{625}{1296},
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(A)=1-\frac{625}{1296}=\frac{671}{1296}\approx 0.5177.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Probability for two dice thrown $24$ times
>
> On a single throw of two dice, the probability of getting a double-$6$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{36},
> $$
>
> so the probability of **not** getting a double-$6$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{35}{36}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability of no double-$6$ in $24$ independent throws is
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{35}{36}\right)^{24}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(B)=1-\left(\frac{35}{36}\right)^{24}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{35}{36}\right)^{24}\approx 0.5094,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> P(B)\approx 1-0.5094=0.4906.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Comparison
>
> We have
>
> $$
> P(A)\approx 0.5177,\qquad P(B)\approx 0.4906.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> P(A) > P(B).
> $$
>
> So the player who throws **one die $4$ times** has the greater chance of success.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The greater chance of winning is for **the player throwing one die $4$ times**:
>
> $$
> 1-\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4 \approx 0.5177
> $$
>
> versus
>
> $$
> 1-\left(\frac{35}{36}\right)^{24} \approx 0.4906.
> $$
>
> Therefore, **you have the better chance**.
