> [!example] Question 555 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

1. A larger cube is made up of 27 smaller cubes. If the exterior of the larger cube is painted red and then a small cube is chosen at random and rolled like a die, what is the probability that no red faces are showing? Given that no red faces are showing, what is the probability that the rolled cube is the center cube (which has no red faces)?

2. If I roll two ten-sided dice, which is more likely: rolling two odd numbers or rolling two even numbers?

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Painted $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube
>
> A large cube is made of $27$ small cubes, arranged as a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube. The outside is painted red, and then one small cube is chosen uniformly at random and rolled.
>
> ### Step 1: Classify the small cubes
>
> In a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube:
>
> - $8$ corner cubes have $3$ red faces,
> - $12$ edge-center cubes have $2$ red faces,
> - $6$ face-center cubes have $1$ red face,
> - $1$ center cube has $0$ red faces.
>
> So the only cube with no red faces at all is the unique center cube.
>
> ### Step 2: Probability that no red face shows when the chosen cube is rolled
>
> If the chosen cube is:
>
> - a corner cube: it has $3$ non-red faces, so
>   $$
>   P(\text{no red shows} \mid \text{corner})=\frac{3}{6}=\frac12;
>   $$
> - an edge cube: it has $4$ non-red faces, so
>   $$
>   P(\text{no red shows} \mid \text{edge})=\frac{4}{6}=\frac23;
>   $$
> - a face-center cube: it has $5$ non-red faces, so
>   $$
>   P(\text{no red shows} \mid \text{face-center})=\frac{5}{6};
>   $$
> - the center cube: it has $6$ non-red faces, so
>   $$
>   P(\text{no red shows} \mid \text{center})=1.
>   $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(\text{no red shows})
> =
> \frac{8}{27}\cdot\frac12
> +
> \frac{12}{27}\cdot\frac23
> +
> \frac{6}{27}\cdot\frac56
> +
> \frac{1}{27}\cdot 1.
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> \frac{8}{27}\cdot\frac12=\frac{4}{27},\qquad
> \frac{12}{27}\cdot\frac23=\frac{8}{27},\qquad
> \frac{6}{27}\cdot\frac56=\frac{5}{27},\qquad
> \frac{1}{27}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> P(\text{no red shows})=\frac{4+8+5+1}{27}=\frac{18}{27}=\frac23.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Conditional probability that the cube was the center cube, given no red shows
>
> We want
> $$
> P(\text{center} \mid \text{no red shows}).
> $$
>
> By conditional probability,
> $$
> P(\text{center} \mid \text{no red shows})
> =
> \frac{P(\text{center and no red shows})}{P(\text{no red shows})}.
> $$
>
> Since the center cube always shows no red,
> $$
> P(\text{center and no red shows})=P(\text{center})=\frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{center} \mid \text{no red shows})
> =
> \frac{\frac{1}{27}}{\frac23}
> =
> \frac{1}{27}\cdot\frac32
> =
> \frac{1}{18}.
> $$
>
> ### Answers for the cube problem
>
> $$
> P(\text{no red shows})=\frac23
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(\text{center} \mid \text{no red shows})=\frac{1}{18}.
> $$
>

> ## 2. Two ten-sided dice
>
> Assume each ten-sided die has faces labeled $1$ through $10$.
>
> Each die has:
>
> - $5$ odd outcomes: $1,3,5,7,9$,
> - $5$ even outcomes: $2,4,6,8,10$.
>
> So for one die,
> $$
> P(\text{odd})=\frac{5}{10}=\frac12,
> \qquad
> P(\text{even})=\frac{5}{10}=\frac12.
> $$
>
> For two independent dice,
> $$
> P(\text{two odd})=\frac12 \cdot \frac12=\frac14,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P(\text{two even})=\frac12 \cdot \frac12=\frac14.
> $$
>
> Thus neither is more likely; they are equally likely.
>
> ### Answer for the dice problem
>
> $$
> P(\text{two odd})=P(\text{two even})=\frac14.
> $$
>
> So the two events are **equally likely**.
