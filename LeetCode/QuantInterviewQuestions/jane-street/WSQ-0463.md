> [!example] Question 463 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

If you and your opponent each roll a fair six-sided die, and you win \$1 if your number is larger, what is your expected winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=464)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be your die roll and $Y$ be the opponent’s die roll. Both are independent and uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.
>
> You win $\text{\textdollar}1$ if $X>Y$, and otherwise you win $\text{\textdollar}0$. Thus the expected winning is
>
> $$
> E[\text{winnings}] = 1 \cdot P(X>Y) + 0 \cdot P(X\le Y) = P(X>Y).
> $$
>
> So it remains to compute $P(X>Y)$.
>
> There are $6 \times 6 = 36$ equally likely outcomes $(X,Y)$. Count the outcomes with $X>Y$:
>
> - If $X=1$, then there are $0$ values of $Y$ with $Y<1$.
> - If $X=2$, there is $1$ value of $Y$ with $Y<2$.
> - If $X=3$, there are $2$ values.
> - If $X=4$, there are $3$ values.
> - If $X=5$, there are $4$ values.
> - If $X=6$, there are $5$ values.
>
> Hence the number of winning outcomes is
>
> $$
> 0+1+2+3+4+5 = 15.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X>Y) = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}.
> $$
>
> So the expected winning is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{12}\text{ dollars}}.
> $$
