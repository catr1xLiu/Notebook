> [!example] Question 605 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

At 12:00, the hands of a clock are perfectly aligned. Assuming continuous motion, at what time will they be aligned again?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=606)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The minute hand moves at a rate of
>
> $$360^\circ \text{ per hour},$$
>
> while the hour hand moves at a rate of
>
> $$30^\circ \text{ per hour}.$$
>
> Thus, the minute hand gains on the hour hand at the relative rate
>
> $$360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ \text{ per hour}.$$
>
> To be aligned again after $12{:}00$, the minute hand must gain exactly $360^\circ$ on the hour hand. Hence the required time is
>
> $$t=\frac{360}{330}\text{ hour}=\frac{12}{11}\text{ hour}.$$
>
> Now convert $\frac{12}{11}$ hour into minutes:
>
> $$\frac{12}{11}\cdot 60=\frac{720}{11}\text{ minutes}=65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}.$$
>
> So the next alignment occurs $65\frac{5}{11}$ minutes after $12{:}00$, that is,
>
> $$1{:}05\frac{5}{11}.$$
>
> Equivalently, this is
>
> $$1{:}05{:}27\frac{3}{11}.$$
>
> Therefore, the hands are aligned again at
>
> $$\boxed{1{:}05\frac{5}{11}}$$
>
> after $12{:}00$.
