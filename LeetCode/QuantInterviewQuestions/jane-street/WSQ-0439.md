> [!example] Question 439 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You are presented with three games and must decide which one to play:
1) Throw a standard die and take the square of the resulting value as your score.
2) Throw two dice and take the product of the results as your score.
3) Throw five dice and take the square of the mode of the results as your score.
Which game should you choose to maximize your expected payoff?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=440)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To decide which game to play, compare their expected payoffs.
>
> ## Game 1: One die, square the result
>
> Let $X$ be the outcome of a fair six-sided die. The payoff is $X^2$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E[X^2] = \frac{1^2+2^2+3^2+4^2+5^2+6^2}{6}
> = \frac{1+4+9+16+25+36}{6}
> = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> So the expected payoff is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{91}{6} \approx 15.17}.
> $$
>
> ## Game 2: Two dice, take the product
>
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be independent fair dice. The payoff is $XY$.
>
> Using independence,
> $$
> E[XY] = E[X]E[Y].
> $$
> Now
> $$
> E[X] = E[Y] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> E[XY] = \left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{49}{4}.
> $$
>
> So the expected payoff is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{49}{4} = 12.25}.
> $$
>
> ## Game 3: Five dice, square the mode
>
> Interpret the mode as follows: among the five results, take the value occurring most frequently; if there is a tie for highest frequency, choose uniformly among the tied values. The payoff is the square of that chosen mode.
>
> Let $M$ denote the selected mode. We compute $E[M^2]$ by classifying outcomes according to multiplicity pattern.
>
> There are $6^5=7776$ equally likely outcomes.
>
> ### 1. All five dice different: pattern $(1,1,1,1,1)$
>
> Number of outcomes:
> $$
> 6\cdot 5\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 2 = 720.
> $$
> All five values are tied as modes, so the chosen mode is uniformly one of the five shown values. By symmetry, its distribution is uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, hence
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (1,1,1,1,1)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 2. One pair and three singles: pattern $(2,1,1,1)$
>
> Choose the paired value in $6$ ways, the three single values in $\binom{5}{3}=10$ ways, and arrange:
> $$
> \frac{5!}{2!}=60.
> $$
> Total:
> $$
> 6\cdot 10\cdot 60 = 3600.
> $$
> The unique mode is the paired value, which is uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, so
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (2,1,1,1)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 3. Two pairs and one single: pattern $(2,2,1)$
>
> Choose the two paired values in $\binom{6}{2}=15$ ways, the single value in $4$ ways, and arrange:
> $$
> \frac{5!}{2!2!}=30.
> $$
> Total:
> $$
> 15\cdot 4\cdot 30 = 1800.
> $$
> The modes are the two paired values; choose uniformly between them. By symmetry, the chosen mode is again uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, so
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (2,2,1)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 4. Three of a kind and two singles: pattern $(3,1,1)$
>
> Choose the triple value in $6$ ways, the two single values in $\binom{5}{2}=10$ ways, and arrange:
> $$
> \frac{5!}{3!}=20.
> $$
> Total:
> $$
> 6\cdot 10\cdot 20 = 1200.
> $$
> The unique mode is the triple value, uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, so
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (3,1,1)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 5. Full house: pattern $(3,2)$
>
> Choose the triple value in $6$ ways, the pair value in $5$ ways, and arrange:
> $$
> \frac{5!}{3!2!}=10.
> $$
> Total:
> $$
> 6\cdot 5\cdot 10 = 300.
> $$
> The unique mode is the triple value, uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, so
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (3,2)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 6. Four of a kind and one single: pattern $(4,1)$
>
> Choose the quadruple value in $6$ ways, the single value in $5$ ways, and arrange:
> $$
> \frac{5!}{4!}=5.
> $$
> Total:
> $$
> 6\cdot 5\cdot 5 = 150.
> $$
> Again,
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (4,1)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### 7. Five of a kind: pattern $(5)$
>
> There are $6$ outcomes, and the mode is that repeated value, uniform on $\{1,\dots,6\}$, so
> $$
> E[M^2 \mid (5)] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### Conclusion for Game 3
>
> In every possible multiplicity pattern, the selected mode is uniformly distributed over $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. Therefore
> $$
> E[M^2] = \frac{91}{6}.
> $$
>
> So the expected payoff is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{91}{6} \approx 15.17}.
> $$
>
> ## Comparison
>
> The expected values are:
> $$
> \text{Game 1}: \frac{91}{6} \approx 15.17,
> $$
> $$
> \text{Game 2}: \frac{49}{4} = 12.25,
> $$
> $$
> \text{Game 3}: \frac{91}{6} \approx 15.17.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the best choice is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Game 1 or Game 3}}
> $$
>
> since both have the largest expected payoff,
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{91}{6}}.
> $$
