> [!example] Question 60 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A robot wakes up every morning and, with equal (1/4) probability, does one of the following: 1) self-destructs; 2) does nothing; 3) clones itself (resulting in 2 robots); or 4) clones itself twice (resulting in 3 robots). If you start with one robot on the first day, what is the probability that, eventually, you will have no robots remaining?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=61)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $q$ denote the probability that a lineage starting from a single robot eventually dies out.
>
> This is a branching process. Each robot, independently, produces the following number of robots the next morning:
>
> - $0$ robots with probability $\frac14$,
> - $1$ robot with probability $\frac14$,
> - $2$ robots with probability $\frac14$,
> - $3$ robots with probability $\frac14$.
>
> If one robot has $k$ robots after a day, then extinction occurs exactly when each of those $k$ descendant lineages eventually dies out. Hence $q$ satisfies
>
> $$
> q=\frac14\left(q^0+q^1+q^2+q^3\right)
> =\frac14\left(1+q+q^2+q^3\right).
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 4q=1+q+q^2+q^3,
> $$
>
> which gives
>
> $$
> q^3+q^2-3q+1=0.
> $$
>
> Factor the cubic:
>
> $$
> q^3+q^2-3q+1=(q-1)(q^2+2q-1).
> $$
>
> Thus the roots are
>
> $$
> q=1,\qquad q=-1+\sqrt{2},\qquad q=-1-\sqrt{2}.
> $$
>
> Since a probability must lie in $[0,1]$, the possible values are $q=1$ and $q=\sqrt2-1$.
>
> To choose the correct one, compute the expected number of robots produced by one robot:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X]=\frac14(0+1+2+3)=\frac{6}{4}=\frac32>1.
> $$
>
> Because the process is supercritical, extinction does not occur with probability $1$, so the extinction probability is the smaller root.
>
> Therefore, the probability that eventually there are no robots is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\sqrt{2}-1}.
> $$
