> [!example] Question 478 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You roll a 100-sided die and may either accept the face value in dollars, or pay an additional dollar to re-roll. You can play as many times as you like. What is the optimal strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=479)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $V$ denote the maximum expected payoff when it is your turn to decide whether to stop or reroll.
>
> If the die shows $x \in \{1,2,\dots,100\}$, then you have two choices:
>
> - Stop and take $x$ dollars.
> - Reroll, in which case you pay $1$ dollar and return to the same decision problem, whose expected value is again $V$.
>
> Thus, after seeing $x$, the optimal choice is to take
>
> $$
> \max\{x,\; V-1\}.
> $$
>
> Since each roll is uniformly distributed on $\{1,\dots,100\}$, the value $V$ must satisfy
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{x=1}^{100}\max\{x,\;V-1\}.
> $$
>
> This implies there is a threshold: stop when $x$ is large enough, reroll when it is too small. Let
>
> $$
> T=V-1.
> $$
>
> Then the optimal rule is:
>
> - reroll if $x<T$,
> - stop if $x\ge T$.
>
> Because the die outcomes are integers, the threshold will be an integer cutoff. Suppose the strategy is:
>
> - reroll on $1,2,\dots,k-1$,
> - stop on $k,k+1,\dots,100$.
>
> Then the expected value is
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{100}\left((k-1)(V-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{100}x\right).
> $$
>
> Now test the candidate $k=87$, corresponding to rerolling on $1,2,\dots,86$ and stopping on $87,88,\dots,100$. Then
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=87}^{100} x
> = \frac{(87+100)\cdot 14}{2}
> = 1309.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1}{100}\left(86(V-1)+1309\right).
> $$
>
> Solving,
>
> $$
> 100V=86V-86+1309=86V+1223,
> $$
>
> $$
> 14V=1223,
> $$
>
> $$
> V=\frac{1223}{14}\approx 87.357.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V-1=\frac{1209}{14}\approx 86.357.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - if the roll is at least $87$, then $x > V-1$, so you should stop;
> - if the roll is at most $86$, then $x < V-1$, so you should reroll.
>
> This is consistent with the assumed threshold, confirming that $k=87$ is the correct cutoff.
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Reroll on }1\text{ through }86,\quad \text{stop on }87\text{ through }100.}
> $$
>
> Under this strategy, the expected value of the game is
>
> $$
> \boxed{V=\frac{1223}{14}\approx 87.357\text{ dollars}.}
> $$
