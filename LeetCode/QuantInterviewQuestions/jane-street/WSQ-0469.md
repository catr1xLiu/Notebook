> [!example] Question 469 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

If you have 5 digits, what is the largest number you can create such that the product of its digits is 120? Can you make the number arbitrarily large if the restriction on the number of digits is removed?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=470)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To maximize a number formed from digits whose product is fixed, the key idea is:
>
> - For a fixed number of digits, arranging them in descending order gives the largest number.
> - So, among all ways to factor the target product into that many one-digit factors ($1$ through $9$), we want the factorization whose digits, sorted in descending order, form the largest number. Since the leading digit dominates the size of the number, the first priority is to make the largest digit as large as possible.
>
> ## Part 1: Exactly 5 digits
>
> We want five digits $d_1,\dots,d_5\in\{1,\dots,9\}$ such that
> $$
> d_1d_2d_3d_4d_5 = 120,
> $$
> forming the largest possible $5$-digit number.
>
> Factorize:
> $$
> 120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5.
> $$
>
> To make the number as large as possible, try to make the largest digit as big as possible. Digits $9$ and $7$ cannot appear, since $120/9$ and $120/7$ are not integers. But $120/8=15$, so $8$ **can** be used as a digit: it absorbs the entire factor $2^3$ at once, leaving
> $$
> 120/8=15=5\times3
> $$
> to be split among the remaining $4$ digits. Using $1$'s to fill out the rest without wasting any factors, $15=5\times3\times1\times1$, giving the digit set
> $$
> \{8,5,3,1,1\}.
> $$
> Arranged in descending order, this is
> $$
> 85311.
> $$
>
> Since no digit larger than $8$ is achievable, and $8$ is achievable as a leading digit, no other factorization of $120$ into $5$ digits — such as $6\times5\times2\times2\times1=120$ (giving $65221$) or $5\times4\times3\times2\times1=120$ (giving $54321$) — can beat a number that starts with $8$, regardless of the remaining digits. Hence the maximum $5$-digit number is
> $$
> \boxed{85311}.
> $$
>
> ## Part 2: If the number of digits is unrestricted
>
> Now ask whether one can make the number arbitrarily large while keeping the product equal to $120$.
>
> Yes. Since multiplying by $1$ does not change the product, we may append as many $1$'s as we like:
> $$
> 85311,\qquad 853111,\qquad 8531111,
> $$
> and so on all have digit product $120$. Because appending another digit on the right always produces a larger integer, we can construct numbers of arbitrarily large size. Therefore there is no largest such number when the number of digits is unrestricted.
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - With exactly $5$ digits, the largest number is
> $$
> \boxed{85311}.
> $$
> - Without restricting the number of digits, there is **no largest number**; it can be made arbitrarily large by appending more $1$'s.
