> [!example] Question 524 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

It is noon. The hour hand and minute hand overlap. When is the next time these two hands overlap again?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=525)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> At noon, both hands are at the $12$.
>
> The minute hand moves at
>
> $$
> 360^\circ \text{ per hour},
> $$
>
> while the hour hand moves at
>
> $$
> 30^\circ \text{ per hour}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the minute hand gains on the hour hand at the relative rate
>
> $$
> 360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ \text{ per hour}.
> $$
>
> For the hands to overlap again, the minute hand must gain a full circle of $360^\circ$ on the hour hand. Hence the required time is
>
> $$
> \frac{360}{330} = \frac{12}{11} \text{ hours}.
> $$
>
> Now convert $\frac{12}{11}$ hours:
>
> $$
> \frac{12}{11} \cdot 60 = \frac{720}{11} = 65 \frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes}.
> $$
>
> So the next overlap occurs $65 \frac{5}{11}$ minutes after noon, that is,
>
> $$
> 1 \text{ hour } 5 \frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes after } 12:00.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the next time the hands overlap is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1:05\frac{5}{11}}
> $$
>
> that is, approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{1:05:27.27\ldots}
> $$
