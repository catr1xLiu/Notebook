> [!example] Question 214 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Intern Interview

If I roll two dice and multiply the two outcomes, what is the probability that the product is a perfect square?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=215)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability, count how many ordered outcomes $(a,b)$ from two fair six-sided dice make the product $ab$ a perfect square.
>
> ## Step 1: Total number of outcomes
>
> Each die has $6$ outcomes, so the total number of ordered pairs is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 6 = 36.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Determine when the product is a perfect square
>
> The possible die values are
>
> $$
> 1,2,3,4,5,6.
> $$
>
> We check, for each first die value $a$, which second die values $b$ make $ab$ a perfect square.
>
> ### If $a=1$
> Then $ab=b$, so $b$ must itself be a perfect square in $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$:
>
> $$
> b=1,4.
> $$
>
> So there are $2$ possibilities.
>
> ### If $a=2$
> We need $2b$ to be a perfect square. Testing $b=1,\dots,6$:
>
> $$
> 2\cdot 2=4
> $$
>
> is a perfect square, and no other value works. So there is $1$ possibility: $b=2$.
>
> ### If $a=3$
> We need $3b$ to be a perfect square. Testing gives
>
> $$
> 3\cdot 3=9,
> $$
>
> so only $b=3$ works. Thus there is $1$ possibility.
>
> ### If $a=4$
> We need $4b$ to be a perfect square. Since $4$ is already a square, this happens when $b$ is a perfect square:
>
> $$
> b=1,4.
> $$
>
> So there are $2$ possibilities.
>
> ### If $a=5$
> We need $5b$ to be a perfect square. The only possibility in $\{1,\dots,6\}$ is
>
> $$
> 5\cdot 5=25.
> $$
>
> So there is $1$ possibility.
>
> ### If $a=6$
> We need $6b$ to be a perfect square. The only possibility is
>
> $$
> 6\cdot 6=36.
> $$
>
> So there is $1$ possibility.
>
> ## Step 3: Count favorable outcomes
>
> Total favorable ordered pairs:
>
> $$
> 2+1+1+2+1+1=8.
> $$
>
> These pairs are
>
> $$
> (1,1),(1,4),(2,2),(3,3),(4,1),(4,4),(5,5),(6,6).
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Compute the probability
>
> $$
> P(\text{perfect square})=\frac{8}{36}=\frac{2}{9}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{9}}
> $$
