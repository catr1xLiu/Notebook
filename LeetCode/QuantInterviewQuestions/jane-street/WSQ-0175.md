> [!example] Question 175 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

In a best-of-7 game between two players, you wish to place 50/50 bets before each point such that if player A wins the series you win £1000, and if player B wins the series you lose £1000. How can you structure your bets to achieve this outcome?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=176)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the stake after each point be chosen so that the current value of the position equals the fair value of a contract paying
>
> - +£1000 if player A wins the best-of-7 match,
> - -£1000 if player B wins the match.
>
> Assume each point is a fair 50/50 event, and before each point you may place a bet on that point only.
>
> ## 1. State value
>
> In a best-of-7 match, a player wins the match upon reaching 4 points.
>
> If A currently has $a$ points and B has $b$ points, define $V(a,b)$ to be the fair value of the desired final payoff from that state.
>
> Since the final payoff is $+1000$ if $A$ eventually wins and $-1000$ if $B$ eventually wins,
>
> $$
> V(a,b)=1000\Big(2p(a,b)-1\Big),
> $$
>
> where $p(a,b)$ is the probability that $A$ wins the match from score $(a,b)$.
>
> The boundary values are
>
> $$
> V(4,b)=1000,\qquad V(a,4)=-1000.
> $$
>
> Because the next point is fair,
>
> $$
> V(a,b)=\frac{V(a+1,b)+V(a,b+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus the process is a martingale, and the required bet before each point is determined by the change in value after that point.
>
> ## 2. How the bet is chosen
>
> Suppose the current score is $(a,b)$, and before the next point you bet an amount $x$ on player A to win that point at even odds.
>
> Then:
>
> - if A wins the point, your wealth changes by $+x$;
> - if B wins the point, your wealth changes by $-x$.
>
> To replicate the desired contract, these changes must match the changes in state value:
>
> $$
> V(a+1,b)-V(a,b)=x,
> $$
>
> and equivalently
>
> $$
> V(a,b+1)-V(a,b)=-x.
> $$
>
> Since $V(a,b)$ is the average of the two successor values, these are consistent. Therefore the required bet is
>
> $$
> x=\frac{V(a+1,b)-V(a,b+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Positive $x$ means bet on A for the next point; negative $x$ means bet on B.
>
> ## 3. Compute the state values
>
> First compute $p(a,b)$, the probability that A wins the match from each state. This can be done recursively from
>
> $$
> p(4,b)=1,\qquad p(a,4)=0,\qquad p(a,b)=\frac{p(a+1,b)+p(a,b+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, from score $(a,b)$ the match lasts at most $n=(4-a)+(4-b)-1$ further points, and A wins outright exactly when A takes at least $4-a$ of those $n$ points, so $p(a,b)$ is a tail probability of a fair binomial distribution. The resulting values are:
>
> - $p(0,0)=\tfrac12,\ p(0,1)=\tfrac{11}{32},\ p(0,2)=\tfrac{3}{16},\ p(0,3)=\tfrac{1}{16}$
> - $p(1,0)=\tfrac{21}{32},\ p(1,1)=\tfrac12,\ p(1,2)=\tfrac{5}{16},\ p(1,3)=\tfrac18$
> - $p(2,0)=\tfrac{13}{16},\ p(2,1)=\tfrac{11}{16},\ p(2,2)=\tfrac12,\ p(2,3)=\tfrac14$
> - $p(3,0)=\tfrac{15}{16},\ p(3,1)=\tfrac78,\ p(3,2)=\tfrac34,\ p(3,3)=\tfrac12$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> V(a,b)=1000(2p(a,b)-1),
> $$
>
> so
>
> - $V(0,0)=0,\ V(0,1)=-312.5,\ V(0,2)=-625,\ V(0,3)=-875$
> - $V(1,0)=312.5,\ V(1,1)=0,\ V(1,2)=-375,\ V(1,3)=-750$
> - $V(2,0)=625,\ V(2,1)=375,\ V(2,2)=0,\ V(2,3)=-500$
> - $V(3,0)=875,\ V(3,1)=750,\ V(3,2)=500,\ V(3,3)=0$
>
> with terminal values
>
> $$
> V(4,b)=1000,\qquad V(a,4)=-1000.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Required bet at each score
>
> The bet before the next point is
>
> $$
> x=\frac{V(a+1,b)-V(a,b+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Using the table above gives:
>
> - $x(0,0)=312.5,\ x(0,1)=312.5,\ x(0,2)=250,\ x(0,3)=125$
> - $x(1,0)=312.5,\ x(1,1)=375,\ x(1,2)=375,\ x(1,3)=250$
> - $x(2,0)=250,\ x(2,1)=375,\ x(2,2)=500,\ x(2,3)=500$
> - $x(3,0)=125,\ x(3,1)=250,\ x(3,2)=500,\ x(3,3)=1000$
>
> These are the amounts to bet on A at the current score $(a,b)$.
>
> For example:
>
> - at $0$–$0$, bet £312.50 on the next point for A;
> - if A wins that point (score $1$–$0$), the next bet is again £312.50 on A;
> - if instead B wins that point (score $0$–$1$), the next bet is still £312.50 on A;
> - if the score reaches $2$–$0$, the next bet drops to £250 on A;
> - if the score reaches $1$–$1$, the next bet rises to £375 on A;
> - and so on according to the table.
>
> ## 5. Why this works
>
> Start with initial wealth
>
> $$
> V(0,0)=0.
> $$
>
> After each point, adjust the next stake according to the current score. By construction, after every point your wealth equals $V(a,b)$ for the new score.
>
> When the match ends, the score is either $(4,b)$ or $(a,4)$, so your wealth is
>
> - $V(4,b)=1000$ if A wins the match,
> - $V(a,4)=-1000$ if B wins the match.
>
> Thus the strategy exactly produces the desired outcome.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Bet on player A before each point according to the current score $(a,b)$, using the amounts:
>
> - score $(0,0)$: bet £312.50; $(0,1)$: £312.50; $(0,2)$: £250; $(0,3)$: £125
> - score $(1,0)$: bet £312.50; $(1,1)$: £375; $(1,2)$: £375; $(1,3)$: £250
> - score $(2,0)$: bet £250; $(2,1)$: £375; $(2,2)$: £500; $(2,3)$: £500
> - score $(3,0)$: bet £125; $(3,1)$: £250; $(3,2)$: £500; $(3,3)$: £1000
>
> The amount shown at score $(a,b)$ is the amount to bet on A winning the next point. Updating the stake after each point in this way guarantees a final result of exactly
>
> - +£1000 if A wins the best-of-7 match,
> - -£1000 if B wins the match.
