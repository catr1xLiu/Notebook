> [!example] Question 428 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

I have a square and place three dots randomly along the four edges. (1) What is the probability that the dots, when connected, do not form a triangle? (2) What is the probability that the dots lie on distinct edges?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=429)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the square have sides labeled $1,2,3,4$. Each dot is placed independently and uniformly on the perimeter, so each dot lands on any given edge with probability $\tfrac14$.
>
> There are therefore
> $$
> 4^3 = 64
> $$
> equally likely assignments of the three dots to edges.
>
> ## 1. Probability that the three dots do not form a triangle
>
> Three points fail to form a triangle exactly when they are collinear. On a square, this can happen only if all three points lie on the same edge, since no straight line can intersect three distinct edges of a square in points on the boundary.
>
> For a fixed edge, the probability that all three dots lie on that edge is
> $$
> \left(\frac14\right)^3 = \frac1{64}.
> $$
> Since there are $4$ edges, the probability that all three dots lie on one common edge is
> $$
> 4 \cdot \frac1{64} = \frac4{64} = \frac1{16}.
> $$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> \boxed{\frac1{16}}
> $$
> is the probability that the dots do not form a triangle.
>
> ## 2. Probability that the dots lie on distinct edges
>
> For the three dots to lie on distinct edges, assign them to three different edges.
>
> Choose an edge for the first dot: $4$ choices.
>
> Choose a different edge for the second dot: $3$ choices.
>
> Choose a different edge for the third dot: $2$ choices.
>
> Thus the number of favorable assignments is
> $$
> 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24.
> $$
> Since there are $64$ total assignments, the probability is
> $$
> \frac{24}{64} = \frac38.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \boxed{\frac38}
> $$
> is the probability that the three dots lie on distinct edges.
