> [!example] Question 395 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

A has 5 fair coins and B has 4 fair coins. A wins only if he flips more heads than B does. What is the probability that A wins?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=396)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained by $A$, and let $Y$ be the number of heads obtained by $B$.
>
> Since the coins are fair and independent,
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Bin}(5,\tfrac12), \qquad Y \sim \operatorname{Bin}(4,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(A\text{ wins}) = P(X>Y).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Write the distributions
>
> For $X$,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{\binom{5}{k}}{2^5}, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4,5.
> $$
>
> For $Y$,
>
> $$
> P(Y=j)=\frac{\binom{4}{j}}{2^4}, \qquad j=0,1,2,3,4.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute $P(X>Y)$
>
> Using independence,
>
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{5} P(X=k)\,P(Y<k).
> $$
>
> First compute the cumulative probabilities for $Y$:
>
> - $P(Y<1)=P(Y=0)=\dfrac{1}{16}$
> - $P(Y<2)=P(Y=0)+P(Y=1)=\dfrac{1+4}{16}=\dfrac{5}{16}$
> - $P(Y<3)=\dfrac{1+4+6}{16}=\dfrac{11}{16}$
> - $P(Y<4)=\dfrac{1+4+6+4}{16}=\dfrac{15}{16}$
> - $P(Y<5)=1$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\frac{1,5,10,10,5,1}{32} \quad \text{for } k=0,1,2,3,4,5.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> P(X>Y)
> &= P(X=1)P(Y<1)+P(X=2)P(Y<2)+P(X=3)P(Y<3) \\
> &\quad +P(X=4)P(Y<4)+P(X=5)P(Y<5) \\
> &= \frac{5}{32}\cdot\frac{1}{16} + \frac{10}{32}\cdot\frac{5}{16} + \frac{10}{32}\cdot\frac{11}{16} + \frac{5}{32}\cdot\frac{15}{16} + \frac{1}{32}\cdot 1.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Convert all terms to denominator $512$:
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{512}+\frac{50}{512}+\frac{110}{512}+\frac{75}{512}+\frac{16}{512}
> = \frac{256}{512}
> = \frac12.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> $$
