> [!example] Question 443 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the smallest number whose digits multiply to 10,000?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=444)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make a number whose digits multiply to $10{,}000$, factor $10{,}000$ into single-digit factors and then arrange them to get the smallest possible number.
>
> First factor:
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 10^4 = 2^4 \cdot 5^4
> $$
>
> Each digit must be between $1$ and $9$, so we combine the prime factors into digits:
> $$
> 2 \cdot 5 = 10
> $$
> but $10$ is not a digit, so instead use
> $$
> 4 = 2^2,\qquad 5=5
> $$
> Thus,
> $$
> 10{,}000 = (2^2\cdot 5)(2^2\cdot 5)(5)(5)=4\cdot 5\cdot 4\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5
> $$
> A better grouping is:
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 8\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5
> $$
> since
> $$
> 8\cdot 5^4 = 8\cdot 625 = 5000
> $$
> so this is not enough. Instead:
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5
> $$
> Group into largest possible digits:
> $$
> 2\cdot 5 = 10 \text{ not allowed,}
> $$
> so use
> $$
> 4\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 2\cdot 2
> $$
> But the cleanest valid digit factorization is
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 8\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot \frac{2}{?}
> $$
> which again fails.
>
> So use:
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 2\cdot 5\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 2\cdot 5
> $$
> and combine the $2$'s:
> $$
> 2\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 8,\qquad 2
> $$
> giving
> $$
> 10{,}000 = 8\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5
> $$
> Now check:
> $$
> 8\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5
> =16\cdot 625
> =10{,}000
> $$
>
> Thus the digits can be $8,2,5,5,5,5$. To make the number as small as possible, arrange them in increasing order:
> $$
> 255558
> $$
>
> Check:
> $$
> 2\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 5\cdot 8 = 10{,}000
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest such number is
> $$
> \boxed{255558}
> $$
