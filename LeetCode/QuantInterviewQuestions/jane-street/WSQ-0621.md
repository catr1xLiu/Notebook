> [!example] Question 621 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

There are 10 lightbulbs in a row, each of which can be either on or off. No two adjacent lightbulbs may be on at the same time. How many possible combinations are there?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=622)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $a_n$ denote the number of valid on/off arrangements of $n$ lightbulbs in a row such that no two adjacent bulbs are on.
>
> We seek $a_{10}$.
>
> ## Recurrence relation
>
> Consider the last bulb.
>
> - If the last bulb is **off**, then the first $n-1$ bulbs can be any valid arrangement. This gives $a_{n-1}$ possibilities.
> - If the last bulb is **on**, then the $(n-1)$st bulb must be **off**. So the first $n-2$ bulbs can be any valid arrangement. This gives $a_{n-2}$ possibilities.
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2}.
> $$
>
> This is the Fibonacci recurrence.
>
> ## Initial values
>
> - For $n=1$, the valid arrangements are:
>   $$
>   0,\ 1
>   $$
>   so
>   $$
>   a_1 = 2.
>   $$
>
> - For $n=2$, the valid arrangements are:
>   $$
>   00,\ 01,\ 10
>   $$
>   so
>   $$
>   a_2 = 3.
>   $$
>
> ## Compute up to $n=10$
>
> Using
> $$
> a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2},
> $$
> we get:
>
> $$
> a_3 = 3+2 = 5
> $$
>
> $$
> a_4 = 5+3 = 8
> $$
>
> $$
> a_5 = 8+5 = 13
> $$
>
> $$
> a_6 = 13+8 = 21
> $$
>
> $$
> a_7 = 21+13 = 34
> $$
>
> $$
> a_8 = 34+21 = 55
> $$
>
> $$
> a_9 = 55+34 = 89
> $$
>
> $$
> a_{10} = 89+55 = 144
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{144}
> $$
