> [!example] Question 422 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

What is the expected value of the maximum of two fair six-sided dice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=423)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be the outcomes of two fair six-sided dice, and let
>
> $$
> M = \max(X,Y).
> $$
>
> We seek $E[M]$.
>
> A convenient method is to use the distribution of $M$. For each $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$,
>
> $$
> P(M \le k) = P(X \le k \text{ and } Y \le k) = \left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> P(M = k) = P(M \le k) - P(M \le k-1)
> = \left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{k-1}{6}\right)^2
> = \frac{k^2-(k-1)^2}{36}
> = \frac{2k-1}{36}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[M] = \sum_{k=1}^6 k\,P(M=k)
> = \sum_{k=1}^6 k \cdot \frac{2k-1}{36}.
> $$
>
> Compute the sum:
>
> $$
> E[M] = \frac{1}{36}\sum_{k=1}^6 (2k^2-k)
> = \frac{1}{36}\left(2\sum_{k=1}^6 k^2 - \sum_{k=1}^6 k\right).
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^6 k = 21,
> \qquad
> \sum_{k=1}^6 k^2 = 91,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[M] = \frac{1}{36}(2\cdot 91 - 21)
> = \frac{1}{36}(182 - 21)
> = \frac{161}{36}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected value of the maximum is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{161}{36} \approx 4.4722}.
> $$
