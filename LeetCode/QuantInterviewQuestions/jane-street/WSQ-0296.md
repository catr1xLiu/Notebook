> [!example] Question 296 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Interview

If there is a 0.3 probability it will rain on Saturday and a 0.4 probability it will rain on Sunday, what is the range of possible probabilities that it will rain on at least one day?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=297)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $B$ be the event that it rains on Sunday. We are given
>
> $$
> P(A)=0.3,\qquad P(B)=0.4.
> $$
>
> We want the possible values of
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B),
> $$
>
> the probability that it rains on at least one of the two days.
>
> Using the addition rule,
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)=0.3+0.4-P(A\cap B)=0.7-P(A\cap B).
> $$
>
> So the range depends on the possible values of $P(A\cap B)$).
>
> The intersection must satisfy
>
> $$
> 0 \le P(A\cap B) \le \min(0.3,0.4)=0.3.
> $$
>
> - The **largest** possible value of $P(A\cup B)$ occurs when $P(A\cap B)=0$:
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=0.7.
> $$
>
> - The **smallest** possible value of $P(A\cup B)$ occurs when $P(A\cap B)=0.3$:
>
> $$
> P(A\cup B)=0.7-0.3=0.4.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the possible range is
>
> $$
> 0.4 \le P(\text{rain on at least one day}) \le 0.7.
> $$
>
> So the probability can be any value from **$0.4$ to $0.7$**.
