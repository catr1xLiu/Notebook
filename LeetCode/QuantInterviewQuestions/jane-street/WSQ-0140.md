> [!example] Question 140 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

If it rains today, will that affect the probability that it rains tomorrow? Explain your reasoning using probability theory.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=141)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Whether rain today affects the probability of rain tomorrow depends on the probabilistic model being assumed.
>
> ### Independent days
> If rainfall on different days is modeled as **independent**, then rain today has no effect on tomorrow. In that case,
> $$
> P(\text{rain tomorrow} \mid \text{rain today}) = P(\text{rain tomorrow}).
> $$
>
> ### Dependent days
> In many realistic weather models, rainfall on consecutive days is **not** independent. Weather systems often persist, so knowing that it rains today can change the chance of rain tomorrow. Then,
> $$
> P(\text{rain tomorrow} \mid \text{rain today}) \neq P(\text{rain tomorrow}).
> $$
>
> For example, if a storm system tends to last several days, one might have
> $$
> P(\text{rain tomorrow} \mid \text{rain today}) > P(\text{rain tomorrow}).
> $$
>
> ### Conclusion
> So the answer is: **not necessarily**. If the days are independent, rain today does not affect tomorrow’s probability; if the weather has dependence over time, it does.
