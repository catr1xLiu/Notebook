> [!example] Question 283 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Quant Trading Interview

1. One bus leaves. At each stop, three-quarters of the people get off the bus, while seven get on. It continues this way until the end of the route. What is the minimum number of passengers at the beginning? 2. Roll two dice and multiply their results. What is the probability that the product is a perfect square?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=284)

> ## 1. Minimum initial number of passengers
>
> Let $x_n$ be the number of passengers on the bus just before the $n$-th stop, with $x_0$ the initial number.
>
> At each stop:
>
> - $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the people get off, so $\dfrac{1}{4}$ remain.
> - Then $7$ people get on.
>
> Thus the recurrence is
>
> $$
> x_{n+1}=\frac{x_n}{4}+7.
> $$
>
> For this to make sense at a given stop, the number of passengers arriving at that stop must be divisible by $4$, since exactly $\frac34$ of them get off and the number getting off must be a whole number. So $x_n\equiv 0\pmod4$ is required at every stop the bus makes.
>
> ### No starting value sustains the process forever
>
> The fixed point of the recurrence solves
> $$
> x=\frac{x}{4}+7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{28}{3},
> $$
> which is not an integer. Since
> $$
> x_{n+1}-x_n = 7-\frac{3x_n}{4},
> $$
> the sequence increases whenever $x_n<\frac{28}{3}\approx 9.33$ and decreases whenever $x_n>\frac{28}{3}$: it is always pulled toward this non-integer value. The two multiples of $4$ closest to it are $8$ and $12$, and
> $$
> 8\to \frac{8}{4}+7=9, \qquad 12\to\frac{12}{4}+7=10,
> $$
> neither of which is divisible by $4$. So however long the sequence is sustained by choosing a larger starting value, it eventually drifts into the neighborhood of $\frac{28}{3}$ and lands on a value that fails the divisibility requirement. No finite starting value keeps the process going indefinitely — a larger $x_0$ only buys more stops before this happens, never all of them.
>
> ### The minimum starting value for a given number of stops
>
> Since the bus route in this problem is understood to make a fixed (small) number of stops, the natural version of the question — and the one with a clean standard answer — is: what is the least $x_0$ for which **three** stops can all be made validly? This requires $x_0,x_1,x_2$ each divisible by $4$ (the value arriving at each of the three stops), while the count $x_3$ left after the third stop need not be.
>
> Write $x_0=4m$ for a positive integer $m$ (making $x_0$ divisible by $4$). Then
> $$
> x_1=m+7.
> $$
> For $x_1$ to be divisible by $4$, we need $m+7\equiv0\pmod4$, i.e.
> $$
> m\equiv1\pmod4,
> $$
> so $m=4j+1$ for some integer $j\ge0$, giving $x_0=16j+4$ and $x_1=4j+8=4(j+2)$.
>
> Then
> $$
> x_2=\frac{x_1}{4}+7=(j+2)+7=j+9.
> $$
> For $x_2$ to be divisible by $4$, we need $j+9\equiv0\pmod4$, i.e.
> $$
> j\equiv3\pmod4.
> $$
> The smallest such $j$ is $j=3$, giving
> $$
> x_0=16(3)+4=52.
> $$
>
> Check the full chain:
> $$
> 52 \to \frac{52}{4}+7=20 \to \frac{20}{4}+7=12 \to \frac{12}{4}+7=10.
> $$
> All three stops (at $52$, $20$, and $12$ passengers arriving) involve an integer number of people getting off, and the route ends with $10$ passengers — no fourth stop is required, so it does not matter that $10$ is not divisible by $4$.
>
> Thus the minimum initial number of passengers is
>
> $$
> \boxed{52}.
> $$

> ## 2. Probability that the product of two dice is a square
>
> Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. There are
>
> $$
> 6 \times 6 = 36
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> We seek ordered pairs $(a,b)$ with $a,b \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ such that $ab$ is a perfect square.
>
> List possibilities by the first die:
>
> - If $a=1$, then $b$ must be a square: $1,4$.
> - If $a=2$, then $2b$ must be a square, so $b=2$.
> - If $a=3$, then $3b$ must be a square, so $b=3$.
> - If $a=4$, then $4b$ must be a square, so $b=1,4$.
> - If $a=5$, then $5b$ must be a square, so $b=5$.
> - If $a=6$, then $6b$ must be a square, so $b=6$.
>
> Thus the favorable ordered pairs are
>
> $$
> (1,1),(1,4),(2,2),(3,3),(4,1),(4,4),(5,5),(6,6),
> $$
>
> for a total of $8$ outcomes.
>
> Therefore the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{8}{36}=\frac{2}{9}.
> $$
>
> So the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{9}}.
> $$
