> [!example] Question 237 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

Given that the probability it rains on Sunday is 40% and the probability it rains on the weekend (Saturday or Sunday) is 60%, what is the probability it rains on Saturday?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=238)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $S$ be the event that it rains on Saturday, and let $U$ be the event that it rains on Sunday.
>
> We are given
>
> $$
> P(U) = 0.40
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U) = 0.60
> $$
>
> We use the addition rule:
>
> $$
> P(S \cup U) = P(S) + P(U) - P(S \cap U)
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 0.60 = P(S) + 0.40 - P(S \cap U)
> $$
>
> which gives
>
> $$
> P(S) = 0.20 + P(S \cap U)
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability it rains on Saturday cannot be determined exactly unless more information is given about $P(S \cap U)$, the probability that it rains on both days.
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 0 \le P(S \cap U) \le P(U) = 0.40,
> $$
>
> it follows that
>
> $$
> 0.20 \le P(S) \le 0.60.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability it rains on Saturday is **not uniquely determined** from the given information alone. It could be any value between
>
> $$
> 20\% \text{ and } 60\%.
> $$
