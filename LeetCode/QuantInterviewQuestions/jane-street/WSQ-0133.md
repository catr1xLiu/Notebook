> [!example] Question 133 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

You and a friend are playing a coin tossing game. You toss a fair coin repeatedly and track the results. Each of you has a sequence you are watching for: your sequence is HTT and your friend's sequence is HHT. The player whose sequence appears first wins the game. Would you want to play? What is your probability of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=134)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A=\text{HTT}$ be your sequence and $B=\text{HHT}$ be your friend’s sequence. We seek the probability that $A$ appears before $B$ in an infinite sequence of fair coin tosses.
>
> ## Approach
>
> Track the game by the longest suffix of the toss history that is also a prefix of either target sequence. The relevant states are:
>
> - $S_0$: no useful partial match
> - $H$: the current suffix is $H$
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $HH$
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $HT$
>
> Let $p_0, p_H, p_{HH}, p_{HT}$ denote the probability that $A=\text{HTT}$ appears before $B=\text{HHT}$ starting from each state.
>
> Our goal is $p_0$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the equations
>
> From state $S_0$:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to state $H$
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, staying in $S_0$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> p_0=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_0.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $HH$
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $HT$
>
> So,
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Toss $T$ gives $HHT$, so your friend wins immediately
> - Toss $H$ leaves the suffix $HH$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HH},
> $$
> which implies
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - Toss $T$ gives $HTT$, so you win immediately
> - Toss $H$ makes the new suffix $H$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> p_0=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_0,
> $$
> we get
> $$
> p_0=p_H.
> $$
>
> Since $p_{HH}=0$, the equation for $p_H$ becomes
> $$
> p_H=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_{HT}=\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> And
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> Substitute into $p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}$:
> $$
> p_H=\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p_H\right)
> =\frac14+\frac14 p_H.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> p_H-\frac14 p_H=\frac14,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \frac34 p_H=\frac14,
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> p_H=\frac13.
> $$
>
> Since $p_0=p_H$,
> $$
> p_0=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Your probability of winning is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac13}.
> $$
>
> So you would **not** want to play if the game is fair, because your friend’s sequence $\text{HHT}$ appears first with probability
>
> $$
> 1-\frac13=\frac23.
> $$
>
> Thus your friend has the advantage.
