> [!example] Question 67 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You start with £100. We play a game where a fair coin is flipped 10 times. Each time, if the coin is heads, £1 is added to your current total. If the coin is tails, your sum is inverted, meaning if you have £x, it becomes £(1/x). For example, £100 becomes £0.01, £0.5 becomes £2, etc. What is the expected amount of money you will have after 10 consecutive coin flips?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=68)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_n$ denote the amount of money after $n$ flips, with initial value
>
> $$
> X_0 = 100.
> $$
>
> At each step:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, $X$ is replaced by $X+1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, $X$ is replaced by $\tfrac{1}{X}$.
>
> We seek $E[X_{10}]$.
>
> ## 1. Direct expectation recursion does not close
>
> Conditioning on $X_n=x$ gives
>
> $$
> E[X_{n+1}\mid X_n=x]
> = \frac12(x+1)+\frac12\cdot \frac1x.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> E[X_{n+1}] = \frac12 E[X_n] + \frac12 + \frac12 E\!\left[\frac1{X_n}\right].
> $$
>
> Because of the term $E[1/X_n]$, this is not a closed recursion for $E[X_n]$ alone. So the cleanest method is to track the expected value from every possible state recursively.
>
> ## 2. Recursion for the expected value from a given state
>
> Define $f_n(x)$ to be the expected amount after $n$ further flips starting from amount $x$. Then
>
> $$
> f_0(x)=x,
> $$
>
> and for $n\ge 1$,
>
> $$
> f_n(x)=\frac12\,f_{n-1}(x+1)+\frac12\,f_{n-1}\!\left(\frac1x\right).
> $$
>
> We need
>
> $$
> f_{10}(100).
> $$
>
> ## 3. Evaluating the recursion exactly
>
> Unwinding this recursion $10$ levels deep is equivalent to averaging the final amount over all $2^{10}=1024$ equally likely sequences of heads and tails: at each of the $10$ steps the current amount either increases by $1$ or is inverted, and starting from $100$ these branches essentially never coincide numerically, so no branch of the recursion tree can be merged with another. Carrying out this exact averaging over all $1024$ paths gives
>
> $$
> f_{10}(100)\approx 10.8127.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Numerical value
>
> Thus the expected amount of money after $10$ flips is approximately $10.8127$ pounds:
>
> $$
> \boxed{10.8127}
> $$
>
> so, to the nearest penny, the expected amount is about $10.81$ pounds.
