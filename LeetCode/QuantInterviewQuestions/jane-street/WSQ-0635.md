> [!example] Question 635 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

I drop 4 coins on the table. Given that one is heads, what is the probability that the rest are tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=636)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the four coin outcomes be equally likely, with each of the $2^4=16$ outcomes having probability $\frac{1}{16}$.
>
> We are told that **at least one** of the four coins is heads. Thus the sample space is reduced to all outcomes except $TTTT$. So there are
>
> $$
> 16-1=15
> $$
>
> possible outcomes consistent with the given information.
>
> We want the probability that **the rest are tails**, meaning there is exactly one head among the four coins. The favorable outcomes are
>
> $$
> HTTT,\quad THTT,\quad TTHT,\quad TTTH
> $$
>
> so there are $4$ such outcomes.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{exactly one head} \mid \text{at least one head})=\frac{4}{15}.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{15}}.
> $$
