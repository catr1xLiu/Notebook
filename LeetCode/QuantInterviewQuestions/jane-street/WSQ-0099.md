> [!example] Question 99 · Jane Street
> ## Trading Interview

You begin with \$100. You flip a fair coin. For each heads, you gain \$1. For each tails, your current amount of money is inverted (i.e., after the first tails, if you previously had \$x, your new amount is \$1/x). What is the expected value of your money after 7 flips? (Hint: use recursion)

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=100)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_n$ denote the amount of money after $n$ flips, with initial value
>
> $$
> X_0 = 100.
> $$
>
> On each flip:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the amount increases by $1$, so $x \mapsto x+1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac12$, the amount is inverted, so $x \mapsto \tfrac{1}{x}$.
>
> We seek $E[X_7]$.
>
> ## Recursive expectation formula
>
> Define
>
> $$
> f_n(x) = E[X_n \mid X_0 = x].
> $$
>
> Then $f_0(x)=x$, and for $n \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> f_n(x) = \frac12 f_{n-1}(x+1) + \frac12 f_{n-1}\!\left(\frac1x\right).
> $$
>
> Each application of this recursion doubles the number of arguments that must be tracked, since $x+1$ and $1/x$ are generally distinct and each spawns its own pair of sub-cases at the next step. Carrying the recursion out to $n=7$ therefore amounts to averaging $X_7$ over all $2^7=128$ possible sequences of heads and tails, starting at $x=100$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1
>
> $$
> f_1(x)=\frac12(x+1)+\frac12\cdot \frac1x.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> f_1(100)=\frac12(101)+\frac12\left(\frac1{100}\right)=50.505.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2
>
> Using $f_1(y)=\tfrac12(y+1)+\tfrac12/y$,
>
> $$
> f_2(100)=\frac12 f_1(101)+\frac12 f_1\!\left(\frac1{100}\right).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> f_1(101)=\frac12(102)+\frac12\left(\frac1{101}\right)
> =51+\frac1{202},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> f_1\!\left(\frac1{100}\right)
> =\frac12\left(1+\frac1{100}\right)+\frac12(100)
> =50.505.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> f_2(100)=\frac12\left(51+\frac1{202}\right)+\frac12(50.505)
> \approx 50.754975.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Steps 3 through 7
>
> From here the recursion branches too quickly to expand symbolically by hand at every stage: $f_3(100)$ already depends on $4$ arguments, $f_4(100)$ on $8$, and so on, up to the $128$ arguments feeding into $f_7(100)$. The recursion
>
> $$
> f_n(x)=\frac12 f_{n-1}(x+1)+\frac12 f_{n-1}\!\left(\frac1x\right),
> \qquad f_0(x)=x,
> $$
>
> is nonetheless completely explicit, and is most reliably evaluated by carrying it through mechanically (equivalently, by enumerating all $128$ head/tail sequences over $7$ flips starting from $x=100$ and averaging the resulting values of $X_7$). Doing so gives, at $x=100$,
>
> $$
> f_1(100)\approx 50.505,\quad
> f_2(100)\approx 50.755,\quad
> f_3(100)\approx 38.629,
> $$
>
> $$
> f_4(100)\approx 32.597,\quad
> f_5(100)\approx 26.560,\quad
> f_6(100)\approx 22.033,\quad
> f_7(100)\approx 18.2608.
> $$
>
> The sequence decreases markedly after the second flip: since inverting any of the moderately large values that arise here produces a number close to $0$, and the chance of at least one tails occurring somewhere in $7$ flips is
> $$
> 1-\left(\frac12\right)^7=\frac{127}{128},
> $$
> almost every sequence includes at least one inversion, which pulls the resulting average down well below the starting value of $100$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Therefore, the expected value after $7$ flips is
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[X_7] \approx 18.2608}.
> $$
