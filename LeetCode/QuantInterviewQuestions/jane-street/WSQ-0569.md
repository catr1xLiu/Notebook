> [!example] Question 569 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have a die and are told that when you roll it, you receive the amount rolled. What is the expected value? What if you have the option to roll again and choose the higher result, what is the expected value? What if you have the option to roll a third time and choose the highest roll, what is the expected value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=570)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Assume the die is fair, with outcomes $1,2,3,4,5,6$, each occurring with probability $\frac{1}{6}$.
>
> ### 1. One roll only
>
> If you must accept the result of a single roll, the expected value is
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Option to roll again once
>
> Now suppose after seeing the first roll, you may either keep it or discard it and roll a second time, but then you must accept the second roll.
>
> Let $V_1 = 3.5$ be the value of having one roll left.
>
> On the first roll, you should keep the result if it is at least as good as the expected value of rerolling, namely $3.5$. Since outcomes are integers, this means:
>
> - keep $4,5,6$,
> - reroll on $1,2,3$.
>
> Thus the expected value with up to two rolls is
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> V_2 = \frac{10.5 + 15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. Option to roll a third time
>
> Now suppose you may roll up to three times total, choosing after each of the first two rolls whether to keep the result or continue.
>
> Let $V_2 = 4.25$ be the value of having two rolls remaining.
>
> On the first roll, you compare the result to $4.25$:
>
> - keep $5,6$,
> - reroll on $1,2,3,4$.
>
> Therefore the expected value with up to three rolls is
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{1}{6}(4.25+4.25+4.25+4.25+5+6).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> V_3 = \frac{17 + 11}{6} = \frac{28}{6} = \frac{14}{3}.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answers
>
> - One roll: $$\boxed{3.5}$$
> - Up to two rolls: $$\boxed{4.25}$$
> - Up to three rolls: $$\boxed{\frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667}$$
