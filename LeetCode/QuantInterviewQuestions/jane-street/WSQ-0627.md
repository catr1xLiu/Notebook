> [!example] Question 627 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

How would you simulate the outcome of a six-sided die using only a fair coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=628)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To simulate a fair $6$-sided die using a fair coin, use enough coin flips to generate at least $6$ equally likely outcomes.
>
> ## Method
>
> Flip the coin $3$ times. Since each flip has $2$ outcomes, $3$ flips produce
>
> $$
> 2^3 = 8
> $$
>
> equally likely sequences:
>
> $$
> HHH,\ HHT,\ HTH,\ HTT,\ THH,\ THT,\ TTH,\ TTT.
> $$
>
> Assign the first $6$ sequences to the die faces:
>
> - $HHH \to 1$
> - $HHT \to 2$
> - $HTH \to 3$
> - $HTT \to 4$
> - $THH \to 5$
> - $THT \to 6$
>
> The remaining two sequences,
>
> $$
> TTH,\ TTT,
> $$
>
> are rejected. If one of these occurs, ignore it and flip $3$ coins again.
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Each sequence of $3$ flips has probability
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8}.
> $$
>
> Conditioned on obtaining one of the accepted $6$ sequences, each die face has probability
>
> $$
> \frac{1/8}{6/8} = \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Hence the procedure produces a uniform random variable on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.
>
> ## Final procedure
>
> 1. Flip a fair coin $3$ times.
> 2. If the result is one of the six assigned patterns, output the corresponding number.
> 3. If the result is $TTH$ or $TTT$, repeat from step $1$.
>
> This simulates a fair $6$-sided die.
