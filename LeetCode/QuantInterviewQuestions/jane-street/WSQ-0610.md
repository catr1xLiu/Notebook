> [!example] Question 610 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Flip a fair coin repeatedly until either the pattern HHT or HTT appears. What is the probability that HHT appears before HTT? Please elaborate your reasoning.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=611)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> We stop the first time either the pattern $HHT$ or the pattern $HTT$ appears as a consecutive block in the sequence of fair-coin flips. We want
> $$
> \Pr(\text{$HHT$ occurs before $HTT$}).
> $$
>
> The natural method is to track the relevant recent history of flips, since only the last few flips matter for whether one of the target patterns is about to occur.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Let $p$ be the desired probability starting from no relevant previous flips.
>
> We introduce states according to the longest suffix of the current sequence that is also a prefix of one of the target patterns.
>
> The target patterns are:
>
> - $HHT$
> - $HTT$
>
> Their relevant prefixes are:
> $$
> \varnothing,\quad H,\quad HH,\quad HT.
> $$
>
> So define:
>
> - $p$: probability of eventually getting $HHT$ before $HTT$ starting from scratch.
> - $p_H$: probability starting from suffix $H$.
> - $p_{HH}$: probability starting from suffix $HH$.
> - $p_{HT}$: probability starting from suffix $HT$.
>
> There are also two absorbing outcomes:
>
> - if $HHT$ appears, the probability is $1$;
> - if $HTT$ appears, the probability is $0$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Write recursive equations
>
> ### From the initial state
>
> On the first flip:
>
> - with probability $\frac12$, we get $H$, so move to state $H$;
> - with probability $\frac12$, we get $T$, which gives no useful prefix, so we are back to the initial state.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### From state $H$
>
> From suffix $H$:
>
> - flip $H$ with probability $\frac12$, giving suffix $HH$;
> - flip $T$ with probability $\frac12$. The sequence ending in $HT$ gives suffix $HT$.
>
> So
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### From state $HH$
>
> From suffix $HH$:
>
> - flip $T$ with probability $\frac12$, and then $HHT$ occurs immediately, so success;
> - flip $H$ with probability $\frac12$, and the ending is still effectively $HH$ (the last two flips are $HH$).
>
> Hence
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> Solving,
> $$
> p_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### From state $HT$
>
> From suffix $HT$:
>
> - flip $T$ with probability $\frac12$, and then $HTT$ occurs immediately, so failure;
> - flip $H$ with probability $\frac12$, and the new ending is $TH$, whose relevant suffix is just $H$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_H=\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> We already have
> $$
> p_{HH}=1,
> \qquad
> p_{HT}=\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> Substitute into the equation for $p_H$:
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}
> =\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\left(\frac12 p_H\right).
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> p_H=\frac12+\frac14 p_H.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> \frac34 p_H=\frac12,
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> p_H=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Now use the initial-state equation:
> $$
> p=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> \frac12 p=\frac12 p_H,
> $$
> which gives
> $$
> p=p_H=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The probability that $HHT$ occurs before $HTT$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}.
> $$
>
> So $HHT$ is more likely to appear first, with probability $\frac{2}{3}$.
