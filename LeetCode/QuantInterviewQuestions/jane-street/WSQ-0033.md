> [!example] Question 33 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You play a game with a 64-sided die. On your turn, you may either take the value shown on the die in dollars, or pay \$1 to roll again. What is the expected value of this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=34)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $V$ denote the expected value of the game when a roll is available.
>
> On any roll, after seeing the outcome $x \in \{1,2,\dots,64\}$, there are two choices:
>
> - stop and take $x$ dollars;
> - pay $1$ dollar and restart, whose expected value is then $V-1$.
>
> Thus the optimal strategy is to stop when
> $$
> x \ge V-1,
> $$
> and reroll when
> $$
> x < V-1.
> $$
>
> So $V$ must satisfy the optimality equation
> $$
> V=\frac1{64}\sum_{x=1}^{64}\max(x,V-1).
> $$
>
> Suppose the optimal cutoff is the integer $k$, meaning one rerolls on $1,2,\dots,k-1$ and keeps $k,k+1,\dots,64$. Every reroll costs one dollar and then leads into a fresh game worth $V$ in expectation, so a reroll outcome contributes $V-1$ to the equation, not $V$. Accounting for this cost, the optimality equation becomes
>
> $$
> V=\frac1{64}\left((k-1)(V-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{64}x\right).
> $$
>
> Multiply by $64$:
>
> $$
> 64V=(k-1)(V-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{64}x.
> $$
>
> Expand and collect the $V$ terms on the left:
>
> $$
> 64V-(k-1)V=-(k-1)+\sum_{x=k}^{64}x,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> (65-k)V=\sum_{x=k}^{64}x-(k-1).
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \sum_{x=k}^{64}x=\frac{(k+64)(65-k)}{2},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> (65-k)V=\frac{(k+64)(65-k)}{2}-(k-1),
> $$
>
> hence, dividing through by $65-k$,
>
> $$
> V(k)=\frac{k+64}{2}-\frac{k-1}{65-k}.
> $$
>
> ## Choosing the optimal cutoff
>
> This formula gives the value of the "reroll below $k$, keep at or above $k$" strategy for each integer cutoff $k$. We choose $k$ to maximize $V(k)$.
>
> Evaluating near the middle of the range:
>
> $$
> V(53)=\frac{117}{2}-\frac{52}{12}=\frac{325}{6}\approx 54.1667,
> $$
>
> $$
> V(54)=\frac{118}{2}-\frac{53}{11}=\frac{596}{11}\approx 54.1818,
> $$
>
> $$
> V(55)=\frac{119}{2}-\frac{54}{10}=\frac{541}{10}=54.1.
> $$
>
> Since $V(54)$ exceeds both of its neighbors, it is the maximum over all integer cutoffs: the optimal cutoff is $k=54$.
>
> As a consistency check: the rule keeps $x$ whenever $x\ge V-1$. With $V=\frac{596}{11}\approx 54.18$, we have $V-1\approx 53.18$, so the rule keeps exactly $x=54,55,\dots,64$ and rerolls $x=1,\dots,53$ — precisely the cutoff $k=54$ assumed above. The solution is self-consistent.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected value of the game is
>
> $$
> V=\frac{596}{11}\approx 54.18.
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{\dfrac{596}{11}\approx 54.18}
> $$
>
> The corresponding optimal strategy is to keep any roll of $54$ or higher, and pay one dollar to reroll anything from $1$ through $53$.
