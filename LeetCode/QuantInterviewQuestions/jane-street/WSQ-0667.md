> [!example] Question 667 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting exactly 4 heads in 9 coin tosses?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=668)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the coin is fair, so each toss has probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of landing heads.
>
> The number of heads in $9$ tosses follows a binomial distribution with parameters $n=9$ and $p=\tfrac{1}{2}$. Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X=4)=\binom{9}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^5
> $$
>
> Combine the powers of $\tfrac{1}{2}$:
>
> $$
> P(X=4)=\binom{9}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^9
> $$
>
> Now compute the binomial coefficient:
>
> $$
> \binom{9}{4}=\frac{9!}{4!5!}=126
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> P(X=4)=126\cdot \frac{1}{512}=\frac{126}{512}=\frac{63}{256}
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of getting exactly $4$ heads in $9$ tosses is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{63}{256}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.2461}
> $$
