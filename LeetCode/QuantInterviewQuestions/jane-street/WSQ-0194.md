> [!example] Question 194 · Jane Street
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

What is the smallest integer whose digits multiply to 108?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=195)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To make the integer as small as possible, its digits should:
>
> 1. multiply to $108$, and  
> 2. be arranged in increasing order.
>
> So first factor $108$ into single-digit factors in a way that uses as few digits as possible.
>
> $$
> 108 = 2^2 \cdot 3^3
> $$
>
> Combine these into largest possible single-digit factors:
>
> $$
> 108 = 9 \cdot 6 \cdot 2
> $$
>
> since
>
> $$
> 9 = 3^2, \qquad 6 = 2\cdot 3
> $$
>
> Thus the digits can be $2, 6, 9$, whose product is
>
> $$
> 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 9 = 108.
> $$
>
> To get the smallest integer, arrange them in ascending order:
>
> $$
> 269
> $$
>
> Therefore, the smallest integer whose digits multiply to $108$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{269}
> $$
