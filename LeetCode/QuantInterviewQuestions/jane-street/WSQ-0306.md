> [!example] Question 306 · Jane Street
> ## Analyst Interview

What is the probability of drawing two red cards in succession from a standard deck of 52 cards and from a double deck of 104 cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=307)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability of drawing $2$ red cards, interpret the experiment as drawing $2$ cards without replacement from the deck.
>
> A standard $52$-card deck has $26$ red cards and $26$ black cards.
>
> A $104$-card deck (two standard decks combined) has $52$ red cards and $52$ black cards.
>
> ## 1. For a $52$-card deck
>
> The probability that the first card is red is
>
> $$
> \frac{26}{52}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Given that the first card is red, there are now $25$ red cards left out of $51$ total cards, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{second red} \mid \text{first red})=\frac{25}{51}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{2 red cards})=\frac{26}{52}\cdot\frac{25}{51}
> =\frac{25}{102}.
> $$
>
> ## 2. For a $104$-card deck
>
> Now there are $52$ red cards out of $104$ total cards.
>
> The probability that the first card is red is
>
> $$
> \frac{52}{104}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Given that the first card is red, there are $51$ red cards left out of $103$ cards, so
>
> $$
> P(\text{second red} \mid \text{first red})=\frac{51}{103}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{2 red cards})=\frac{52}{104}\cdot\frac{51}{103}
> =\frac{51}{206}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answers
>
> - For a $52$-card deck:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{25}{102}}
> $$
>
> - For a $104$-card deck:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{51}{206}}
> $$
