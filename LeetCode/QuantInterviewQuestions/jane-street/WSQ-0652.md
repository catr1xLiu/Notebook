> [!example] Question 652 · Jane Street
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

How many throws of a fair 6-sided die are expected until each number appears at least twice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=653)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $T$ be the number of throws needed until each face of a fair $6$-sided die has appeared at least twice. We seek $E[T]$.
>
> ## Approach
>
> A convenient way to compute the expectation is to track the process by states. Let a state be $(a,b)$ where:
>
> - $a$ = number of faces not yet seen at all,
> - $b$ = number of faces seen exactly once,
> - the remaining $6-a-b$ faces have been seen at least twice.
>
> We start at $(6,0)$, and we stop when we reach $(0,0)$.
>
> Let $E(a,b)$ denote the expected additional number of throws needed from state $(a,b)$.
>
> ## Recurrence
>
> From state $(a,b)$:
>
> - with probability $\dfrac{a}{6}$, a new face appears, so the state becomes $(a-1,b+1)$;
> - with probability $\dfrac{b}{6}$, a face seen once appears again, so the state becomes $(a,b-1)$;
> - with probability $\dfrac{6-a-b}{6}$, a face already seen at least twice appears, so the state stays $(a,b)$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E(a,b)=1+\frac{a}{6}E(a-1,b+1)+\frac{b}{6}E(a,b-1)+\frac{6-a-b}{6}E(a,b).
> $$
>
> Rearranging,
> $$
> (a+b)E(a,b)=6+aE(a-1,b+1)+bE(a,b-1),
> $$
> so
> $$
> E(a,b)=\frac{6+aE(a-1,b+1)+bE(a,b-1)}{a+b}.
> $$
>
> The boundary condition is $E(0,0)=0$.
>
> Notice that $E(a-1,b+1)$ has the same total $a+b$ as $E(a,b)$ itself, so all states sharing a given total $n=a+b$ must be resolved together, in order of increasing $a$, before moving on to the next total.
>
> ## Compute the values
>
> Work upward, level by level, by the total $n=a+b$.
>
> ### Level $n=1$
>
> $$
> E(0,1)=\frac{6+1\cdot E(0,0)}{1}=6,
> $$
> $$
> E(1,0)=\frac{6+1\cdot E(0,1)}{1}=6+6=12.
> $$
>
> ### Level $n=2$
>
> $$
> E(0,2)=\frac{6+2E(0,1)}{2}=\frac{6+12}{2}=9,
> $$
> $$
> E(1,1)=\frac{6+1\cdot E(0,2)+1\cdot E(1,0)}{2}=\frac{6+9+12}{2}=\frac{27}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E(2,0)=\frac{6+2E(1,1)}{2}=3+\frac{27}{2}=\frac{33}{2}.
> $$
>
> ### Level $n=3$
>
> $$
> E(0,3)=\frac{6+3E(0,2)}{3}=\frac{6+27}{3}=11,
> $$
> $$
> E(1,2)=\frac{6+1\cdot E(0,3)+2\cdot E(1,1)}{3}=\frac{6+11+27}{3}=\frac{44}{3},
> $$
> $$
> E(2,1)=\frac{6+2E(1,2)+1\cdot E(2,0)}{3}=\frac{6+\frac{88}{3}+\frac{33}{2}}{3}=\frac{311}{18},
> $$
> $$
> E(3,0)=\frac{6+3E(2,1)}{3}=2+\frac{311}{18}=\frac{347}{18}.
> $$
>
> ### Level $n=4$
>
> $$
> E(0,4)=\frac{6+4E(0,3)}{4}=\frac{6+44}{4}=\frac{25}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E(1,3)=\frac{6+E(0,4)+3E(1,2)}{4}=\frac{6+\frac{25}{2}+44}{4}=\frac{125}{8},
> $$
> $$
> E(2,2)=\frac{6+2E(1,3)+2E(2,1)}{4}=\frac{6+\frac{125}{4}+\frac{311}{9}}{4}=\frac{2585}{144},
> $$
> $$
> E(3,1)=\frac{6+3E(2,2)+E(3,0)}{4}=\frac{6+\frac{2585}{48}+\frac{347}{18}}{4}=\frac{11395}{576},
> $$
> $$
> E(4,0)=\frac{6+4E(3,1)}{4}=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{11395}{576}=\frac{12259}{576}.
> $$
>
> ### Level $n=5$
>
> $$
> E(0,5)=\frac{6+5E(0,4)}{5}=\frac{6+\frac{125}{2}}{5}=\frac{137}{10},
> $$
> $$
> E(1,4)=\frac{6+E(0,5)+4E(1,3)}{5}=\frac{6+\frac{137}{10}+\frac{125}{2}}{5}=\frac{411}{25},
> $$
> $$
> E(2,3)=\frac{6+2E(1,4)+3E(2,2)}{5}=\frac{6+\frac{822}{25}+\frac{7755}{144}}{5}=\frac{111281}{6000},
> $$
> $$
> E(3,2)=\frac{6+3E(2,3)+2E(3,1)}{5}=\frac{6+\frac{333843}{6000}+\frac{11395}{288}}{5}=\frac{3643433}{180000},
> $$
> $$
> E(4,1)=\frac{6+4E(3,2)+E(4,0)}{5}=\frac{6+\frac{14573732}{180000}+\frac{12259}{576}}{5}=\frac{38969339}{1800000},
> $$
> $$
> E(5,0)=\frac{6+5E(4,1)}{5}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{38969339}{1800000}=\frac{41129339}{1800000}.
> $$
>
> ### Level $n=6$
>
> $$
> E(0,6)=\frac{6+6E(0,5)}{6}=1+\frac{137}{10}=\frac{147}{10},
> $$
> $$
> E(1,5)=\frac{6+E(0,6)+5E(1,4)}{6}=\frac{6+\frac{147}{10}+\frac{2055}{25}}{6}=\frac{343}{20},
> $$
> $$
> E(2,4)=\frac{6+2E(1,5)+4E(2,3)}{6}=\frac{6+\frac{343}{10}+\frac{445124}{6000}}{6}=\frac{171731}{9000},
> $$
> $$
> E(3,3)=\frac{6+3E(2,4)+3E(3,2)}{6}=\frac{6+\frac{515193}{9000}+\frac{10930299}{180000}}{6}=\frac{2479351}{120000},
> $$
> $$
> E(4,2)=\frac{6+4E(3,3)+2E(4,1)}{6}=\frac{6+\frac{9917404}{120000}+\frac{77938678}{1800000}}{6}=\frac{118749869}{5400000},
> $$
> $$
> E(5,1)=\frac{6+5E(4,2)+E(5,0)}{6}=\frac{6+\frac{593749345}{5400000}+\frac{41129339}{1800000}}{6}=\frac{374768681}{16200000},
> $$
> $$
> E(6,0)=\frac{6+6E(5,1)}{6}=1+E(5,1)=1+\frac{374768681}{16200000}=\frac{390968681}{16200000}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> E[T]=\frac{390968681}{16200000}\approx24.134.
> $$
>
> So you should expect to need about $24.13$ throws for every face to appear at least twice.
>
> $$
> \boxed{\dfrac{390968681}{16200000}\approx24.134}
> $$
