> [!example] Question 321 · Jane Street
> ## Trader Interview

Compute the expected value of the median when rolling a fair six-sided die three times.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=322)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1, X_2, X_3$ be the outcomes of three independent fair six-sided die rolls, each uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. We seek the expected value of the median of these three rolls.
>
> ## Step 1: Interpret the median
>
> For three numbers, the median is the middle value when the numbers are arranged in increasing order. Let $M$ denote this median.
>
> Then
> $$
> E[M] = \sum_{k=1}^6 k \, P(M = k).
> $$
>
> So the task reduces to finding $P(M=k)$ for each $k$.
>
> ## Step 2: Find $P(M \le k)$
>
> The median is at most $k$ exactly when at least two of the three rolls are at most $k$.
>
> For a single die roll,
> $$
> P(X_i \le k) = \frac{k}{6}.
> $$
>
> Thus, if $p=\frac{k}{6}$, then
> $$
> P(M \le k) = P(\text{at least two of the three rolls are } \le k).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial distribution,
> $$
> P(M \le k) = \binom{3}{2}p^2(1-p) + p^3 = 3p^2(1-p)+p^3 = 3p^2-2p^3.
> $$
>
> Substituting $p=\frac{k}{6}$,
> $$
> P(M \le k) = 3\left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^2 - 2\left(\frac{k}{6}\right)^3.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compute $P(M=k)$
>
> Use
> $$
> P(M=k)=P(M\le k)-P(M\le k-1).
> $$
>
> Rather than compute all six probabilities separately, it is more efficient to use the tail-sum formula for expectation of a positive integer-valued random variable:
> $$
> E[M] = \sum_{k=1}^6 P(M \ge k).
> $$
>
> Now $M \ge k$ means at least two of the three rolls are at least $k$.
>
> For a single roll,
> $$
> P(X_i \ge k)=\frac{7-k}{6}.
> $$
>
> Let $q=\frac{7-k}{6}$. Then
> $$
> P(M \ge k)=3q^2(1-q)+q^3=3q^2-2q^3.
> $$
>
> Compute these for $k=1,2,3,4,5,6$:
>
> - For $k=1$, $q=1$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge1)=1.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=2$, $q=\frac{5}{6}$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge2)=3\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2-2\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^3
>   =\frac{25}{36}\cdot 3-\frac{250}{216}
>   =\frac{200}{216}
>   =\frac{25}{27}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=3$, $q=\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3}$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge3)=3\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2-2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3
>   =\frac{12}{9}-\frac{16}{27}
>   =\frac{20}{27}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=4$, $q=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge4)=3\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2-2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3
>   =\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}
>   =\frac{1}{2}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=5$, $q=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge5)=3\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2-2\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3
>   =\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{27}
>   =\frac{7}{27}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=6$, $q=\frac{1}{6}$:
>   $$
>   P(M\ge6)=3\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^2-2\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^3
>   =\frac{3}{36}-\frac{2}{216}
>   =\frac{16}{216}
>   =\frac{2}{27}.
>   $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E[M]
> =1+\frac{25}{27}+\frac{20}{27}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{7}{27}+\frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> Combine:
> $$
> \frac{25+20+7+2}{27}=\frac{54}{27}=2,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E[M]=1+2+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{7}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> E[\text{median of three die rolls}] = \frac{7}{2} = 3.5.
> $$
