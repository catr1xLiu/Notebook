> [!example] Question 183 · Jane Street
> ## Intern Interview

If you flip ten coins, what is the expected value of the product of the number of heads and the number of tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=184)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $10$ fair coin flips. Then the number of tails is
>
> $$
> T = 10 - H.
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> E[HT] = E\bigl[H(10-H)\bigr] = 10E[H] - E[H^2].
> $$
>
> Since $H \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(10, \tfrac12)$,
>
> $$
> E[H] = 10 \cdot \tfrac12 = 5,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Var}(H) = 10 \cdot \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac{5}{2}.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> E[H^2] = \mathrm{Var}(H) + (E[H])^2,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> E[H^2] = \tfrac{5}{2} + 25 = \tfrac{55}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[HT] = 10 \cdot 5 - \tfrac{55}{2} = 50 - 27.5 = 22.5.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{22.5}.
> $$
>
> A reasonable range to report after quick mental estimation would be
>
> $$
> \boxed{22 \text{ to } 23}.
> $$
