> [!example] Question 644 · Jane Street
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

What is the expected value of the absolute difference between the outcomes of two 30-sided dice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=645)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be the outcomes of two independent fair $30$-sided dice, each uniformly distributed on $\{1,2,\dots,30\}$. We seek
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\,|X-Y|\,].
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Count pairs with a given difference
>
> For a fixed difference $d \in \{0,1,2,\dots,29\}$:
>
> - If $d=0$, then $X=Y$, which occurs in $30$ ordered pairs.
> - If $d \ge 1$, then $|X-Y|=d$ occurs when either $X-Y=d$ or $Y-X=d$.
>
> The number of pairs with $X-Y=d$ is $30-d$, since $Y$ can be any of $1,2,\dots,30-d$.
> Similarly, the number of pairs with $Y-X=d$ is also $30-d$.
>
> Thus, for $d \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \#\{(x,y): |x-y|=d\} = 2(30-d).
> $$
>
> Since there are $30^2=900$ total ordered pairs, we have
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\,|X-Y|\,]
> = \frac{1}{900}\sum_{d=1}^{29} d \cdot 2(30-d).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Evaluate the sum
>
> Compute
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\,|X-Y|\,]
> = \frac{2}{900}\sum_{d=1}^{29} d(30-d)
> = \frac{2}{900}\left(30\sum_{d=1}^{29} d - \sum_{d=1}^{29} d^2\right).
> $$
>
> Use the formulas
>
> $$
> \sum_{d=1}^{29} d = \frac{29\cdot 30}{2} = 435,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \sum_{d=1}^{29} d^2 = \frac{29\cdot 30\cdot 59}{6} = 8555.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 30\sum_{d=1}^{29} d - \sum_{d=1}^{29} d^2
> = 30\cdot 435 - 8555
> = 13050 - 8555
> = 4495.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\,|X-Y|\,]
> = \frac{2\cdot 4495}{900}
> = \frac{8990}{900}
> = \frac{899}{90}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\mathbb{E}[\,|X-Y|\,] = \frac{899}{90} \approx 9.99.}
> $$
