> [!example] Question 836 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the limit of x^x as x approaches 0?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=837)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider
> $$
> f(x)=x^x.
> $$
>
> To study the limit as $x \to 0$, rewrite:
> $$
> x^x = e^{x\ln x}.
> $$
>
> So the problem reduces to evaluating
> $$
> \lim_{x\to 0^+} x\ln x.
> $$
>
> Since $\ln x$ is defined only for $x>0$, the relevant limit is the **right-hand limit**. Compute:
> $$
> x\ln x=\frac{\ln x}{1/x}.
> $$
> Apply L'Hôpital's rule:
> $$
> \lim_{x\to 0^+}\frac{\ln x}{1/x}
> =
> \lim_{x\to 0^+}\frac{1/x}{-1/x^2}
> =
> \lim_{x\to 0^+}(-x)=0.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \lim_{x\to 0^+} x^x
> =
> \lim_{x\to 0^+} e^{x\ln x}
> =
> e^0
> =
> 1.
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> \boxed{\lim_{x\to 0^+} x^x = 1.}
> $$
>
> There is no two-sided real limit at $x=0$, because $x^x$ is not real-valued for all $x<0$.
