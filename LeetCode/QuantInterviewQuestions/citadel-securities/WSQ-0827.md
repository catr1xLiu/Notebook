> [!example] Question 827 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

If you toss a fair coin N times, what is the probability that you will get an even number of heads? Follow-up: Now, what if you have N - 1 fair coins, and the Nth coin is biased and lands on heads with probability p?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=828)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ denote the total number of heads.
>
> ## 1. $N$ fair coin tosses
>
> Each sequence of $N$ tosses is equally likely, with probability $2^{-N}$. The number of sequences with exactly $k$ heads is $\binom{N}{k}$. Hence
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\text{ even}}}^{N}\binom{N}{k}2^{-N}.
> $$
>
> To evaluate this sum, use the binomial identities
>
> $$
> (1+1)^N=\sum_{k=0}^N \binom{N}{k}=2^N
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> (1-1)^N=\sum_{k=0}^N \binom{N}{k}(-1)^k=0.
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> E=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\text{ even}}}^{N}\binom{N}{k}, 
> \qquad
> O=\sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\text{ odd}}}^{N}\binom{N}{k}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> E+O=2^N,
> \qquad
> E-O=0.
> $$
>
> So $E=O=2^{N-1}$. Therefore
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})=\frac{E}{2^N}=\frac{2^{N-1}}{2^N}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus, for $N\ge 1$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\Pr(H \text{ is even})=\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> (If $N=0$, then there are $0$ heads, which is even, so the probability is $1$.)
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. $N-1$ fair coins and one biased coin with $\Pr(\text{heads})=p$
>
> Let $X$ be the number of heads among the first $N-1$ fair coins, and let $Y$ be the indicator that the last coin is heads, so
>
> $$
> \Pr(Y=1)=p,\qquad \Pr(Y=0)=1-p.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> H=X+Y.
> $$
>
> For the first $N-1$ fair coins,
>
> $$
> \Pr(X \text{ is even})=\Pr(X \text{ is odd})=\frac{1}{2},
> $$
>
> provided $N-1\ge 1$, i.e. $N\ge 2$.
>
> Now $H$ is even in exactly two disjoint cases:
>
> - $X$ is even and $Y=0$,
> - $X$ is odd and $Y=1$.
>
> By independence,
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})
> =\Pr(X \text{ even})\Pr(Y=0)+\Pr(X \text{ odd})\Pr(Y=1).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})
> =\frac{1}{2}(1-p)+\frac{1}{2}p
> =\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> So for $N\ge 2$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\Pr(H \text{ is even})=\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> ### Edge case: $N=1$
>
> Then there are no fair coins, only the biased coin. The number of heads is even exactly when the biased coin lands tails, so
>
> $$
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})=1-p.
> $$
>
> Therefore the complete answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \Pr(H \text{ is even})=
> \begin{cases}
> \frac{1}{2}, & N\ge 2,\\[4pt]
> 1-p, & N=1.
> \end{cases}
> }
> $$
