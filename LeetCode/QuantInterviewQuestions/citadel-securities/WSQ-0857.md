> [!example] Question 857 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

If you find a coin on the street, what is the probability that it is a biased (unfair) coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=858)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The question cannot be answered numerically without additional assumptions.
>
> Let $F$ denote the event that the coin is unfair, and let $D$ denote the event that you found a coin on the street. The quantity asked for is
>
> $$
> P(F \mid D).
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(F \mid D)=\frac{P(D \mid F)\,P(F)}{P(D)}.
> $$
>
> Thus the answer depends on at least:
>
> 1. the prior probability $P(F)$ that a randomly encountered coin is unfair,
> 2. the probability $P(D \mid F)$ that an unfair coin ends up being found on the street,
> 3. the overall probability $P(D)$ of finding a coin on the street.
>
> Without a model for how many coins are unfair and whether unfair coins are more or less likely to be lost or discarded, $P(F \mid D)$ is indeterminate.
>
> If one assumes that finding a coin is independent of whether it is fair, then
>
> $$
> P(D \mid F)=P(D),
> $$
>
> and Bayes' theorem simplifies to
>
> $$
> P(F \mid D)=P(F).
> $$
>
> So under that assumption, the chance the found coin is unfair is simply the same as the fraction of all coins that are unfair.
>
> Therefore, the correct answer is:
>
> $$
> \text{The probability cannot be determined from the information given.}
> $$
