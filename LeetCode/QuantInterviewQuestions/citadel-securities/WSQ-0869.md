> [!example] Question 869 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Intern Interview

What is the expected value of the maximum among N normally distributed random variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=870)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1,\dots,X_N$ be independent and identically distributed normal random variables with
> $$
> X_i \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2).
> $$
> We seek
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right].
> $$
>
> ## 1. Reduce to the standard normal case
>
> Write
> $$
> X_i=\mu+\sigma Z_i,
> $$
> where $Z_1,\dots,Z_N$ are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. Then
> $$
> \max_{1\le i\le N} X_i
> =
> \mu+\sigma \max_{1\le i\le N} Z_i.
> $$
> Taking expectation,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> =
> \mu+\sigma\, \mathbb{E}[M_N],
> $$
> where
> $$
> M_N:=\max_{1\le i\le N} Z_i.
> $$
> Thus it suffices to compute $\mathbb{E}[M_N]$.
>
> ## 2. Distribution of the maximum
>
> Let $\Phi$ and $\phi$ denote the standard normal cdf and pdf:
> $$
> \Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x \phi(t)\,dt,
> \qquad
> \phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}.
> $$
> Since the $Z_i$ are independent,
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M_N\le x)=\mathbb{P}(Z_1\le x,\dots,Z_N\le x)=\Phi(x)^N.
> $$
> Therefore the density of $M_N$ is
> $$
> f_{M_N}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\Phi(x)^N
> =
> N\,\phi(x)\Phi(x)^{N-1}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Expectation formula
>
> Hence
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M_N]
> =
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\, f_{M_N}(x)\,dx
> =
> N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx.
> $$
> So the expectation of the maximum is
> $$
> \boxed{
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx
> }.
> $$
> Equivalently, using the quantile substitution $u=\Phi(x)$,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_0^1 \Phi^{-1}(u)\,u^{N-1}\,du.
> $$
>
> ## 4. No elementary closed form in general
>
> For general $N$, this integral does not simplify to an elementary closed form. It is usually evaluated numerically.
>
> ## 5. Useful approximation for large $N$
>
> For large $N$, extreme-value theory gives
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M_N]
> \approx
> \sqrt{2\log N}
> -\frac{\log\log N+\log(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\log N}},
> $$
> with a more refined approximation
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M_N]
> \approx
> a_N+\gamma b_N,
> $$
> where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant,
> $$
> a_N=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right),
> \qquad
> b_N=\frac{1}{N\,\phi(a_N)}.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> \approx
> \mu+\sigma\left(
> \sqrt{2\log N}
> -\frac{\log\log N+\log(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\log N}}
> \right).
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If $X_1,\dots,X_N$ are i.i.d. $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, then
> $$
> \boxed{
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> =
> \mu+\sigma\,\mathbb{E}[M_N]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx
> }
> $$
> where $\phi$ and $\Phi$ are the standard normal pdf and cdf. In general there is no elementary closed form, but for large $N$,
> $$
> \boxed{
> \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\le i\le N} X_i\right]
> \approx
> \mu+\sigma\left(
> \sqrt{2\log N}
> -\frac{\log\log N+\log(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\log N}}
> \right).
> }
> $$
