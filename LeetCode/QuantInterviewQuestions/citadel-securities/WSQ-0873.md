> [!example] Question 873 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Intern Interview

How do you implement a rolling mean and variance for a data stream in O(1) time per update?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=874)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Rolling mean and variance in $O(1)$ time
>
> To maintain the **mean** and **variance** over a **fixed-size sliding window** of length $W$ in a data stream, the key is to store only a constant amount of aggregate information and update it when one value enters the window and one value leaves it.
>
> ### 1. Quantities to maintain
>
> For the current window, keep:
>
> - the sum
>   $$
>   S = \sum_{i=1}^{W} x_i
>   $$
> - the sum of squares
>   $$
>   Q = \sum_{i=1}^{W} x_i^2
>   $$
>
> Also keep the last $W$ values in a circular buffer so that when a new value arrives, the oldest value can be removed in $O(1)$ time.
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Mean and variance from these aggregates
>
> The rolling mean is
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{S}{W}.
> $$
>
> The population variance is
>
> $$
> \sigma^2 = \frac{Q}{W} - \left(\frac{S}{W}\right)^2.
> $$
>
> If the sample variance is desired, use
>
> $$
> s^2 = \frac{Q - \frac{S^2}{W}}{W-1},
> $$
>
> provided that $W > 1$.
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. Update rule when a new value arrives
>
> Suppose the oldest value leaving the window is $x_{\text{old}}$, and the new incoming value is $x_{\text{new}}$.
>
> Then update:
>
> $$
> S \leftarrow S - x_{\text{old}} + x_{\text{new}},
> $$
>
> $$
> Q \leftarrow Q - x_{\text{old}}^2 + x_{\text{new}}^2.
> $$
>
> After this, compute
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{S}{W},
> \qquad
> \sigma^2 = \frac{Q}{W} - \mu^2.
> $$
>
> Each update uses only a fixed number of arithmetic operations, so the time per element is $O(1)$.
>
> ---
>
> ### 4. Initialization
>
> Before the window is full, let the current window size be $n \le W$.
>
> Maintain
>
> $$
> S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i,
> \qquad
> Q = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2.
> $$
>
> Then use
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{S}{n},
> $$
>
> $$
> \sigma^2 = \frac{Q}{n} - \left(\frac{S}{n}\right)^2
> $$
>
> for population variance, or
>
> $$
> s^2 = \frac{Q - \frac{S^2}{n}}{n-1}
> $$
>
> for sample variance when $n > 1$.
>
> Once $n = W$, switch to the sliding-window update above.
>
> ---
>
> ### 5. Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> initialize circular buffer of size W
> S = 0
> Q = 0
> n = 0
> idx = 0
>
> for each new value x:
>     if n < W:
>         buffer[idx] = x
>         S += x
>         Q += x*x
>         n += 1
>         idx = (idx + 1) mod W
>     else:
>         old = buffer[idx]
>         buffer[idx] = x
>         S += x - old
>         Q += x*x - old*old
>         idx = (idx + 1) mod W
>
>     mean = S / n          if n < W else S / W
>     var  = Q / n - mean^2 if n < W else Q / W - mean^2
> ```
>
> For sample variance, replace the variance formula accordingly.
>
> ---
>
> ### 6. Numerical stability
>
> The formula
>
> $$
> \sigma^2 = \frac{Q}{W} - \left(\frac{S}{W}\right)^2
> $$
>
> is efficient, but it can suffer from cancellation when the variance is very small compared with the magnitude of the data. In many practical cases it is acceptable; if very high numerical stability is required, more sophisticated sliding-window variants of Welford’s method may be used, though they are more involved.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A rolling mean and variance over a window of size $W$ can be implemented in $O(1)$ time per update by maintaining:
>
> $$
> S = \sum x_i, \qquad Q = \sum x_i^2,
> $$
>
> and a circular buffer of the last $W$ values. When the oldest value $x_{\text{old}}$ is replaced by a new value $x_{\text{new}}$, update
>
> $$
> S \leftarrow S - x_{\text{old}} + x_{\text{new}},
> \qquad
> Q \leftarrow Q - x_{\text{old}}^2 + x_{\text{new}}^2.
> $$
>
> Then compute
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{S}{W},
> \qquad
> \sigma^2 = \frac{Q}{W} - \mu^2,
> $$
>
> or, for sample variance,
>
> $$
> s^2 = \frac{Q - \frac{S^2}{W}}{W-1}.
> $$
>
> This gives constant-time updates and $O(W)$ memory.
