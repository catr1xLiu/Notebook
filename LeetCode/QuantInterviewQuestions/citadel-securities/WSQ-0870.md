> [!example] Question 870 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher (Intern) Interview

Given a number, determine whether it can be represented as the sum of two or more consecutive integers.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=871)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> A positive integer can be written as a sum of $2$ or more consecutive positive integers **if and only if** it is **not** a power of $2$.
>
> ### Reason
>
> Suppose
>
> $$
> n = a + (a+1) + (a+2) + \cdots + (a+k-1),
> $$
>
> where $k \ge 2$. Then
>
> $$
> n = ka + \frac{k(k-1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Multiplying by $2$ gives
>
> $$
> 2n = k\bigl(2a + k - 1\bigr).
> $$
>
> This shows that representing $n$ as a sum of consecutive integers is tied to having an odd factor. In fact, powers of $2$ are exactly the numbers that do **not** admit such a decomposition, while every other positive integer does.
>
> ### Criterion
>
> Check whether the number is a power of $2$:
>
> - If it **is** a power of $2$, return `False`.
> - Otherwise, return `True`.
>
> ### Efficient test
>
> A positive integer $n$ is a power of $2$ exactly when
>
> $$
> n \,\&\, (n-1) = 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore the answer is
>
> $$
> \text{return } n > 0 \text{ and } (n \,\&\, (n-1)) \ne 0.
> $$
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> Return `True` iff the number is **not** a power of $2$.  
> Equivalently:
>
> ```python
> def consecutive_numbers_sum(n):
>     return n > 0 and (n & (n - 1)) != 0
> ```
