> [!example] Question 863 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quant Research Intern Interview

If N people each put their hat in a room and then each randomly pick one hat, what is the probability that a given person gets their own hat back?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=864)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Model the random redistribution of hats as a uniformly random permutation of the $N$ hats among the $N$ people.
>
> Let $A_i$ be the event that person $i$ gets their own hat back. Then:
>
> - The probability that a particular person gets their own hat is
>   $$
>   P(A_i)=\frac{1}{N}.
>   $$
>
> If the question asks for the probability that **a given person** gets their own hat back, the answer is therefore
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{N}}.
> $$
>
> If instead the question asks for the probability that **everyone** gets their own hat back, that occurs only when the random permutation is the identity permutation. Since there are $N!$ possible permutations and only one identity permutation,
>
> $$
> P(\text{everyone gets their own hat})=\frac{1}{N!}.
> $$
>
> Thus:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{N}} \quad \text{for one specified person},
> \qquad
> \boxed{\frac{1}{N!}} \quad \text{for all $N$ people}.
> $$
