> [!example] Question 867 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

Coin A shows heads with probability 2/3, and coin B shows heads with probability 1/3. If a coin is chosen at random and flipped three times, each resulting in heads, what is the probability that the coin is coin A?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=868)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ denote the event that the chosen coin is coin $A$, and $B$ denote the event that the chosen coin is coin $B$. Assume each coin is equally likely to be chosen initially, so
>
> $$
> P(A) = P(B) = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Let $H^3$ be the event of getting $3$ heads in a row.
>
> The conditional probabilities of $3$ heads are
>
> $$
> P(H^3 \mid A) = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{8}{27},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> P(H^3 \mid B) = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(A \mid H^3)
> = \frac{P(H^3 \mid A)P(A)}{P(H^3 \mid A)P(A) + P(H^3 \mid B)P(B)}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> P(A \mid H^3)
> = \frac{\frac{8}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{8}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> The common factors $\frac{1}{27}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ cancel:
>
> $$
> P(A \mid H^3) = \frac{8}{8+1} = \frac{8}{9}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability that the coin is coin $A$ given that $3$ flips in a row were heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{8}{9}}.
> $$
