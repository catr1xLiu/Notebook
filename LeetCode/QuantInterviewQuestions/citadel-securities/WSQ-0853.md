> [!example] Question 853 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the probability that a coin is biased (always lands heads), given that you observe five heads in a row?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=854)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To determine the probability that the coin is biased given that $5$ heads were observed in a row, a prior model is needed.
>
> Assume the coin is chosen from the following two possibilities:
>
> - with prior probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, it is a fair coin, so
>   $$P(H^5 \mid \text{fair}) = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^5 = \tfrac{1}{32};$$
> - with prior probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, it is a biased coin that always lands heads, so
>   $$P(H^5 \mid \text{biased}) = 1.$$
>
> Using Bayes’ theorem,
>
> $$
> P(\text{biased} \mid H^5)
> =
> \frac{P(H^5 \mid \text{biased})P(\text{biased})}
> {P(H^5 \mid \text{biased})P(\text{biased}) + P(H^5 \mid \text{fair})P(\text{fair})}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> P(\text{biased} \mid H^5)
> =
> \frac{(1)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}
> {(1)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\tfrac{1}{32}\right)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}.
> $$
>
> Simplifying,
>
> $$
> P(\text{biased} \mid H^5)
> =
> \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}}{\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{64}}
> =
> \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}}{\tfrac{33}{64}}
> =
> \frac{32}{33}.
> $$
>
> Thus, under equal prior probabilities for “fair” and “always heads,” the probability the coin is biased after seeing $5$ heads in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{32}{33} \approx 0.9697.}
> $$
>
> If different prior probabilities are intended, the answer changes accordingly.
