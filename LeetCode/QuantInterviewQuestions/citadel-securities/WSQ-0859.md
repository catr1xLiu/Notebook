> [!example] Question 859 · Citadel Securities
> ## Fundamental Analyst Interview

Two tennis players, A and B, are at deuce. Player A wins a point with a 60% chance, and Player B wins a point with a 40% chance. What is the probability that A wins the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=860)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p=0.6$ be the probability that $A$ wins a point, and let $q=0.4$ be the probability that $B$ wins a point.
>
> At deuce, a player must win two consecutive points to win the game.
>
> ## Method 1: Recursion from deuce
>
> Let $P$ be the probability that $A$ wins the game starting from deuce.
>
> From deuce, three things can happen over the next two points:
>
> - $A$ wins both points: probability $p^2$, and then $A$ wins the game.
> - $B$ wins both points: probability $q^2$, and then $A$ loses the game.
> - They split the two points: probability $2pq$, and the score returns to deuce.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P = p^2 + 2pq\,P.
> $$
>
> Solve for $P$:
> $$
> P(1-2pq)=p^2
> $$
> so
> $$
> P=\frac{p^2}{1-2pq}.
> $$
>
> Substitute $p=0.6$ and $q=0.4$:
> $$
> P=\frac{0.6^2}{1-2(0.6)(0.4)}
> =\frac{0.36}{1-0.48}
> =\frac{0.36}{0.52}
> =\frac{36}{52}
> =\frac{9}{13}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{9}{13} \approx 0.6923}
> $$
>
> So the probability that $A$ wins the game is about $69.23\%$.
