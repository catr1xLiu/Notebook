> [!example] Question 879 · Citadel Securities
> ## Analyst Interview

Estimate how many basketballs can fit in a standard school bus.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=880)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Estimate the number of basketballs by comparing volumes and then applying a packing factor.
>
> ## 1. Model the school bus as a rectangular box
>
> A typical full-size school bus is about
>
> - length: $35\ \text{ft}$
> - width: $8\ \text{ft}$
> - interior height: $6\ \text{ft}$
>
> So the interior volume is approximately
>
> $$
> V_{\text{bus}} = 35 \cdot 8 \cdot 6 = 1680\ \text{ft}^3.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Model a basketball as a sphere
>
> A regulation basketball has diameter about $9.5\ \text{in}$, so radius
>
> $$
> r = 4.75\ \text{in} = \frac{4.75}{12}\ \text{ft} \approx 0.396\ \text{ft}.
> $$
>
> Its volume is
>
> $$
> V_{\text{ball}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3
> \approx \frac{4}{3}\pi (0.396)^3
> \approx 0.26\ \text{ft}^3.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Compute the ideal volume ratio
>
> If space could be used perfectly, the number of basketballs would be
>
> $$
> N_{\text{ideal}} = \frac{V_{\text{bus}}}{V_{\text{ball}}}
> \approx \frac{1680}{0.26}
> \approx 6460.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Adjust for sphere packing
>
> Spheres do not fill space completely. A realistic dense packing fraction is about
>
> $$
> 0.64 \text{ to } 0.74.
> $$
>
> Using a practical estimate of about $0.70$,
>
> $$
> N \approx 6460 \times 0.70 \approx 4520.
> $$
>
> ## 5. Account for seats and unusable interior space
>
> If the seats remain in the bus, much of the volume is unavailable. A rough reduction might cut the capacity to about half, giving
>
> $$
> N_{\text{seats in}} \approx 2000 \text{ to } 3000.
> $$
>
> If the bus is emptied of seats and filled nearly wall-to-wall, the estimate is
>
> $$
> N_{\text{empty bus}} \approx 4500.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> A reasonable estimate is:
>
> - about **$4500$ basketballs** for an empty full-size school bus,
> - about **$2000$ to $3000$ basketballs** if the seats stay in.
