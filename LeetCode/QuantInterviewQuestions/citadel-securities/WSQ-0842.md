> [!example] Question 842 · Citadel Securities
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Reverse the words in a sentence string in-place.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=843)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To reverse the words of a sentence **in-place**, the standard approach is:
>
> 1. Reverse the entire string.
> 2. Reverse each individual word within the reversed string.
>
> This works because reversing the whole sentence places the words in reverse order, but each word itself becomes reversed as well. Reversing each word again restores its correct character order.
>
> ### Example
>
> Given:
>
> $$
> \text{"reverse the words"}
> $$
>
> After reversing the whole string:
>
> $$
> \text{"sdrow eht esrever"}
> $$
>
> Then reversing each word:
>
> $$
> \text{"words the reverse"}
> $$
>
> ## In-place algorithm
>
> Assume the string is stored as a mutable character array.
>
> ### Step 1: Reverse the entire array
>
> If the array is
>
> $$
> [a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}]
> $$
>
> swap characters symmetrically:
>
> - $a_0 \leftrightarrow a_{n-1}$
> - $a_1 \leftrightarrow a_{n-2}$
> - and so on
>
> This takes $O(n)$ time.
>
> ### Step 2: Reverse each word
>
> Scan the array from left to right.
>
> - When a non-space character is found, mark the start of a word.
> - Continue until a space or the end of the array is reached.
> - Reverse that segment in-place.
> - Continue scanning.
>
> This also takes $O(n)$ time.
>
> ## Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> reverseWords(s):
>     reverse(s, 0, length(s) - 1)
>
>     start = 0
>     for i from 0 to length(s):
>         if i == length(s) or s[i] == ' ':
>             reverse(s, start, i - 1)
>             start = i + 1
>
> reverse(s, left, right):
>     while left < right:
>         swap s[left], s[right]
>         left += 1
>         right -= 1
> ```
>
> ## Correctness
>
> Let the sentence consist of words
>
> $$
> w_1, w_2, \dots, w_k
> $$
>
> separated by spaces.
>
> The original string is:
>
> $$
> w_1 \; w_2 \; \dots \; w_k
> $$
>
> After reversing the entire string, the order becomes:
>
> $$
> \operatorname{rev}(w_k)\; \dots \; \operatorname{rev}(w_2)\; \operatorname{rev}(w_1)
> $$
>
> where $\operatorname{rev}(w)$ denotes the characters of word $w$ in reverse order.
>
> Reversing each individual word transforms this into:
>
> $$
> w_k \; w_{k-1} \; \dots \; w_1
> $$
>
> which is exactly the desired result.
>
> ## Complexity
>
> - Time: $O(n)$
> - Extra space: $O(1)$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Reverse the whole sentence first, then reverse each word individually. This achieves word-order reversal **in-place** in $O(n)$ time and $O(1)$ extra space.
