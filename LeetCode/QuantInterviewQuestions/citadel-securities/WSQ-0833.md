> [!example] Question 833 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given n balloons, each balloon is painted with a number on it represented by an array nums. You are to burst all the balloons. If you burst balloon i, you get nums\[left\] \* nums\[i\] \* nums\[right\] coins. Here, left and right are adjacent indices of i. After the burst, the left and right balloons become adjacent. Find the maximum coins you can collect by bursting the balloons wisely.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=834)

> ## Solution
>
> Let $nums$ be the array of balloon values. When a balloon $i$ is burst, the coins gained are
>
> $$
> nums[left] \cdot nums[i] \cdot nums[right],
> $$
>
> where $left$ and $right$ are the nearest unburst balloons adjacent to $i$ at that moment.
>
> The difficulty is that the neighbors of a balloon change as other balloons are removed. A direct simulation leads to exponential branching. The key dynamic programming idea is to choose **which balloon is burst last** in an interval.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Add virtual boundary balloons
>
> To avoid edge cases, add balloons of value $1$ to both ends:
>
> $$
> arr = [1] + nums + [1].
> $$
>
> If the original length is $n$, then $arr$ has length $n+2$.
>
> These boundary balloons are never burst; they only serve as fixed neighbors.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Define the subproblem
>
> Let
>
> $$
> dp[l][r]
> $$
>
> denote the maximum coins obtainable by bursting **all balloons strictly between** indices $l$ and $r$ in $arr$.
>
> Thus the interval is open: the balloons at $l$ and $r$ remain intact.
>
> If there are no balloons between $l$ and $r$, then
>
> $$
> dp[l][r] = 0.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Transition by choosing the last balloon
>
> Suppose balloon $k$ is the **last** balloon burst among those between $l$ and $r$, where
>
> $$
> l < k < r.
> $$
>
> At that final moment, all balloons between $l$ and $k$ and between $k$ and $r$ have already been removed. So the neighbors of $k$ are exactly $l$ and $r$.
>
> Therefore, bursting $k$ last contributes
>
> $$
> arr[l] \cdot arr[k] \cdot arr[r].
> $$
>
> Before that, we must optimally burst the balloons in the two subintervals:
>
> - between $l$ and $k$, contributing $dp[l][k]$,
> - between $k$ and $r$, contributing $dp[k][r]$.
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> dp[l][r] = \max_{l < k < r} \left( dp[l][k] + dp[k][r] + arr[l] \cdot arr[k] \cdot arr[r] \right).
> $$
>
> This is the core recurrence.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 4: Order of computation
>
> Since $dp[l][r]$ depends on smaller intervals, compute intervals by increasing length.
>
> Let the interval length be
>
> $$
> len = r - l.
> $$
>
> We need at least one balloon inside, so start from $len = 2$.
>
> The final answer is
>
> $$
> dp[0][n+1].
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 5: Correctness argument
>
> We prove that the recurrence computes the optimal answer.
>
> Consider any interval $(l, r)$. In any valid bursting order of balloons strictly between $l$ and $r$, there is some balloon $k$ that is burst last.
>
> At the moment $k$ is burst last in $(l, r)$:
>
> - every balloon in $(l, k)$ has already been burst,
> - every balloon in $(k, r)$ has already been burst,
> - thus the adjacent remaining balloons are exactly $l$ and $r$.
>
> So the total coins for such an order are
>
> $$
> (\text{coins from } (l,k)) + (\text{coins from } (k,r)) + arr[l] \cdot arr[k] \cdot arr[r].
> $$
>
> The first two parts are independent and must themselves be optimal for their subintervals; otherwise the overall order would not be optimal. Therefore any optimal solution has value
>
> $$
> dp[l][k] + dp[k][r] + arr[l] \cdot arr[k] \cdot arr[r]
> $$
>
> for some $k$ with $l < k < r$. Taking the maximum over all such $k$ yields the optimal value for $dp[l][r]$.
>
> Thus the recurrence is correct.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 6: Complexity
>
> There are $O(n^2)$ states $dp[l][r]$.
>
> For each state, we try all possible last balloons $k$, which is $O(n)$.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - **Time complexity:** $O(n^3)$
> - **Space complexity:** $O(n^2)$
>
> ---
>
> ## Reference implementation
>
> ```python
> class Solution:
>     def maxCoins(self, nums: List[int]) -> int:
>         arr = [1] + nums + [1]
>         n = len(arr)
>         
>         dp = [[0] * n for _ in range(n)]
>         
>         for length in range(2, n):  # r - l
>             for l in range(0, n - length):
>                 r = l + length
>                 for k in range(l + 1, r):
>                     dp[l][r] = max(
>                         dp[l][r],
>                         dp[l][k] + dp[k][r] + arr[l] * arr[k] * arr[r]
>                     )
>         
>         return dp[0][n - 1]
> ```
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Use interval dynamic programming with the state
>
> $$
> dp[l][r] = \text{maximum coins from bursting all balloons strictly between } l \text{ and } r.
> $$
>
> The recurrence is
>
> $$
> dp[l][r] = \max_{l < k < r} \left( dp[l][k] + dp[k][r] + arr[l] \cdot arr[k] \cdot arr[r] \right),
> $$
>
> where $arr = [1] + nums + [1]$. Compute states by increasing interval length. The final result is
>
> $$
> dp[0][n-1].
> $$
>
> This yields an $O(n^3)$ time and $O(n^2)$ space solution.
