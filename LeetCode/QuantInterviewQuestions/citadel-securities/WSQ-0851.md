> [!example] Question 851 · Citadel Securities
> ## Trader Interview

What is the shape of the gamma curve for a call option?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=852)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For a European call option, the **gamma** is the second derivative of the option price with respect to the underlying asset price:
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2} = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial S}.
> $$
>
> It describes how quickly the **delta** of the call changes as the stock price $S$ changes.
>
> ## Shape of the gamma curve
>
> As a function of the underlying price $S$, the gamma curve for a call option is:
>
> - **Always positive**
> - **Bell-shaped**
> - **Highest when the option is at-the-money**
> - **Small when the option is deep in-the-money or deep out-of-the-money**
>
> Thus, gamma is largest when $S$ is near the strike price $K$, and it declines toward $0$ as $S$ moves far above or far below $K$.
>
> ## Black–Scholes formula for call gamma
>
> Under Black–Scholes, call gamma is
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \frac{\phi(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}},
> $$
>
> where
>
> $$
> d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}},
> $$
>
> and $\phi(\cdot)$ is the standard normal density.
>
> Since $\phi(d_1) > 0$, gamma is positive for both calls and puts.
>
> ## Interpretation
>
> - When the call is **at-the-money**, delta changes most rapidly, so gamma is high.
> - When the call is **deep out-of-the-money**, delta is already near $0$, so gamma is low.
> - When the call is **deep in-the-money**, delta is already near $1$, so gamma is also low.
>
> ## Effect of time to expiry
>
> As expiry approaches:
>
> - gamma becomes **more sharply peaked** around $S = K$,
> - the maximum gamma increases,
> - away from the strike, gamma becomes very small.
>
> So for a near-expiry call, the gamma curve is a **tall, narrow spike** near the strike price.
>
> ## Final description
>
> The gamma curve for a call option is a **positive hump-shaped curve**, centered roughly around the strike price, with the peak strongest near expiration and flatter for longer-dated options.
