> [!example] Question 876 · Citadel Securities
> ## Summer Internship Interview

Three random variables X, Y, and Z are such that corr(X, Y) = corr(Y, Z) = corr(Z, X) = r. Give the upper and lower bounds for r.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=877)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let
> $$
> \rho_{XY}=\rho_{YZ}=\rho_{ZX}=r.
> $$
> We seek all possible values of $r$ for which such random variables can exist.
>
> ## Approach
> Use the fact that any correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite. The correlation matrix of $(X,Y,Z)$ is
> $$
> R=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & r & r\\
> r & 1 & r\\
> r & r & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
> Thus $R$ must be positive semidefinite.
>
> ## Step 1: Necessary conditions from principal minors
> For a positive semidefinite matrix, all principal minors must be nonnegative.
>
> The $2\times 2$ principal minors give
> $$
> \det\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\ r&1\end{pmatrix}
> =1-r^2 \ge 0,
> $$
> so
> $$
> -1 \le r \le 1.
> $$
>
> Now compute the determinant of $R$:
> $$
> \det(R)=
> \begin{vmatrix}
> 1 & r & r\\
> r & 1 & r\\
> r & r & 1
> \end{vmatrix}.
> $$
> A standard evaluation gives
> $$
> \det(R)=(1-r)^2(1+2r).
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> (1-r)^2(1+2r)\ge 0.
> $$
> Since $(1-r)^2\ge 0$ always, this requires
> $$
> 1+2r\ge 0,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> r\ge -\frac12.
> $$
>
> Combining with $r\le 1$, we obtain
> $$
> -\frac12 \le r \le 1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Sufficiency
> It remains to note that for every $r$ in this interval, the matrix $R$ is indeed positive semidefinite, so it is a valid correlation matrix. Thus such random variables exist for all
> $$
> -\frac12 \le r \le 1.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
> The correlation coefficient $r$ must satisfy
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac12 \le r \le 1.}
> $$
> So the lower bound is
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac12}
> $$
> and the upper bound is
> $$
> \boxed{1}.
> $$
