> ## Pricing an American Option via Backward Induction in Python
>
> An American option can be exercised at any time up to maturity, so its value is found by comparing:
>
> - the **immediate exercise value**, and
> - the **continuation value** from holding the option.
>
> A standard discrete-time method is the **binomial tree** with **backward induction**.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Binomial model setup
>
> Let
>
> - $S_0$ = initial stock price,
> - $K$ = strike,
> - $T$ = maturity,
> - $r$ = risk-free rate,
> - $\sigma$ = volatility,
> - $N$ = number of time steps.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \Delta t = \frac{T}{N},
> \qquad
> u = e^{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}},
> \qquad
> d = \frac{1}{u}.
> $$
>
> Under the risk-neutral measure, the probability of an up move is
>
> $$
> p = \frac{e^{r \Delta t} - d}{u - d}.
> $$
>
> The discount factor per step is
>
> $$
> \beta = e^{-r \Delta t}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Terminal payoff
>
> At maturity, for each node with stock price $S$, the option value is:
>
> - American call:
>   $$
>   \max(S - K, 0)
>   $$
> - American put:
>   $$
>   \max(K - S, 0)
>   $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Backward induction
>
> At each earlier node, compute:
>
> 1. **Continuation value**
>    $$
>    V_{\text{cont}} = \beta \left( p V_{\text{up}} + (1-p)V_{\text{down}} \right)
>    $$
>
> 2. **Immediate exercise value**
>    - Call:
>      $$
>      V_{\text{ex}} = \max(S-K,0)
>      $$
>    - Put:
>      $$
>      V_{\text{ex}} = \max(K-S,0)
>      $$
>
> 3. **American option value**
>    $$
>    V = \max(V_{\text{cont}}, V_{\text{ex}})
>    $$
>
> This max operation is what distinguishes the American option from the European option.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Python implementation
>
> ```python
> import math
>
> def price_american_option_binomial(S0, K, T, r, sigma, N, option_type="put"):
>     dt = T / N
>     u = math.exp(sigma * math.sqrt(dt))
>     d = 1 / u
>     p = (math.exp(r * dt) - d) / (u - d)
>     disc = math.exp(-r * dt)
>
>     # Stock prices at maturity
>     stock_prices = [S0 * (u ** j) * (d ** (N - j)) for j in range(N + 1)]
>
>     # Option values at maturity
>     if option_type == "call":
>         option_values = [max(S - K, 0.0) for S in stock_prices]
>     elif option_type == "put":
>         option_values = [max(K - S, 0.0) for S in stock_prices]
>     else:
>         raise ValueError("option_type must be 'call' or 'put'")
>
>     # Backward induction
>     for i in range(N - 1, -1, -1):
>         new_values = []
>         for j in range(i + 1):
>             S = S0 * (u ** j) * (d ** (i - j))
>             continuation = disc * (p * option_values[j + 1] + (1 - p) * option_values[j])
>
>             if option_type == "call":
>                 exercise = max(S - K, 0.0)
>             else:
>                 exercise = max(K - S, 0.0)
>
>             new_values.append(max(continuation, exercise))
>         option_values = new_values
>
>     return option_values[0]
> ```
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Example
>
> Price an American put with
>
> - $S_0 = 100$,
> - $K = 100$,
> - $T = 1$,
> - $r = 0.05$,
> - $\sigma = 0.2$,
> - $N = 200$.
>
> ```python
> price = price_american_option_binomial(
>     S0=100,
>     K=100,
>     T=1.0,
>     r=0.05,
>     sigma=0.2,
>     N=200,
>     option_type="put"
> )
>
> print(f"American put price: {price:.4f}")
> ```
>
> A corresponding American call can be priced by setting `option_type="call"`.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Remarks
>
> 1. For a **non-dividend-paying stock**, an American call should satisfy
>    $$
>    C_{\text{American}} = C_{\text{European}},
>    $$
>    since early exercise is not optimal.
>
> 2. For an American put, early exercise may be optimal, so
>    $$
>    P_{\text{American}} \geq P_{\text{European}}.
>    $$
>
> 3. Increasing $N$ improves accuracy, though at greater computational cost.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> A standard way to price an American option in Python is to build a binomial tree and apply backward induction, taking at each node
>
> $$
> V = \max\left(\text{exercise value},\; e^{-r\Delta t}\left[pV_{\text{up}} + (1-p)V_{\text{down}}\right]\right).
> $$
>
> The Python function above implements this method and returns the American option price.
