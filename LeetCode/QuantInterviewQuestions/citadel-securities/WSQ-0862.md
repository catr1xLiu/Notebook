> [!example] Question 862 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

Coin A lands heads with probability 2/3, and coin B lands heads with probability 1/3. If you flip the selected coin three times and observe heads each time, what is the probability that the coin is coin A?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=863)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ denote the event that the chosen coin is coin $A$, and $B$ the event that the chosen coin is coin $B$.
>
> Assume the coin was chosen at random, so
>
> $$
> P(A)=P(B)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Let $HHH$ be the event of getting $3$ heads in a row.
>
> For coin $A$,
>
> $$
> P(HHH \mid A)=\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3=\frac{8}{27}.
> $$
>
> For coin $B$,
>
> $$
> P(HHH \mid B)=\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3=\frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(A \mid HHH)=\frac{P(HHH \mid A)P(A)}{P(HHH \mid A)P(A)+P(HHH \mid B)P(B)}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> P(A \mid HHH)
> =
> \frac{\frac{8}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2}}
> {\frac{8}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
>
> The factors $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{27}$ cancel, giving
>
> $$
> P(A \mid HHH)=\frac{8}{8+1}=\frac{8}{9}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that the coin is coin $A$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{8}{9}}.
> $$
