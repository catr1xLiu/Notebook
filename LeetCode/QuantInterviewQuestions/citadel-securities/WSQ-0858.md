> [!example] Question 858 · Citadel Securities
> ## Fundamental Analyst Interview

Price an option where the underlying asset is the outcome of a fair six-sided die roll.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=859)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To price an option on a fair die, one must specify the option’s payoff. A standard choice is a European call option on the die outcome.
>
> Let the die outcome be the random variable $S \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, with
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(S=k)=\frac{1}{6}, \qquad k=1,\dots,6.
> $$
>
> Assume zero interest rate, so the option price is the expected payoff under the risk-neutral measure. Since the die is fair, the natural pricing measure is uniform.
>
> ## European call option
>
> For strike $K$, the payoff is
>
> $$
> (S-K)^+ = \max(S-K,0).
> $$
>
> Thus the price is
>
> $$
> C(K)=\mathbb{E}[(S-K)^+]
> =\frac{1}{6}\sum_{s=1}^{6}\max(s-K,0).
> $$
>
> This is the general pricing formula.
>
> ### If $K$ is an integer
>
> For integer $K$, only outcomes $s>K$ contribute, so
>
> $$
> C(K)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{s=K+1}^{6}(s-K).
> $$
>
> Let $n=6-K$. Then
>
> $$
> C(K)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{n} j
> =\frac{1}{6}\cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
> =\frac{(6-K)(7-K)}{12},
> $$
>
> valid for $K=1,2,3,4,5$. Also,
>
> $$
> C(K)=0 \quad \text{for } K\ge 6.
> $$
>
> If $K\le 1$, then every outcome is in the money, so
>
> $$
> C(K)=\mathbb{E}[S-K]=3.5-K.
> $$
>
> ## Example: strike $K=3$
>
> The payoff is
>
> $$
> (S-3)^+ =
> \begin{cases}
> 0, & S=1,2,3,\\
> 1, & S=4,\\
> 2, & S=5,\\
> 3, & S=6.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> C(3)=\frac{0+0+0+1+2+3}{6}
> =\frac{6}{6}
> =1.
> $$
>
> So a call option on a fair die with strike $3$ is worth
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}.
> $$
>
> ## Put option
>
> If instead the option is a European put with strike $K$, its payoff is
>
> $$
> (K-S)^+,
> $$
>
> and its price is
>
> $$
> P(K)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{s=1}^{6}\max(K-s,0).
> $$
>
> For example, with $K=3$,
>
> $$
> P(3)=\frac{2+1+0+0+0+0}{6}
> =\frac{3}{6}
> =\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Without a specified payoff, the price of an option on a fair die is its expected payoff:
>
> $$
> \text{Price}=\mathbb{E}[\text{payoff}].
> $$
>
> For a European call with strike $K$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{C(K)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{s=1}^{6}\max(s-K,0)}.
> $$
>
> In particular, for strike $K=3$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{C(3)=1}.
> $$
