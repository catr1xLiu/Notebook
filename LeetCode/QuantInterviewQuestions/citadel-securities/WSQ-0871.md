> [!example] Question 871 · Citadel Securities
> ## Semi-Systematic Trader Interview

What is the expected number of rolls of a fair six-sided die required until each face has appeared at least twice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=872)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $T$ be the number of rolls of a fair six-sided die needed until each face has appeared at least twice. We seek $E[T]$.
>
> ## 1. Decompose the waiting time
>
> Track how many faces have appeared exactly $0$ times, exactly $1$ time, and at least $2$ times. Let a state be $(a,b)$, where
>
> - $a$ = number of faces not yet seen
> - $b$ = number of faces seen exactly once
>
> Then $6-a-b$ faces have already been seen at least twice, and we want the expected time to reach $(0,0)$ starting from $(6,0)$.
>
> Let $E_{a,b}$ denote the expected additional number of rolls needed from state $(a,b)$. Then $E_{0,0}=0$, and for any other state:
>
> - with probability $\dfrac{a}{6}$, we roll a face never seen before, so the new state is $(a-1,b+1)$
> - with probability $\dfrac{b}{6}$, we roll a face seen exactly once, so the new state is $(a,b-1)$
> - with probability $\dfrac{6-a-b}{6}$, we roll a face already seen at least twice, so the state stays $(a,b)$
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_{a,b}
> =
> 1+\frac{a}{6}E_{a-1,b+1}
> +\frac{b}{6}E_{a,b-1}
> +\frac{6-a-b}{6}E_{a,b}.
> $$
>
> Rearranging,
> $$
> \frac{a+b}{6}E_{a,b}
> =
> 1+\frac{a}{6}E_{a-1,b+1}
> +\frac{b}{6}E_{a,b-1},
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_{a,b}
> =
> \frac{6}{a+b}
> +
> \frac{a}{a+b}E_{a-1,b+1}
> +
> \frac{b}{a+b}E_{a,b-1}.
> $$
>
> We now compute backward, starting from the states with $a=0$.
>
> ## 2. Compute the values
>
> ### States with a=0
>
> For $b=1,2,\dots,6$,
> $$
> E_{0,b}=\frac{6}{b}+E_{0,b-1},
> $$
> since only singleton faces matter.
>
> Starting from $E_{0,0}=0$:
> $$
> E_{0,1}=6,
> $$
> $$
> E_{0,2}=3+6=9,
> $$
> $$
> E_{0,3}=2+9=11,
> $$
> $$
> E_{0,4}=\frac{3}{2}+11=\frac{25}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E_{0,5}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{25}{2}=\frac{137}{10},
> $$
> $$
> E_{0,6}=1+\frac{137}{10}=\frac{147}{10}.
> $$
>
> ### States with a=1
>
> Using
> $$
> E_{1,b}=\frac{6}{b+1}+\frac{1}{b+1}E_{0,b+1}+\frac{b}{b+1}E_{1,b-1},
> $$
> we get:
> $$
> E_{1,0}=6+E_{0,1}=12,
> $$
> $$
> E_{1,1}=3+\frac{1}{2}E_{0,2}+\frac{1}{2}E_{1,0}
> =3+\frac{9}{2}+6=\frac{27}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E_{1,2}=2+\frac{1}{3}E_{0,3}+\frac{2}{3}E_{1,1}
> =2+\frac{11}{3}+9=\frac{44}{3},
> $$
> $$
> E_{1,3}=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{4}E_{0,4}+\frac{3}{4}E_{1,2}
> =\frac{3}{2}+\frac{25}{8}+11=\frac{125}{8},
> $$
> $$
> E_{1,4}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{1}{5}E_{0,5}+\frac{4}{5}E_{1,3}
> =\frac{6}{5}+\frac{137}{50}+\frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{125}{8}
> =\frac{411}{25},
> $$
> $$
> E_{1,5}=1+\frac{1}{6}E_{0,6}+\frac{5}{6}E_{1,4}
> =1+\frac{147}{60}+\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{411}{25}
> =\frac{343}{20}.
> $$
>
> ### States with a=2
>
> Using
> $$
> E_{2,b}=\frac{6}{b+2}+\frac{2}{b+2}E_{1,b+1}+\frac{b}{b+2}E_{2,b-1},
> $$
> we obtain:
> $$
> E_{2,0}=3+E_{1,1}=3+\frac{27}{2}=\frac{33}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E_{2,1}=2+\frac{2}{3}E_{1,2}+\frac{1}{3}E_{2,0}
> =2+\frac{88}{9}+\frac{11}{2}
> =\frac{311}{18},
> $$
> $$
> E_{2,2}=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2}E_{1,3}+\frac{1}{2}E_{2,1}
> =\frac{3}{2}+\frac{125}{16}+\frac{311}{36}
> =\frac{2585}{144},
> $$
> $$
> E_{2,3}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{2}{5}E_{1,4}+\frac{3}{5}E_{2,2}
> =\frac{6}{5}+\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{411}{25}+\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{2585}{144}
> =\frac{111281}{6000},
> $$
> $$
> E_{2,4}=1+\frac{1}{3}E_{1,5}+\frac{2}{3}E_{2,3}
> =1+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{343}{20}+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{111281}{6000}
> =\frac{171731}{9000}.
> $$
>
> ### States with a=3
>
> Using
> $$
> E_{3,b}=\frac{6}{b+3}+\frac{3}{b+3}E_{2,b+1}+\frac{b}{b+3}E_{3,b-1},
> $$
> we get:
> $$
> E_{3,0}=2+E_{2,1}=2+\frac{311}{18}=\frac{347}{18},
> $$
> $$
> E_{3,1}=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{4}E_{2,2}+\frac{1}{4}E_{3,0}
> =\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{2585}{144}+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{347}{18}
> =\frac{11395}{576},
> $$
> $$
> E_{3,2}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{3}{5}E_{2,3}+\frac{2}{5}E_{3,1}
> =\frac{6}{5}+\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{111281}{6000}+\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{11395}{576}
> =\frac{3643433}{180000},
> $$
> $$
> E_{3,3}=1+\frac{1}{2}E_{2,4}+\frac{1}{2}E_{3,2}
> =1+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{171731}{9000}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3643433}{180000}
> =\frac{2479351}{120000}.
> $$
>
> ### States with a=4
>
> Using
> $$
> E_{4,b}=\frac{6}{b+4}+\frac{4}{b+4}E_{3,b+1}+\frac{b}{b+4}E_{4,b-1},
> $$
> we find:
> $$
> E_{4,0}=\frac{3}{2}+E_{3,1}
> =\frac{3}{2}+\frac{11395}{576}
> =\frac{12259}{576},
> $$
> $$
> E_{4,1}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{4}{5}E_{3,2}+\frac{1}{5}E_{4,0}
> =\frac{6}{5}+\frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{3643433}{180000}+\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{12259}{576}
> =\frac{38969339}{1800000},
> $$
> $$
> E_{4,2}=1+\frac{2}{3}E_{3,3}+\frac{1}{3}E_{4,1}
> =1+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2479351}{120000}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{38969339}{1800000}
> =\frac{118749869}{5400000}.
> $$
>
> ### States with a=5
>
> Using
> $$
> E_{5,b}=\frac{6}{b+5}+\frac{5}{b+5}E_{4,b+1}+\frac{b}{b+5}E_{5,b-1},
> $$
> we get:
> $$
> E_{5,0}=\frac{6}{5}+E_{4,1}
> =\frac{6}{5}+\frac{38969339}{1800000}
> =\frac{41129339}{1800000},
> $$
> $$
> E_{5,1}=1+\frac{5}{6}E_{4,2}+\frac{1}{6}E_{5,0}
> =1+\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{118749869}{5400000}+\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{41129339}{1800000}
> =\frac{374768681}{16200000}.
> $$
>
> ### Initial state (6,0)
>
> Finally,
> $$
> E_{6,0}=1+E_{5,1}
> =1+\frac{374768681}{16200000}
> =\frac{390968681}{16200000}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final answer
>
> Therefore, the expected number of rolls needed until every face has appeared at least twice is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{390968681}{16200000}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{24.134}.
> $$
