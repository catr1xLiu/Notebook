> [!example] Question 855 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Arrange the numbers 1 through 5 randomly around a circle. What is the expected number of numbers that are smaller than both of their neighbors?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=856)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of positions around the circle whose number is smaller than both of its neighbors. We seek $\mathbb{E}[X]$.
>
> ## Step 1: Define indicator variables
>
> For each number $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$, define
> $$
> I_k =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if } k \text{ is smaller than both of its neighbors},\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> X = I_1 + I_2 + I_3 + I_4 + I_5.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{k=1}^5 \mathbb{E}[I_k] = \sum_{k=1}^5 \Pr(I_k=1).
> $$
>
> So it remains to compute, for a given number, the probability that it is smaller than both of its neighbors.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute $\Pr(I_k=1)$
>
> Fix a particular number $k$. In a random circular arrangement, its two neighbors are equally likely to be any $2$-element subset of the other $4$ numbers. Thus the pair of neighbors is uniformly distributed among
> $$
> \binom{4}{2} = 6
> $$
> possible pairs.
>
> The event $I_k=1$ occurs exactly when both neighbors are larger than $k$.
>
> - For $k=1$, all other numbers are larger, so every pair works:
>   $$
>   \Pr(I_1=1)=\frac{\binom{4}{2}}{6}=1.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=2$, the larger numbers are $\{3,4,5\}$, so favorable neighbor pairs are
>   $$
>   \binom{3}{2}=3,
>   $$
>   hence
>   $$
>   \Pr(I_2=1)=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=3$, the larger numbers are $\{4,5\}$, so favorable pairs are
>   $$
>   \binom{2}{2}=1,
>   $$
>   hence
>   $$
>   \Pr(I_3=1)=\frac{1}{6}.
>   $$
>
> - For $k=4$ or $k=5$, there are fewer than $2$ larger numbers, so
>   $$
>   \Pr(I_4=1)=\Pr(I_5=1)=0.
>   $$
>
> ## Step 3: Add the probabilities
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X]
> = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} + 0 + 0
> = \frac{6}{6}+\frac{3}{6}+\frac{1}{6}
> = \frac{10}{6}
> = \frac{5}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{3}}
> $$
