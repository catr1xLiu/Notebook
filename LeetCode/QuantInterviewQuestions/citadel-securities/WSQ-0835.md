> [!example] Question 835 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

In ridge regression, what happens to the expected value of the estimate if you perturb your data matrix?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=836)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> In ridge regression, the estimator is
>
> $$
> \hat{\beta}_\lambda = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1} X^\top y,
> $$
>
> with $\lambda > 0$. To study what happens to its expected value when the data matrix is perturbed, let the perturbed design matrix be
>
> $$
> \tilde{X} = X + \Delta X.
> $$
>
> Then the perturbed ridge estimator is
>
> $$
> \tilde{\beta}_\lambda
> = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top y.
> $$
>
> Assume the standard linear model
>
> $$
> y = X\beta + \varepsilon, \qquad E[\varepsilon] = 0.
> $$
>
> ## Expected value without perturbation
>
> Conditioning on $X$, the expectation of the ridge estimator is
>
> $$
> E[\hat{\beta}_\lambda \mid X]
> = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1} X^\top X \,\beta.
> $$
>
> Thus ridge regression is generally biased, since
>
> $$
> E[\hat{\beta}_\lambda \mid X] \neq \beta
> $$
>
> unless $\lambda = 0$ or in special cases.
>
> ## Expected value with perturbed data matrix
>
> With $\tilde{X} = X + \Delta X$, the conditional expectation becomes
>
> $$
> E[\tilde{\beta}_\lambda \mid \tilde{X}]
> = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top X\,\beta,
> $$
>
> if the response is still generated from the original model $y = X\beta + \varepsilon$.
>
> If instead the model is taken to be generated consistently with the perturbed matrix,
>
> $$
> y = \tilde{X}\beta + \varepsilon,
> $$
>
> then
>
> $$
> E[\tilde{\beta}_\lambda \mid \tilde{X}]
> = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X}\,\beta.
> $$
>
> ## Interpretation
>
> Perturbing the data matrix changes the expected value through the matrix factor
>
> $$
> (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top X
> \quad \longrightarrow \quad
> (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X}
> $$
>
> or, under a mismatch between the true and perturbed design,
>
> $$
> (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top X
> \quad \longrightarrow \quad
> (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top X.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected value of the ridge estimate generally shifts when the data matrix is perturbed. In particular:
>
> - ridge regression remains biased,
> - the bias depends explicitly on the perturbed Gram matrix $\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X}$,
> - small perturbations in $X$ usually cause small changes in $E[\hat{\beta}_\lambda]$ when $X^\top X + \lambda I$ is well-conditioned.
>
> ## Final statement
>
> If the design matrix is perturbed from $X$ to $\tilde{X}$, the expected ridge estimate changes from
>
> $$
> E[\hat{\beta}_\lambda \mid X]
> = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^\top X\,\beta
> $$
>
> to
>
> $$
> E[\tilde{\beta}_\lambda \mid \tilde{X}]
> = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X}\,\beta
> $$
>
> when the model uses the perturbed design, or to
>
> $$
> E[\tilde{\beta}_\lambda \mid \tilde{X}]
> = (\tilde{X}^\top \tilde{X} + \lambda I)^{-1}\tilde{X}^\top X\,\beta
> $$
>
> when the response still comes from the original design. Hence perturbing the data matrix alters the expectation, and therefore the bias, of the ridge estimator.
