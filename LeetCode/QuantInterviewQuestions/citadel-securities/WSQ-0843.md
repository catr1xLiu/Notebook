> [!example] Question 843 · Citadel Securities
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Design a random number generator that, given a list of probabilities corresponding to a discrete set of outcomes, outputs each outcome with its specified probability.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=844)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Given a finite list of probabilities
> $$
> p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n
> $$
> with
> $$
> p_i \ge 0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n p_i = 1,
> $$
> the goal is to design a random generator that returns outcome $i$ with probability $p_i$.
>
> ### Approach: cumulative probabilities
>
> Construct the cumulative sums
> $$
> c_k = \sum_{i=1}^k p_i, \qquad k=1,2,\dots,n.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> 0 \le c_1 \le c_2 \le \cdots \le c_n = 1.
> $$
>
> Generate a random number
> $$
> U \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1).
> $$
> Return the smallest index $k$ such that
> $$
> U < c_k.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, partition the interval $[0,1)$ into subintervals:
> $$
> [0,p_1),\ [p_1,p_1+p_2),\ \dots,\ [c_{n-1},c_n).
> $$
> If $U$ falls in the $k$th interval, output $k$.
>
> ### Correctness
>
> The $k$th interval has length
> $$
> c_k - c_{k-1} = p_k,
> $$
> where $c_0 = 0$. Since $U$ is uniform on $[0,1)$,
> $$
> \Pr(\text{output } k) = p_k.
> $$
> Thus the generator produces the required distribution.
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> 1. Compute cumulative sums:
>    $$
>    c_1 = p_1,\quad c_2 = p_1+p_2,\quad \dots,\quad c_n = 1.
>    $$
> 2. Draw $U \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$.
> 3. Find the first $k$ such that
>    $$
>    U < c_k.
>    $$
> 4. Return $k$.
>
> ### Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> input: probabilities p[1..n] with sum 1
>
> c[1] = p[1]
> for k = 2 to n:
>     c[k] = c[k-1] + p[k]
>
> U = random number uniformly in [0,1)
>
> for k = 1 to n:
>     if U < c[k]:
>         return k
> ```
>
> ### Example
>
> Suppose
> $$
> (p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4) = (0.1,0.2,0.5,0.2).
> $$
> Then the cumulative sums are
> $$
> (c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4) = (0.1,0.3,0.8,1.0).
> $$
> So:
>
> - output $1$ if $U \in [0,0.1)$,
> - output $2$ if $U \in [0.1,0.3)$,
> - output $3$ if $U \in [0.3,0.8)$,
> - output $4$ if $U \in [0.8,1.0)$.
>
> Hence the probabilities are exactly
> $$
> 0.1,\ 0.2,\ 0.5,\ 0.2.
> $$
>
> ### Complexity
>
> - Preprocessing cumulative sums: $O(n)$
> - One sample using linear search: $O(n)$
> - If many samples are needed, binary search on the cumulative array reduces sampling time to
>   $$
>   O(\log n).
>   $$
>
> ### Final answer
>
> A random generator for a list of probabilities is obtained by computing cumulative probabilities, generating
> $$
> U \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1),
> $$
> and returning the first index $k$ such that
> $$
> U < \sum_{i=1}^k p_i.
> $$
> This returns outcome $k$ with probability exactly
> $$
> p_k.
> $$
