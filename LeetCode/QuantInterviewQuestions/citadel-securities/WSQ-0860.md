> [!example] Question 860 · Citadel Securities
> ## Fundamental Analyst Interview

You and a friend each independently receive a value drawn uniformly from (0,1). After seeing your value, you can choose to switch values with your friend or keep your original value. What is the probability that you end up with a higher value than your friend at the end of the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=861)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let your value be $X$ and your friend’s value be $Y$, where
>
> $$
> X,Y \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \text{Uniform}(0,1).
> $$
>
> You observe $X$, but not $Y$. After seeing $X$, you may either keep $X$ or switch and take $Y$. The goal is to maximize the probability that your final value exceeds your friend’s final value.
>
> ## 1. Optimal strategy
>
> If you keep $X$, then you win exactly when
>
> $$
> X > Y.
> $$
>
> Given $X=x$, this has probability
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(Y < x)=x.
> $$
>
> If instead you switch, then your final value is $Y$ and your friend gets $X$, so you win exactly when
>
> $$
> Y > X.
> $$
>
> Given $X=x$, this has probability
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(Y > x)=1-x.
> $$
>
> Therefore, after observing $X=x$, you should choose the larger of $x$ and $1-x$:
>
> - keep if $x > \tfrac12$,
> - switch if $x < \tfrac12$,
> - either choice if $x=\tfrac12$.
>
> So the optimal winning probability conditional on $X=x$ is
>
> $$
> \max(x,1-x).
> $$
>
> ## 2. Overall winning probability
>
> Average over $X \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\text{win})=\int_0^1 \max(x,1-x)\,dx.
> $$
>
> Split at $x=\tfrac12$:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\text{win})
> = \int_0^{1/2} (1-x)\,dx + \int_{1/2}^1 x\,dx.
> $$
>
> Compute each integral:
>
> $$
> \int_0^{1/2} (1-x)\,dx
> = \left[x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^{1/2}
> = \frac12 - \frac18
> = \frac38,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \int_{1/2}^1 x\,dx
> = \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{1/2}^1
> = \frac12 - \frac18
> = \frac38.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\text{win})=\frac38+\frac38=\frac34.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Conclusion
>
> The optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \text{switch if } X<\frac12,\quad \text{keep if } X>\frac12.
> $$
>
> With this strategy, the probability of winning is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac34}.
> $$
