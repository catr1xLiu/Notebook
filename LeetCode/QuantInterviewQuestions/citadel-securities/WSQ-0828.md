> [!example] Question 828 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What happens if two features are highly collinear in Lasso regression?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=829)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> In Lasso regression, highly collinear features create instability in how the model assigns coefficients.
>
> ### Key effect
>
> Lasso solves
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta} \; \frac{1}{2n}\|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\beta\|_1.
> $$
>
> Because of the $\ell_1$ penalty, Lasso prefers sparse solutions. When two predictors are highly collinear, they contain nearly the same information, so the model can fit the data almost equally well using either one. As a result, Lasso often:
>
> - assigns a nonzero coefficient to **one** of the correlated features,
> - shrinks the other coefficient to **zero**,
> - or distributes weight between them in an unstable way as $\lambda$ or the sample changes slightly.
>
> ### Consequences
>
> 1. **Arbitrary variable selection**  
>    Among highly correlated features, Lasso tends to select one and discard the others, but the choice may be somewhat arbitrary.
>
> 2. **Coefficient instability**  
>    Small changes in the data can cause the selected feature to switch from one correlated predictor to another.
>
> 3. **Reduced interpretability**  
>    If the features are all meaningful, dropping some of them may make interpretation misleading, since exclusion does not necessarily mean irrelevance.
>
> 4. **Prediction may still be good**  
>    Even though coefficient estimates are unstable, predictive performance can remain strong because the correlated variables carry similar signal.
>
> ### Comparison with Ridge
>
> Unlike Lasso, Ridge regression uses an $\ell_2$ penalty,
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta} \; \frac{1}{2n}\|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\beta\|_2^2,
> $$
>
> and tends to **share the weight** across correlated variables rather than selecting only one. Thus Ridge is usually more stable under multicollinearity.
>
> ### Practical remedy
>
> If correlated features are expected, common alternatives are:
>
> - **Elastic Net**, which combines $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ penalties and tends to keep groups of correlated variables together,
> - feature grouping or dimension reduction,
> - removing redundant predictors based on domain knowledge.
>
> ### Final conclusion
>
> When two features are highly collinear in Lasso regression, the model typically selects one and suppresses the other, leading to unstable coefficient estimates and variable selection, even if prediction accuracy remains acceptable.
