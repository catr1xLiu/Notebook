> [!example] Question 878 · Citadel Securities
> ## Fundamental Analyst Intern Interview

If you flip 8 fair coins, what is the probability that more than 4 of them are heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=879)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $8$ fair coins are flipped. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n=8,p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X>4)=P(X=5)+P(X=6)+P(X=7)+P(X=8).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{8}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^8.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> P(X>4)=\left[\binom{8}{5}+\binom{8}{6}+\binom{8}{7}+\binom{8}{8}\right]\left(\tfrac12\right)^8.
> $$
>
> Compute the binomial coefficients:
>
> $$
> \binom{8}{5}=56,\quad \binom{8}{6}=28,\quad \binom{8}{7}=8,\quad \binom{8}{8}=1.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> P(X>4)=\frac{56+28+8+1}{256}=\frac{93}{256}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that more than $4$ of the $8$ coins are heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{93}{256}}.
> $$
