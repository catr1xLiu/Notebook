> [!example] Question 865 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quant Research Intern Interview

Given n random variables from the same distribution, where the correlation between each pair of two variables is the same, what is the range of possible values for the correlation?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=866)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Suppose $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ are random variables from the same distribution, and every pair has the same correlation:
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Corr}(X_i, X_j) = \rho \qquad \text{for } i \ne j.
> $$
>
> The goal is to determine the possible range of $\rho$.
>
> ## Step 1: Write the correlation matrix
>
> Since all variables have the same variance and each off-diagonal correlation is $\rho$, the correlation matrix is
>
> $$
> R =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \rho & \cdots & \rho \\
> \rho & 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho \\
> \rho & \rho & 1 & \cdots & \rho \\
> \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
> \rho & \rho & \rho & \cdots & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> This is called an equicorrelation matrix.
>
> ## Step 2: Use the fact that a correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite
>
> Any correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite. Therefore all eigenvalues of $R$ must be nonnegative.
>
> For this matrix, the eigenvalues are:
>
> - one eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = 1 + (n-1)\rho$
> - and the remaining $n-1$ eigenvalues $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = \cdots = \lambda_n = 1 - \rho$
>
> ## Step 3: Impose nonnegativity of the eigenvalues
>
> We require
>
> $$
> 1 + (n-1)\rho \ge 0
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 1 - \rho \ge 0.
> $$
>
> These give
>
> $$
> \rho \ge -\frac{1}{n-1}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Therefore the common pairwise correlation must satisfy
>
> $$
> \rho \ge -\frac{1}{n-1} \quad\text{and}\quad \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> So the range of the correlation is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\rho \in \left[-\frac{1}{n-1},\,1\right]}.
> $$
>
> ## Remarks
>
> - The upper bound $\rho = 1$ corresponds to all variables being perfectly positively correlated.
> - The lower bound $\rho = -\frac{1}{n-1}$ is the most negative value possible while keeping the correlation matrix valid, and it depends on $n$: it approaches $0$ as $n$ grows, since it becomes harder for every pair among more variables to be simultaneously very negatively correlated.
