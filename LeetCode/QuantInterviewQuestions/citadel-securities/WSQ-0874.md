> [!example] Question 874 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quantitative Researcher Intern Interview

What is the expected value of the maximum of N independent normally distributed variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=875)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1,\dots,X_N$ be independent and identically distributed normal random variables with
> $$
> X_i \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2).
> $$
> Define
> $$
> M_N=\max\{X_1,\dots,X_N\}.
> $$
> We seek $E[M_N]$.
>
> ## 1. Standardization
>
> Write each variable as
> $$
> X_i=\mu+\sigma Z_i,
> $$
> where $Z_i\sim N(0,1)$ are i.i.d. Then
> $$
> M_N=\mu+\sigma \max\{Z_1,\dots,Z_N\}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> E[M_N]=\mu+\sigma\,E[Z_{(N)}],
> $$
> where
> $$
> Z_{(N)}=\max\{Z_1,\dots,Z_N\}.
> $$
>
> Thus the problem reduces to finding the expectation of the maximum of $N$ standard normal variables.
>
> ## 2. Distribution of the maximum
>
> Let $\Phi$ and $\phi$ denote the standard normal cdf and pdf:
> $$
> \Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x \phi(t)\,dt,
> \qquad
> \phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}.
> $$
> Since the $Z_i$ are independent,
> $$
> P(Z_{(N)}\le x)=P(Z_1\le x,\dots,Z_N\le x)=\Phi(x)^N.
> $$
> Therefore the density of $Z_{(N)}$ is
> $$
> f_{Z_{(N)}}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\Phi(x)^N
> = N\,\phi(x)\,\Phi(x)^{N-1}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Expectation formula
>
> Using the density,
> $$
> E[Z_{(N)}]
> =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,f_{Z_{(N)}}(x)\,dx
> = N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\,\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> E[M_N]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\,\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx.
> $$
>
> This is the exact general expression. Equivalently, using the quantile transform,
> $$
> E[Z_{(N)}]
> =
> N\int_0^1 \Phi^{-1}(u)\,u^{N-1}\,du,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E[M_N]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_0^1 \Phi^{-1}(u)\,u^{N-1}\,du.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Closed form?
>
> For general $N$, there is no simple elementary closed form. The expectation is usually evaluated numerically.
>
> For small $N$, some exact values are known:
>
> - For $N=1$,
>   $$
>   E[M_1]=\mu.
>   $$
>
> - For $N=2$,
>   $$
>   E[Z_{(2)}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}},
>   $$
>   so
>   $$
>   E[M_2]=\mu+\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\pi}}.
>   $$
>
> ## 5. Large-$N$ approximation
>
> For large $N$, extreme-value theory gives
> $$
> E[Z_{(N)}]\approx \sqrt{2\ln N}
> -\frac{\ln\ln N+\ln(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> E[M_N]
> \approx
> \mu+\sigma\left(
> \sqrt{2\ln N}
> -\frac{\ln\ln N+\ln(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}
> \right).
> $$
> A more refined asymptotic includes Euler’s constant $\gamma$:
> $$
> E[Z_{(N)}]
> =
> b_N+\frac{\gamma}{a_N}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{a_N}\right),
> $$
> where
> $$
> a_N=\sqrt{2\ln N},
> \qquad
> b_N=\sqrt{2\ln N}-\frac{\ln\ln N+\ln(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If $X_1,\dots,X_N\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, then for
> $$
> M_N=\max\{X_1,\dots,X_N\},
> $$
> the expectation is
> $$
> E[M_N]
> =
> \mu+\sigma N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\phi(x)\,\Phi(x)^{N-1}\,dx,
> $$
> where $\phi$ and $\Phi$ are the standard normal pdf and cdf.
>
> Equivalently,
> $$
> E[M_N]
> =
> \mu+\sigma\,E[Z_{(N)}],
> \qquad
> E[Z_{(N)}]
> =
> N\int_0^1 \Phi^{-1}(u)\,u^{N-1}\,du.
> $$
> For large $N$,
> $$
> E[M_N]
> \approx
> \mu+\sigma\left(
> \sqrt{2\ln N}
> -\frac{\ln\ln N+\ln(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}}
> \right).
> $$
