> [!example] Question 881 · Citadel Securities
> ## Quant Developer Interview

You have a scale and 18 balls. One of the balls is slightly heavier than the rest, which are identical. What is the minimum number of times you need to use the scale to determine which ball is the heavy ball?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=882)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each weighing on a balance scale has $3$ possible outcomes:
>
> - left side heavier,
> - right side heavier,
> - balanced.
>
> Thus, with $k$ weighings, one can distinguish at most $3^k$ possibilities.
>
> Here there are $18$ possible candidates for the heavy ball, so we need the smallest $k$ such that
>
> $$
> 3^k \ge 18.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 3^2 = 9 < 18,\qquad 3^3 = 27 \ge 18,
> $$
>
> at least $3$ weighings are necessary.
>
> Now show that $3$ weighings are sufficient:
>
> - Divide the $18$ balls into $3$ groups of $6$.
> - Weigh $6$ against $6$.
>   - If one side is heavier, the heavy ball is in that group of $6$.
>   - If they balance, the heavy ball is in the remaining $6$.
> - Now divide those $6$ balls into $3$ groups of $2$ and weigh $2$ against $2$.
>   - Again, the heavier side (or the unweighed pair if balanced) contains the heavy ball.
> - Finally, from the remaining $2$ balls, weigh $1$ against $1$.
>   - The heavier one is the heavy ball.
>
> Therefore, the minimum number of weighings is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}.
> $$
