> [!example] Question 702 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability that X \> Y, where X is distributed as N(0, 2) and Y is distributed as N(0, 1)?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=703)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let  
> $$
> X \sim N(0,2), \qquad Y \sim N(0,1),
> $$
> and assume $X$ and $Y$ are independent. We want
> $$
> P(X>Y).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Rewrite the event
>
> The event $X>Y$ is equivalent to
> $$
> X-Y>0.
> $$
>
> So it suffices to find the distribution of $X-Y$.
>
> ## Step 2: Distribution of $X-Y$
>
> Since independent normal random variables are closed under linear combinations,
> $$
> X-Y \sim N\!\left(0-0,\;2+1\right)=N(0,3).
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> X-Y
> $$
> is a normal random variable with mean $0$ and variance $3$.
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the probability
>
> Because $X-Y \sim N(0,3)$ is centered at $0$, its distribution is symmetric about $0$. Therefore,
> $$
> P(X-Y>0)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Hence,
> $$
> P(X>Y)=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(X>Y)=\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
