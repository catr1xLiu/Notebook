> [!example] Question 714 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You have r red balls and w white balls in a bag. If you keep drawing balls from the bag until only balls of a single color remain (i.e., you run out of one color), what is the probability you run out of white balls first? Express your answer in terms of r and w.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=715)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the bag initially contain $r$ red balls and $w$ white balls, and suppose balls are drawn uniformly at random without replacement until one color is exhausted.
>
> We seek the probability that the white balls are exhausted first.
>
> ## Key observation
>
> The order in which the balls are drawn is a uniformly random ordering of the $r+w$ balls. The process stops when all balls of one color have appeared among the draws.
>
> White balls are exhausted first exactly when the **last white ball** appears before the **last red ball** in this random ordering.
>
> Equivalently, among the final ball of each color, the last one overall must be red.
>
> ## Symmetry via the last ball
>
> In a uniformly random ordering of all $r+w$ balls, every individual ball is equally likely to be the very last ball drawn. Since there are $r$ red balls and $w$ white balls,
>
> - the probability the last ball is red is $\dfrac{r}{r+w}$,
> - the probability the last ball is white is $\dfrac{w}{r+w}$.
>
> If the last ball is red, then all white balls must have been drawn earlier, so the white balls ran out first.
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{r}{r+w}}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability that you run out of white balls first is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{r}{r+w}}.
> $$
