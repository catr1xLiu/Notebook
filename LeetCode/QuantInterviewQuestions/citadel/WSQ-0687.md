> [!example] Question 687 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Given a matrix and the top-left and bottom-right coordinates of a submatrix, calculate the sum of the submatrix in (1) linear time and (2) constant time. You are allowed to preprocess the matrix.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=688)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Approach
>
> To answer submatrix-sum queries efficiently, preprocess the matrix into a **2D prefix-sum array** (also called an **integral image**).
>
> Let the matrix be $A$ with dimensions $m \times n$, and suppose a query asks for the sum of entries in the rectangle with:
>
> - top-left corner $(r_1, c_1)$
> - bottom-right corner $(r_2, c_2)$
>
> where indices are inclusive.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Linear-time query
>
> Without preprocessing, the submatrix sum is obtained by iterating through all cells in the rectangle:
>
> $$
> \text{sum}=\sum_{i=r_1}^{r_2}\sum_{j=c_1}^{c_2} A[i][j]
> $$
>
> This takes time proportional to the number of elements in the queried rectangle:
>
> $$
> O\big((r_2-r_1+1)(c_2-c_1+1)\big)
> $$
>
> In the worst case, this is $O(mn)$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Constant-time query with preprocessing
>
> ### Prefix-sum definition
>
> Construct a matrix $P$ of size $(m+1)\times(n+1)$ such that:
>
> $$
> P[i][j] = \sum_{r=0}^{i-1}\sum_{c=0}^{j-1} A[r][c]
> $$
>
> Thus, $P[i][j]$ stores the sum of the rectangle from $(0,0)$ to $(i-1,j-1)$.
>
> To simplify boundary cases, define:
>
> $$
> P[0][j]=0,\qquad P[i][0]=0
> $$
>
> ### Preprocessing recurrence
>
> For $1 \le i \le m$ and $1 \le j \le n$:
>
> $$
> P[i][j] = A[i-1][j-1] + P[i-1][j] + P[i][j-1] - P[i-1][j-1]
> $$
>
> ### Why this works
>
> - $P[i-1][j]$ includes everything above,
> - $P[i][j-1]$ includes everything to the left,
> - their overlap $P[i-1][j-1]$ is counted twice, so subtract it once,
> - then add the current element $A[i-1][j-1]$.
>
> Preprocessing takes:
>
> $$
> O(mn)
> $$
>
> time and
>
> $$
> O(mn)
> $$
>
> space.
>
> ---
>
> ## Constant-time query formula
>
> Using the prefix sums, the sum of the submatrix from $(r_1,c_1)$ to $(r_2,c_2)$ is:
>
> $$
> \text{sum}(r_1,c_1,r_2,c_2)
> =
> P[r_2+1][c_2+1]
> -
> P[r_1][c_2+1]
> -
> P[r_2+1][c_1]
> +
> P[r_1][c_1]
> $$
>
> ### Derivation
>
> - $P[r_2+1][c_2+1]$ gives the sum from $(0,0)$ to $(r_2,c_2)$.
> - Subtract the area above the query rectangle:
>   $$
>   P[r_1][c_2+1]
>   $$
> - Subtract the area to the left:
>   $$
>   P[r_2+1][c_1]
>   $$
> - The top-left overlap was subtracted twice, so add it back:
>   $$
>   P[r_1][c_1]
>   $$
>
> Thus each query is answered in:
>
> $$
> O(1)
> $$
>
> time.
>
> ---
>
> ## Example
>
> Suppose
>
> $$
> A=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> 1 & 2 & 3 \\
> 4 & 5 & 6 \\
> 7 & 8 & 9
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$
>
> The prefix-sum array $P$ is:
>
> $$
> P=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
> 0 & 1 & 3 & 6 \\
> 0 & 5 & 12 & 21 \\
> 0 & 12 & 27 & 45
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$
>
> To find the sum of the submatrix from $(1,1)$ to $(2,2)$, namely
>
> $$
> \begin{bmatrix}
> 5 & 6 \\
> 8 & 9
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$
>
> use:
>
> $$
> P[3][3]-P[1][3]-P[3][1]+P[1][1]
> =45-6-12+1=28
> $$
>
> which is correct since:
>
> $$
> 5+6+8+9=28
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - **Linear-time method:** directly sum all entries in the queried rectangle:
>   $$
>   O\big((r_2-r_1+1)(c_2-c_1+1)\big)
>   $$
> - **Constant-time method:** preprocess a $2$D prefix-sum array in
>   $$
>   O(mn)
>   $$
>   time, then answer each query by
>   $$
>   \text{sum}(r_1,c_1,r_2,c_2)
>   =
>   P[r_2+1][c_2+1]
>   -
>   P[r_1][c_2+1]
>   -
>   P[r_2+1][c_1]
>   +
>   P[r_1][c_1]
>   $$
>   in
>   $$
>   O(1)
>   $$
>   time.
