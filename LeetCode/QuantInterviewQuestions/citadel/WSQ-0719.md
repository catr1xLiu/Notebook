> [!example] Question 719 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the expected number of coin flips required to see 2 heads in a series of fair coin tosses?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=720)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E_k$ denote the expected number of additional flips needed to obtain a total of $2$ heads, given that $k$ heads have already been seen, where $k=0,1,2$.
>
> Clearly,
> $$
> E_2 = 0,
> $$
> since no more flips are needed once $2$ heads have occurred.
>
> We now set up equations for $E_1$ and $E_0$.
>
> ### Step 1: Compute $E_1$
>
> If $1$ head has already been seen, then on the next flip:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, a head occurs, and the process ends;
> - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, a tail occurs, and we are still in the state of having seen $1$ head.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_2 + \frac{1}{2}E_1.
> $$
> Substituting $E_2=0$ gives
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_1.
> $$
> Hence,
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}E_1 = 1,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_1 = 2.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Compute $E_0$
>
> If no heads have yet been seen, then on the next flip:
>
> - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, a head occurs, and we move to state $E_1$;
> - with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, a tail occurs, and we remain in state $E_0$.
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
> Substitute $E_1=2$:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}(2) + \frac{1}{2}E_0
> = 2 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
> Thus,
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}E_0 = 2,
> $$
> and so
> $$
> E_0 = 4.
> $$
>
> ### Conclusion
>
> The expected number of flips needed to see $2$ heads is
> $$
> \boxed{4}.
> $$
