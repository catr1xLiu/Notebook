> [!example] Question 785 · Citadel
> ## Internship Interview

Calculate the Sharpe ratio of a 60/40 coin toss where each toss results in a gain of \$1 or a loss of \$1, respectively.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=786)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For a single fair coin toss, let the payoff $X$ be
>
> $$
> X =
> \begin{cases}
> +1, & \text{with probability } \frac{1}{2},\\
> -1, & \text{with probability } \frac{1}{2}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The Sharpe ratio is
>
> $$
> \text{Sharpe} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X] - r_f}{\sigma_X},
> $$
>
> where $r_f$ is the risk-free rate. If no risk-free rate is specified, take $r_f = 0$.
>
> Compute the mean:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X] = \frac{1}{2}(1) + \frac{1}{2}(-1) = 0.
> $$
>
> Compute the variance:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X^2] = \frac{1}{2}(1^2) + \frac{1}{2}((-1)^2) = 1,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2 = 1 - 0^2 = 1.
> $$
>
> Hence the standard deviation is
>
> $$
> \sigma_X = \sqrt{1} = 1.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \text{Sharpe} = \frac{0}{1} = 0.
> $$
>
> Thus, the Sharpe ratio is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> If instead “$60/40$ coin toss” means a biased coin with probability $0.6$ of $+1$ and probability $0.4$ of $-1$, then
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X] = 0.6(1) + 0.4(-1) = 0.2,
> $$
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[X^2] = 1,
> \qquad
> \mathrm{Var}(X) = 1 - (0.2)^2 = 0.96,
> $$
>
> $$
> \sigma_X = \sqrt{0.96}.
> $$
>
> So the Sharpe ratio would be
>
> $$
> \text{Sharpe} = \frac{0.2}{\sqrt{0.96}} \approx 0.204.
> $$
>
> Hence:
>
> - for a fair $50/50$ coin toss: $\boxed{0}$,
> - for a $60/40$ favorable coin toss: $\boxed{0.204 \text{ (approximately)}}$.
