> [!example] Question 796 · Citadel
> ## Quant Research Intern Interview

Sample points randomly from a unit sphere. If you generate independent standard normal variables X1, X2, X3 for the three coordinates and normalize by dividing by sqrt(X1^2 + X2^2 + X3^2), then (X1, X2, X3) / sqrt(X1^2 + X2^2 + X3^2) is a uniform random point on the sphere. What is the variance of each coordinate of the resulting uniform distribution on the sphere?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=797)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let
> $$
> Z=(X_1,X_2,X_3),
> $$
> where $X_1,X_2,X_3$ are independent standard normal random variables, $X_i\sim N(0,1)$.
>
> To obtain a point uniformly distributed on the **unit sphere** in $\mathbb{R}^3$, one normalizes by the Euclidean norm:
> $$
> U=\frac{Z}{\|Z\|}=\frac{(X_1,X_2,X_3)}{\sqrt{X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2}}.
> $$
> (So the denominator must be $\sqrt{X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2}$, not $\sqrt{X_1+X_2+X_3}$.)
>
> Then $U=(U_1,U_2,U_3)$ is uniformly distributed on the surface of the unit sphere
> $$
> S^2=\{(x,y,z):x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}.
> $$
>
> ## Mean and variance of each coordinate
>
> By symmetry, each coordinate has the same distribution, and also
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[U_i]=0, \qquad i=1,2,3.
> $$
>
> Since $U$ lies on the unit sphere,
> $$
> U_1^2+U_2^2+U_3^2=1.
> $$
> Taking expectations gives
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[U_1^2]+\mathbb{E}[U_2^2]+\mathbb{E}[U_3^2]=1.
> $$
> Again by symmetry,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[U_1^2]=\mathbb{E}[U_2^2]=\mathbb{E}[U_3^2].
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> 3\,\mathbb{E}[U_1^2]=1,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[U_1^2]=\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(U_1)=\mathbb{E}[U_1^2]-\bigl(\mathbb{E}[U_1]\bigr)^2
> =\frac{1}{3}-0
> =\frac{1}{3}.
> $$
> The same holds for $U_2$ and $U_3$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If
> $$
> U=\frac{(X_1,X_2,X_3)}{\sqrt{X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2}},
> $$
> then $U$ is uniform on the unit sphere, and each coordinate has variance
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{3}}.
> $$
