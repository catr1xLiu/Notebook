> [!example] Question 720 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

The chance that a student passes a test is 10%. What is the probability that at least 50 out of 400 students pass the test? Choose the closest answer from: 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=721)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of students who pass. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n=400,p=0.1).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 50).
> $$
>
> ## 1. Mean and standard deviation
>
> For a binomial random variable,
>
> $$
> \mu = np = 400 \cdot 0.1 = 40,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)}=\sqrt{400\cdot 0.1\cdot 0.9}=\sqrt{36}=6.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Normal approximation with continuity correction
>
> Since $n$ is large, approximate $X$ by a normal random variable:
>
> $$
> X \approx N(40,6^2).
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 50) \approx P\left(Y \ge 49.5\right),
> $$
>
> where $Y \sim N(40,6^2)$.
>
> Standardizing,
>
> $$
> P(Y \ge 49.5)=P\left(Z \ge \frac{49.5-40}{6}\right)
> = P(Z \ge 1.5833).
> $$
>
> From the standard normal table,
>
> $$
> P(Z \ge 1.58) \approx 0.057.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(X \ge 50) \approx 5.7\%.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Closest offered answer
>
> Among $5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%, 25\%$, the closest is
>
> $$
> \boxed{5\%}.
> $$
