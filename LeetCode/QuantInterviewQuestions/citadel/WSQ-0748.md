> [!example] Question 748 · Citadel
> ## Trader Interview

What is the probability of picking two diamonds and one card that is not a diamond from a standard deck of 52 cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=749)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> From a standard deck of $52$ cards, there are $13$ diamonds and $39$ non-diamonds.
>
> We want the probability that in drawing $3$ cards, exactly $2$ are diamonds and $1$ is not a diamond.
>
> The total number of ways to choose $3$ cards from $52$ is
>
> $$
> \binom{52}{3}.
> $$
>
> The number of favorable outcomes is:
>
> - choose $2$ diamonds from $13$: $\binom{13}{2}$,
> - choose $1$ non-diamond from $39$: $\binom{39}{1}$.
>
> Thus the number of favorable selections is
>
> $$
> \binom{13}{2}\binom{39}{1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{\binom{13}{2}\binom{39}{1}}{\binom{52}{3}}.
> $$
>
> Now compute:
>
> $$
> \binom{13}{2} = 78,\qquad \binom{39}{1} = 39,\qquad \binom{52}{3} = 22100.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \frac{78\cdot 39}{22100} = \frac{3042}{22100} = \frac{117}{850}.
> $$
>
> Hence the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{117}{850}}.
> $$
>
> In decimal form,
>
> $$
> \frac{117}{850} \approx 0.1376.
> $$
