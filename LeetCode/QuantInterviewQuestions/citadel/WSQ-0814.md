> [!example] Question 814 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Intern Interview

Two people plan to meet at a train station. Each arrives at a random time between 4:00 pm and 5:00 pm and will wait 10 minutes for the other. What is the probability that they will meet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=815)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be the arrival times of the two people, measured in minutes after $4{:}00\text{ pm}$. Assume
>
> $$
> X, Y \sim \text{Uniform}(0,60)
> $$
>
> independently.
>
> They meet if the difference in their arrival times is at most $10$ minutes, that is,
>
> $$
> |X-Y| \le 10.
> $$
>
> ## Geometric interpretation
>
> The pair $(X,Y)$ is uniformly distributed over the square
>
> $$
> [0,60]\times[0,60],
> $$
>
> whose total area is
>
> $$
> 60\cdot 60 = 3600.
> $$
>
> They fail to meet when one arrives more than $10$ minutes after the other, i.e.
>
> $$
> |X-Y|>10.
> $$
>
> In the square, this corresponds to the two corner triangles outside the band between the lines
>
> $$
> y=x+10 \quad \text{and} \quad y=x-10.
> $$
>
> Each triangle has side length
>
> $$
> 60-10=50,
> $$
>
> so each has area
>
> $$
> \frac12\cdot 50\cdot 50 = 1250.
> $$
>
> Thus the total area where they do **not** meet is
>
> $$
> 1250+1250=2500.
> $$
>
> Therefore the area where they **do** meet is
>
> $$
> 3600-2500=1100.
> $$
>
> Since the distribution is uniform, the desired probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{1100}{3600}=\frac{11}{36}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{36}}
> $$
>
> So the probability that they meet is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{11}{36}\approx 0.3056}.
> $$
