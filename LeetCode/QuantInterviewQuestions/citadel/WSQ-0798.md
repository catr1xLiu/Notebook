> [!example] Question 798 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

How do you generate a uniform distribution over a disk given access to two independent uniform random variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=799)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To generate a point uniformly over a disk using two independent uniform random variables, use one variable for the angle and the other for the radius, but transform the radius correctly.
>
> ## Step 1: Why the radius cannot be chosen uniformly
>
> Suppose the disk has radius $R$.  
> If one chooses
>
> - $\Theta \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,2\pi)$,
> - $R' \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,R)$,
>
> and then sets
> $$
> X = R' \cos \Theta, \qquad Y = R' \sin \Theta,
> $$
> the resulting distribution is **not** uniform on the disk.
>
> The reason is that area in polar coordinates is
> $$
> dA = r \, dr \, d\theta.
> $$
> There is more area near the outer edge than near the center, so a radius chosen uniformly in $[0,R]$ puts too much mass near the center.
>
> ## Step 2: Determine the correct radial distribution
>
> For a point to be uniform on the disk of radius $R$, the probability of falling inside a smaller disk of radius $r$ must equal the ratio of areas:
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\rho \le r) = \frac{\pi r^2}{\pi R^2} = \frac{r^2}{R^2},
> \qquad 0 \le r \le R.
> $$
> Thus the cumulative distribution function of the radius $\rho$ must be
> $$
> F_\rho(r) = \frac{r^2}{R^2}.
> $$
> If $U \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, then setting
> $$
> \rho = R\sqrt{U}
> $$
> gives
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(\rho \le r)
> = \mathbb{P}(R\sqrt{U} \le r)
> = \mathbb{P}\!\left(U \le \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)
> = \frac{r^2}{R^2},
> $$
> which is exactly the desired distribution.
>
> ## Step 3: Use the second uniform variable for the angle
>
> Let $V \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$ independently. Define
> $$
> \Theta = 2\pi V.
> $$
> Then $\Theta$ is uniform on $[0,2\pi)$.
>
> ## Step 4: Convert to Cartesian coordinates
>
> Set
> $$
> X = R\sqrt{U}\cos(2\pi V), \qquad
> Y = R\sqrt{U}\sin(2\pi V).
> $$
> Then $(X,Y)$ is uniformly distributed over the disk
> $$
> \{(x,y) : x^2 + y^2 \le R^2\}.
> $$
>
> ## Final construction
>
> Given independent random variables $U,V \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, a uniform point on the disk of radius $R$ is
> $$
> (X,Y) = \left(R\sqrt{U}\cos(2\pi V),\; R\sqrt{U}\sin(2\pi V)\right).
> $$
>
> For the unit disk $(R=1)$, this simplifies to
> $$
> (X,Y) = \left(\sqrt{U}\cos(2\pi V),\; \sqrt{U}\sin(2\pi V)\right).
> $$
