> [!example] Question 698 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

There are three random variables, X, Y, Z. The correlations between each pair of variables are the same, i.e., ρ = Corr(X,Y) = Corr(Y,Z) = Corr(Z,X). What is the tightest bound you can give for ρ? How about the general case for n random variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=699)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the common pairwise correlation be $\rho$. Then the correlation matrix of $(X,Y,Z)$ is
>
> $$
> R_3=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \rho\\
> \rho & 1 & \rho\\
> \rho & \rho & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> A correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite, so all eigenvalues of $R_3$ must be nonnegative.
>
> ## Case of three variables
>
> For the matrix with $1$ on the diagonal and $\rho$ off the diagonal, the eigenvalues are
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \quad \text{(with multiplicity $2$)}, \qquad 1+2\rho \quad \text{(with multiplicity $1$)}.
> $$
>
> Thus positive semidefiniteness requires
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \ge 0
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> 1+2\rho \ge 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \rho \le 1
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \rho \ge -\frac12.
> $$
>
> Hence the tightest possible bound is
>
> $$
> -\frac12 \le \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> This bound is sharp: every $\rho$ in this interval yields a positive semidefinite matrix, so such a correlation structure is attainable.
>
> ---
>
> ## General case of $n$ variables
>
> If $X_1,\dots,X_n$ have the same pairwise correlation $\rho$, then the correlation matrix is
>
> $$
> R_n=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho\\
> \rho & 1 & \cdots & \rho\\
> \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
> \rho & \rho & \cdots & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> R_n=(1-\rho)I_n+\rho J_n,
> $$
>
> where $I_n$ is the identity matrix and $J_n$ is the all-ones matrix.
>
> The eigenvalues of $R_n$ are:
>
> - $1-\rho$, with multiplicity $n-1$,
> - $1+(n-1)\rho$, with multiplicity $1$.
>
> So positive semidefiniteness gives
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \ge 0
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> 1+(n-1)\rho \ge 0.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> \rho \le 1
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \rho \ge -\frac{1}{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the tightest bound in the general case is
>
> $$
> -\frac{1}{n-1} \le \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> So:
>
> - for $3$ variables: $\displaystyle -\frac12 \le \rho \le 1$,
> - for $n$ variables: $\displaystyle -\frac{1}{n-1} \le \rho \le 1$.
