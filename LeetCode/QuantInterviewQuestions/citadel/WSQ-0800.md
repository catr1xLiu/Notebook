> [!example] Question 800 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Given a matrix of size N by T, where the entry at the i-th row and j-th column represents the price of buying i shares of a stock at time j, determine the minimum total cost of purchasing N shares by the end of time T.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=801)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the given matrix be $C$ of size $N \times T$, where $C[i][j]$ denotes the cost of buying exactly $i$ shares at time $j$.
>
> We want the minimum total cost to buy exactly $N$ shares using the times $1,2,\dots,T$, where at each time we may buy any number of shares, and the total bought over all times must be $N$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Dynamic programming formulation
>
> Define
>
> $$
> dp[i][j]
> $$
>
> to be the minimum cost of buying exactly $i$ shares using only the first $j$ times.
>
> ### Base cases
>
> - Buying $0$ shares costs nothing:
>
> $$
> dp[0][j] = 0 \qquad \text{for all } j
> $$
>
> - If only the first time is available, then the only way to buy $i$ shares is to buy them all at time $1$:
>
> $$
> dp[i][1] = C[i][1] \qquad \text{for } 1 \le i \le N
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Transition
>
> For $i \ge 1$ and $j \ge 2$, suppose we buy $k$ shares at time $j$, where $0 \le k \le i$.
>
> Then:
>
> - the cost of buying $k$ shares at time $j$ is $C[k][j]$,
> - the remaining $i-k$ shares must be bought in the first $j-1$ times, with cost $dp[i-k][j-1]$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> dp[i][j] = \min_{0 \le k \le i} \left( dp[i-k][j-1] + C[k][j] \right)
> $$
>
> where we interpret
>
> $$
> C[0][j] = 0
> $$
>
> for all $j$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The minimum cost of buying exactly $N$ shares by the end of time $T$ is
>
> $$
> dp[N][T]
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Algorithm
>
> Compute the table $dp$ for all $0 \le i \le N$ and $1 \le j \le T$ using the recurrence above.
>
> ### Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> for j = 1 to T:
>     dp[0][j] = 0
>
> for i = 1 to N:
>     dp[i][1] = C[i][1]
>
> for j = 2 to T:
>     for i = 1 to N:
>         dp[i][j] = infinity
>         for k = 0 to i:
>             dp[i][j] = min(dp[i][j], dp[i-k][j-1] + C[k][j])
>
> answer = dp[N][T]
> ```
>
> ---
>
> ## Complexity
>
> There are $N \times T$ states, and each state tries all $k$ from $0$ to $i$.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - Time complexity:
>
> $$
> O(TN^2)
> $$
>
> - Space complexity:
>
> $$
> O(TN)
> $$
>
> which can be reduced to
>
> $$
> O(N)
> $$
>
> by keeping only the previous column of the dynamic programming table.
>
> ---
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The problem is solved by dynamic programming with recurrence
>
> $$
> dp[i][j] = \min_{0 \le k \le i} \left( dp[i-k][j-1] + C[k][j] \right),
> $$
>
> and the minimum total cost is
>
> $$
> \boxed{dp[N][T]}.
> $$
