> [!example] Question 774 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Describe an algorithm for calculating the square root of x.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=775)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Square-root algorithm
>
> A standard method for computing $\sqrt{x}$ for $x \ge 0$ is **Newton's method** applied to the equation
>
> $$
> f(y) = y^2 - x = 0.
> $$
>
> The positive root of this equation is $\sqrt{x}$.
>
> ### Derivation
>
> Newton's iteration for solving $f(y)=0$ is
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = y_n - \frac{f(y_n)}{f'(y_n)}.
> $$
>
> Here,
>
> $$
> f(y) = y^2 - x, \qquad f'(y) = 2y.
> $$
>
> Substituting gives
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = y_n - \frac{y_n^2 - x}{2y_n}
> = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right).
> $$
>
> Thus the iteration is
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right).
> $$
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> Given $x \ge 0$:
>
> 1. If $x = 0$, return $0$.
> 2. Choose an initial guess $y_0 > 0$.
>    - A simple choice is $y_0 = x$ if $x \ge 1$ and $y_0 = 1$ if $0 < x < 1$.
> 3. Repeat:
>    $$
>    y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right)
>    $$
>    until the change is sufficiently small, for example until
>    $$
>    |y_{n+1} - y_n| < \varepsilon
>    $$
>    for a prescribed tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$.
> 4. Output $y_{n+1}$ as an approximation to $\sqrt{x}$.
>
> ### Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> function sqrt(x, ε):
>     if x == 0:
>         return 0
>
>     if x >= 1:
>         y = x
>     else:
>         y = 1
>
>     repeat:
>         y_new = (y + x / y) / 2
>         if |y_new - y| < ε:
>             return y_new
>         y = y_new
> ```
>
> ### Why it works
>
> If $y_n > 0$, then every iterate remains positive. Moreover, once the estimate is reasonably close, Newton's method converges very rapidly: the number of correct digits roughly doubles at each step. This makes the method efficient in practice.
>
> ### Example
>
> To compute $\sqrt{10}$, start with $y_0 = 10$:
>
> $$
> y_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(10 + \frac{10}{10}\right) = \frac{11}{2} = 5.5,
> $$
>
> $$
> y_2 = \frac{1}{2}\left(5.5 + \frac{10}{5.5}\right) \approx 3.6591,
> $$
>
> $$
> y_3 = \frac{1}{2}\left(3.6591 + \frac{10}{3.6591}\right) \approx 3.1960,
> $$
>
> $$
> y_4 \approx 3.1625,
> \qquad
> y_5 \approx 3.1623.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> \sqrt{10} \approx 3.1623,
> $$
>
> the method quickly approaches the correct value.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> An effective algorithm for calculating $\sqrt{x}$ is Newton's iteration
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right),
> $$
>
> starting from any positive initial guess $y_0$, and repeating until successive values differ by less than a chosen tolerance.
