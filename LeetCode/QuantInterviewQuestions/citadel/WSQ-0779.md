> [!example] Question 779 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

You have a worker and a gold bar. The worker will work for you for 15 days. He must have x/15 of the gold bar after the xth day of work. What is the minimum number of cuts needed to divide the gold bar so that you can pay the worker accordingly?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=780)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To be able to pay the worker correctly at the end of each day, one may both **give** and **take back** pieces, as long as after day $x$ the worker possesses exactly $\frac{x}{15}$ of the bar.
>
> Thus the problem is equivalent to choosing piece sizes so that every amount
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{15},\frac{2}{15},\dots,\frac{15}{15}
> $$
>
> can be formed by distributing some pieces to the worker and possibly exchanging previously given pieces.
>
> It is convenient to scale the whole bar to have total value $15$. Then we seek a partition of $15$ into as few pieces as possible such that every integer amount from $1$ to $15$ can be made.
>
> Each cut increases the number of pieces by $1$, so minimizing cuts is the same as minimizing the number of pieces.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> If the pieces are arranged in increasing order
>
> $$
> a_1 \le a_2 \le \cdots \le a_n,
> $$
>
> then to be able to make every amount from $1$ up to $15$, the optimal choice is obtained by making each new piece at most one more than the sum of all previous pieces:
>
> $$
> a_{k} \le 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} a_i.
> $$
>
> To maximize the reachable total with $n$ pieces, we therefore choose
>
> $$
> a_1 = 1,\quad a_2 = 2,\quad a_3 = 4,\quad a_4 = 8,
> $$
>
> which gives total
>
> $$
> 1+2+4+8=15.
> $$
>
> Thus with $4$ pieces, namely
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{15},\ \frac{2}{15},\ \frac{4}{15},\ \frac{8}{15},
> $$
>
> every amount from $\frac{1}{15}$ to $\frac{15}{15}$ can be formed.
>
> For example:
>
> - day $1$: give $\frac{1}{15}$
> - day $2$: take back $\frac{1}{15}$, give $\frac{2}{15}$
> - day $3$: give $\frac{1}{15}$
> - day $4$: take back $\frac{1}{15}$ and $\frac{2}{15}$, give $\frac{4}{15}$
> - etc.
>
> All totals from $1$ to $15$ are representable in binary.
>
> ## Why fewer pieces are impossible
>
> With only $3$ pieces, the largest total achievable under the same optimal construction is
>
> $$
> 1+2+4=7 < 15.
> $$
>
> So $3$ pieces cannot suffice.
>
> Hence the minimum number of pieces is $4$, and therefore the minimum number of cuts is
>
> $$
> 4-1=3.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}
> $$
