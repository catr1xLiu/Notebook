> [!example] Question 820 · Citadel
> ## Trading Intern Interview

If 0.1% of people have a certain disease, and you have a test that is correct 99% of the time, what is the probability that someone who tests positive actually has the disease?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=821)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D$ denote the event that a person has the disease, and let $+$ denote the event that the test is positive.
>
> We are given:
>
> - Prevalence:  
>   $$
>   P(D) = 0.001
>   $$
> - Therefore,  
>   $$
>   P(D^c) = 0.999
>   $$
> - The test is $99\%$ correct, meaning:
>   $$
>   P(+ \mid D) = 0.99
>   $$
>   $$
>   P(- \mid D^c) = 0.99
>   $$
>   so the false positive rate is
>   $$
>   P(+ \mid D^c) = 0.01
>   $$
>
> We want to find
> $$
> P(D \mid +)
> $$
> using Bayes' theorem:
> $$
> P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D)P(D)}{P(+)}
> $$
>
> Now compute $P(+)$ by the law of total probability:
> $$
> P(+) = P(+ \mid D)P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)P(D^c)
> $$
> so
> $$
> P(+) = (0.99)(0.001) + (0.01)(0.999)
> $$
> $$
> P(+) = 0.00099 + 0.00999 = 0.01098
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P(D \mid +) = \frac{0.99 \cdot 0.001}{0.01098}
> = \frac{0.00099}{0.01098}
> \approx 0.0902
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that someone who tests positive actually has the disease is
> $$
> \boxed{0.0902 \approx 9.0\%}
> $$
>
> So despite the test being $99\%$ accurate, a positive result means the person has the disease only about $9\%$ of the time, because the disease is very rare.
