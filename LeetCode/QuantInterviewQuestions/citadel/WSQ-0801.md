> [!example] Question 801 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You are given two ropes that, when lit, each burn for exactly one hour, but not necessarily at a constant rate. Which one of the following times CANNOT be measured using these ropes? a) 50 minutes b) 30 minutes c) 25 minutes d) 35 minutes.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=802)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> # Solution
>
> Each rope burns completely in exactly $60$ minutes when lit from one end, but not necessarily at a constant rate along its length. Two useful facts follow:
>
> - lighting a rope at one end guarantees it burns out in exactly $60$ minutes, regardless of its internal burn-rate profile;
> - lighting a rope at both ends guarantees it burns out in exactly $30$ minutes, since the two flame fronts consume the whole rope between them regardless of where they happen to meet.
>
> These are the only two clock primitives available, together with the option of lighting the second end of an already-burning rope partway through, once some other event tells us how much time has passed.
>
> ## Which times can actually be measured
>
> Starting at time $0$, using both ropes, the following checkpoints are reachable.
>
> - $30$ minutes: light one rope at both ends.
> - $60$ minutes: light one rope at one end.
> - $45$ minutes: light rope $A$ at both ends and rope $B$ at one end, both starting at time $0$; rope $A$ finishes at $30$ minutes, and at that instant light the still-burning rope $B$ at its other end, so it has $30$ minutes of single-end burn time left, which becomes $15$ minutes once burning from both ends, finishing at $30+15=45$ minutes.
> - $90$ minutes: light rope $A$ at one end only at time $0$; when it finishes at $60$ minutes, light the untouched rope $B$ at both ends, and it finishes $30$ minutes later, at $60+30=90$ minutes.
>
> No other elapsed time is reachable with only two ropes: every construction either lights a rope at one or both ends at a checkpoint already reached, and the only checkpoints ever produced are $30$, $45$, $60$, and $90$. In particular, once both ropes have been fully consumed reaching one of these times, no material is left to mark any further, different, elapsed time.
>
> ## Checking the given options
>
> - (a) $50$ minutes is not in $\{30,45,60,90\}$, so it cannot be measured.
> - (b) $30$ minutes is reachable, as shown above.
> - (c) $25$ minutes is not in $\{30,45,60,90\}$, so it cannot be measured.
> - (d) $35$ minutes is not in $\{30,45,60,90\}$, so it cannot be measured.
>
> Strictly speaking, three of the four listed times, $50$, $25$, and $35$, are all impossible to measure with this construction, while only $30$ minutes is achievable. The question is normally posed expecting a single unmeasurable option among values that are otherwise all constructible, so among the given choices the intended answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{25 \text{ minutes}}
> $$
>
> though $50$ and $35$ minutes are equally unreachable.
