> [!example] Question 728 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Find the minimum value of the function f(x) = x^x for real x \> 0.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=729)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the function
> $$
> f(x)=x^x,
> $$
> which is defined for $x>0$.
>
> To find its minimum, differentiate. It is convenient to take logarithms first:
> $$
> y=x^x \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \ln y = x\ln x.
> $$
> Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
> $$
> \frac{y'}{y}=\ln x+1.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> y'=x^x(\ln x+1).
> $$
>
> Critical points occur when
> $$
> x^x(\ln x+1)=0.
> $$
> Since $x^x>0$ for $x>0$, this gives
> $$
> \ln x+1=0,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \ln x=-1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x=e^{-1}=\frac{1}{e}.
> $$
>
> Now determine whether this gives a minimum. Since $x^x>0$, the sign of $f'(x)$ is the sign of $\ln x+1$:
> - if $0<x<\frac{1}{e}$, then $\ln x<-1$, so $f'(x)<0$;
> - if $x>\frac{1}{e}$, then $\ln x>-1$, so $f'(x)>0$.
>
> Thus $f(x)$ decreases on $\left(0,\frac{1}{e}\right)$ and increases on $\left(\frac{1}{e},\infty\right)$, so the minimum occurs at
> $$
> x=\frac{1}{e}.
> $$
>
> The minimum value is
> $$
> f\!\left(\frac{1}{e}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{e}\right)^{1/e}=e^{-1/e}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the minimum of $x^x$ is
> $$
> \boxed{e^{-1/e}}
> $$
> attained at
> $$
> \boxed{x=\frac{1}{e}}.
> $$
