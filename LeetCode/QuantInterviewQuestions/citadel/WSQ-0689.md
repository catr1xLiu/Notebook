> [!example] Question 689 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

You have a worker and a gold bar. The worker will work for you for 15 days. He must have x/15 of the gold bar after the xth day of work. What is the minimum number of cuts you need to make to the gold bar to pay the worker accordingly?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=690)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To be able to pay the worker correctly at the end of each day, the pieces of the gold bar must allow every amount
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{15},\frac{2}{15},\frac{3}{15},\dots,\frac{15}{15}
> $$
>
> to be formed, possibly by giving and taking back pieces as payment is adjusted from one day to the next.
>
> Thus the problem is to partition the bar into as few pieces as possible so that every integer amount from $1$ to $15$ can be represented as a sum of some of the piece-sizes.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> If the pieces have sizes
>
> $$
> a_1 \le a_2 \le \cdots \le a_n,
> $$
>
> then to make all amounts from $1$ up to $15$, the most efficient choice is to use powers of $2$:
>
> $$
> 1,2,4,8.
> $$
>
> These sum to
>
> $$
> 1+2+4+8=15,
> $$
>
> and with them one can form every integer from $1$ to $15$.
>
> Since $4$ pieces suffice, the number of cuts needed is
>
> $$
> 4-1=3.
> $$
>
> ## Why fewer cuts cannot work
>
> With only $2$ cuts, there are $3$ pieces. Even in the best case, to maximize the number of representable sums, the pieces should grow as
>
> $$
> 1,2,4,
> $$
>
> which gives total
>
> $$
> 1+2+4=7,
> $$
>
> so $3$ pieces cannot cover all amounts up to $15$.
>
> Equivalently, with $n$ pieces, the largest total for which all amounts from $1$ up to that total can be formed is achieved by
>
> $$
> 1,2,4,\dots,2^{n-1},
> $$
>
> whose sum is
>
> $$
> 2^n-1.
> $$
>
> To cover $15$, we need
>
> $$
> 2^n-1 \ge 15 \implies 2^n \ge 16 \implies n \ge 4.
> $$
>
> So at least $4$ pieces are necessary, hence at least $3$ cuts.
>
> ## Payment schedule
>
> Cut the bar into pieces of sizes
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{15},\frac{2}{15},\frac{4}{15},\frac{8}{15}.
> $$
>
> Then daily payments can be made by exchanging pieces as needed:
>
> - Day $1$: give $\frac{1}{15}$
> - Day $2$: take back $\frac{1}{15}$, give $\frac{2}{15}$
> - Day $3$: give $\frac{1}{15}$
> - Day $4$: take back $\frac{1}{15}$ and $\frac{2}{15}$, give $\frac{4}{15}$
> - etc.
>
> In this way, after day $x$ the worker has exactly $\frac{x}{15}$ of the bar.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{3}
> $$
>
> cuts are required.
