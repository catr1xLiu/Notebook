> [!example] Question 709 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Research Interview

Given that the probability of getting heads on a coin flip is p, what is the expected number of flips required to get three heads in a row?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=710)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ be the expected number of tosses needed to obtain $3$ consecutive heads for the first time, where each toss is a head with probability $p$ and a tail with probability $1-p$.
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Use states according to the current run of consecutive heads:
>
> - $E_0$: expected additional tosses starting with $0$ consecutive heads,
> - $E_1$: expected additional tosses starting with $1$ consecutive head,
> - $E_2$: expected additional tosses starting with $2$ consecutive heads,
> - $E_3 = 0$: already have $3$ consecutive heads.
>
> We want $E_0$.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up recurrence relations
>
> From state $0$:
>
> - one toss is used,
> - with probability $p$, move to state $1$,
> - with probability $1-p$, remain in state $0$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + pE_1 + (1-p)E_0.
> $$
>
> From state $1$:
>
> - one toss is used,
> - with probability $p$, move to state $2$,
> - with probability $1-p$, return to state $0$.
>
> So,
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + pE_2 + (1-p)E_0.
> $$
>
> From state $2$:
>
> - one toss is used,
> - with probability $p$, reach state $3$ and stop,
> - with probability $1-p$, return to state $0$.
>
> Hence,
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + pE_3 + (1-p)E_0 = 1 + (1-p)E_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> From the first equation,
> $$
> E_0 - (1-p)E_0 = 1 + pE_1
> $$
> so
> $$
> pE_0 = 1 + pE_1,
> $$
> and therefore
> $$
> E_0 = \frac{1}{p} + E_1.
> $$
>
> Next,
> $$
> E_2 = 1 + (1-p)E_0.
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + pE_2 + (1-p)E_0
> = 1 + p\bigl(1 + (1-p)E_0\bigr) + (1-p)E_0.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + p + \bigl(p(1-p) + (1-p)\bigr)E_0
> = 1 + p + (1-p)(1+p)E_0
> = 1 + p + (1-p^2)E_0.
> $$
>
> Substitute into $E_0 = \frac{1}{p} + E_1$:
> $$
> E_0 = \frac{1}{p} + 1 + p + (1-p^2)E_0.
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E_0 - (1-p^2)E_0 = \frac{1}{p} + 1 + p,
> $$
> so
> $$
> p^2 E_0 = \frac{1}{p} + 1 + p.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> E_0 = \frac{\frac{1}{p} + 1 + p}{p^2}
> = \frac{1 + p + p^2}{p^3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of tosses needed to get $3$ heads in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1+p+p^2}{p^3}}.
> $$
>
> For a fair coin, $p=\frac12$, this becomes
> $$
> \frac{1+\frac12+\frac14}{(1/2)^3}
> = \frac{7/4}{1/8}
> = 14.
> $$
