> [!example] Question 733 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

How do you estimate the square root of 5?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=734)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To estimate $\sqrt{5}$, use nearby perfect squares.
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 2^2 = 4
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 3^2 = 9,
> $$
>
> it follows that
>
> $$
> 2 < \sqrt{5} < 3.
> $$
>
> To refine the estimate, test decimals:
>
> $$
> 2.2^2 = 4.84,
> \qquad
> 2.3^2 = 5.29.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 2.2 < \sqrt{5} < 2.3.
> $$
>
> Refine further:
>
> $$
> 2.23^2 = 4.9729,
> \qquad
> 2.24^2 = 5.0176.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> 2.23 < \sqrt{5} < 2.24.
> $$
>
> A better approximation is
>
> $$
> \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236.
> $$
>
> Indeed,
>
> $$
> 2.236^2 \approx 4.9997,
> $$
>
> which is very close to $5$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\sqrt{5} \approx 2.236}
> $$
>
> and to two decimal places,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\sqrt{5} \approx 2.24}.
> $$
