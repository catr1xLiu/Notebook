> [!example] Question 758 · Citadel
> ## Quant Research Interview

What is the difference between LASSO and ridge regression?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=759)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Difference Between LASSO and Ridge Regression
>
> Both **LASSO** and **ridge regression** are regularization methods used to prevent overfitting in linear regression by adding a penalty term to the loss function.
>
> Suppose the ordinary least squares objective is
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta_0,\beta} \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - x_i^\top \beta\right)^2.
> $$
>
> Regularization modifies this objective by penalizing large coefficients.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Ridge Regression
>
> Ridge regression adds an $L_2$ penalty:
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta_0,\beta} \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - x_i^\top \beta\right)^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2.
> $$
>
> ### Key properties
> - The penalty is based on the **sum of squares** of the coefficients.
> - It **shrinks** coefficients toward $0$, but typically does **not make them exactly zero**.
> - Therefore, ridge regression usually keeps **all predictors** in the model.
> - It is especially useful when predictors are highly correlated.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. LASSO
>
> LASSO adds an $L_1$ penalty:
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta_0,\beta} \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - x_i^\top \beta\right)^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j|.
> $$
>
> ### Key properties
> - The penalty is based on the **sum of absolute values** of the coefficients.
> - It shrinks coefficients toward $0$ and can force some coefficients to be **exactly zero**.
> - Thus, LASSO performs **variable selection** as well as shrinkage.
> - It is useful when only a subset of predictors is believed to be truly relevant.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Main Conceptual Difference
>
> The main difference is the form of the penalty:
>
> - **Ridge:** $L_2$ penalty, $\sum \beta_j^2$
> - **LASSO:** $L_1$ penalty, $\sum |\beta_j|$
>
> This leads to different behavior:
>
> - **Ridge regression** gives **small but nonzero** coefficients.
> - **LASSO** can give **exactly zero** coefficients.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Effect on Model Interpretation
>
> - **Ridge regression:** harder to interpret if there are many predictors, since all remain in the model.
> - **LASSO:** easier to interpret because unimportant variables may be removed.
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Behavior with Correlated Predictors
>
> When predictors are strongly correlated:
>
> - **Ridge** tends to distribute the coefficient weights among them.
> - **LASSO** often selects one predictor and sets others to zero.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Geometric Intuition
>
> The difference comes from the shape of the constraint region:
>
> - Ridge uses an $L_2$ ball, which is smooth and round.
> - LASSO uses an $L_1$ ball, which has corners.
>
> Because the corners of the $L_1$ region lie on the axes, the LASSO solution often occurs where some coefficients are exactly $0$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Summary Table
>
> - **Method Ridge regression** — Penalty: $L_2$: $\lambda \sum \beta_j^2$; Effect on coefficients: Shrinks coefficients; Variable selection: No
> - **Method LASSO** — Penalty: $L_1$: $\lambda \sum |\beta_j|$; Effect on coefficients: Shrinks and may set some to $0$; Variable selection: Yes
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> LASSO and ridge regression are both regularized regression methods, but they differ in the penalty they use. Ridge regression uses an $L_2$ penalty and shrinks coefficients toward zero without usually making them exactly zero, while LASSO uses an $L_1$ penalty and can set some coefficients exactly equal to zero. As a result, ridge is mainly a **shrinkage** method, whereas LASSO is both a **shrinkage** and **feature selection** method.
