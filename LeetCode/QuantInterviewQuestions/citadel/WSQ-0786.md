> [!example] Question 786 · Citadel
> ## Internship Interview

What is a probability density function?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=787)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A **probability density function** (pdf) is a function used to describe the distribution of a **continuous** random variable.
>
> If $X$ is a continuous random variable, then a function $f(x)$ is called its probability density function if it satisfies:
>
> 1. **Nonnegativity**
>    $$
>    f(x) \ge 0 \quad \text{for all } x
>    $$
>
> 2. **Total probability equals $1$**
>    $$
>    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1
>    $$
>
> 3. **Probabilities are found by integrating**
>    For any interval $[a,b]$,
>    $$
>    P(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx
>    $$
>
> ## Interpretation
>
> The value $f(x)$ itself is **not** usually a probability. Instead, it represents how densely probability is concentrated near $x$. Since probability over a single point is zero for a continuous variable,
> $$
> P(X = x) = 0
> $$
> for any specific $x$.
>
> Only probabilities over intervals are meaningful, and these are obtained by area under the curve of $f(x)$.
>
> ## Example
>
> Consider
> $$
> f(x)=
> \begin{cases}
> 2x, & 0 \le x \le 1,\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> First check that it is a pdf:
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx
> =
> \int_0^1 2x\,dx
> =
> \left[x^2\right]_0^1
> =
> 1
> $$
>
> So it is a valid probability density function.
>
> Now, for example,
> $$
> P\left(\tfrac{1}{4} \le X \le \tfrac{3}{4}\right)
> =
> \int_{1/4}^{3/4} 2x\,dx
> =
> \left[x^2\right]_{1/4}^{3/4}
> =
> \frac{9}{16}-\frac{1}{16}
> =
> \frac{1}{2}
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> A probability density function is a nonnegative function $f(x)$ with total area $1$, such that probabilities for a continuous random variable are given by areas under the curve:
> $$
> P(a \le X \le b)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.
> $$
