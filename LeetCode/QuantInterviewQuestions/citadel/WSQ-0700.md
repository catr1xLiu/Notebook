> [!example] Question 700 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

If 75 customers are randomly assigned to three equal-sized databases, and all partitions are equally likely, what is the probability that two randomly selected customers, Bob and Ben, are in the same database?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=701)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> # Solution
>
> We have $75$ customers partitioned uniformly at random into three databases of equal size $25$, with every such partition equally likely. We want the probability that two particular customers, Bob and Ben, land in the same database.
>
> ## Approach: symmetry argument
>
> Think of building the random partition by uniformly shuffling all $75$ customers and assigning the first $25$ to Database 1, the next $25$ to Database 2, and the last $25$ to Database 3.
>
> Fix Bob's position in the partition; by symmetry it does not matter which database he ends up in. Once Bob's database is determined, it has $24$ remaining spots, out of the $74$ spots not occupied by Bob, reserved for $24$ of the other $74$ customers, while the remaining $50$ spots belong to the other two databases.
>
> Because the partition is uniformly random, Ben, one specific customer among the other $74$, is equally likely to occupy any of these $74$ remaining spots. So
>
> $$
> P(\text{Ben in Bob's database}) = \frac{24}{74} = \frac{12}{37}.
> $$
>
> ## Verification by direct counting
>
> The number of ways to split $75$ labeled customers into three labeled groups of $25$ is
>
> $$
> \binom{75}{25}\binom{50}{25}.
> $$
>
> To count partitions where Bob and Ben share a database, choose which of the $3$ databases holds both of them, choose the remaining $23$ members of that database from the other $73$ customers, and split the remaining $50$ customers into the other two databases:
>
> $$
> 3\binom{73}{23}\binom{50}{25}.
> $$
>
> Dividing,
>
> $$
> P = \frac{3\binom{73}{23}\binom{50}{25}}{\binom{75}{25}\binom{50}{25}} = \frac{3\binom{73}{23}}{\binom{75}{25}}.
> $$
>
> Since $\binom{75}{25} = \binom{73}{23}\cdot \dfrac{75\cdot 74}{25\cdot 24}$, this simplifies to
>
> $$
> P = \frac{3 \cdot 25 \cdot 24}{75 \cdot 74} = \frac{12}{37},
> $$
>
> confirming the symmetry argument.
>
> ## Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{P = \frac{12}{37} \approx 0.3243}
> $$
>
> This is not $\frac{1}{3}$: that simpler value would hold only if each customer were assigned independently and uniformly to one of the three databases, with no constraint on the final group sizes. Because the partition is required to split the customers into equal groups of $25$, knowing Bob's database uses up one of the $25$ slots in it, making Ben's placement slightly dependent on Bob's and pulling the probability below $\frac13$.
