> [!example] Question 691 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Find the number of ways to get from one corner of a grid to the opposite corner, moving only down or right at each step.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=692)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To go from one corner of an $m \times n$ grid to the opposite corner using only moves to the right or down, every valid path consists of exactly
>
> - $m$ downward moves, and
> - $n$ rightward moves.
>
> Thus each path is a sequence of $m+n$ moves containing $m$ identical down-moves and $n$ identical right-moves.
>
> The number of such sequences is the number of ways to choose where the $m$ down-moves occur among the $m+n$ total moves:
>
> $$
> \binom{m+n}{m}
> $$
>
> Equivalently, one may choose the positions of the $n$ right-moves:
>
> $$
> \binom{m+n}{n}
> $$
>
> Since these are equal, the total number of paths is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{m+n}{m}=\binom{m+n}{n}}.
> $$
>
> If the grid is square, say $n \times n$, then the number of paths is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{2n}{n}}.
> $$
