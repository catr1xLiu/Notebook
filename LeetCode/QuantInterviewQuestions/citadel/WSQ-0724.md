> [!example] Question 724 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given a series of real numbers, what is the optimal point on the real line that minimizes the sum of the distances to all these numbers?
Follow-up: What is the best algorithm to calculate the median of a sequence of numbers?
Follow-up 2: Design an algorithm that can update the median of a sequence as new numbers are added.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=725)

> ## 1. Point on the real line minimizing total distance
>
> Given real numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$, consider the function
>
> $$
> f(a) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - a|.
> $$
>
> The problem is to find $a \in \mathbb{R}$ minimizing $f(a)$.
>
> ### Claim
> Any **median** of the numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ minimizes
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - a|.
> $$
>
> ### Proof
> Sort the numbers:
>
> $$
> x_{(1)} \le x_{(2)} \le \cdots \le x_{(n)}.
> $$
>
> For a point $a$ not equal to any $x_i$, the derivative of $f(a)$ is
>
> $$
> f'(a) = \#\{i : x_i < a\} - \#\{i : x_i > a\}.
> $$
>
> Indeed, each term $|x_i-a|$ contributes:
>
> - $-1$ if $a < x_i$,
> - $+1$ if $a > x_i$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - if more than half the points lie to the right of $a$, then $f'(a) < 0$, so moving right decreases $f$;
> - if more than half the points lie to the left of $a$, then $f'(a) > 0$, so moving left decreases $f$.
>
> Therefore, a minimizer occurs exactly when at least half the numbers are on each side of $a$, which is the definition of a median.
>
> ### Consequences
> - If $n$ is odd, the unique minimizer is
>
> $$
> x_{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}.
> $$
>
> - If $n$ is even, every point in the interval
>
> $$
> [x_{(n/2)},\, x_{(n/2+1)}]
> $$
>
> minimizes the sum of distances. The usual median choice is often
>
> $$
> \frac{x_{(n/2)} + x_{(n/2+1)}}{2},
> $$
>
> but any point in that interval is optimal.
>
> ## 2. Best algorithm to compute the median of a fixed sequence
>
> There are several notions of “best,” depending on whether one wants simplicity or optimal asymptotic running time.
>
> ### Method 1: Sort and take the middle
> 1. Sort the array.
> 2. Return the middle element (or average of the two middle elements).
>
> Running time:
>
> $$
> O(n \log n).
> $$
>
> This is simple and practical.
>
> ### Method 2: Selection algorithm
> Since only the median is needed, full sorting is unnecessary. One may find the $k$-th smallest element directly, where
>
> $$
> k = \left\lfloor \frac{n+1}{2} \right\rfloor.
> $$
>
> Algorithms:
>
> - **Quickselect**: expected time
>
> $$
> O(n)
> $$
>
> worst case
>
> $$
> O(n^2).
> $$
>
> - **Median-of-medians selection**: worst-case time
>
> $$
> O(n).
> $$
>
> Thus, from a theoretical standpoint, the optimal asymptotic algorithm for finding the median of an unsorted sequence is a **linear-time selection algorithm**.
>
> ### Recommendation
> - In practice: use **Quickselect**.
> - For guaranteed worst-case performance: use **median-of-medians**.
>
> ## 3. Algorithm to update the median as new numbers arrive
>
> For a stream of incoming numbers, the standard solution uses **two heaps**.
>
> ### Data structure
> Maintain:
>
> - a **max-heap** $L$ containing the lower half of the numbers,
> - a **min-heap** $R$ containing the upper half of the numbers.
>
> ### Invariants
> Maintain:
>
> 1. Every element of $L$ is $\le$ every element of $R$.
> 2. The sizes satisfy
>
> $$
> |\,|L| - |R|\,| \le 1.
> $$
>
> 3. Usually, either
>
> $$
> |L| = |R|
> $$
>
> or
>
> $$
> |L| = |R| + 1.
> $$
>
> ### Update rule for a new number $x$
> 1. If $L$ is empty or $x \le \max(L)$, insert $x$ into $L$.
> 2. Otherwise insert $x$ into $R$.
> 3. Rebalance:
>    - if $|L| > |R| + 1$, move the maximum of $L$ into $R$;
>    - if $|R| > |L|$, move the minimum of $R$ into $L$.
>
> ### Median query
> - If $|L| > |R|$, median is
>
> $$
> \max(L).
> $$
>
> - If $|L| = |R|$, median is often taken as
>
> $$
> \frac{\max(L) + \min(R)}{2}.
> $$
>
> If one wants any median minimizing total absolute deviation, one may return either $\max(L)$ or $\min(R)$.
>
> ### Complexity
> Each insertion into a heap costs
>
> $$
> O(\log n),
> $$
>
> and each rebalance step also costs
>
> $$
> O(\log n).
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - **update time**: $O(\log n)$ per new number,
> - **median query time**: $O(1)$.
>
> ## 4. Pseudocode for streaming median
>
> ```text
> initialize max-heap L
> initialize min-heap R
>
> function add(x):
>     if L is empty or x <= top(L):
>         push x into L
>     else:
>         push x into R
>
>     if size(L) > size(R) + 1:
>         move top(L) from L to R
>     else if size(R) > size(L):
>         move top(R) from R to L
>
> function median():
>     if size(L) > size(R):
>         return top(L)
>     else:
>         return (top(L) + top(R)) / 2
> ```
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - The point minimizing
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i - a|
> $$
>
> is any **median** of the numbers.
>
> - To compute the median of a fixed unsorted sequence, the asymptotically optimal approach is a **selection algorithm** in
>
> $$
> O(n)
> $$
>
> time; sorting gives a simpler
>
> $$
> O(n \log n)
> $$
>
> method.
>
> - To maintain the median as numbers arrive online, use **two heaps**:
>   a max-heap for the lower half and a min-heap for the upper half, supporting
>
> $$
> O(\log n)
> $$
>
> update time and
>
> $$
> O(1)
> $$
>
> median retrieval.
