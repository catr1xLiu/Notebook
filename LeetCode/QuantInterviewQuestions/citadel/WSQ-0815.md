> [!example] Question 815 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher (Intern) Interview

What is implied volatility? What is the probability distribution of X + Y, where X and Y are independent and identically distributed uniform random variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=816)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Implied volatility
>
> Implied volatility is the value of the volatility parameter $\sigma$ in an option-pricing model—most commonly the Black–Scholes model—that makes the model price equal to the observed market price of the option.
>
> More precisely, if the model price of an option is written as
> $$
> C = C(S_0, K, r, T, \sigma),
> $$
> then the implied volatility is the value $\sigma_{\text{imp}}$ satisfying
> $$
> C(S_0, K, r, T, \sigma_{\text{imp}}) = C_{\text{market}}.
> $$
>
> Thus, implied volatility is not directly observed; it is inferred from market prices. It reflects the market’s assessment of future uncertainty in the underlying asset price, as encoded by the pricing model.
>

> ## Distribution of $X+Y$ when $X$ and $Y$ are i.i.d. uniform
>
> Assume
> $$
> X, Y \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,1),
> $$
> independently and identically distributed. Let
> $$
> Z = X+Y.
> $$
>
> To find the distribution of $Z$, use convolution.
>
> ### Step 1: Densities of $X$ and $Y$
>
> The density of each variable is
> $$
> f_X(x) = f_Y(y) =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & 0 \le x \le 1,\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Convolution formula
>
> The density of $Z$ is
> $$
> f_Z(z) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_X(x) f_Y(z-x)\,dx.
> $$
>
> Since both densities are nonzero only on $[0,1]$, the integrand equals $1$ when
> $$
> 0 \le x \le 1
> \quad \text{and} \quad
> 0 \le z-x \le 1.
> $$
> The second condition is equivalent to
> $$
> z-1 \le x \le z.
> $$
> So the valid range of integration is
> $$
> x \in [0,1] \cap [z-1, z].
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Compute the density piecewise
>
> - If $0 \le z \le 1$, then the interval is $[0,z]$, whose length is $z$. Hence
>   $$
>   f_Z(z) = z.
>   $$
>
> - If $1 \le z \le 2$, then the interval is $[z-1,1]$, whose length is $2-z$. Hence
>   $$
>   f_Z(z) = 2-z.
>   $$
>
> - Otherwise, there is no overlap, so
>   $$
>   f_Z(z) = 0.
>   $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> f_Z(z) =
> \begin{cases}
> z, & 0 \le z \le 1,\\
> 2-z, & 1 \le z \le 2,\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> This is the triangular distribution on $[0,2]$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Cumulative distribution function
>
> Integrating the density gives the cdf:
> $$
> F_Z(z) =
> \begin{cases}
> 0, & z < 0,\\
> \dfrac{z^2}{2}, & 0 \le z \le 1,\\
> 1 - \dfrac{(2-z)^2}{2}, & 1 \le z \le 2,\\
> 1, & z > 2.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If $X$ and $Y$ are i.i.d. $\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, then $X+Y$ has density
> $$
> f_{X+Y}(z) =
> \begin{cases}
> z, & 0 \le z \le 1,\\
> 2-z, & 1 \le z \le 2,\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
> and cdf
> $$
> F_{X+Y}(z) =
> \begin{cases}
> 0, & z < 0,\\
> \dfrac{z^2}{2}, & 0 \le z \le 1,\\
> 1 - \dfrac{(2-z)^2}{2}, & 1 \le z \le 2,\\
> 1, & z > 2.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Implied volatility is the volatility parameter that, when inserted into an option-pricing model, reproduces the observed market price of the option.
