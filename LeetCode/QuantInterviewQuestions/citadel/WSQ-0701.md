> [!example] Question 701 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Suppose you have several random variables that all have equal pairwise correlation. What is the possible range of this correlation value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=702)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be random variables such that every pair has the same correlation:
> $$
> \operatorname{Corr}(X_i,X_j)=\rho \qquad (i\neq j).
> $$
> The question is to determine the possible range of $\rho$.
>
> ## Correlation matrix
>
> The correlation matrix has the form
> $$
> R=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \rho & \cdots & \rho\\
> \rho & 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho\\
> \rho & \rho & 1 & \cdots & \rho\\
> \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
> \rho & \rho & \rho & \cdots & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
> Any correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite. Therefore $R$ must satisfy
> $$
> x^\top R x \ge 0
> \qquad \text{for all } x\in \mathbb{R}^n.
> $$
>
> ## Eigenvalues of $R$
>
> Write
> $$
> R=(1-\rho)I_n+\rho J_n,
> $$
> where $I_n$ is the identity matrix and $J_n$ is the $n\times n$ matrix of all $1$'s.
>
> The matrix $J_n$ has eigenvalues:
> - $n$, corresponding to the vector $(1,1,\dots,1)^\top$,
> - $0$, with multiplicity $n-1$.
>
> Hence the eigenvalues of $R$ are:
> - $1-\rho+n\rho = 1+(n-1)\rho$,
> - $1-\rho$, with multiplicity $n-1$.
>
> For $R$ to be positive semidefinite, all eigenvalues must be nonnegative. Thus:
> $$
> 1-\rho \ge 0
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 1+(n-1)\rho \ge 0.
> $$
> These give
> $$
> \rho \le 1
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \rho \ge -\frac{1}{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the allowable range is
> $$
> -\frac{1}{n-1} \le \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> If $n$ random variables have a common pairwise correlation $\rho$, then necessarily
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac{1}{n-1} \le \rho \le 1.}
> $$
> This range is also attainable, so it is the exact range.
