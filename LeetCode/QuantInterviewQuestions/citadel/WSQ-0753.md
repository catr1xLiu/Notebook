> [!example] Question 753 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Intern Interview

Given an array v, find the number of pairs of indices (i, j) such that v\[i\] = v\[j\] = v\[i+1\] + v\[i+2\] + ... + v\[j-1\]. Your solution should have time complexity O(n log n).

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=754)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let
>
> $$
> S_k = \sum_{t=1}^k v[t], \qquad S_0 = 0.
> $$
>
> We seek the number of pairs $(i,j)$ such that
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j] = v[i+1] + v[i+2] + \cdots + v[j-1].
> $$
>
> Assume $i<j$; otherwise the middle sum is not defined in the usual sense.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Rewrite the condition with prefix sums
>
> The middle sum is
>
> $$
> v[i+1] + \cdots + v[j-1] = S_{j-1} - S_i.
> $$
>
> Thus the condition becomes
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j] = S_{j-1} - S_i.
> $$
>
> Using
>
> $$
> v[i] = S_i - S_{i-1}, \qquad v[j] = S_j - S_{j-1},
> $$
>
> we get the two equations
>
> $$
> S_i - S_{i-1} = S_{j-1} - S_i,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_j - S_{j-1} = S_{j-1} - S_i.
> $$
>
> Rearranging,
>
> $$
> 2S_i = S_{i-1} + S_{j-1},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_j = 2S_{j-1} - S_i.
> $$
>
> This form is not the most convenient for counting directly. A better transformation is obtained from the original equality
>
> $$
> v[i] = S_{j-1} - S_i.
> $$
>
> Since $S_i = S_{i-1} + v[i]$, we have
>
> $$
> v[i] = S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} - v[i],
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> 2v[i] = S_{j-1} - S_{i-1}.
> $$
>
> Similarly, from $v[j] = S_{j-1} - S_i$ and $S_j = S_{j-1} + v[j]$,
>
> $$
> 2v[j] = S_j - S_i.
> $$
>
> But the simplest useful observation is the following.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. A symmetric reformulation
>
> Let
>
> $$
> A_k = S_{k-1} + S_k.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> A_i = S_{i-1} + S_i, \qquad A_j = S_{j-1} + S_j.
> $$
>
> Now compute:
>
> If
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j] = S_{j-1} - S_i,
> $$
>
> then using $S_i = S_{i-1}+v[i]$ and $S_j=S_{j-1}+v[j]$,
>
> $$
> A_i = S_{i-1}+S_i = S_{i-1} + (S_{i-1}+v[i]) = 2S_{i-1}+v[i],
> $$
>
> $$
> A_j = S_{j-1}+S_j = S_{j-1} + (S_{j-1}+v[j]) = 2S_{j-1}+v[j].
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> S_{j-1}-S_i = v[i], \qquad S_i=S_{i-1}+v[i],
> $$
>
> we obtain
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} = S_{i-1}+2v[i].
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> A_j = 2(S_{i-1}+2v[i]) + v[i] = 2S_{i-1}+5v[i].
> $$
>
> Also
>
> $$
> A_i = 2S_{i-1}+v[i].
> $$
>
> So this does not collapse to a simple equality. Thus this route is not ideal.
>
> Instead, derive a direct counting criterion.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Direct algebraic criterion
>
> From
>
> $$
> v[i] = S_{j-1} - S_i
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> v[j] = S_{j-1} - S_i,
> $$
>
> we immediately have
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j].
> $$
>
> So the pair $(i,j)$ is valid iff
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j]
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_i = v[i].
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> S_i = S_{i-1} + v[i],
> $$
>
> this becomes
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} - v[i] = v[i],
> $$
>
> hence
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} = 2v[i].
> $$
>
> Because $v[i]=v[j]$, this is equivalent to
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} = 2v[j].
> $$
>
> Now use
>
> $$
> v[j] = S_j - S_{j-1}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} = 2(S_j - S_{j-1}),
> $$
>
> or
>
> $$
> S_{i-1} = 3S_{j-1} - 2S_j.
> $$
>
> This still couples adjacent prefix sums, but it suggests indexing by the right endpoint.
>
> A cleaner formulation is obtained by defining for each position $k$:
>
> $$
> L_k = S_{k-1} + 2v[k] = S_{k-1} + 2(S_k-S_{k-1}) = 2S_k - S_{k-1}.
> $$
>
> Then the condition
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} - S_{i-1} = 2v[i]
> $$
>
> becomes
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} = S_{i-1} + 2v[i] = L_i.
> $$
>
> Also, since $v[j]=v[i]$ and $v[j]=S_j-S_{j-1}$, we have
>
> $$
> L_j = S_{j-1} + 2v[j] = S_{j-1} + 2v[i].
> $$
>
> But from $S_{j-1}=S_{i-1}+2v[i]$,
>
> $$
> L_j = S_{i-1} + 4v[i].
> $$
>
> Again not enough alone. So let us instead count in the most direct way:
>
> A pair $(i,j)$ is valid iff
>
> $$
> v[i]=v[j]
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_{j-1} = S_{i-1} + 2v[i].
> $$
>
> This is already sufficient for an $O(n\log n)$ algorithm.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Counting in $O(n\log n)$
>
> For a fixed $j$, we need to count prior indices $i<j$ such that
>
> $$
> v[i] = v[j]
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_{i-1} = S_{j-1} - 2v[j].
> $$
>
> Thus for each position $j$, the number of valid $i$ is exactly the number of previous positions $i$ with key
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[i],\, S_{i-1}\bigr)
> $$
>
> equal to
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[j],\, S_{j-1} - 2v[j]\bigr).
> $$
>
> So we scan from left to right, maintaining a map from pairs
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[i],\, S_{i-1}\bigr)
> $$
>
> to their frequency among indices already processed.
>
> For each $j$:
>
> 1. Compute the target pair
>    $$
>    \bigl(v[j],\, S_{j-1}-2v[j]\bigr).
>    $$
> 2. Add its current frequency to the answer.
> 3. Insert the pair
>    $$
>    \bigl(v[j],\, S_{j-1}\bigr)
>    $$
>    into the map.
>
> Each operation costs $O(\log n)$ with a balanced tree map, so total complexity is $O(n\log n)$.
>
> With hashing, expected time is $O(n)$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Correctness proof
>
> Maintain a map containing exactly all pairs
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[i], S_{i-1}\bigr)
> $$
>
> for indices $i<j$ when processing position $j$.
>
> A previous index $i<j$ forms a valid pair with $j$ iff
>
> $$
> v[i]=v[j]=v[i+1]+\cdots+v[j-1].
> $$
>
> This is equivalent to
>
> $$
> v[i]=v[j]
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> S_{j-1}-S_i=v[i].
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> S_i=S_{i-1}+v[i],
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> S_{j-1}-(S_{i-1}+v[i])=v[i]
> $$
>
> which is equivalent to
>
> $$
> S_{i-1}=S_{j-1}-2v[i].
> $$
>
> Because $v[i]=v[j]$, this becomes
>
> $$
> S_{i-1}=S_{j-1}-2v[j].
> $$
>
> Therefore $(i,j)$ is valid exactly when
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[i],S_{i-1}\bigr)=\bigl(v[j],S_{j-1}-2v[j]\bigr).
> $$
>
> Hence the number added at step $j$ is precisely the number of valid pairs ending at $j$. Summing over all $j$ counts all valid pairs exactly once.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Pseudocode
>
> ```cpp
> long long count_pairs(vector<long long>& v) {
>     int n = v.size();
>     map<pair<long long,long long>, long long> cnt;
>     long long ans = 0;
>     long long pref = 0; // this is S_{j-1} before processing j
>
>     for (int j = 0; j < n; ++j) {
>         long long x = v[j];
>
>         // count i < j with v[i] = x and S_{i-1} = S_{j-1} - 2x
>         ans += cnt[{x, pref - 2 * x}];
>
>         // insert current index j as a future left endpoint
>         cnt[{x, pref}]++;
>
>         pref += x;
>     }
>     return ans;
> }
> ```
>
> If the array is $1$-indexed, the same logic applies with $pref = S_{j-1}$ at step $j$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Final answer
>
> The required number of pairs can be found in $O(n\log n)$ by scanning left to right and maintaining frequencies of pairs
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[i],\, S_{i-1}\bigr),
> $$
>
> where $S_k$ is the prefix sum. For each $j$, the number of valid $i<j$ is the frequency of
>
> $$
> \bigl(v[j],\, S_{j-1}-2v[j]\bigr).
> $$
>
> Thus the algorithm is:
>
> - compute prefix sums on the fly,
> - store counts of pairs $\bigl(v[i],S_{i-1}\bigr)$ in a map,
> - for each $j$, add the count of $\bigl(v[j],S_{j-1}-2v[j]\bigr)$,
> - then insert $\bigl(v[j],S_{j-1}\bigr)$.
>
> This is correct and runs in $O(n\log n)$ time.
