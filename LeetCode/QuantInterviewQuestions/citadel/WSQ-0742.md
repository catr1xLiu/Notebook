> [!example] Question 742 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

What is covariance?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=743)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Covariance measures how two random variables vary together.
>
> If $X$ and $Y$ are random variables with means $E[X]$ and $E[Y]$, their covariance is defined by
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E\big[(X-E[X])(Y-E[Y])\big].
> $$
>
> An equivalent formula is
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y].
> $$
>
> ## Interpretation
>
> - If $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)>0$, then $X$ and $Y$ tend to increase or decrease together.
> - If $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)<0$, then when one tends to be large, the other tends to be small.
> - If $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=0$, then there is no linear relationship between them.
>
> However, covariance equal to $0$ does **not** necessarily mean that $X$ and $Y$ are independent.
>
> ## Special case: variance
>
> The variance of a random variable is covariance with itself:
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X)=\operatorname{Cov}(X,X).
> $$
>
> ## Example
>
> Suppose $X$ and $Y$ satisfy
>
> $$
> E[X]=2,\qquad E[Y]=3,\qquad E[XY]=8.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]=8-(2)(3)=2.
> $$
>
> So the covariance is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Limitation
>
> The size of covariance depends on the units of $X$ and $Y$, so it is often hard to compare across different settings. For this reason, one often uses the correlation coefficient:
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}.
> $$
>
> This standardizes covariance to lie between $-1$ and $1$.
