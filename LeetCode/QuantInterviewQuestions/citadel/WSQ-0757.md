> [!example] Question 757 · Citadel
> ## Quant Researcher Interview

There are three random variables, X, Y, and Z. The three correlations between the variables are equal, i.e., ρ = Corr(X, Y) = Corr(Y, Z) = Corr(Z, X). What is the tightest possible bound you can give for ρ? How about the general case for n random variables?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=758)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the variables be standardized, so that each has mean $0$ and variance $1$. Then their correlation matrix is
>
> $$
> R_3=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \rho\\
> \rho & 1 & \rho\\
> \rho & \rho & 1
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> A correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite, so all eigenvalues of $R_3$ must be nonnegative.
>
> ## Case of three variables
>
> Write
>
> $$
> R_3=(1-\rho)I_3+\rho J_3,
> $$
>
> where $I_3$ is the $3\times 3$ identity matrix and $J_3$ is the $3\times 3$ all-ones matrix.
>
> The eigenvalues of $J_3$ are $3$ (once) and $0$ (twice), so the eigenvalues of $R_3$ are
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \quad \text{(with multiplicity $2$)},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 1-\rho+3\rho=1+2\rho.
> $$
>
> Positive semidefiniteness requires
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \ge 0
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 1+2\rho \ge 0.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> \rho \le 1
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \rho \ge -\frac12.
> $$
>
> Therefore the tightest possible bound is
>
> $$
> -\frac12 \le \rho \le 1.
> $$
>
> Both endpoints are attainable:
>
> - $\rho=1$ occurs when $X=Y=Z$ almost surely.
> - $\rho=-\frac12$ occurs, for example, when $X+Y+Z=0$ almost surely and the three variables have equal variances.
>
> So for three variables,
>
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac12 \le \rho \le 1.}
> $$
>
> ## General case of $n$ variables
>
> If $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are random variables with common pairwise correlation $\rho$, then the correlation matrix is
>
> $$
> R_n=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & \rho & \cdots & \rho\\
> \rho & 1 & \cdots & \rho\\
> \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
> \rho & \rho & \cdots & 1
> \end{pmatrix}
> =(1-\rho)I_n+\rho J_n.
> $$
>
> Again, $J_n$ has eigenvalues $n$ (once) and $0$ (with multiplicity $n-1$). Hence $R_n$ has eigenvalues
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \quad \text{(with multiplicity $n-1$)},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 1-\rho+n\rho = 1+(n-1)\rho.
> $$
>
> Requiring all eigenvalues to be nonnegative gives
>
> $$
> 1-\rho \ge 0
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 1+(n-1)\rho \ge 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> \rho \le 1
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \rho \ge -\frac{1}{n-1}.
> $$
>
> Hence the sharp range is
>
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac{1}{n-1} \le \rho \le 1.}
> $$
>
> This is the tightest possible bound for an equicorrelation structure on $n$ random variables.
