> [!example] Question 717 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Nine fair coins are tossed. What is the probability that an odd number of heads will land?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=718)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $9$ fair coin tosses. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(9,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd})=\sum_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{9} \binom{9}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^9.
> $$
>
> A standard symmetry argument shows that, for fair coin tosses, the number of outcomes with an odd number of heads equals the number of outcomes with an even number of heads. Indeed, flipping the first coin changes any outcome with an even number of heads into one with an odd number of heads, and vice versa, giving a one-to-one correspondence.
>
> Since all $2^9$ outcomes are equally likely, exactly half have an odd number of heads. Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X \text{ is odd})=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
