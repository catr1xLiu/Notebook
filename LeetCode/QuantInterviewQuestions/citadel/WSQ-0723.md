> [!example] Question 723 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the difference between regressing y on x and regressing x on y?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=724)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Regression of $y$ on $x$ and regression of $x$ on $y$ are different because they answer different questions and minimize different errors.
>
> ## 1. Regression of $y$ on $x$
>
> This models $y$ as the dependent variable and $x$ as the explanatory variable:
>
> $$
> y = a + bx + \varepsilon.
> $$
>
> The least-squares line is chosen to minimize the sum of squared **vertical** deviations:
>
> $$
> \sum_i \bigl(y_i - a - bx_i\bigr)^2.
> $$
>
> Thus it is used when the goal is to predict $y$ from $x$.
>
> Its slope is
>
> $$
> b_{y|x} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)}{\operatorname{Var}(x)}
> = r\frac{s_y}{s_x},
> $$
>
> where $r$ is the correlation coefficient, and $s_x, s_y$ are the sample standard deviations.
>
> ## 2. Regression of $x$ on $y$
>
> This models $x$ as the dependent variable and $y$ as the explanatory variable:
>
> $$
> x = c + dy + \eta.
> $$
>
> The least-squares line is chosen to minimize the sum of squared **horizontal** deviations:
>
> $$
> \sum_i \bigl(x_i - c - dy_i\bigr)^2.
> $$
>
> Thus it is used when the goal is to predict $x$ from $y$.
>
> Its slope is
>
> $$
> d_{x|y} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)}{\operatorname{Var}(y)}
> = r\frac{s_x}{s_y}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Why they are not the same
>
> In general,
>
> $$
> b_{y|x} \neq \frac{1}{d_{x|y}}.
> $$
>
> Indeed,
>
> $$
> b_{y|x} \, d_{x|y} = r^2.
> $$
>
> So the two regression lines coincide only in the special case $|r| = 1$, that is, when the data lie exactly on a straight line.
>
> ## 4. Geometric interpretation
>
> - Regressing $y$ on $x$ minimizes **vertical** distances to the line.
> - Regressing $x$ on $y$ minimizes **horizontal** distances to the line.
>
> Because vertical and horizontal errors are different, the fitted lines are usually different.
>
> ## 5. Conclusion
>
> The difference is:
>
> - **Regression of $y$ on $x$** predicts $y$ from $x$ and minimizes squared vertical errors.
> - **Regression of $x$ on $y$** predicts $x$ from $y$ and minimizes squared horizontal errors.
>
> They are generally not interchangeable, and one should choose the form according to which variable is being predicted.
