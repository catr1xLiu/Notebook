> [!example] Question 765 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

How can a deadlock occur?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=766)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A deadlock occurs when a set of processes or threads become permanently blocked because each is waiting for a resource held by another. As a result, none of them can proceed.
>
> ## How deadlock arises
>
> A deadlock can occur when the following four conditions hold simultaneously:
>
> 1. **Mutual exclusion**  
>    At least one resource is non-shareable; only one process can use it at a time.
>
> 2. **Hold and wait**  
>    A process is holding at least one resource while waiting to acquire additional resources.
>
> 3. **No preemption**  
>    Resources cannot be forcibly taken away; they must be released voluntarily by the process holding them.
>
> 4. **Circular wait**  
>    A circular chain of processes exists such that each process is waiting for a resource held by the next process in the chain.
>
> If all four conditions are present, deadlock is possible.
>
> ## Example
>
> Suppose there are two processes, $P_1$ and $P_2$, and two resources, $R_1$ and $R_2$.
>
> - $P_1$ acquires $R_1$.
> - $P_2$ acquires $R_2$.
> - $P_1$ now requests $R_2$, but $R_2$ is held by $P_2$, so $P_1$ waits.
> - $P_2$ now requests $R_1$, but $R_1$ is held by $P_1$, so $P_2$ waits.
>
> Now:
>
> - $P_1$ is waiting for $R_2$,
> - $P_2$ is waiting for $R_1$,
>
> so neither can continue. This is a deadlock.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> A deadlock occurs when processes compete for limited resources in such a way that each process waits indefinitely for resources held by others, typically under the four conditions listed above.
