> [!example] Question 683 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Describe an algorithm for calculating the square root of x.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=684)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Square-root algorithm
>
> A standard method for computing $\sqrt{x}$ for $x \ge 0$ is **Newton’s method** applied to the equation
>
> $$
> f(y) = y^2 - x = 0.
> $$
>
> We seek the positive root of this equation.
>
> ### Derivation
>
> Newton’s iteration for solving $f(y)=0$ is
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = y_n - \frac{f(y_n)}{f'(y_n)}.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> f(y) = y^2 - x
> \qquad \text{and} \qquad
> f'(y) = 2y,
> $$
>
> the iteration becomes
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = y_n - \frac{y_n^2 - x}{2y_n}
> = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right).
> $$
>
> Thus, starting from an initial guess $y_0 > 0$, repeatedly compute
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right)
> $$
>
> until the values stabilize.
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> Given $x \ge 0$:
>
> 1. If $x = 0$, return $0$.
> 2. Choose an initial guess $y_0 > 0$; for example, $y_0 = x$ if $x \ge 1$, or $y_0 = 1$ if $0 < x < 1$.
> 3. Repeat:
>    $$
>    y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right).
>    $$
> 4. Stop when the change is sufficiently small, for example when
>    $$
>    |y_{n+1} - y_n| < \varepsilon
>    $$
>    for a prescribed tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$.
> 5. Output $y_{n+1}$ as the approximation to $\sqrt{x}$.
>
> ### Why it works
>
> If the iteration converges to some value $y > 0$, then passing to the limit in
>
> $$
> y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right)
> $$
>
> gives
>
> $$
> y = \frac{1}{2}\left(y + \frac{x}{y}\right).
> $$
>
> Multiplying by $2y$ yields
>
> $$
> 2y^2 = y^2 + x,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> y^2 = x.
> $$
>
> Since $y > 0$, the limit is $\sqrt{x}$.
>
> ### Example
>
> To compute $\sqrt{10}$, take $y_0 = 3$.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> y_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(3 + \frac{10}{3}\right) = 3.1667,
> $$
>
> $$
> y_2 = \frac{1}{2}\left(3.1667 + \frac{10}{3.1667}\right) \approx 3.1623,
> $$
>
> $$
> y_3 = \frac{1}{2}\left(3.1623 + \frac{10}{3.1623}\right) \approx 3.1623.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{10} \approx 3.1623.
> $$
>
> ### Final algorithm
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \text{For } x \ge 0,\quad
> y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(y_n + \frac{x}{y_n}\right)
> \text{ until convergence.}
> }
> $$
>
> This is an efficient and widely used algorithm for calculating $\sqrt{x}$.
