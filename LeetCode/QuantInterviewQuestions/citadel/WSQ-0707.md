> [!example] Question 707 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

1. In the context of the German tank problem, explain how to construct a statistical estimator for the maximum number of tanks produced, given a sample of observed serial numbers. 2. Calculate the mean and standard deviation of this estimator.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=708)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. German tank problem: constructing an estimator
>
> Suppose a population consists of serial numbers $1,2,\dots,N$, and a simple random sample without replacement of size $n$ is observed:
> $$
> X_1,\dots,X_n.
> $$
> The goal is to estimate the unknown maximum serial number $N$.
>
> A standard estimator is based on the sample maximum
> $$
> M=\max\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 1: Mean of the sample maximum
>
> For sampling without replacement from $\{1,\dots,N\}$, the expectation of $M$ is
> $$
> E[M]=\frac{n(N+1)}{n+1}.
> $$
>
> This shows that $M$ is biased downward as an estimator of $N$, since typically $M<N$.
>
> ### Step 2: Remove the bias
>
> Seek an estimator of the form
> $$
> \hat N=aM+b
> $$
> such that
> $$
> E[\hat N]=N.
> $$
> Using linearity of expectation,
> $$
> E[\hat N]=aE[M]+b
> = a\frac{n(N+1)}{n+1}+b.
> $$
> To make this equal to $N$ for all $N$, choose $a$ and $b$ so that
> $$
> a\frac{n}{n+1}=1,
> \qquad
> a\frac{n}{n+1}+b=0.
> $$
> From the first equation,
> $$
> a=\frac{n+1}{n}.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> b=-1.
> $$
>
> Hence the unbiased estimator is
> $$
> \boxed{\hat N=\frac{n+1}{n}M-1.}
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
> $$
> \boxed{\hat N=M+\frac{M}{n}-1.}
> $$
>
> This is the classical estimator in the German tank problem.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Mean and standard deviation
>
> There are two natural interpretations here:
>
> - the mean and standard deviation of the sample maximum $M$,
> - the mean and standard deviation of the estimator $\hat N$.
>
> Both are given below.
>
> ---
>
> ### Mean and standard deviation of $M$
>
> For the maximum $M$ of a sample of size $n$ from $\{1,\dots,N\}$ without replacement,
>
> $$
> E[M]=\frac{n(N+1)}{n+1}.
> $$
>
> Its variance is
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(M)=\frac{n(N+1)(N-n)}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the standard deviation is
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{SD}(M)=
> \sqrt{\frac{n(N+1)(N-n)}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}}.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Mean and standard deviation of the estimator $\hat N$
>
> Since
> $$
> \hat N=\frac{n+1}{n}M-1,
> $$
> its mean is
> $$
> E[\hat N]=\frac{n+1}{n}E[M]-1
> =\frac{n+1}{n}\cdot \frac{n(N+1)}{n+1}-1
> =N.
> $$
> So $\hat N$ is unbiased.
>
> Its variance is
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(\hat N)
> =\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^2 \operatorname{Var}(M).
> $$
> Substituting the variance of $M$,
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(\hat N)
> =\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^2
> \frac{n(N+1)(N-n)}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}
> =
> \frac{(N+1)(N-n)}{n(n+2)}.
> $$
> Hence the standard deviation is
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{SD}(\hat N)=
> \sqrt{\frac{(N+1)(N-n)}{n(n+2)}}.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The classical unbiased estimator for the German tank problem is
> $$
> \boxed{\hat N=\frac{n+1}{n}M-1, \qquad M=\max(X_1,\dots,X_n).}
> $$
>
> Its mean is
> $$
> \boxed{E[\hat N]=N,}
> $$
> and its standard deviation is
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{SD}(\hat N)=\sqrt{\frac{(N+1)(N-n)}{n(n+2)}}.}
> $$
>
> For the sample maximum itself,
> $$
> \boxed{E[M]=\frac{n(N+1)}{n+1}},
> \qquad
> \boxed{\operatorname{SD}(M)=\sqrt{\frac{n(N+1)(N-n)}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}}.}
> $$
