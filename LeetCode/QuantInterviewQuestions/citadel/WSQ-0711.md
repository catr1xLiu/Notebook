> [!example] Question 711 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Calculate the variance of x, denoted var(x), given that the data points are distributed uniformly on the surface of a 3D sphere.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=712)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume $X=(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ is uniformly distributed **inside** a solid sphere of radius $R$ centered at the origin. By symmetry,
> $$
> E[X_1]=E[X_2]=E[X_3]=0,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X_1)=E[X_1^2].
> $$
>
> Also, since
> $$
> X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2 = r^2,
> $$
> symmetry gives
> $$
> E[X_1^2]=E[X_2^2]=E[X_3^2]=\frac{1}{3}E[r^2].
> $$
> Thus it suffices to compute $E[r^2]$ for a point uniformly distributed in the ball.
>
> The radial density in a $3$-dimensional ball is proportional to $r^2$, so
> $$
> f_r(r)=\frac{3r^2}{R^3}, \qquad 0\le r\le R.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> E[r^2]=\int_0^R r^2 f_r(r)\,dr
> = \int_0^R r^2 \frac{3r^2}{R^3}\,dr
> = \frac{3}{R^3}\int_0^R r^4\,dr
> = \frac{3}{R^3}\cdot \frac{R^5}{5}
> = \frac{3R^2}{5}.
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X_1)=E[X_1^2]=\frac{1}{3}E[r^2]
> =\frac{1}{3}\cdot \frac{3R^2}{5}
> =\frac{R^2}{5}.
> $$
>
> So for any coordinate $x$ of a point uniformly distributed in a $3$D sphere,
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{Var}(x)=\frac{R^2}{5}}.
> $$
>
> If instead the points are distributed uniformly on the **surface** of the sphere, then
> $$
> E[r^2]=R^2,
> $$
> and similarly
> $$
> \boxed{\operatorname{Var}(x)=\frac{R^2}{3}}.
> $$
