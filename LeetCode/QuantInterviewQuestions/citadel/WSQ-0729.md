> [!example] Question 729 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You have two eggs and a 100-story building. If an egg is dropped from a floor below floor X, it will not break; if dropped from floor X or higher, it will always break. What strategy would you use to determine the exact value of X with the minimum number of drops in the worst-case scenario?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=730)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let $X$ be the critical floor: an egg does not break from any floor $< X$, and it does break from any floor $\ge X$. We have $2$ eggs and a building with $100$ floors. The goal is to determine $X$ while minimizing the **maximum** number of drops required.
>
> ---
>
> ### 1. Why equal intervals are not optimal
>
> If one were to drop the first egg every $k$ floors, then after it breaks, the second egg would need to check floors one by one in the previous interval. The worst-case number of drops would then be
>
> $$
> \frac{100}{k} + (k-1),
> $$
>
> which is not minimized well by fixed spacing.
>
> A better idea is to choose the first-egg drops so that, no matter when the first egg breaks, the total number of drops is the same.
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Optimal decreasing-gap strategy
>
> Suppose the first egg is dropped first from floor $n$.
>
> - If it breaks there, then the second egg must test floors $1,2,\dots,n-1$, requiring at most $n-1$ additional drops.
> - So in that case the total is at most $n$ drops.
>
> To keep the same worst-case bound of $n$, the next drop of the first egg should be $n-1$ floors higher. Then if it breaks on that second drop, the second egg has at most $n-2$ floors to test, so again the total is at most $n$.
>
> Continuing this way, the first egg should be dropped at floors
>
> $$
> n,\quad n+(n-1),\quad n+(n-1)+(n-2),\quad \dots
> $$
>
> Thus the gaps decrease by $1$ each time.
>
> To cover all $100$ floors, we need
>
> $$
> n + (n-1) + (n-2) + \cdots + 1 \ge 100.
> $$
>
> That sum is
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \ge 100.
> $$
>
> Now compute the smallest such $n$:
>
> $$
> \frac{13 \cdot 14}{2} = 91 < 100,
> $$
>
> $$
> \frac{14 \cdot 15}{2} = 105 \ge 100.
> $$
>
> So the minimum worst-case number of drops is
>
> $$
> 14.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### 3. The actual strategy
>
> Drop the first egg from floors:
>
> $$
> 14,\ 27,\ 39,\ 50,\ 60,\ 69,\ 77,\ 84,\ 90,\ 95,\ 99,\ 100
> $$
>
> where the increments are
>
> $$
> 14,\ 13,\ 12,\ 11,\ 10,\ 9,\ 8,\ 7,\ 6,\ 5,\ 4,\ 1.
> $$
>
> More systematically, the first egg is dropped from floors obtained by adding decreasing step sizes:
>
> - first at $14$,
> - then $14+13=27$,
> - then $27+12=39$,
> - then $39+11=50$,
> - and so on.
>
> When the first egg breaks, use the second egg to test floors one by one starting from the floor just above the previous safe floor.
>
> ---
>
> ### 4. Why this gives at most $14$ drops
>
> For example:
>
> - If the first egg breaks at floor $14$, then test floors $1$ through $13$ with the second egg.  
>   Maximum total: $1+13=14$ drops.
>
> - If the first egg breaks at floor $27$, then the second egg tests floors $15$ through $26$.  
>   Maximum total: $2+12=14$ drops.
>
> - If the first egg breaks at floor $39$, then the second egg tests floors $28$ through $38$.  
>   Maximum total: $3+11=14$ drops.
>
> And so on. In every case, the total number of drops is at most $14$.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> Use a **decreasing-step strategy**: drop the first egg from floors chosen so that the step sizes decrease by $1$ each time. Let the first drop be from floor $n$, where $n$ is the smallest integer satisfying
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \ge 100.
> $$
>
> This gives
>
> $$
> n=14.
> $$
>
> So the optimal strategy is to drop the first egg from floors
>
> $$
> 14,\ 27,\ 39,\ 50,\ 60,\ 69,\ 77,\ 84,\ 90,\ 95,\ 99,\ 100.
> $$
>
> When the first egg breaks, use the second egg to test the floors in the previous interval one by one.
>
> This guarantees finding $X$ in at most
>
> $$
> 14
> $$
>
> drops in the worst case, which is minimal.
