> [!example] Question 819 · Citadel
> ## Trading Interview

You are tossing a fair coin. What is the expected number of tosses until you get two heads in a row?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=820)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of tosses needed to obtain two consecutive heads.
>
> To compute $E$, introduce states according to the recent outcome:
>
> - State $S_0$: no previous head to build on.
> - State $S_1$: the last toss was a head, so one more head ends the process.
>
> Let
>
> - $E_0$ be the expected remaining number of tosses starting from $S_0$,
> - $E_1$ be the expected remaining number of tosses starting from $S_1$.
>
> We seek $E_0$.
>
> ## Step 1: Set up equations
>
> From state $S_0$:
>
> - We make one toss.
> - With probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, we get a tail and remain in $S_0$.
> - With probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, we get a head and move to $S_1$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0 + \frac{1}{2}E_1.
> $$
>
> From state $S_1$:
>
> - We make one toss.
> - With probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, we get a head and finish.
> - With probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$, we get a tail and return to $S_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 0 + \frac{1}{2}E_0
> = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve the system
>
> Substitute $E_1 = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}E_0$ into the first equation:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0 + \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0\right).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}E_0
> = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{4}E_0.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E_0 - \frac{3}{4}E_0 = \frac{3}{2},
> $$
> $$
> \frac{1}{4}E_0 = \frac{3}{2},
> $$
> $$
> E_0 = 6.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of tosses required to get two heads in a row is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6}.
> $$
