> [!example] Question 816 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher (Intern) Interview

Given a list of stock prices for consecutive days, determine the optimal strategy to maximize profit from buying and selling stocks. You may complete as many transactions as you like (i.e., buy and sell multiple times), but you must sell the stock before you buy again.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=817)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> You are given a list $prices$, where $prices[i]$ is the stock price on day $i$. You may complete as many buy-sell transactions as you like, but you must sell your current holding before buying again. The goal is to maximize total profit.
>
> ### Approach
>
> Because any number of transactions is allowed, the optimal strategy is to capture every profitable up-move in the price sequence. Whenever the price increases from one day to the next, that increase can always be captured by buying the day before the rise and selling the day after it, and consecutive rising days can be chained together, selling and immediately rebuying, without losing any profit compared to holding through the whole rise.
>
> This means the maximum total profit is simply the sum of all positive consecutive differences:
>
> $$
> \text{maxProfit} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \max(prices[i]-prices[i-1],\ 0).
> $$
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> 1. Initialize $maxProfit = 0$.
> 2. For each consecutive pair of days $i-1,i$ with $prices[i] > prices[i-1]$, add $prices[i]-prices[i-1]$ to $maxProfit$.
> 3. Return $maxProfit$.
>
> ### Example
>
> For $prices = [7,1,5,3,6,4]$:
>
> - $1-7=-6$: no gain, skip.
> - $5-1=4$: gain, add $4$.
> - $3-5=-2$: no gain, skip.
> - $6-3=3$: gain, add $3$.
> - $4-6=-2$: no gain, skip.
>
> Total profit:
>
> $$
> 4+3=7.
> $$
>
> This corresponds to buying at $1$ and selling at $5$ for a profit of $4$, then buying at $3$ and selling at $6$ for a profit of $3$.
>
> ### Correctness
>
> Summing every positive day-to-day difference is optimal because any achievable sequence of buy-sell transactions has total profit equal to the sum of the price changes over the days it holds the stock, and each such change is at most $\max(prices[i]-prices[i-1],0)$. Capturing every positive difference, and none of the negative ones, attains the largest possible total, and this target is always achievable since consecutive positive differences can be chained together with a sell-then-immediately-rebuy at the shared day.
>
> ### Complexity
>
> - Time: $O(n)$
> - Space: $O(1)$
>
> ### Reference implementation
>
> ```python
> def maxProfit(prices):
>     profit = 0
>     for i in range(1, len(prices)):
>         if prices[i] > prices[i-1]:
>             profit += prices[i] - prices[i-1]
>     return profit
> ```
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> Since unlimited transactions are allowed, the optimal strategy is to buy just before every price increase and sell just after it, capturing each positive day-to-day change. The maximum profit is the sum of all positive consecutive differences in the price sequence, computable in $O(n)$ time and $O(1)$ space.
