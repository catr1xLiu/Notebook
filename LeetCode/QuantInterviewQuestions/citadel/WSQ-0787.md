> [!example] Question 787 · Citadel
> ## Internship Interview

Explain how to use a binomial tree to determine the price of a European call option.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=788)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Pricing a European Call Option with a Binomial Tree
>
> A binomial tree prices an option by modeling the stock price over discrete time steps. At each step, the stock can move either **up** by a factor $u$ or **down** by a factor $d$. The option value is then found by **backward induction** under the **risk-neutral measure**.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Set up the model
>
> Suppose:
>
> - Current stock price: $S_0$
> - Strike price: $K$
> - Time to maturity: $T$
> - Number of time steps: $N$
> - Risk-free interest rate: $r$
> - Volatility: $\sigma$
>
> Let the length of each step be
>
> $$
> \Delta t = \frac{T}{N}.
> $$
>
> A common choice for the up and down factors is the Cox–Ross–Rubinstein specification:
>
> $$
> u = e^{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}, 
> \qquad
> d = e^{-\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}} = \frac{1}{u}.
> $$
>
> The risk-neutral probability is
>
> $$
> p = \frac{e^{r \Delta t} - d}{u - d}.
> $$
>
> This requires
>
> $$
> d < e^{r\Delta t} < u,
> $$
>
> so that $0 < p < 1$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Build the stock-price tree
>
> At step $n$, after $j$ up moves and $n-j$ down moves, the stock price is
>
> $$
> S_{n,j} = S_0 u^j d^{\,n-j},
> \qquad j = 0,1,\dots,n.
> $$
>
> Thus, at maturity $n = N$, the possible stock prices are
>
> $$
> S_{N,j} = S_0 u^j d^{\,N-j},
> \qquad j = 0,1,\dots,N.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Compute the call payoff at maturity
>
> For a European call option, the payoff at maturity is
>
> $$
> C_{N,j} = \max\!\left(S_{N,j} - K,\, 0\right).
> $$
>
> This gives the option value at the final nodes of the tree.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Work backward through the tree
>
> Because the option is European, it can only be exercised at maturity. Therefore, at each earlier node, its value is the discounted expected value under the risk-neutral probability:
>
> $$
> C_{n,j} = e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p\,C_{n+1,j+1} + (1-p)\,C_{n+1,j}\right],
> $$
>
> for
>
> $$
> n = N-1, N-2, \dots, 0.
> $$
>
> Continue this recursion until the initial node is reached. The option price is then
>
> $$
> C_0 = C_{0,0}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Closed-form binomial expression
>
> Equivalently, after $N$ steps the European call price can be written as
>
> $$
> C_0
> =
> e^{-rT}
> \sum_{j=0}^{N}
> \binom{N}{j}
> p^j(1-p)^{N-j}
> \max\!\left(S_0 u^j d^{\,N-j} - K,\, 0\right).
> $$
>
> This is the discounted risk-neutral expected payoff.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Numerical example
>
> Take:
>
> - $S_0 = 100$
> - $K = 100$
> - $T = 1$
> - $N = 2$
> - $r = 0.05$
> - $\sigma = 0.20$
>
> ### Step 1: Compute tree parameters
>
> Since
>
> $$
> \Delta t = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> u = e^{0.2\sqrt{0.5}} \approx 1.1519,
> \qquad
> d = e^{-0.2\sqrt{0.5}} \approx 0.8681.
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> e^{r\Delta t} = e^{0.05 \cdot 0.5} = e^{0.025} \approx 1.0253.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> p = \frac{1.0253 - 0.8681}{1.1519 - 0.8681}
> \approx 0.5539.
> $$
>
> The discount factor per step is
>
> $$
> e^{-r\Delta t} = e^{-0.025} \approx 0.9753.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 2: Stock prices at maturity
>
> At $N=2$, the possible stock prices are:
>
> - Up-up:
>
> $$
> S_{2,2} = 100u^2 \approx 100(1.1519)^2 \approx 132.69
> $$
>
> - Up-down (or down-up):
>
> $$
> S_{2,1} = 100ud = 100
> $$
>
> - Down-down:
>
> $$
> S_{2,0} = 100d^2 \approx 100(0.8681)^2 \approx 75.36
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 3: Call payoffs at maturity
>
> $$
> C_{2,2} = \max(132.69 - 100, 0) = 32.69
> $$
>
> $$
> C_{2,1} = \max(100 - 100, 0) = 0
> $$
>
> $$
> C_{2,0} = \max(75.36 - 100, 0) = 0
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Step 4: Backward induction
>
> At the up node at time $1$,
>
> $$
> C_{1,1}
> =
> e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p\,C_{2,2} + (1-p)\,C_{2,1}\right]
> $$
>
> $$
> =
> 0.9753\left[0.5539(32.69) + 0.4461(0)\right]
> \approx 17.66
> $$
>
> At the down node at time $1$,
>
> $$
> C_{1,0}
> =
> e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p\,C_{2,1} + (1-p)\,C_{2,0}\right]
> =
> 0
> $$
>
> Finally, at time $0$,
>
> $$
> C_0
> =
> e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p\,C_{1,1} + (1-p)\,C_{1,0}\right]
> $$
>
> $$
> =
> 0.9753\left[0.5539(17.66) + 0.4461(0)\right]
> \approx 9.54
> $$
>
> So the European call option price is
>
> $$
> C_0 \approx 9.54.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Summary of the procedure
>
> To price a European call with a binomial tree:
>
> 1. Choose $N$ and compute
>    $$
>    \Delta t = \frac{T}{N}.
>    $$
> 2. Compute
>    $$
>    u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}, \qquad d = e^{-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}.
>    $$
> 3. Compute the risk-neutral probability
>    $$
>    p = \frac{e^{r\Delta t} - d}{u - d}.
>    $$
> 4. Build stock prices
>    $$
>    S_{n,j} = S_0 u^j d^{\,n-j}.
>    $$
> 5. At maturity, assign payoffs
>    $$
>    C_{N,j} = \max(S_{N,j}-K,0).
>    $$
> 6. Discount backward using
>    $$
>    C_{n,j} = e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p\,C_{n+1,j+1} + (1-p)\,C_{n+1,j}\right].
>    $$
> 7. The initial value $C_{0,0}$ is the option price.
>
> Thus, the binomial tree determines the European call price by computing the discounted risk-neutral expected payoff through a recombining stock-price lattice.
