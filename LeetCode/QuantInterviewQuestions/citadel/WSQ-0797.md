> [!example] Question 797 · Citadel
> ## Quant Research Intern Interview

Given the numbers 1 to 1000 arranged around a circle in order, start at 1 and delete every alternate number (i.e., remove 1, then 3, then 5, and so on). Continue this process of deleting every other remaining number, wrapping around the circle as necessary, until only one number remains. Determine this final remaining number.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=798)

> # Solution
>
> This is the Josephus problem with step size $2$, but with a small twist in the starting rule: instead of the usual convention where the first person is skipped and the second person is the first to be eliminated, here the elimination begins immediately with person $1$.
>
> ## Step 1: The classical Josephus formula
>
> Let $J(n)$ denote the surviving position, among $n$ people labeled $1$ through $n$ around a circle, under the classical convention where counting starts at person $1$, person $2$ is eliminated first, and every second remaining person is removed thereafter.
>
> A standard closed form for step size $2$ is
> $$
> J(n) = 2\ell+1, \qquad \text{where } n = 2^m+\ell,\ 0\le \ell < 2^m,
> $$
> so that $2^m$ is the largest power of $2$ not exceeding $n$.
>
> For $n=1000$,
> $$
> 2^9=512, \qquad 1000 = 512+488,
> $$
> giving
> $$
> J(1000) = 2\cdot 488+1 = 977.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Adjusting for this problem's starting rule
>
> Here, person $1$ is removed first rather than skipped, so the entire elimination pattern is shifted back by exactly one position relative to the classical process described above: eliminating person $1$ first produces exactly the same sequence of eliminations as the classical process, but with every label reduced by $1$, wrapping around from $1$ to $n$.
>
> To check this on a small case, take $n=4$. Direct simulation of this problem's rule gives
> $$
> \text{remove } 1,\ \ \text{skip } 2,\ \ \text{remove } 3,\ \ \text{skip } 4,\ \ \text{remove } 2,
> $$
> leaving $4$ as the sole survivor. The classical formula gives $J(4)=1$, since $4=4+0$ so $\ell=0$. Shifting $J(4)=1$ down by one position, with wraparound, gives survivor $n=4$, matching the direct simulation.
>
> In general, shifting the classical survivor down by one position, with wraparound, gives
>
> $$
> \text{survivor} = \big((J(n)-2) \bmod n\big) + 1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Apply to $n=1000$
>
> Since $J(1000)=977$,
>
> $$
> \text{survivor} = ((977-2) \bmod 1000)+1 = 975+1 = 976.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the last remaining number is
>
> $$
> \boxed{976}.
> $$
