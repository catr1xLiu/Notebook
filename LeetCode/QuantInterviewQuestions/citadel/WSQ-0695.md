> [!example] Question 695 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given three variables x, y, and z, where x is distributed as N(0,1), z is distributed as N(0, sigma^2), and y = x + z, with x independent of z: For a given observed value of y, what is the conditional distribution of x given y, i.e., p(x\|y)?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=696)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Since
> $$
> x \sim N(0,1), \qquad z \sim N(0,\sigma^2), \qquad y=x+z,
> $$
> with $x$ independent of $z$, this is a standard linear-Gaussian model. Therefore, the conditional distribution $p(x\mid y)$ is also Gaussian.
>
> ## Step 1: Determine the joint distribution of $(x,y)$
>
> Because
> $$
> y=x+z,
> $$
> we have
> $$
> E[x]=0, \qquad E[y]=E[x+z]=0.
> $$
>
> The variances and covariance are
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(x)=1,
> $$
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(y)=\operatorname{Var}(x+z)=\operatorname{Var}(x)+\operatorname{Var}(z)=1+\sigma^2,
> $$
> since $x$ and $z$ are independent, and
> $$
> \operatorname{Cov}(x,y)=\operatorname{Cov}(x,x+z)=\operatorname{Var}(x)+\operatorname{Cov}(x,z)=1.
> $$
>
> Thus,
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}
> x\\
> y
> \end{pmatrix}
> \sim N\!\left(
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0\\
> 0
> \end{pmatrix},
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & 1\\
> 1 & 1+\sigma^2
> \end{pmatrix}
> \right).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Use the Gaussian conditioning formula
>
> For jointly Gaussian variables, the conditional distribution of $x$ given $y$ is
> $$
> x\mid y \sim N\!\left(
> \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)}{\operatorname{Var}(y)}\,y,\;
> \operatorname{Var}(x)-\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(x,y)^2}{\operatorname{Var}(y)}
> \right).
> $$
>
> Substituting the values:
> $$
> E[x\mid y]=\frac{1}{1+\sigma^2}y,
> $$
> and
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(x\mid y)=1-\frac{1^2}{1+\sigma^2}
> =\frac{\sigma^2}{1+\sigma^2}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> p(x\mid y)=N\!\left(\frac{y}{1+\sigma^2},\; \frac{\sigma^2}{1+\sigma^2}\right).
> $$
>
> Equivalently, the density is
> $$
> p(x\mid y)
> =
> \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,\sigma^2/(1+\sigma^2)}}
> \exp\!\left(
> -\frac{\left(x-\frac{y}{1+\sigma^2}\right)^2}{2\,\sigma^2/(1+\sigma^2)}
> \right).
> $$
>
> So, given an observed $y$, the posterior expectation of $x$ is
> $$
> E[x\mid y]=\frac{y}{1+\sigma^2},
> $$
> with posterior uncertainty
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(x\mid y)=\frac{\sigma^2}{1+\sigma^2}.
> $$
