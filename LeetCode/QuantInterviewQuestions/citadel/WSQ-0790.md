> [!example] Question 790 · Citadel
> ## Quantitative Research Analyst Interview

Suppose you have three random variables. The correlation between A and B is 0.6, and the correlation between A and C is 0.8. What is the possible range of the correlation between B and C?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=791)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $\rho_{AB} = 0.6$, $\rho_{AC} = 0.8$, and let $\rho_{BC} = x$. We seek all possible values of $x$ such that these can be the pairwise correlations of three random variables.
>
> ## Approach
>
> A valid correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite. Thus the matrix
>
> $$
> R=
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 1 & 0.6 & 0.8 \\
> 0.6 & 1 & x \\
> 0.8 & x & 1
> \end{pmatrix}
> $$
>
> must be positive semidefinite.
>
> For a $3 \times 3$ correlation matrix, this requires in particular that its determinant be nonnegative:
>
> $$
> \det(R) \ge 0.
> $$
>
> ## Compute the determinant
>
> Using the standard formula,
>
> $$
> \det(R)=1+2(0.6)(0.8)x-(0.6)^2-(0.8)^2-x^2.
> $$
>
> Now simplify:
>
> $$
> \det(R)=1+0.96x-0.36-0.64-x^2.
> $$
>
> Since $0.36+0.64=1$, this becomes
>
> $$
> \det(R)=0.96x-x^2.
> $$
>
> So we require
>
> $$
> 0.96x-x^2 \ge 0.
> $$
>
> Factor:
>
> $$
> x(0.96-x)\ge 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> 0 \le x \le 0.96.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The correlation between $B$ and $C$ must lie in the range
>
> $$
> \boxed{0 \le \rho_{BC} \le 0.96}.
> $$
