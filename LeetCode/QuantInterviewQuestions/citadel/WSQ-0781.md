> [!example] Question 781 · Citadel
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Find the number of ways to get from one corner of a grid to the opposite corner, moving only down or right at each step.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=782)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To go from one corner of an $m \times n$ grid to the opposite corner by moving only right or down, every valid path must consist of exactly
>
> - $m$ moves in one direction, and
> - $n$ moves in the other direction.
>
> Thus each path is a sequence of $m+n$ moves containing:
>
> - $m$ downs and $n$ rights, or equivalently
> - $n$ rights and $m$ downs.
>
> So the problem reduces to counting how many distinct arrangements there are of these moves.
>
> The number of such arrangements is
>
> $$
> \binom{m+n}{m}
> $$
>
> since we choose which $m$ of the $m+n$ positions are downward moves. Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \binom{m+n}{n}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the number of paths is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{m+n}{m}=\binom{m+n}{n}}.
> $$
>
> In factorial form, this is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{(m+n)!}{m!\,n!}}.
> $$
