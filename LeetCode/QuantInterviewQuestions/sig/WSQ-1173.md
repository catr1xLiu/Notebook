> [!example] Question 1173 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Person A completes a job in 100 minutes. Person B completes the same job in 120 minutes. They work together for 30 minutes, then person C joins. Together, the three complete the job in another 20 minutes. How long would it take for person C to complete the job alone?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1174)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the whole job be $1$ unit.
>
> Person A’s rate is
> $$
> \frac{1}{100}
> $$
> job per minute, and person B’s rate is
> $$
> \frac{1}{120}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> Thus, A and B together work at the rate
> $$
> \frac{1}{100}+\frac{1}{120}
> = \frac{6}{600}+\frac{5}{600}
> = \frac{11}{600}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> In the first $30$ minutes, they complete
> $$
> 30 \cdot \frac{11}{600} = \frac{11}{20}
> $$
> of the job.
>
> So the remaining work is
> $$
> 1-\frac{11}{20}=\frac{9}{20}.
> $$
>
> This remaining $\frac{9}{20}$ of the job is completed in the next $20$ minutes by A, B, and C together. Therefore their combined rate is
> $$
> \frac{\frac{9}{20}}{20}=\frac{9}{400}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> Hence person C’s rate is
> $$
> \frac{9}{400}-\frac{11}{600}
> = \frac{27}{1200}-\frac{22}{1200}
> = \frac{5}{1200}
> = \frac{1}{240}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> Therefore, person C alone would take
> $$
> 240
> $$
> minutes to complete the job.
>
> $$
> \boxed{240 \text{ minutes}}
> $$
