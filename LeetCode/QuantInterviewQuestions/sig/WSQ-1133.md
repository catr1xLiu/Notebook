> [!example] Question 1133 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have 1,000 bottles of wine, one of which is poisoned. You have a number of slaves you can use for testing, and you want to minimize the number of deaths while guaranteeing you find the poisoned bottle. The poison is fast-acting and fatal within an hour, and you have just over an hour before your guests arrive. Each slave can taste test any number of bottles. What strategy will allow you to find the poisoned bottle using the fewest number of slaves?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1134)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To minimize the number of slaves who die, one should minimize the number of slaves used at all, since any slave who tastes the poisoned bottle may die, and with only one testing round available, the dead/alive pattern after one hour is the only information obtained.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> Each slave has two possible outcomes after the test:
>
> - alive,
> - dead.
>
> Thus, with $n$ slaves, there are at most $2^n$ distinct outcome patterns. To be able to identify exactly one poisoned bottle among $1000$ bottles, we need
>
> $$
> 2^n \ge 1000.
> $$
>
> Now,
>
> $$
> 2^9 = 512 < 1000,\qquad 2^{10} = 1024 \ge 1000.
> $$
>
> So at least $10$ slaves are necessary, and $10$ slaves are sufficient.
>
> ## Strategy
>
> Label the bottles from $0$ to $999$. Write each bottle number in $10$-bit binary form.
>
> Also label the $10$ slaves as slave $1$ through slave $10$, corresponding to the $10$ binary digit positions.
>
> For each bottle:
>
> - if the $k$th binary digit of its label is $1$, then slave $k$ drinks a sample from that bottle;
> - if the $k$th binary digit is $0$, slave $k$ does not drink from that bottle.
>
> Since slaves can taste many bottles, this is feasible in one round.
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Exactly one bottle is poisoned. A slave dies precisely when that slave tasted from the poisoned bottle. Therefore, after one hour, the set of dead slaves gives the binary representation of the poisoned bottle’s number:
>
> - dead slave $\leftrightarrow 1$,
> - living slave $\leftrightarrow 0$.
>
> This outcome uniquely identifies the poisoned bottle.
>
> ## Example
>
> If bottle $357$ is poisoned, then
>
> $$
> 357 = 0101100101_2
> $$
>
> (in $10$ bits). The slaves corresponding to the $1$'s in that binary expansion die, and the dead/alive pattern reads off $357$.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The fewest number of slaves needed is
>
> $$
> \boxed{10}.
> $$
>
> Use the binary encoding strategy above to guarantee identification of the poisoned bottle in one hour.
