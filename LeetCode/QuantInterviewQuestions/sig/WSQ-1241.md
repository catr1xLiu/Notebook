> [!example] Question 1241 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Research Associate Interview

If you toss 100 fair coins, what is the probability of getting more than 60 heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1242)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $100$ fair coins are tossed. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n=100,p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X>60)=P(X\ge 61).
> $$
>
> Using the binomial formula,
>
> $$
> P(X\ge 61)=\sum_{k=61}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(X>60)=\frac{1}{2^{100}}\sum_{k=61}^{100}\binom{100}{k}.
> $$
>
> Numerically, this is
>
> $$
> P(X>60)\approx 0.0176.
> $$
>
> So the probability of getting more than $60$ heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.0176 \text{ (approximately)}}
> $$
>
> which is about
>
> $$
> \boxed{1.76\%}.
> $$
