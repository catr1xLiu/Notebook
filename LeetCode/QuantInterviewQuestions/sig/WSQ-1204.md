> [!example] Question 1204 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

Aaron, Bryce, and Craig are all of different integer ages. They had the following conversation: (i) Bryce to Craig: 'You're the youngest.' (ii) Aaron to Bryce: 'Your age is exactly 70% greater than mine.' (iii) Aaron to Craig: 'Your age is the average of my age and Bryce's age.' (iv) Craig to Aaron: 'I'm at least 8 years older than you.' However, not all of these statements are true. Whenever speaking to someone older, a speaker always tells the truth; when speaking to someone younger, a speaker always lies. How old is Craig?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1205)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let Aaron’s, Bryce’s, and Craig’s ages be $A$, $B$, and $C$, respectively, where $A$, $B$, $C$ are distinct integers.
>
> We use the rule:
>
> - if a person speaks to someone older, the statement is true;
> - if a person speaks to someone younger, the statement is false.
>
> There are four statements:
>
> 1. Bryce to Craig: “You’re the youngest one.”
> 2. Aaron to Bryce: “Your age is exactly $70\%$ greater than mine.”
> 3. Aaron to Craig: “Your age is the average of my age and Bryce’s age.”
> 4. Craig to Aaron: “I’m at least $8$ years older than you.”
>
> We determine the age ordering and then solve the resulting equations.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 1: Translate each statement
>
> ### Statement 1: Bryce to Craig
> Bryce speaks to Craig.
>
> - If $C>B$, then Bryce is speaking to someone older, so the statement is true.
> - If $C<B$, then Bryce is speaking to someone younger, so the statement is false.
>
> The content of the statement is: Craig is the youngest, i.e. $C<A$ and $C<B$.
>
> But if $C>B$, then Craig cannot be younger than Bryce, so Craig cannot be the youngest. Thus if $C>B$, the statement would have to be true by the rule, but its content would be impossible. Therefore $C>B$ cannot happen.
>
> Hence we must have $C<B$, so Bryce is speaking to someone younger and therefore lying. That is consistent, since Craig is not the youngest.
>
> So we obtain
>
> $$
> B>C.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ### Statement 2: Aaron to Bryce
> Aaron speaks to Bryce.
>
> The statement is
>
> $$
> B = 1.7A = \frac{17}{10}A.
> $$
>
> Since $B$ is an integer, $A$ must be a multiple of $10$.
>
> Truth depends on whether $B>A$ or $B<A$:
>
> - if $B>A$, Aaron speaks to someone older, so the statement is true;
> - if $B<A$, Aaron speaks to someone younger, so the statement is false.
>
> Because the equation itself implies $B>A$, the only way this can be consistent is that the statement is true. Therefore
>
> $$
> B>A
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> B=\frac{17}{10}A.
> $$
>
> So let
>
> $$
> A=10k,\qquad B=17k
> $$
>
> for some positive integer $k$.
>
> ---
>
> ### Statement 3: Aaron to Craig
> Aaron speaks to Craig.
>
> The statement is
>
> $$
> C=\frac{A+B}{2}.
> $$
>
> Substitute $A=10k$ and $B=17k$:
>
> $$
> C=\frac{10k+17k}{2}=\frac{27k}{2}.
> $$
>
> If this statement were true, then $k$ would have to be even.
>
> Truth depends on whether $C>A$ or $C<A$:
>
> - if $C>A$, Aaron speaks to someone older, so the statement is true;
> - if $C<A$, Aaron speaks to someone younger, so the statement is false.
>
> ---
>
> ### Statement 4: Craig to Aaron
> Craig speaks to Aaron.
>
> The statement is
>
> $$
> C\ge A+8.
> $$
>
> Truth depends on whether $A>C$ or $A<C$:
>
> - if $A>C$, Craig speaks to someone older, so the statement is true;
> - if $A<C$, Craig speaks to someone younger, so the statement is false.
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 2: Use the ordering information
>
> From Statement 2, we already have
>
> $$
> B>A.
> $$
>
> From Statement 1, we have
>
> $$
> B>C.
> $$
>
> So Bryce is oldest, or at least older than both Aaron and Craig. The only remaining question is whether $C>A$ or $A>C$.
>
> ### Could $A>C$?
> Then in Statement 3, Aaron speaks to someone younger, so Statement 3 must be false. That is possible.
>
> But in Statement 4, Craig speaks to someone older, so Statement 4 must be true:
>
> $$
> C\ge A+8.
> $$
>
> This is impossible if $A>C$.
>
> Therefore $A>C$ cannot happen.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> C>A.
> $$
>
> So the age order is
>
> $$
> B>C>A.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 3: Enforce truth/lie conditions with this order
>
> Since $C>A$, Statement 3 is spoken to an older person, so it must be true:
>
> $$
> C=\frac{A+B}{2}.
> $$
>
> Using $A=10k$ and $B=17k$,
>
> $$
> C=\frac{27k}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus $k$ must be even. Write
>
> $$
> k=2m.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> A=20m,\qquad B=34m,\qquad C=27m.
> $$
>
> Now use Statement 4. Since $C>A$, Craig is speaking to someone younger, so Statement 4 must be false. Therefore
>
> $$
> C<A+8.
> $$
>
> Substitute:
>
> $$
> 27m<20m+8
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> 7m<8.
> $$
>
> Since $m$ is a positive integer, this forces
>
> $$
> m=1.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> A=20,\qquad B=34,\qquad C=27.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Step 4: Verify all statements
>
> - Bryce to Craig: since $34>27$, Bryce speaks to someone younger, so he lies.  
>   “Craig is the youngest” is false, since Aaron is $20$.
>
> - Aaron to Bryce: since $34>20$, Aaron speaks to someone older, so he tells the truth.  
>   $34$ is exactly $70\%$ greater than $20$.
>
> - Aaron to Craig: since $27>20$, Aaron speaks to someone older, so he tells the truth.  
>   $$27=\frac{20+34}{2}.$$
>
> - Craig to Aaron: since $27>20$, Craig speaks to someone younger, so he lies.  
>   “I am at least $8$ years older than you” is false, because $27-20=7$.
>
> All conditions are satisfied.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{27}
> $$
