> [!example] Question 1237 · SIG
> ## Equity Research Analyst Interview

What is the fair price of a call option, given the strike price and the probabilities of the underlying stock reaching specific prices?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1238)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To find the fair price of a call option, use the option’s payoff in each possible future stock-price scenario and take its expected value under the given probabilities.
>
> ### Step 1: Call option payoff
>
> If the strike price is $K$ and the stock price at expiration is $S$, then the payoff of a call option is
>
> $$
> \max(S - K, 0).
> $$
>
> This means:
>
> - if $S > K$, the payoff is $S - K$,
> - if $S \le K$, the payoff is $0$.
>
> ### Step 2: Compute the expected payoff
>
> Suppose the stock can take values $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_n$ with corresponding probabilities $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n$. Then the fair price of the call option is
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n p_i \max(S_i - K, 0).
> $$
>
> If discounting is required because the payoff occurs in the future, then the present fair price is
>
> $$
> \text{Call Price} = \frac{1}{1+r} \sum_{i=1}^n p_i \max(S_i - K, 0),
> $$
>
> where $r$ is the risk-free interest rate for the period.
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> The fair price of the call option is the expected value of its payoff:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Call Price} = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i \max(S_i - K, 0)}
> $$
>
> or, if discounting to the present is needed,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Call Price} = \frac{1}{1+r}\sum_{i=1}^n p_i \max(S_i - K, 0)}.
> $$
>
> If specific stock prices, probabilities, and a strike price are given, substitute them directly into this formula.
