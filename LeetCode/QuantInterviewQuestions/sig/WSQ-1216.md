> [!example] Question 1216 · SIG
> ## Graduate Quant Trader Interview

Pick three numbers between 1 and 20. What is the probability that one of the numbers is the average of the other two?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1217)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the three numbers are chosen uniformly at random **without replacement** from the integers $1,2,\dots,20$.
>
> We want the probability that among the three chosen numbers, one is the average of the other two. This means the three numbers form an arithmetic progression.
>
> ## Step 1: Count the total number of ways to choose $3$ numbers
>
> The total number of unordered selections is
>
> $$
> \binom{20}{3} = 1140.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Count the favorable selections
>
> Let the three numbers be $a<b<c$.  
> For one of them to be the average of the other two, we must have
>
> $$
> b=\frac{a+c}{2},
> $$
>
> so equivalently,
>
> $$
> a,b,c
> $$
>
> must be an arithmetic progression.
>
> Write the triple as
>
> $$
> a,\ a+d,\ a+2d
> $$
>
> with common difference $d\ge 1$.
>
> Since all numbers must lie between $1$ and $20$,
>
> $$
> a+2d \le 20.
> $$
>
> For a fixed $d$, the starting value $a$ can be any integer from $1$ to $20-2d$, giving
>
> $$
> 20-2d
> $$
>
> choices.
>
> Now determine the possible values of $d$:
>
> $$
> a+2d \le 20 \implies 1+2d \le 20 \implies d \le 9.
> $$
>
> So $d=1,2,\dots,9$.
>
> Hence the number of favorable triples is
>
> $$
> \sum_{d=1}^{9} (20-2d)
> = 18+16+14+12+10+8+6+4+2.
> $$
>
> This is an arithmetic series with $9$ terms, so
>
> $$
> \sum_{d=1}^{9} (20-2d)=\frac{9(18+2)}{2}=90.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P=\frac{90}{1140}=\frac{3}{38}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{3}{38}}
> $$
