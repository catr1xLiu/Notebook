> [!example] Question 1125 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

I have a painting. If it is an original, it is worth \$500,000; if it is not, it is worth \$10,000. The probability that it is an original is 0.2. I have an option to pay \$100,000 for the painting after inspection. What is the value of this option?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1126)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the painting’s value be the random variable $V$, where
>
> $$
> V =
> \begin{cases}
> 500{,}000 & \text{with probability } 0.2,\\
> 10{,}000 & \text{with probability } 0.8.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> You have an option to buy the painting for $100{,}000$ **after inspection**. Since the inspection reveals whether the painting is original, you will exercise the option only when it is profitable to do so.
>
> The payoff from the option is
>
> $$
> \max(V - 100{,}000,\, 0).
> $$
>
> Compute this in each case:
>
> - If the painting is original, the payoff is
>
> $$
> 500{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 400{,}000.
> $$
>
> - If the painting is not original, the payoff is
>
> $$
> 10{,}000 - 100{,}000 = -90{,}000,
> $$
>
> so you do not exercise, and the payoff is
>
> $$
> 0.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected value of the option is
>
> $$
> 0.2 \cdot 400{,}000 + 0.8 \cdot 0 = 80{,}000.
> $$
>
> So the value of the option is
>
> $$
> \boxed{80{,}000}.
> $$
>
> For comparison, without the option the expected value of the painting itself is
>
> $$
> 0.2 \cdot 500{,}000 + 0.8 \cdot 10{,}000 = 108{,}000,
> $$
>
> but the option is worth less because you must still pay $100{,}000$ to acquire it when you choose to exercise.
