> [!example] Question 1235 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

If you break a unit stick randomly in two places, what is the expected length of the smallest piece?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1236)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two break points be $U$ and $V$, chosen independently and uniformly on $[0,1]$. After ordering them, write
>
> $$
> X=\min(U,V), \qquad Y=\max(U,V).
> $$
>
> Then the three piece lengths are
>
> $$
> X,\qquad Y-X,\qquad 1-Y.
> $$
>
> We seek
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\big[\min(X,\;Y-X,\;1-Y)\big].
> $$
>
> ## 1. Joint density on the ordered region
>
> Since $(U,V)$ is uniform on the unit square, the ordered pair $(X,Y)$ has joint density
>
> $$
> f_{X,Y}(x,y)=2, \qquad 0\le x\le y\le 1.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\min(X,Y-X,1-Y)]
> =
> 2\iint_{0\le x\le y\le 1} \min(x,y-x,1-y)\,dx\,dy.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Use the tail expectation formula
>
> For a nonnegative random variable $M$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]=\int_0^\infty \mathbb{P}(M>t)\,dt.
> $$
>
> Here,
>
> $$
> M=\min(X,Y-X,1-Y).
> $$
>
> The event $M>t$ means all three pieces exceed $t$:
>
> $$
> X>t,\qquad Y-X>t,\qquad 1-Y>t.
> $$
>
> These inequalities are equivalent to
>
> $$
> x>t,\qquad y>x+t,\qquad y<1-t.
> $$
>
> For such points to exist, we need $t\le \tfrac13$.
>
> So for $0\le t\le \tfrac13$, the region is
>
> $$
> t<x<1-2t,\qquad x+t<y<1-t.
> $$
>
> Its area is
>
> $$
> \int_t^{\,1-2t} \big((1-t)-(x+t)\big)\,dx
> =
> \int_t^{\,1-2t} (1-2t-x)\,dx.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \int_t^{\,1-2t} (1-2t-x)\,dx
> =
> \left[(1-2t)x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{x=t}^{x=1-2t}
> =
> \frac12-3t+\frac{9}{2}t^2
> =
> \frac12(1-3t)^2.
> $$
>
> Since the density is $2$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M>t)=2\cdot \frac12(1-3t)^2=(1-3t)^2,
> \qquad 0\le t\le \frac13.
> $$
>
> And for $t>\frac13$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(M>t)=0.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \int_0^{1/3} (1-3t)^2\,dt.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Evaluate the integral
>
> Let $u=1-3t$, so $du=-3\,dt$ and $dt=-\frac13\,du$. When $t=0$, $u=1$; when $t=\frac13$, $u=0$. Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[M]
> =
> \int_0^{1/3} (1-3t)^2\,dt
> =
> \frac13\int_0^1 u^2\,du
> =
> \frac13\cdot \frac13
> =
> \frac19.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{9}}
> $$
