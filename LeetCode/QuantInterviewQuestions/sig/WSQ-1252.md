> [!example] Question 1252 · SIG
> ## Technology Associate Interview

What is the worst-case construction of a binary search tree?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1253)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The worst-case construction of a binary search tree occurs when the keys are inserted in **sorted order** (either increasing or decreasing).
>
> ## Explanation
>
> A binary search tree stores each new key by comparing it to existing nodes and moving left or right until an empty position is found. If the insertion order is already sorted, then each new key is placed as a child of the previous one, creating a completely unbalanced tree.
>
> For example, inserting
>
> $$
> 1,2,3,4,5
> $$
>
> produces
>
> $$
> 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5
> $$
>
> as a chain to the right. Similarly, inserting
>
> $$
> 5,4,3,2,1
> $$
>
> produces a chain to the left.
>
> ## Why this is worst-case
>
> In this situation, the height of the tree becomes
>
> $$
> h = n-1
> $$
>
> for $n$ nodes, so the tree behaves like a linked list rather than a balanced tree. As a result:
>
> - Search takes $$O(n)$$
> - Insertion takes $$O(n)$$
> - Deletion takes $$O(n)$$
>
> instead of the ideal
>
> $$
> O(\log n)
> $$
>
> for a balanced binary search tree.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The worst-case construction of a binary search tree is obtained by inserting the keys in **monotonic order**—that is, strictly increasing or strictly decreasing order—so that the tree becomes completely skewed.
