> [!example] Question 1110 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Calculate the probability of having a disease given that a diagnostic test result is positive. What is the probability if the test result is positive twice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1111)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D$ denote the event that a person has the disease, and let $+$ denote a positive test result.
>
> To calculate the probability of disease given a positive test, use **Bayes’ theorem**:
>
> $$
> P(D \mid +)=\frac{P(+\mid D)P(D)}{P(+\mid D)P(D)+P(+\mid D^c)P(D^c)}.
> $$
>
> Here:
>
> - $P(D)$ is the prevalence of the disease,
> - $P(+\mid D)$ is the sensitivity of the test,
> - $P(+\mid D^c)$ is the false positive rate.
>
> Since no numerical values are provided, the answer can only be given in formula form.
>
> ## One positive test
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(D \mid +)=\frac{P(+\mid D)P(D)}{P(+\mid D)P(D)+P(+\mid D^c)\bigl(1-P(D)\bigr)}.
> $$
>
> ## Two positive tests
>
> If the two test results are conditionally independent given whether the person has the disease, then
>
> $$
> P(++\mid D)=P(+\mid D)^2,
> \qquad
> P(++\mid D^c)=P(+\mid D^c)^2.
> $$
>
> Applying Bayes’ theorem again,
>
> $$
> P(D\mid ++)
> =
> \frac{P(+\mid D)^2P(D)}
> {P(+\mid D)^2P(D)+P(+\mid D^c)^2\bigl(1-P(D)\bigr)}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> P(D \mid +)=\frac{P(+\mid D)P(D)}{P(+\mid D)P(D)+P(+\mid D^c)\bigl(1-P(D)\bigr)}
> $$
>
> and, assuming independent repeated tests,
>
> $$
> P(D\mid ++)
> =
> \frac{P(+\mid D)^2P(D)}
> {P(+\mid D)^2P(D)+P(+\mid D^c)^2\bigl(1-P(D)\bigr)}.
> $$
>
> If numerical values for prevalence, sensitivity, and false positive rate are given, these formulas can be evaluated directly.
