> [!example] Question 1167 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A painting is on sale for X amount. The probability that it is real is Y. If it is real, it is worth A; if it is a fake, it is worth B. What is the expected gain on purchasing this painting?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1168)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the purchase price be $x$, and let the probability that the painting is genuine be $y$. Then the probability that it is fake is $1-y$.
>
> If the painting is real, its value is $a$, so the gain from purchasing it is
>
> $$
> a - x.
> $$
>
> If the painting is fake, its value is $b$, so the gain from purchasing it is
>
> $$
> b - x.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the expected gain is
>
> $$
> E[\text{gain}] = y(a-x) + (1-y)(b-x).
> $$
>
> Simplifying,
>
> $$
> E[\text{gain}] = ya - yx + b - by - x + yx
> = ya + (1-y)b - x.
> $$
>
> Hence, the expected gain on purchasing the painting is
>
> $$
> \boxed{y(a-x) + (1-y)(b-x)}
> $$
>
> or equivalently,
>
> $$
> \boxed{ya + (1-y)b - x}.
> $$
>
> If this quantity is positive, the purchase has positive expected gain; if it is negative, it has negative expected gain.
