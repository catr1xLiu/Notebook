> [!example] Question 1213 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

There are different levels of smokers, with some being more likely to die than others. If a person has died, what is the probability that they were a heavy smoker?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1214)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the population be divided into three groups:
>
> - Nonsmokers: $P(N)=0.50$
> - Light smokers: $P(L)=0.20$
> - Heavy smokers: $P(H)=0.30$
>
> Let the probability of death for a nonsmoker be $x$.  
> Then, according to the given information,
>
> - Light smokers are twice as likely to die as nonsmokers: $P(D \mid L)=2x$
> - Heavy smokers are twice as likely to die as light smokers: $P(D \mid H)=4x$
>
> We want $P(H \mid D)$.
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
>
> $$
> P(H \mid D)=\frac{P(D \mid H)P(H)}{P(D)}.
> $$
>
> First compute $P(D)$ using the law of total probability:
>
> $$
> P(D)=P(D \mid N)P(N)+P(D \mid L)P(L)+P(D \mid H)P(H).
> $$
>
> Substitute the values:
>
> $$
> P(D)=x(0.50)+2x(0.20)+4x(0.30).
> $$
>
> $$
> P(D)=0.50x+0.40x+1.20x=2.10x.
> $$
>
> Now compute $P(H \mid D)$:
>
> $$
> P(H \mid D)=\frac{4x \cdot 0.30}{2.10x}
> =\frac{1.20x}{2.10x}
> =\frac{1.20}{2.10}
> =\frac{12}{21}
> =\frac{4}{7}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(H \mid D)=\frac{4}{7}\approx 0.5714.
> $$
>
> So the probability that a person was a heavy smoker given that the person died is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{7}\approx 57.1\%}.
> $$
