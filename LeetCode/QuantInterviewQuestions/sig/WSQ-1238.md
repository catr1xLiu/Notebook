> ## Quantitative Research Associate Interview

List and explain the properties of a nilpotent matrix.

> ## Properties of a Nilpotent Matrix
>
> A square matrix $A \in M_n(F)$ is called **nilpotent** if there exists a positive integer $k$ such that
>
> $$
> A^k = 0.
> $$
>
> The smallest such positive integer $k$ is called the **index of nilpotency**.
>
> ### Main Properties
>
> 1. **All eigenvalues are zero**
>
>    If $A$ is nilpotent and $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then for some nonzero vector $v$,
>
>    $$
>    Av = \lambda v.
>    $$
>
>    Applying $A^k$ gives
>
>    $$
>    A^k v = \lambda^k v.
>    $$
>
>    Since $A^k = 0$, it follows that
>
>    $$
>    \lambda^k v = 0.
>    $$
>
>    Because $v \neq 0$, we must have
>
>    $$
>    \lambda^k = 0,
>    $$
>
>    hence
>
>    $$
>    \lambda = 0.
>    $$
>
> 2. **Its characteristic polynomial is $x^n$**
>
>    Since all eigenvalues are zero, the characteristic polynomial of an $n \times n$ nilpotent matrix is
>
>    $$
>    p_A(x) = x^n.
>    $$
>
> 3. **Its minimal polynomial is $x^m$ for some $1 \le m \le n$**
>
>    Because $A^k = 0$, the matrix $A$ satisfies the polynomial equation
>
>    $$
>    x^k = 0.
>    $$
>
>    Therefore the minimal polynomial must divide $x^k$, so it has the form
>
>    $$
>    \mu_A(x) = x^m
>    $$
>
>    for some integer $m \ge 1$.
>
> 4. **Its determinant is zero**
>
>    The determinant is the product of the eigenvalues. Since all eigenvalues are zero,
>
>    $$
>    \det(A) = 0.
>    $$
>
>    Thus every nilpotent matrix is **singular**.
>
> 5. **Its trace is zero**
>
>    The trace is the sum of the eigenvalues. Since all eigenvalues are zero,
>
>    $$
>    \operatorname{tr}(A) = 0.
>    $$
>
> 6. **It is not invertible unless $A = 0$ in the trivial case**
>
>    If $A$ were invertible and $A^k = 0$, then multiplying by $A^{-k}$ would give
>
>    $$
>    I = 0,
>    $$
>
>    which is impossible. Hence a nonzero nilpotent matrix is never invertible.
>
> 7. **Its Jordan canonical form consists of Jordan blocks with eigenvalue $0$**
>
>    Over an algebraically closed field, every nilpotent matrix is similar to a block diagonal matrix of the form
>
>    $$
>    J = \operatorname{diag}(J_{r_1}(0), J_{r_2}(0), \dots, J_{r_t}(0)),
>    $$
>
>    where each block is
>
>    $$
>    J_r(0) =
>    \begin{pmatrix}
>    0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
>    0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\
>    \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
>    0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 \\
>    0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0
>    \end{pmatrix}.
>    $$
>
> 8. **The index of nilpotency equals the size of the largest Jordan block**
>
>    If the largest Jordan block in the Jordan form has size $m$, then
>
>    $$
>    A^m = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad A^{m-1} \neq 0.
>    $$
>
> 9. **For an $n \times n$ nilpotent matrix, $A^n = 0$**
>
>    By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, $A$ satisfies its characteristic polynomial $x^n$, so
>
>    $$
>    A^n = 0.
>    $$
>
>    Thus the index of nilpotency is at most $n$.
>
> 10. **Every nilpotent matrix is similar to a strictly upper triangular matrix**
>
>     In a suitable basis, a nilpotent matrix can be written in the form
>
>     $$
>     \begin{pmatrix}
>     0 & * & * & \cdots & * \\
>     0 & 0 & * & \cdots & * \\
>     0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & * \\
>     \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
>     0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0
>     \end{pmatrix}.
>     $$
>
>     Hence every strictly upper triangular matrix is nilpotent, and every nilpotent matrix is similar to one.
>
> 11. **If $A$ is nilpotent, then $I - A$ is invertible**
>
>     If $A^k = 0$, then
>
>     $$
>     (I - A)(I + A + A^2 + \cdots + A^{k-1}) = I - A^k = I.
>     $$
>
>     Therefore,
>
>     $$
>     (I - A)^{-1} = I + A + A^2 + \cdots + A^{k-1}.
>     $$
>
> 12. **The rank tends to decrease under powers**
>
>     For powers of a nilpotent matrix,
>
>     $$
>     \operatorname{rank}(A) \ge \operatorname{rank}(A^2) \ge \operatorname{rank}(A^3) \ge \cdots \ge 0,
>     $$
>
>     and eventually
>
>     $$
>     A^k = 0.
>     $$
>
> ### Example
>
> Consider
>
> $$
> A =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & 1 \\
> 0 & 0
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> A^2 =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & 0 \\
> 0 & 0
> \end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> so $A$ is nilpotent of index $2$.
>
> Its properties are:
>
> - eigenvalues: $0, 0$,
> - determinant: $0$,
> - trace: $0$,
> - characteristic polynomial: $x^2$,
> - minimal polynomial: $x^2$.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> A nilpotent matrix is a square matrix $A$ such that $A^k = 0$ for some $k \ge 1$. Its fundamental properties are:
>
> - all eigenvalues are $0$,
> - $\det(A) = 0$,
> - $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$,
> - its characteristic polynomial is $x^n$,
> - its minimal polynomial is $x^m$,
> - it is similar to a strictly upper triangular matrix,
> - its Jordan form consists only of Jordan blocks corresponding to eigenvalue $0$,
> - $I - A$ is invertible.
