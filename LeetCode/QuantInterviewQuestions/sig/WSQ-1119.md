> [!example] Question 1119 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You are a player on a basketball team that is losing by 2 points. You have the ball, and there are 3 seconds remaining. You have two options: pass to a teammate for a 3-point shot (with a 37% probability of making it and winning the game), or pass for a 2-point shot (with a 77% probability of making it and tying the game). In the event of a tie, your team goes to overtime, where there is a 50% chance of winning. What should you do, and why?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1120)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two options be evaluated by their probability of ultimately winning the game.
>
> ## Option 1: Pass for a $3$-point shot
>
> The team wins immediately if the shooter makes the shot.
>
> Given probability of making the $3$-pointer:
> $$
> P(\text{make }3) = 0.37
> $$
>
> So the probability of winning under this option is
> $$
> P(\text{win via }3) = 0.37
> $$
>
> ## Option 2: Pass for a $2$-point shot
>
> If the $2$-pointer is made, the game is tied and goes to overtime. The probability of making the $2$-pointer is
> $$
> P(\text{make }2) = 0.77
> $$
>
> Once in overtime, the team has probability
> $$
> P(\text{win in OT}) = 0.50
> $$
>
> Thus the total probability of winning under this option is
> $$
> P(\text{win via }2) = P(\text{make }2)\cdot P(\text{win in OT})
> = 0.77 \cdot 0.50
> = 0.385
> $$
>
> ## Comparison
>
> $$
> P(\text{win via }3) = 0.37
> $$
>
> $$
> P(\text{win via }2) = 0.385
> $$
>
> Since
> $$
> 0.385 > 0.37
> $$
> the $2$-point option gives a higher probability of eventually winning.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> Pass for the $2$-point shot, because it gives a $38.5\%$ chance to win, compared with a $37\%$ chance if you pass for the $3$-point shot.
