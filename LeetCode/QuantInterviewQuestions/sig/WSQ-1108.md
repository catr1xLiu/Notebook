> [!example] Question 1108 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You toss a fair coin repeatedly. What is the probability that the sequence HTH appears before the sequence HHT?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1109)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the desired probability be $p$, where
>
> $$
> p=\Pr(\text{HTH occurs before HHT}).
> $$
>
> We compute $p$ by tracking the relevant current suffix of the toss sequence.
>
> ## State definitions
>
> Let
>
> - $S$: start state, with no useful partial match,
> - $H$: the current suffix is $H$,
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $HT$,
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $HH$.
>
> Let the corresponding probabilities of eventually getting $\text{HTH}$ before $\text{HHT}$ be
>
> $$
> p_S,\; p_H,\; p_{HT},\; p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ## Recurrence equations
>
> From the start state $S$:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to state $H$.
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, staying effectively in state $S$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> p_S=p_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - Toss $H$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $HH$.
> - Toss $T$ with probability $\tfrac12$, moving to $HT$.
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - Toss $H$ gives the pattern $\text{HTH}$ immediately, so success.
> - Toss $T$ gives suffix $T$, which is irrelevant and returns to start state $S$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12 \cdot 1+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - Toss $T$ gives the pattern $\text{HHT}$ immediately, so failure.
> - Toss $H$ leaves suffix $HH$ again.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 \cdot 0.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Solve
>
> Since $p_S=p_H$, we have
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p_S=\frac12+\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> Substitute into $p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}$:
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p_H\right)
> =\frac14+\frac14 p_H.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \frac34 p_H=\frac14,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac13.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> p_S=p_H=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The probability that $\text{HTH}$ appears before $\text{HHT}$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac13}.
> $$
>
> Thus the odds in favor are
>
> $$
> \boxed{1:2}.
> $$
