> [!example] Question 1109 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability of getting the sequence HTH before HHT when flipping a fair coin repeatedly?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1110)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the states record the longest suffix of the current sequence that is also a prefix of either target pattern $HTH$ or $HHT$.
>
> We seek the probability that $HTH$ appears before $HHT$.
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Let
>
> - $S$: start state,
> - $H$: the current relevant suffix is $H$,
> - $HT$: the current relevant suffix is $HT$,
> - $HH$: the current relevant suffix is $HH$.
>
> The absorbing outcomes are:
>
> - success: $HTH$ occurs first,
> - failure: $HHT$ occurs first.
>
> Let the corresponding probabilities of eventual success be
>
> $$
> p_S,\quad p_H,\quad p_{HT},\quad p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ## Step 2: Write recursive equations
>
> From the start state:
>
> - if the first flip is $H$, move to state $H$;
> - if the first flip is $T$, no useful prefix is formed, so remain at $S$.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - flip $H$ $\to$ state $HH$,
> - flip $T$ $\to$ state $HT$.
>
> So
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - flip $H$ gives $HTH$, so success,
> - flip $T$ leaves suffix $T$, which matches no relevant prefix, so return to $S$.
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - flip $T$ gives $HHT$, so failure,
> - flip $H$ leaves suffix $HH$ again.
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HH},
> $$
>
> which implies
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> From
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_S,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> p_S=p_H.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}=\frac12 p_{HT},
> $$
>
> since $p_{HH}=0$.
>
> Also,
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_{HT}
> =\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p_S\right)
> =\frac14+\frac14 p_S.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \frac34 p_S=\frac14,
> $$
>
> and therefore
>
> $$
> p_S=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac13}
> $$
>
> So the probability of getting $HTH$ before $HHT$ is $\boxed{\frac13}$.
