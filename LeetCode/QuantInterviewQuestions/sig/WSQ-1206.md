> [!example] Question 1206 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

Given 5 fair dice, what is the probability of obtaining a result where exactly 3 of the dice show the same value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1207)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To interpret “results containing $3$ same values,” take it to mean that among the $5$ dice, there is **at least one value appearing exactly $3$ times**.
>
> The sample space has
>
> $$
> 6^5 = 7776
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> Count favorable outcomes by cases.
>
> ## Case 1: Exactly one triple and two other distinct values
>
> Choose the value that appears $3$ times:
>
> $$
> 6
> $$
>
> Choose the positions of the triple among the $5$ dice:
>
> $$
> \binom{5}{3} = 10
> $$
>
> Choose the remaining two values, distinct from the triple value and distinct from each other:
>
> $$
> 5 \cdot 4
> $$
>
> Thus the number of outcomes in this case is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 10 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 = 1200.
> $$
>
> ## Case 2: Full house pattern $3+2$
>
> Choose the value that appears $3$ times:
>
> $$
> 6
> $$
>
> Choose the value that appears $2$ times:
>
> $$
> 5
> $$
>
> Choose the positions of the triple:
>
> $$
> \binom{5}{3} = 10
> $$
>
> Thus the number of outcomes in this case is
>
> $$
> 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 10 = 300.
> $$
>
> ## Total favorable outcomes
>
> $$
> 1200 + 300 = 1500.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{1500}{7776} = \frac{125}{648}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{125}{648}}
> $$
