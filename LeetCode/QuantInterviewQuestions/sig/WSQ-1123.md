> [!example] Question 1123 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Every morning, Mike the security guard at CP High School opens all 1000 doors in the building, numbered 1 to 1000. The next guard closes all even-numbered doors. Each subsequent guard toggles (opens if closed, closes if open) every nth door, where n is the guard's number, until the 1000th guard toggles only door 1000. How many doors are left open in the end?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1124)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Each door is toggled once for every positive divisor it has.
>
> - Door $n$ is touched by guard $d$ exactly when $d$ divides $n$.
> - Therefore, door $n$ is toggled $d(n)$ times, where $d(n)$ is the number of positive divisors of $n$.
>
> A door ends open precisely when it is toggled an odd number of times. Thus we must determine for which $n$ the number of divisors is odd.
>
> Divisors usually come in pairs:
> $$
> a \cdot b = n,
> $$
> so if $a$ is a divisor, then $b = \frac{n}{a}$ is also a divisor. Hence divisors are typically paired, giving an even total number. The only time a divisor is unpaired is when
> $$
> a = b,
> $$
> that is,
> $$
> a^2 = n.
> $$
> So a number has an odd number of divisors exactly when it is a perfect square.
>
> Therefore, the doors left open are exactly those numbered by perfect squares from $1$ to $1000$.
>
> The perfect squares in this range are
> $$
> 1^2, 2^2, 3^2, \dots, 31^2,
> $$
> since
> $$
> 31^2 = 961 \le 1000
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> 32^2 = 1024 > 1000.
> $$
>
> So the number of doors left open is
> $$
> 31.
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{31}
> $$
