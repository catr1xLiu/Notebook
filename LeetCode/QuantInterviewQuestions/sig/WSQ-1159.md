> [!example] Question 1159 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

A bag contains 30 balls: 10 black, 5 orange, 10 green, and 5 blue. Without replacement, what is the probability that the second black ball is drawn on the third draw?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1160)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To have the **second black ball occur on the third draw**, the first two draws must contain **exactly one black**, and the third draw must be **black**.
>
> There are $10$ black balls and $20$ non-black balls in a total of $30$ balls.
>
> The required sequences for the first three draws are:
>
> - non-black, black, black
> - black, non-black, black
>
> Compute each:
>
> $$
> P(\text{NBB})=\frac{20}{30}\cdot\frac{10}{29}\cdot\frac{9}{28}
> $$
>
> $$
> P(\text{BNB})=\frac{10}{30}\cdot\frac{20}{29}\cdot\frac{9}{28}
> $$
>
> Add them:
>
> $$
> P = \frac{20}{30}\cdot\frac{10}{29}\cdot\frac{9}{28} + \frac{10}{30}\cdot\frac{20}{29}\cdot\frac{9}{28}
> $$
>
> Since the two terms are equal,
>
> $$
> P = 2\left(\frac{20}{30}\cdot\frac{10}{29}\cdot\frac{9}{28}\right)
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> P = 2\cdot \frac{20\cdot 10\cdot 9}{30\cdot 29\cdot 28}
> = \frac{3600}{24360}
> = \frac{30}{203}
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{30}{203}}
> $$
>
> which is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.1478}
> $$
>
> or about $14.78\%$.
