> [!example] Question 1166 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A frog is traveling from point A(0, 0) to point B(4, 6). Each step can only be 1 unit up or 1 unit to the right. The frog refuses to move three steps in the same direction consecutively. Compute the number of ways the frog can move from A to B.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1167)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To go from $A(0,0)$ to $B(4,6)$, the frog must make
>
> - $4$ steps to the right, denoted by $R$,
> - $6$ steps up, denoted by $U$.
>
> Thus we must count sequences of length $10$ consisting of $4$ $R$'s and $6$ $U$'s, with the restriction that no three consecutive steps are the same. In other words, neither $UUU$ nor $RRR$ may occur.
>
> ## Step 1: Encode runs of identical steps
>
> Because three consecutive equal steps are forbidden, every maximal block of identical steps has length either $1$ or $2$.
>
> ### Up-steps
> The $6$ up-steps must be split into blocks of size $1$ or $2$.
>
> Let the number of up-blocks be $k$. Then we seek compositions of $6$ into $k$ parts, each part equal to $1$ or $2$.
>
> If $a$ parts are $2$'s and $b$ parts are $1$'s, then
> $$
> 2a+b=6,\qquad a+b=k.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> a=6-k,\qquad b=2k-6.
> $$
> So $k$ can be $3,4,5,6$.
>
> For each $k$, the number of such up-block patterns is
> $$
> \binom{k}{6-k},
> $$
> since we choose which $6-k$ of the $k$ blocks are of size $2$.
>
> Thus:
>
> - $k=3$: $\binom{3}{3}=1$
> - $k=4$: $\binom{4}{2}=6$
> - $k=5$: $\binom{5}{1}=5$
> - $k=6$: $\binom{6}{0}=1$
>
> ### Right-steps
> Similarly, the $4$ right-steps must be split into blocks of size $1$ or $2$.
>
> Let the number of right-blocks be $\ell$. Then
> $$
> 2c+d=4,\qquad c+d=\ell,
> $$
> so
> $$
> c=4-\ell,\qquad d=2\ell-4.
> $$
> Hence $\ell$ can be $2,3,4$.
>
> For each $\ell$, the number of right-block patterns is
> $$
> \binom{\ell}{4-\ell}.
> $$
>
> Thus:
>
> - $\ell=2$: $\binom{2}{2}=1$
> - $\ell=3$: $\binom{3}{1}=3$
> - $\ell=4$: $\binom{4}{0}=1$
>
> ## Step 2: Alternate the blocks
>
> Any valid path is obtained by alternating up-blocks and right-blocks. Therefore the numbers of up-blocks and right-blocks can differ by at most $1$:
> $$
> |k-\ell|\le 1.
> $$
>
> Also:
>
> - if $k=\ell$, the sequence may start with either $U$ or $R$, giving $2$ choices;
> - if $k=\ell+1$, it must start and end with $U$, giving $1$ choice;
> - if $\ell=k+1$, it must start and end with $R$, giving $1$ choice.
>
> So we sum over all valid pairs $(k,\ell)$.
>
> Let
> $$
> U_k=\binom{k}{6-k},\qquad R_\ell=\binom{\ell}{4-\ell}.
> $$
>
> We compute:
>
> ### Case 1: $k=\ell$
>
> Possible values are $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$.
>
> - $(3,3)$: $2\cdot U_3R_3=2\cdot 1\cdot 3=6$
> - $(4,4)$: $2\cdot U_4R_4=2\cdot 6\cdot 1=12$
>
> Total:
> $$
> 6+12=18.
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: $k=\ell+1$
>
> Possible values are $(3,2)$, $(4,3)$, $(5,4)$.
>
> - $(3,2)$: $U_3R_2=1\cdot 1=1$
> - $(4,3)$: $U_4R_3=6\cdot 3=18$
> - $(5,4)$: $U_5R_4=5\cdot 1=5$
>
> Total:
> $$
> 1+18+5=24.
> $$
>
> ### Case 3: $\ell=k+1$
>
> Possible values are $(3,4)$ and $(2,3)$, but $k=2$ is impossible for the up-steps, so only $(3,4)$ works.
>
> - $(3,4)$: $U_3R_4=1\cdot 1=1$
>
> Total:
> $$
> 1.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Add all cases
>
> Hence the total number of valid paths is
> $$
> 18+24+1=43.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{43}
> $$
