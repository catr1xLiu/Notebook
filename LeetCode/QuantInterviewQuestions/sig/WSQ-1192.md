> [!example] Question 1192 · SIG
> ## Trading Intern Interview

Given the probabilities of an event occurring on each of two days, find the probability that the event occurs on at least one of those days.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1193)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that it happens on the first day, and let $B$ be the event that it happens on the second day.
>
> The probability that the event happens on **at least one** of the two days is the probability of the union $A \cup B$:
>
> $$
> P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B).
> $$
>
> So the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{at least one day}) = P(\text{day 1}) + P(\text{day 2}) - P(\text{both days})}.
> $$
>
> If the two days are **independent**, then
>
> $$
> P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B),
> $$
>
> and hence
>
> $$
> \boxed{P(\text{at least one day}) = p_1 + p_2 - p_1p_2},
> $$
>
> where $p_1$ and $p_2$ are the probabilities for the two days.
>
> Equivalently, using the complement:
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one day}) = 1 - P(\text{neither day})
> $$
>
> and, under independence,
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one day}) = 1 - (1-p_1)(1-p_2).
> $$
>
> Thus the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 - (1-p_1)(1-p_2)}
> $$
>
> when the two days are independent.
