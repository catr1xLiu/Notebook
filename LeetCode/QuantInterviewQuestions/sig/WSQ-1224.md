> [!example] Question 1224 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

If you randomly cut a cake n times, what is the expected number of pieces?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1225)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume each cut is a straight line in the plane, and the cuts are in **general position**:
>
> - no two cuts are parallel,
> - no three cuts pass through the same point,
> - each new cut intersects all previous cuts.
>
> Also assume the cuts are made with uniformly random orientation/position so that, with probability $1$, the configuration is in general position. Under this model, the number of pieces is actually deterministic, so its expected value equals that number.
>
> ## Step 1: Effect of one new cut
>
> Suppose after $k-1$ cuts the cake is divided into some number of pieces.  
> When the $k$-th cut is made, it intersects each of the previous $k-1$ cuts at distinct points, so it is divided into
>
> $$
> k
> $$
>
> segments.
>
> Each segment passes through one existing piece and splits that piece into two, thereby increasing the total number of pieces by $1$ per segment. Hence the $k$-th cut increases the number of pieces by
>
> $$
> k.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Recurrence
>
> Let $P_n$ be the number of pieces after $n$ cuts. Initially,
>
> $$
> P_0 = 1.
> $$
>
> From the reasoning above,
>
> $$
> P_n = P_{n-1} + n.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P_n = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^n k.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Evaluate the sum
>
> Using
>
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^n k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> P_n = 1 + \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of pieces after $n$ random straight cuts is
>
> $$
> \boxed{1 + \frac{n(n+1)}{2}}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{n^2+n+2}{2}}.
> $$
>
> This is also the maximum possible number of pieces obtainable with $n$ straight cuts.
