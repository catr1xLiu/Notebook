> [!example] Question 1244 · SIG
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

There are 4 green balls and 11 red balls. What is the probability that the 4th green ball is picked on the 9th draw?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1245)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To have the $4$th green ball appear on the $9$th draw, the first $8$ draws must contain exactly $3$ green balls, and then the $9$th draw must be green.
>
> An equivalent counting approach is:
>
> - Choose the positions of the first $3$ green balls among the first $8$ draws.
> - Force the $9$th draw to be the $4$th green.
>
> Among the $15$ total positions, the $4$ green balls are equally likely to occupy any $4$ positions. Thus:
>
> - Total ways to choose positions of the $4$ green balls:  
>   $$
>   \binom{15}{4}
>   $$
> - Favorable ways: choose $3$ positions for green among the first $8$, and place the last green at position $9$:  
>   $$
>   \binom{8}{3}
>   $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{\binom{8}{3}}{\binom{15}{4}}
> $$
>
> Now compute:
>
> $$
> \binom{8}{3} = 56, \qquad \binom{15}{4} = 1365
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(\text{4th green on 9th draw}) = \frac{56}{1365}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{56}{1365}}.
> $$
