> [!example] Question 1230 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Nine white cubes are arranged to form a 3x3 cube, and the outside surfaces are painted green. A single cube is randomly selected and rolled. If the side facing up is white, what is the probability that the selected cube was the middle cube?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1231)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Arrange the $9$ small cubes into a $3 \times 3$ square block of thickness $1$. After painting the outside surface green, the cubes fall into two types:
>
> - **Middle cube:** $1$ cube, with only its top and bottom faces unpainted, so it has $2$ white faces.
> - **Non-middle cubes:** $8$ cubes, each on the boundary of the $3 \times 3$ array. Such a cube has $1$ face exposed on top or bottom and some side faces exposed, so exactly $1$ face is unpainted on the inward side? More directly, in this $3 \times 3 \times 1$ block, every non-middle cube has exactly $1$ white face? This is incorrect, so count carefully.
>
> Since the solid is only one cube thick, every small cube has its top and bottom faces painted green. Thus the only possible white faces are faces shared with neighboring cubes in the $3 \times 3$ layer.
>
> - The **center cube** shares faces with $4$ neighbors, so it has $4$ white faces.
> - An **edge-center cube** (not corner) shares faces with $3$ neighbors, so it has $3$ white faces. There are $4$ such cubes.
> - A **corner cube** shares faces with $2$ neighbors, so it has $2$ white faces. There are $4$ such cubes.
>
> Now choose a cube uniformly at random, then roll it so each of its $6$ faces is equally likely to land upward.
>
> Let $W$ be the event that the upward face is white, and let $M$ be the event that the chosen cube is the middle cube.
>
> Then
> $$
> P(M)=\frac{1}{9}.
> $$
>
> Given the middle cube was chosen, the probability of landing with a white face up is
> $$
> P(W \mid M)=\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> The total probability of getting a white face up is
> $$
> P(W)=\frac{1}{9}\left(\frac{4}{6}\right)
> +\frac{4}{9}\left(\frac{3}{6}\right)
> +\frac{4}{9}\left(\frac{2}{6}\right).
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> P(W)=\frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{4}{6}
> +\frac{4}{9}\cdot\frac{3}{6}
> +\frac{4}{9}\cdot\frac{2}{6}
> =\frac{4+12+8}{54}
> =\frac{24}{54}
> =\frac{4}{9}.
> $$
>
> By Bayes' theorem,
> $$
> P(M \mid W)=\frac{P(W \mid M)P(M)}{P(W)}
> =\frac{\frac{4}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{9}}{\frac{4}{9}}
> =\frac{4}{54}\cdot\frac{9}{4}
> =\frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}.
> $$
