> [!example] Question 1120 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A boat fires torpedoes at another boat. The probability that a torpedo hits is 1/3. If a torpedo hits the boat, the boat is destroyed. Two torpedoes are fired. What is the probability that the ship is destroyed?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1121)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume the two torpedoes hit independently, each with probability $\frac{1}{3}$.
>
> The ship is destroyed if at least one torpedo hits. It is easier to compute the complement:
>
> - Probability one torpedo misses:  
>   $$
>   1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}
>   $$
>
> - Probability both torpedoes miss:  
>   $$
>   \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9}
>   $$
>
> Therefore, the probability the ship is destroyed is
>
> $$
> 1 - \frac{4}{9} = \frac{5}{9}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{9}}.
> $$
