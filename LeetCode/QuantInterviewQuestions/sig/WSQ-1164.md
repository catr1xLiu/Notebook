> [!example] Question 1164 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

If the probability of seeing a meteor outside within one hour is 84 percent, what is the probability of seeing at least one meteor outside in 30 minutes?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1165)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume meteor sightings follow a Poisson process with constant rate $\lambda$ per hour. Then the probability of seeing at least one meteor in time $t$ hours is
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one in } t)=1-e^{-\lambda t}.
> $$
>
> From the given information for one hour,
>
> $$
> 1-e^{-\lambda}=0.84.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> e^{-\lambda}=0.16.
> $$
>
> For $30$ minutes, $t=0.5$ hours. Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one in } 0.5)=1-e^{-0.5\lambda}.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> e^{-0.5\lambda}=\sqrt{e^{-\lambda}}=\sqrt{0.16}=0.4,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one in } 30\text{ minutes})=1-0.4=0.6.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the chance of seeing at least one meteor in $30$ minutes is
>
> $$
> \boxed{60\%}.
> $$
