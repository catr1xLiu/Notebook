What is the difference between bagging and boosting?

> ## Difference Between Bagging and Boosting
>
> Both **bagging** and **boosting** are ensemble learning methods: they combine multiple weak or base learners to produce a stronger model. Their main difference lies in **how the individual models are trained and combined**.
>
> ### 1. Bagging
>
> **Bagging** stands for **Bootstrap Aggregating**.
>
> #### Idea
> Multiple models are trained **independently** on different bootstrap samples of the training data, and their predictions are then aggregated.
>
> #### Procedure
> 1. Draw many bootstrap samples from the original dataset.
> 2. Train one base learner on each sample.
> 3. Combine the predictions:
>    - for classification: by **majority vote**
>    - for regression: by **averaging**
>
> #### Key properties
> - Models are trained **in parallel**.
> - Primary goal: **reduce variance**.
> - Works especially well with high-variance models such as decision trees.
> - Less prone to overfitting than a single complex model.
>
> #### Example
> - **Random Forest** is a classic bagging method.
>
> ---
>
> ### 2. Boosting
>
> **Boosting** trains models **sequentially**, where each new model focuses on correcting the errors made by the previous ones.
>
> #### Idea
> Instead of building many independent models, boosting builds a chain of models, each trying to improve the ensemble’s weaknesses.
>
> #### Procedure
> 1. Train an initial weak learner.
> 2. Increase attention to observations that were predicted poorly.
> 3. Train the next learner to focus more on those difficult cases.
> 4. Combine all learners using a **weighted sum** or **weighted vote**.
>
> #### Key properties
> - Models are trained **sequentially**.
> - Primary goal: **reduce bias** (and often also variance).
> - Can produce very strong predictive performance.
> - More sensitive to noise and overfitting if not carefully regularized.
>
> #### Examples
> - **AdaBoost**
> - **Gradient Boosting**
> - **XGBoost**
>
> ---
>
> ## Main Differences
>
> - **Aspect Training style** — Bagging: Independent, parallel; Boosting: Sequential
> - **Aspect Data usage** — Bagging: Bootstrap samples; Boosting: Reweighted data or residual-focused learning
> - **Aspect Main goal** — Bagging: Reduce variance; Boosting: Reduce bias
> - **Aspect Combination** — Bagging: Simple averaging or voting; Boosting: Weighted combination
> - **Aspect Sensitivity to noise** — Bagging: Lower; Boosting: Higher
> - **Aspect Typical base learner** — Bagging: Deep decision trees; Boosting: Weak learners, often shallow trees
>
> ---
>
> ## Summary
>
> - **Bagging** builds many independent models and aggregates them to make predictions more stable.
> - **Boosting** builds models one after another, with each new model correcting previous mistakes.
>
> Thus, bagging emphasizes **parallel variance reduction**, whereas boosting emphasizes **sequential error correction**.
