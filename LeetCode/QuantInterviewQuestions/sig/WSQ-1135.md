> [!example] Question 1135 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

Solve for x in the equation x^\{x^\{x^\{...\}\}\} = 2, where the power tower extends infinitely.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1136)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let
> $$
> y = x^{x^{x^{\cdot^{\cdot^\cdot}}}}
> $$
> denote the infinite power tower. If the tower converges, then the expression after the first $x$ is again the whole tower, so
> $$
> y = x^y.
> $$
> We are given
> $$
> y = 2.
> $$
> Substituting into the functional equation gives
> $$
> 2 = x^2.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> x = \pm \sqrt{2}.
> $$
>
> Now we must check which value can actually define a convergent infinite power tower.
>
> For a real infinite tower $x^{x^{x^{\cdot^{\cdot^{\cdot}}}}}$ to converge, the base must be positive; otherwise expressions such as $(-\sqrt{2})^{\,(-\sqrt{2})^{\cdot}}$ are not well-defined in the real numbers. Thus $x=-\sqrt{2}$ is not admissible.
>
> So the only real solution is
> $$
> x=\sqrt{2}.
> $$
>
> Indeed, with $x=\sqrt{2}$,
> $$
> \left(\sqrt{2}\right)^2 = 2,
> $$
> so the equation $y=x^y$ with $y=2$ is satisfied, and the infinite tower converges.
>
> Therefore, the solution is
> $$
> \boxed{x=\sqrt{2}}.
> $$
