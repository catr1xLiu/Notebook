> [!example] Question 1113 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Two points are uniformly distributed around the circumference of a unit circle. What is the expected value of the distance between them?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1114)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two random points correspond to angles $\Theta_1$ and $\Theta_2$, each independently and uniformly distributed on $[0,2\pi)$.
>
> The Euclidean distance between the two points is the length of the chord subtending the angular difference
> $$
> \Delta = |\Theta_1-\Theta_2| \pmod{2\pi}.
> $$
> By rotational symmetry, we may fix one point and let the other have angle $\Theta \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,2\pi)$. Then the distance is
> $$
> D = \sqrt{(\cos \Theta - 1)^2 + (\sin \Theta)^2}.
> $$
> Simplify:
> $$
> D = \sqrt{\cos^2\Theta - 2\cos\Theta + 1 + \sin^2\Theta}
> = \sqrt{2 - 2\cos\Theta}.
> $$
> Using the identity $1-\cos\Theta = 2\sin^2(\Theta/2)$,
> $$
> D = \sqrt{4\sin^2(\Theta/2)} = 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\right|.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\right|\, d\Theta.
> $$
> Since $\Theta/2 \in [0,\pi]$, the sine is nonnegative, so
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2\sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\, d\Theta.
> $$
> Let $u=\Theta/2$, so $d\Theta = 2\,du$. Then
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi} 2\sin(u)\cdot 2\,du
> = \frac{4}{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\sin(u)\,du.
> $$
> Now,
> $$
> \int_0^{\pi}\sin(u)\,du = 2,
> $$
> so
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D] = \frac{4}{2\pi}\cdot 2 = \frac{4}{\pi}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected distance between the two points is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{\pi}}.
> $$
