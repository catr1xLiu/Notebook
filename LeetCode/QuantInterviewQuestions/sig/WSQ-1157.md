> [!example] Question 1157 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

Given a character array representing a sentence, reverse the order of words in the sentence in place.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1158)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To reverse the order of words in a character array, the standard in-place approach is:
>
> 1. Reverse the entire character array.
> 2. Reverse each individual word within the reversed array.
>
> This works because reversing the whole array places the words in reverse order, but each word itself becomes reversed. Reversing each word again restores the correct spelling.
>
> ### Example
>
> Given the character array representing
>
> $$
> \text{"the sky is blue"}
> $$
>
> after reversing the entire array, we get
>
> $$
> \text{"eulb si yks eht"}
> $$
>
> Then reversing each word gives
>
> $$
> \text{"blue is sky the"}
> $$
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> Let the array be $arr$ of length $n$.
>
> #### Step 1: Reverse the entire array
> Reverse the characters from index $0$ to $n-1$.
>
> #### Step 2: Reverse each word
> Scan the array from left to right:
> - when a non-space sequence starts, mark its beginning,
> - continue until a space or the end of the array is reached,
> - reverse that word.
>
> ### Pseudocode
>
> ```text
> reverseWords(arr):
>     reverse(arr, 0, length(arr) - 1)
>
>     start = 0
>     for i from 0 to length(arr):
>         if i == length(arr) or arr[i] == ' ':
>             reverse(arr, start, i - 1)
>             start = i + 1
>
> reverse(arr, left, right):
>     while left < right:
>         swap(arr[left], arr[right])
>         left = left + 1
>         right = right - 1
> ```
>
> ### Correctness
>
> - After reversing the whole array, the words appear in the desired reversed order.
> - However, each word’s characters are reversed.
> - Reversing each maximal sequence of non-space characters restores every word to its original form.
> - Therefore, the final array contains the words in reverse order.
>
> ### Complexity
>
> - Reversing the entire array takes $O(n)$ time.
> - Reversing all words together also takes $O(n)$ time.
> - Thus, the total time complexity is
>
> $$
> O(n)
> $$
>
> - Since the reversals are done in place, the space complexity is
>
> $$
> O(1)
> $$
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> Reverse the whole character array, then reverse each word individually. This yields the words in reversed order in $O(n)$ time and $O(1)$ extra space.
