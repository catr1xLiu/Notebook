> [!example] Question 1198 · SIG
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

A deck contains ten cards: two 10s, two Js, two Qs, two Ks, and two As. You are dealt five cards without replacement from the deck. Compute the expected number of pairs.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1199)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of pairs in the $5$-card hand.
>
> There are $5$ ranks: $10, J, Q, K, A$, and for each rank there are exactly $2$ cards. A pair occurs when both cards of some rank are present in the hand.
>
> ## Step 1: Define indicator variables
>
> For each rank $r$, define
> $$
> I_r =
> \begin{cases}
> 1, & \text{if both cards of rank } r \text{ are in the hand},\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> X = I_{10} + I_J + I_Q + I_K + I_A.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> E[X] = E[I_{10}] + E[I_J] + E[I_Q] + E[I_K] + E[I_A].
> $$
>
> By symmetry, each term is the same, so
> $$
> E[X] = 5E[I_{10}].
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute $E[I_{10}]$
>
> Since $I_{10}$ is an indicator variable,
> $$
> E[I_{10}] = P(\text{both 10s are in the hand}).
> $$
>
> The number of $5$-card hands from $10$ cards is
> $$
> \binom{10}{5}.
> $$
>
> To have both $10$s, we must include those $2$ cards and choose the remaining $3$ cards from the other $8$ cards:
> $$
> \binom{8}{3}.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(\text{both 10s are in the hand}) = \frac{\binom{8}{3}}{\binom{10}{5}}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> \frac{\binom{8}{3}}{\binom{10}{5}} = \frac{56}{252} = \frac{2}{9}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> E[X] = 5 \cdot \frac{2}{9} = \frac{10}{9}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{10}{9}}
> $$
