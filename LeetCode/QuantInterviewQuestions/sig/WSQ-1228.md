> [!example] Question 1228 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Calculate the probability that a person has a disease given that the test result is positive. What is the probability if the test result is positive twice?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1229)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $D$ denote the event that a person has the disease, and let $+$ denote a positive test result.
>
> To compute the probability of disease given a positive test, use **Bayes' theorem**:
>
> $$
> P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D)\,P(D)}{P(+ \mid D)\,P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)\,P(D^c)}.
> $$
>
> Thus, if
>
> - $P(D)$ is the prevalence of the disease,
> - $P(+ \mid D)$ is the sensitivity,
> - $P(+ \mid D^c)$ is the false positive rate,
>
> then
>
> $$
> P(D \mid +) = \frac{\text{sensitivity} \cdot \text{prevalence}}{\text{sensitivity} \cdot \text{prevalence} + \text{false positive rate} \cdot (1-\text{prevalence})}.
> $$
>
> For **two positive tests**, assuming the two test results are conditionally independent given whether the person has the disease, Bayes' theorem gives
>
> $$
> P(D \mid ++)
> = \frac{P(++ \mid D)\,P(D)}{P(++ \mid D)\,P(D) + P(++ \mid D^c)\,P(D^c)}.
> $$
>
> Under conditional independence,
>
> $$
> P(++ \mid D) = \bigl(P(+ \mid D)\bigr)^2,
> \qquad
> P(++ \mid D^c) = \bigl(P(+ \mid D^c)\bigr)^2.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(D \mid ++)
> =
> \frac{\bigl(P(+ \mid D)\bigr)^2 P(D)}
> {\bigl(P(+ \mid D)\bigr)^2 P(D) + \bigl(P(+ \mid D^c)\bigr)^2 (1-P(D))}.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - **After one positive test:**
> $$
> \boxed{
> P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D)P(D)}{P(+ \mid D)P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)(1-P(D))}
> }
> $$
>
> - **After two positive tests:**
> $$
> \boxed{
> P(D \mid ++) = \frac{(P(+ \mid D))^2 P(D)}{(P(+ \mid D))^2 P(D) + (P(+ \mid D^c))^2 (1-P(D))}
> }
> $$
>
> A numerical answer requires the values of $P(D)$, $P(+ \mid D)$, and $P(+ \mid D^c)$.
