> [!example] Question 1234 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Given an n-by-n real matrix A where every entry is nonnegative, and A squared is the zero matrix, what is the maximum number of positive entries A can have?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1235)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A = (a_{ij})$ be an $n \times n$ real matrix with $a_{ij} \ge 0$ for all $i,j$, and suppose that
>
> $$
> A^2 = 0.
> $$
>
> We seek the maximum possible number of positive entries in $A$.
>
> ## Step 1: Interpret the condition $A^2 = 0$
>
> The $(i,j)$-entry of $A^2$ is
>
> $$
> (A^2)_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^n a_{ik}a_{kj}.
> $$
>
> Since all entries of $A$ are nonnegative and $A^2=0$, each sum above is $0$. A sum of nonnegative terms can be $0$ only if every term is $0$. Hence, for all $i,j,k$,
>
> $$
> a_{ik}a_{kj} = 0.
> $$
>
> This means:
>
> - if $a_{ik} > 0$, then necessarily $a_{kj} = 0$ for every $j$;
> - equivalently, if row $k$ contains a positive entry, then column $k$ must be entirely zero.
>
> Thus no index $k$ can simultaneously have a positive entry in row $k$ and a positive entry in column $k$.
>
> ## Step 2: Partition the indices
>
> Let
>
> $$
> R = \{\,k : \text{row } k \text{ contains a positive entry}\,\}.
> $$
>
> For each $k \in R$, column $k$ must be zero, by the observation above. Therefore positive entries can occur only in columns whose indices are not in $R$.
>
> Let $|R| = r$. Then:
>
> - there are $r$ rows that may contain positive entries;
> - only the remaining $n-r$ columns can contain positive entries.
>
> Hence the number of positive entries in $A$ is at most
>
> $$
> r(n-r).
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Maximize $r(n-r)$
>
> We now maximize
>
> $$
> f(r) = r(n-r)
> $$
>
> over integers $0 \le r \le n$.
>
> This quadratic is maximized when $r$ is as close as possible to $\frac{n}{2}$. Therefore,
>
> $$
> \max_{0 \le r \le n} r(n-r) = \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor.
> $$
>
> ## Step 4: Show the bound is attainable
>
> It remains to construct such a matrix.
>
> Take a partition of the index set into two parts of sizes
>
> $$
> r = \left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor, 
> \qquad
> n-r = \left\lceil \frac{n}{2} \right\rceil.
> $$
>
> Define $A$ in block form by
>
> $$
> A =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & J \\
> 0 & 0
> \end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> where $J$ is the $r \times (n-r)$ matrix all of whose entries are $1$.
>
> Then $A$ has exactly
>
> $$
> r(n-r) = \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor
> $$
>
> positive entries, and
>
> $$
> A^2 = 
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & J \\
> 0 & 0
> \end{pmatrix}^2
> =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & 0 \\
> 0 & 0
> \end{pmatrix}.
> $$
>
> So this maximum is achieved.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor}
> $$
