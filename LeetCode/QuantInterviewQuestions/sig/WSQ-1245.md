> ## 2. Eight balls, one is slightly heavier: least number of weighings
>
> We have $8$ balls, and exactly one is heavier. We use a balance scale and want to identify the heavier ball in the fewest weighings.
>
> ### Approach
>
> Each weighing on a balance scale has $3$ possible outcomes:
>
> - left side heavier,
> - right side heavier,
> - balance.
>
> Thus, with $2$ weighings, we can distinguish up to
>
> $$
> 3^2 = 9
> $$
>
> possibilities, which is enough for $8$ balls. So $2$ weighings may suffice.
>
> ### Step 1: Divide into groups
>
> Split the $8$ balls into groups of sizes $3$, $3$, and $2$.
>
> Label them:
>
> - Group $A = \{1,2,3\}$
> - Group $B = \{4,5,6\}$
> - Group $C = \{7,8\}$
>
> Weigh group $A$ against group $B$.
>
> ### Case 1: The scale balances
>
> Then none of balls $1$ through $6$ is heavier, so the heavier ball must be in group $C$, namely either ball $7$ or ball $8$.
>
> Now weigh ball $7$ against ball $8$.
>
> - If ball $7$ is heavier, the answer is ball $7$.
> - If ball $8$ is heavier, the answer is ball $8$.
>
> So in this case, the heavier ball is found in $2$ weighings.
>
> ### Case 2: One side is heavier
>
> Suppose group $A$ is heavier than group $B$. Then the heavier ball must be one of $1,2,3$.
>
> Now weigh ball $1$ against ball $2$.
>
> - If one side is heavier, that ball is the heavier ball.
> - If they balance, then ball $3$ is the heavier ball.
>
> Again, this takes $2$ weighings total.
>
> The same reasoning applies if group $B$ is heavier.
>
> ### Conclusion
>
> The minimum number of weighings is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2}
> $$
>
> and the strategy is:
>
> 1. Weigh $3$ balls against $3$ balls.
> 2. Depending on the result, weigh $1$ against $1$ among the remaining candidates.
