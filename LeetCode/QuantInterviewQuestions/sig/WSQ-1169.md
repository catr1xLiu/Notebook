> [!example] Question 1169 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have \$100 and receive an additional \$100 to play a casino game where you bet on flips of a fair coin, with a 4:5 payout. You are required to bet a total of at least \$500 before you can cash out. How do you maximize your expected earnings?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1170)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> All amounts below are in dollars. Let the initial bankroll be $B_0 = 200$, consisting of your own $100$ plus the extra $100$ you were given. You may place any sequence of bets on a fair coin, with payout odds $4:5$, and you are allowed to cash out only after your total amount wagered is at least $500$. The goal is to maximize the expected amount you cash out.
>
> ## 1. Expected value of a single bet
>
> Suppose you bet $b$ on one flip.
>
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, you win and gain $\tfrac45 b$.
> - With probability $\tfrac12$, you lose and lose $b$.
>
> The expected change in bankroll from this bet is
>
> $$
> \frac12\left(\frac45 b\right) + \frac12(-b) = -\frac{1}{10}b.
> $$
>
> So every dollar wagered has expected loss $0.10$. If $W$ denotes your total amount wagered when you stop, then by tracking bankroll plus one-tenth of cumulative wagering as a fair process,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{final bankroll}] = 200 - 0.1\,\mathbb{E}[W].
> $$
>
> To maximize expected earnings, you must therefore minimize $\mathbb{E}[W]$.
>
> ## 2. Why the minimum is not simply $500$
>
> It is tempting to think $W=500$ always, since you cannot cash out below that threshold, so that the best you could do is $200-0.1(500)=150$. But this is not correct: if your bankroll ever reaches $0$, you have nothing left to bet, and the game ends immediately regardless of how much you have wagered so far. On such paths, $W$ is strictly less than $500$. Since bankruptcy is a real possibility, $\mathbb{E}[W]$ can be pushed below $500$, which makes an expected final bankroll above $150$ achievable.
>
> ## 3. The optimal principle
>
> To minimize $\mathbb{E}[W]$, you want bankruptcy, when it occurs, to happen as early as possible, using as few wagered dollars as possible. This means:
>
> - whenever your current bankroll is less than the amount still required, bet your **entire** bankroll, so that a loss immediately ends the game with nothing left to wager;
> - once your bankroll is at least the amount still required, bet **exactly** that remaining amount and stop, since wagering any more than necessary only adds expected loss with no benefit.
>
> ## 4. Applying this to the numbers
>
> Starting bankroll is $200$, and $500$ must be wagered in total.
>
> Since $200<500$, bet the full $200$ on the first flip.
>
> - If you lose (probability $\tfrac12$): bankroll is $0$, total wagered is $200$, and the game is over. You cash out $0$.
> - If you win (probability $\tfrac12$): bankroll becomes
>   $$
>   200+\frac45(200)=360,
>   $$
>   and total wagered so far is $200$, leaving $500-200=300$ still required.
>
> Since $360\ge 300$, bet exactly $300$ on the second flip and then stop regardless of outcome.
>
> - If you win again (probability $\tfrac14$ overall): bankroll becomes
>   $$
>   360+\frac45(300)=360+240=600.
>   $$
> - If you lose (probability $\tfrac14$ overall): bankroll becomes
>   $$
>   360-300=60.
>   $$
>
> Either way, total wagered is now $200+300=500$, so you cash out.
>
> ## 5. Expected value of this strategy
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\text{final bankroll}] = \frac12(0)+\frac14(600)+\frac14(60) = 150+15 = 165.
> $$
>
> As a check, the total wagered is $200$ with probability $\tfrac12$ and $500$ with probability $\tfrac12$, so
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[W] = \frac12(200)+\frac12(500) = 350,
> $$
>
> and indeed $200-0.1(350)=165$, matching the direct computation.
>
> ## 6. Why betting less than the full bankroll first is worse
>
> One might instead try to bet just enough on the first flip so that, upon winning, the remaining requirement can be met in one final bet, choosing the smallest such amount. Solving $200+\tfrac45x \ge 500-x$ gives $x\ge \tfrac{500}{3}\approx 166.7$, so this reasoning suggests betting $\tfrac{500}{3}$ first.
>
> This is worse. If that smaller bet loses, your bankroll is $200-\tfrac{500}{3}=\tfrac{100}{3}>0$, so the game is **not** over: you have not wagered $500$ yet, and you still have money, so you are forced to keep betting at a loss instead of walking away. Carrying that strategy through to completion by continuing to bet the full bankroll at each step, the expected cash-out works out to only
>
> $$
> 150,
> $$
>
> strictly less than $165$. The failure is that betting less than the full bankroll on an early wager does not let a loss terminate the game, so it cannot minimize $\mathbb{E}[W]$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is: bet your entire $200$ on the first flip; if you lose, you are done; if you win, bet exactly the remaining $300$ required and cash out regardless of the outcome. This achieves
>
> - expected final bankroll: $\boxed{165}$;
> - expected net gain relative to your own original $100$: $\boxed{65}$;
> - expected gain relative to the full $200$ starting bankroll: $\boxed{-35}$.
