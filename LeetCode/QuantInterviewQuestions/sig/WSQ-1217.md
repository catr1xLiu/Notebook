> [!example] Question 1217 · SIG
> ## Graduate Quant Trader Interview

What is the probability of getting the sequence HHT before HTH when flipping a fair coin repeatedly?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1218)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ denote the probability that the pattern $HHT$ appears before $HTH$.
>
> The outcome depends on the current relevant suffix of the sequence, so use states based on the longest suffix that is also a prefix of one of the target patterns.
>
> ## States
>
> Let
>
> - $S$: start state
> - $H$: the current suffix is $H$
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $HH$
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $HT$
>
> Let the corresponding winning probabilities for $HHT$ be
>
> - $p_S$
> - $p_H$
> - $p_{HH}$
> - $p_{HT}$
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ## Recurrence equations
>
> From the start state:
>
> - If the first flip is $H$, move to state $H$
> - If the first flip is $T$, remain at the start state
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_S=p_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - On $H$, move to state $HH$
> - On $T$, move to state $HT$
>
> Hence
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - On $T$, the pattern $HHT$ occurs first, so the probability is $1$
> - On $H$, the relevant suffix is still $HH$
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
> So
> $$
> p_{HH}=1.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - On $H$, the pattern $HTH$ occurs first, so the probability is $0$
> - On $T$, no useful prefix remains, so return to the start state
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_S=\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> ## Solve
>
> Since $p_S=p_H$, substitute into the equation for $p_H$:
> $$
> p_S=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12\cdot \frac12 p_S.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> p_S=\frac12+\frac14 p_S.
> $$
> So
> $$
> \frac34 p_S=\frac12,
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> p_S=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
