> [!example] Question 1202 · SIG
> ## Internship Interview

There are two fair dice. For each die, you roll it again if you do not get a 6. Calculate the expected number of times you get a 6 on die 1.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1203)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the total number of times that die $1$ shows a $6$ under the rule:
>
> - Roll both fair dice.
> - For any die that does **not** show $6$, roll that die again.
> - Continue until that die shows $6$.
>
> We are asked for $E[X]$.
>
> ## Step 1: Focus on die $1$
>
> Die $1$ is rolled repeatedly until it first shows $6$. Since each roll is independent and fair, the process stops at the first success, where
>
> $$
> P(\text{success}) = P(\text{die }1 = 6) = \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Once die $1$ shows $6$, it is no longer rolled again. Therefore, die $1$ can show $6$ **exactly once** in the entire procedure.
>
> So the random variable $X$ is not really random:
>
> $$
> X = 1
> $$
>
> always.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the expectation
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> E[X] = E[1] = 1.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}
> $$
