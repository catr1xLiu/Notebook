> [!example] Question 1126 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You start with 1 dollar and your friend starts with 2 dollars. You bet 1 dollar per round until one of you runs out of money. Each round, you have a 2/3 chance of winning the bet. What is the probability that you win all your friend's money before losing yours?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1127)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p=\frac{2}{3}$ be your probability of winning a single bet, and $q=\frac{1}{3}$ be your probability of losing a single bet.
>
> You start with $1$ dollar and your friend starts with $2$ dollars, so the total amount of money is $3$ dollars. The game ends when one player has all $3$ dollars.
>
> This is a gambler’s ruin problem. Let $P_i$ denote the probability that you eventually win the entire game when you currently have $i$ dollars. Then:
>
> - $P_0=0$ because if you have $0$ dollars, you have already lost.
> - $P_3=1$ because if you have $3$ dollars, you have already won.
>
> We want $P_1$.
>
> From state $i$, after one bet:
>
> - with probability $p=\frac{2}{3}$, you move to $i+1$,
> - with probability $q=\frac{1}{3}$, you move to $i-1$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P_1 = pP_2 + qP_0 = \frac{2}{3}P_2,
> $$
> and
> $$
> P_2 = pP_3 + qP_1 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 1 + \frac{1}{3}P_1.
> $$
>
> Substitute the second equation into the first:
> $$
> P_1 = \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3}P_1\right).
> $$
>
> Solve:
> $$
> P_1 = \frac{4}{9} + \frac{2}{9}P_1,
> $$
> so
> $$
> P_1 - \frac{2}{9}P_1 = \frac{4}{9},
> $$
> $$
> \frac{7}{9}P_1 = \frac{4}{9},
> $$
> $$
> P_1 = \frac{4}{7}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, your chance of winning is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{7}}.
> $$
