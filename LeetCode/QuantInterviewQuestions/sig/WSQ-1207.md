> [!example] Question 1207 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

For a fair coin, what is the minimum, maximum, and expected (average) number of flips needed to get either 3 heads or 3 tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1208)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the coin be fair, and let the experiment continue until one side has appeared $3$ times. Thus we stop at the first time we obtain either $3$ heads or $3$ tails.
>
> We seek the minimum, maximum, and average number of flips.
>
> ## 1. Minimum number of flips
>
> The earliest possible stopping time is after $3$ flips, namely if the first three flips are all heads or all tails:
>
> - $HHH$, or
> - $TTT$.
>
> So the minimum number of flips is
>
> $$
> 3.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Maximum number of flips
>
> To avoid stopping, after $4$ flips and after $5$ flips neither side can yet have reached $3$ before the last step. The latest this can happen is if after $4$ flips we have exactly $2$ heads and $2$ tails. Then:
>
> - the $5$th flip makes the counts either $(3,2)$ or $(2,3)$,
> - so the process must stop on flip $5$.
>
> Thus it is impossible to continue to a $6$th flip. Hence the maximum number of flips is
>
> $$
> 5.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Average number of flips
>
> Let $N$ be the number of flips until one side reaches $3$.
>
> Since the stopping time can only be $3$, $4$, or $5$, compute their probabilities.
>
> ### Probability that $N=3$
>
> This happens only for $HHH$ or $TTT$:
>
> $$
> P(N=3)=\frac{2}{2^3}=\frac14.
> $$
>
> ### Probability that $N=4$
>
> To stop on the $4$th flip, after $3$ flips one side must have appeared exactly $2$ times and the other side once, and then the $4$th flip must match the majority side.
>
> Count sequences:
>
> - choose which side reaches $3$: $2$ choices,
> - among the first $3$ flips, choose the position of the minority side: $\binom31=3$ choices,
> - the $4$th flip is then forced.
>
> So there are $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ such sequences out of $2^4=16$:
>
> $$
> P(N=4)=\frac{6}{16}=\frac38.
> $$
>
> ### Probability that $N=5$
>
> This is the remaining probability:
>
> $$
> P(N=5)=1-\frac14-\frac38=\frac38.
> $$
>
> ### Expected number of flips
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E[N]=3\cdot \frac14+4\cdot \frac38+5\cdot \frac38.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> E[N]=\frac34+\frac{12}{8}+\frac{15}{8}
> =\frac34+\frac{27}{8}
> =\frac{6}{8}+\frac{27}{8}
> =\frac{33}{8}.
> $$
>
> So the average number of flips is
>
> $$
> \frac{33}{8}=4.125.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - Minimum number of flips: $$3$$
> - Maximum number of flips: $$5$$
> - Average number of flips: $$\frac{33}{8}=4.125$$
