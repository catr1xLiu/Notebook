> [!example] Question 1221 · SIG
> ## Junior Trader Interview

Suppose you and I are playing a game and have bet \$10, which will go to the winner. At some point, I offer to double the bet to \$20. If you accept, the game continues with the new bet; if you refuse, you lose the game and forfeit the original \$10. What is the minimum probability of winning that you would need to accept the increased bet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1222)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ denote your probability of winning the game at the moment the offer is made.
>
> There are two choices:
>
> - **Refuse**: you immediately lose the game and thus lose the original stake of $\text{\textdollar}10$.
> - **Accept**: the stake becomes $\text{\textdollar}20$, so:
>   - with probability $p$, you win $\text{\textdollar}20$,
>   - with probability $1-p$, you lose $\text{\textdollar}20$.
>
> To find the minimum probability at which accepting is worthwhile, compare the expected payoff from accepting to the certain payoff from refusing.
>
> The expected value of accepting is
>
> $$
> E = 20p + (-20)(1-p).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> E = 20p - 20 + 20p = 40p - 20.
> $$
>
> If you refuse, your payoff is
>
> $$
> -10.
> $$
>
> You should accept when
>
> $$
> 40p - 20 \ge -10.
> $$
>
> Solve for $p$:
>
> $$
> 40p \ge 10
> $$
>
> $$
> p \ge \frac{10}{40} = \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the minimum probability of winning needed to accept the increased bet is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
> $$
>
> that is, $\boxed{25\%}$.
