> [!example] Question 1200 · SIG
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

What is the probability of obtaining 3 heads in a row when tossing an unbiased coin 5 times?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1201)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the coin be fair, and consider all sequences of $5$ tosses. Since each toss has two possible outcomes, there are
>
> $$
> 2^5 = 32
> $$
>
> equally likely outcomes.
>
> We want the probability that the sequence contains **at least one occurrence of $3$ consecutive heads**.
>
> ## Count favorable sequences
>
> A sequence of length $5$ contains $3$ consecutive heads if one of the following blocks appears:
>
> - tosses $1$–$3$: $HHH**$
> - tosses $2$–$4$: $*HHH*$
> - tosses $3$–$5$: $**HHH$
>
> Rather than use inclusion–exclusion, it is simpler to list all sequences of length $5$ having at least one run of $3$ heads:
>
> 1. Exactly one block at positions $1$–$3$:
>    $$
>    HHHHT,\; HHH TH,\; HHH TT
>    $$
>    more systematically:
>    $$
>    HHHHH,\; HHHHT,\; HHHTH,\; HHHTT
>    $$
>
> 2. Block at positions $2$–$4$:
>    $$
>    THHHT
>    $$
>    Also,
>    $$
>    HHHHH,\; HHHHT,\; THHHT,\; HTHHH
>    $$
>    but some are repeated.
>
> 3. Block at positions $3$–$5$:
>    $$
>    TTHHH,\; THHHH,\; HTHHH,\; HHHHH
>    $$
>
> Collecting distinct sequences gives:
>
> $$
> HHHHH,\; HHHHT,\; HHHTH,\; HHHTT,\; THHHT,\; HTHHH,\; TTHHH,\; THHHH
> $$
>
> Thus there are $8$ favorable outcomes.
>
> ## Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one run of }3\text{ heads in }5\text{ tosses}) = \frac{8}{32} = \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
> $$
