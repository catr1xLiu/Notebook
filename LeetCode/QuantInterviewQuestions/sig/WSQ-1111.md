> [!example] Question 1111 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

A painted 3x3 cube consists of 27 small cubes. If one small cube is selected at random and thrown, and you observe only the unpainted sides, what is the probability that the selected cube is completely unpainted?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1112)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. Painted $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube
>
> A large $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube is painted on the outside and then separated into $27$ small cubes.
>
> When one small cube is selected and thrown, the visible faces are all the faces except the one on which it lands. We are told that all visible faces are unpainted, and we want the probability that the selected cube is completely unpainted.
>
> ### Step 1: Classify the small cubes
>
> In a painted $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube:
>
> - Corner cubes: $8$, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - Edge-center cubes: $12$, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - Face-center cubes: $6$, each with $1$ painted face.
> - Interior cube: $1$, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> So only one cube is completely unpainted.
>
> ### Step 2: Determine which cubes could produce the observation
>
> If, after landing, all visible faces are unpainted, then every painted face of the selected cube must be hidden on the bottom.
>
> This is possible only if the cube has at most one painted face:
>
> - Interior cube ($0$ painted faces): always satisfies the condition.
> - Face-center cube ($1$ painted face): satisfies the condition only if it lands on its painted face.
>
> Cubes with $2$ or $3$ painted faces cannot have all painted faces hidden, since only one face is on the bottom.
>
> ### Step 3: Compute conditional probability
>
> Let $A$ be the event that all visible faces are unpainted.
>
> Let $U$ be the event that the cube is completely unpainted.
>
> We compute:
> $$
> P(U \mid A) = \frac{P(U \cap A)}{P(A)}.
> $$
>
> Since the interior cube always satisfies $A$,
> $$
> P(U \cap A) = \frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> For $P(A)$:
>
> - Interior cube: probability $\frac{1}{27}$, and then $A$ occurs with probability $1$.
> - Each face-center cube: there are $6$ such cubes, chosen with probability $\frac{6}{27}$; for such a cube, $A$ occurs only if the painted face is on the bottom, which has probability $\frac{1}{6}$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(A) = \frac{1}{27} + \frac{6}{27}\cdot\frac{1}{6}
> = \frac{1}{27} + \frac{1}{27}
> = \frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(U \mid A) = \frac{\frac{1}{27}}{\frac{2}{27}} = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ### Answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> $$

> ## 2. Three people chosen from $8$ around a table
>
> There are $8$ people sitting around a round table. Three are selected at random. Find the probability that at least two of the three were sitting next to each other.
>
> ### Step 1: Count total selections
>
> The total number of ways to choose $3$ people from $8$ is
> $$
> \binom{8}{3} = 56.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Use the complement
>
> It is easier to count selections in which no two selected people are adjacent.
>
> For $3$ chosen people around a circle of $8$, no two are adjacent.
>
> A standard counting result for choosing $k$ nonadjacent people from $n$ arranged in a circle is
> $$
> \frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}.
> $$
>
> Here $n=8$, $k=3$, so the number is
> $$
> \frac{8}{8-3}\binom{8-3}{3}
> = \frac{8}{5}\binom{5}{3}
> = \frac{8}{5}\cdot 10
> = 16.
> $$
>
> Thus the number of selections with at least one adjacent pair is
> $$
> 56 - 16 = 40.
> $$
>
> Therefore the required probability is
> $$
> \frac{40}{56} = \frac{5}{7}.
> $$
>
> ### Answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{7}}
> $$
