> [!example] Question 1227 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability of getting the sequence HTH before HHT when repeatedly flipping a fair coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1228)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that the pattern $HTH$ appears before the pattern $HHT$ in an infinite sequence of fair coin flips. We compute $P(A)$ by conditioning on the current relevant suffix of flips.
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Track the longest suffix of the current sequence that is also a prefix of either target pattern.
>
> The relevant states are:
>
> - $S$: no relevant partial match
> - $H$: the current suffix is $H$
> - $HT$: the current suffix is $HT$
> - $HH$: the current suffix is $HH$
>
> Let
>
> - $p_S =$ probability of eventually getting $HTH$ before $HHT$ starting from state $S$
> - $p_H, p_{HT}, p_{HH}$ defined similarly
>
> We want $p_S$.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up equations
>
> From state $S$:
>
> - flipping $T$ leaves no relevant suffix, so remain in $S$
> - flipping $H$ moves to $H$
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_S+\frac12 p_H.
> $$
>
> From state $H$:
>
> - flipping $T$ gives suffix $HT$, so move to $HT$
> - flipping $H$ gives suffix $HH$, so move to $HH$
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> From state $HT$:
>
> - flipping $H$ completes $HTH$, so success
> - flipping $T$ gives suffix $T$, which has no relevant prefix, so move to $S$
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> From state $HH$:
>
> - flipping $T$ completes $HHT$, so failure
> - flipping $H$ leaves suffix $HH$, so remain in $HH$
>
> Thus
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12\cdot 0+\frac12 p_{HH}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HH},
> $$
> so
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> p_S=\frac12 p_S+\frac12 p_H,
> $$
> we get
> $$
> p_S=p_H.
> $$
>
> Then
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}+\frac12 p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HT},
> $$
> since $p_{HH}=0$.
>
> Also
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p_S.
> $$
>
> Therefore
> $$
> p_S=p_H=\frac12 p_{HT}=\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p_S\right)
> =\frac14+\frac14 p_S.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> p_S-\frac14 p_S=\frac14,
> $$
> which gives
> $$
> \frac34 p_S=\frac14,
> $$
> and hence
> $$
> p_S=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac13}
> $$
>
> So the probability of getting $HTH$ before $HHT$ is $\frac13$.
