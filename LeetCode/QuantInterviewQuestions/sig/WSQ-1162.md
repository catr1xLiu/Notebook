> [!example] Question 1162 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

We are racing, and at any time we may signal to double our bet. We have each put down 100 to start. If, during the race, I signal that I want to double the bet, what is the minimum probability of winning that you would need in order to accept continuing with the doubled bet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1163)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the current stake be $100$. Under the standard doubling convention, if you decline the double, you concede the current bet and lose $100$ outright; if you accept, the stake becomes $400$, split as $200$ to the winner and $200$ to the loser relative to the current stake.
>
> ## Setting up the comparison
>
> From your point of view, accepting the double means:
>
> - with probability $p$, you win the $400$ pot, a net gain of $200$ relative to the original $100$ stake,
> - with probability $1-p$, you lose, a net result of $-200$ relative to that same baseline.
>
> So the expected value of accepting, measured against your current position, is
>
> $$
> E_{\text{accept}} = 200p - 200(1-p) = 400p-200.
> $$
>
> Declining is not free: by the doubling convention, refusing to continue means conceding the current stake, giving a certain payoff of
>
> $$
> E_{\text{decline}} = -100.
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Compare the two options
>
> You should accept whenever
>
> $$
> 400p-200 \ge -100.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Solve for $p$
>
> $$
> 400p \ge 100,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> p \ge \frac{100}{400} = \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Thus, the minimum winning probability required to accept the doubled bet is
>
> $$
> \boxed{25\%}.
> $$
>
> At exactly $p=25\%$ you are indifferent between accepting and conceding; above that threshold, accepting has the higher expected value. Note that this $25\%$ threshold is a consequence of the doubling convention itself, under which declining forfeits the current stake. If instead you could walk away for free (keeping your $100$ rather than forfeiting it), the comparison would instead be between $400p-200$ and $0$, giving a break-even probability of $50\%$.
