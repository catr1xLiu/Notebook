> [!example] Question 1129 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A group of people wants to determine their average salary on the condition that no individual is able to discover anyone else's salary. How can they accomplish this?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1130)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the salaries be $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$, where person $i$ knows only $s_i$. The goal is to compute
>
> $$
> \frac{s_1+s_2+\cdots+s_n}{n}
> $$
>
> without revealing any individual salary.
>
> ## Method: add private random masks
>
> Each person $i$ chooses a secret random number $r_i$, known only to that person. Then person $i$ computes
>
> $$
> t_i = s_i + r_i - r_{i-1},
> $$
>
> where the indices are taken cyclically, so that person $1$ uses $r_0 = r_n$.
>
> Each person reveals only $t_i$, not $s_i$ or $r_i$.
>
> ## Why this works
>
> Now sum all the announced values:
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n t_i
> =
> \sum_{i=1}^n (s_i + r_i - r_{i-1}).
> $$
>
> Rearranging,
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n t_i
> =
> \sum_{i=1}^n s_i
> +
> \sum_{i=1}^n r_i
> -
> \sum_{i=1}^n r_{i-1}.
> $$
>
> Because the indices are cyclic,
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n r_i = \sum_{i=1}^n r_{i-1},
> $$
>
> so the random terms cancel, leaving
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n t_i = \sum_{i=1}^n s_i.
> $$
>
> Hence everyone can compute the average salary as
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n t_i.
> $$
>
> ## Privacy
>
> The published value $t_i$ is masked by the secret random numbers $r_i$ and $r_{i-1}$, so it does not reveal $s_i$. Since only the total sum is recovered, no individual salary is exposed.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> They can determine the average salary by having each person announce a suitably masked value $t_i$, chosen so that the random masks cancel only in the total sum. Then the group computes
>
> $$
> \text{average salary} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n t_i.
> $$
>
> This reveals the average but not the individual salaries.
