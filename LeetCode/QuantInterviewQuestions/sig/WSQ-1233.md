> [!example] Question 1233 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Research Interview

Two points are randomly selected on the circumference of a circle. What is the expected distance between them?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1234)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the circle have radius $R$. Two points are chosen independently and uniformly on the circumference. We seek the expected length of the chord joining them.
>
> ## 1. Express the distance in terms of the central angle
>
> Let $\Theta$ be the smaller central angle between the two points. By symmetry, $\Theta$ is uniformly distributed on $[0,\pi]$.
>
> For a chord subtending angle $\Theta$, the distance between the points is
>
> $$
> D = 2R \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \mathbb{E}\left[2R \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\right].
> $$
>
> Since $\Theta \sim \text{Uniform}(0,\pi)$, its density is $\frac{1}{\pi}$ on $[0,\pi]$. Thus,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi 2R \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\, d\Theta.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Evaluate the integral
>
> Compute
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{2R}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\, d\Theta.
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> u=\frac{\Theta}{2}, \qquad d\Theta = 2\,du.
> $$
>
> When $\Theta=0$, $u=0$; when $\Theta=\pi$, $u=\frac{\pi}{2}$. Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{2R}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin(u)\cdot 2\,du
> = \frac{4R}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin(u)\,du.
> $$
>
> Now,
>
> $$
> \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin(u)\,du
> = \left[-\cos(u)\right]_0^{\pi/2}
> = 1.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D] = \frac{4R}{\pi}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final answer
>
> The expected distance between two random points on a circle of radius $R$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4R}{\pi}}.
> $$
>
> For the unit circle, this becomes
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{\pi}}.
> $$
