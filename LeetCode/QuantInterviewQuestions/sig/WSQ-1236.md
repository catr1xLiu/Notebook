> [!example] Question 1236 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Three real numbers are chosen independently and uniformly at random from the interval \[0, 3\]. What is the probability that the smallest number is between 0.5 and 1?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1237)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1, X_2, X_3$ be independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on $[0,3]$. We seek
> $$
> P\left(0.5 < \min(X_1,X_2,X_3) < 1\right).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Use the distribution of the minimum
>
> Let
> $$
> M=\min(X_1,X_2,X_3).
> $$
> Then
> $$
> P(M>t)=P(X_1>t,\,X_2>t,\,X_3>t).
> $$
> Since the variables are independent and each is uniform on $[0,3]$,
> $$
> P(X_i>t)=\frac{3-t}{3}, \qquad 0\le t\le 3.
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(M>t)=\left(\frac{3-t}{3}\right)^3.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the desired probability
>
> We want
> $$
> P(0.5<M<1)=P(M>0.5)-P(M\ge 1).
> $$
> Because the distribution is continuous,
> $$
> P(M\ge 1)=P(M>1).
> $$
> So
> $$
> P(0.5<M<1)=\left(\frac{3-0.5}{3}\right)^3-\left(\frac{3-1}{3}\right)^3.
> $$
>
> Now simplify:
> $$
> \frac{3-0.5}{3}=\frac{2.5}{3}=\frac{5}{6},
> \qquad
> \frac{3-1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> P(0.5<M<1)=\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^3-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3
> =\frac{125}{216}-\frac{64}{216}
> =\frac{61}{216}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{61}{216}}
> $$
