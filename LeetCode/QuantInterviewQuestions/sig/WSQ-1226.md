> [!example] Question 1226 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You toss a fair coin repeatedly. What are the odds that the sequence HTH appears before the sequence HHT?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1227)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the game end as soon as either the pattern $A=\text{HTH}$ or the pattern $B=\text{HHT}$ appears. We seek
>
> $$
> P(\text{HTH occurs before HHT}).
> $$
>
> ## Approach
>
> Use states based on the current relevant suffix of the sequence already tossed. Only suffixes that could still be the beginning of one of the target patterns matter.
>
> Let
>
> - $p$ = probability of eventually getting $\text{HTH}$ before $\text{HHT}$ starting from scratch,
> - $p_H$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{H}$,
> - $p_{HH}$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{HH}$,
> - $p_{HT}$ = probability starting from suffix $\text{HT}$.
>
> The absorbing states are:
>
> - from $\text{HT}$, if the next toss is $\text{H}$, we get $\text{HTH}$ and win;
> - from $\text{HH}$, if the next toss is $\text{T}$, we get $\text{HHT}$ and lose.
>
> ## Setting up equations
>
> From the initial state:
>
> $$
> p=\frac12 p_H+\frac12 p.
> $$
>
> This is because tossing $\text{T}$ leaves us effectively in the starting state again, while tossing $\text{H}$ moves us to state $\text{H}$.
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> p=p_H.
> $$
>
> Now analyze the other states.
>
> ### State $\text{H}$
>
> From suffix $\text{H}$:
>
> - toss $\text{H}$ $\to$ suffix $\text{HH}$,
> - toss $\text{T}$ $\to$ suffix $\text{HT}$.
>
> So
>
> $$
> p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT}.
> $$
>
> ### State $\text{HH}$
>
> From suffix $\text{HH}$:
>
> - toss $\text{H}$ $\to$ the last two tosses are still $\text{HH}$,
> - toss $\text{T}$ $\to$ pattern $\text{HHT}$ appears first, so probability $0$.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12\cdot 0.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> p_{HH}=0.
> $$
>
> ### State $\text{HT}$
>
> From suffix $\text{HT}$:
>
> - toss $\text{H}$ $\to$ pattern $\text{HTH}$ appears first, so probability $1$,
> - toss $\text{T}$ $\to$ suffix becomes irrelevant and we return to the starting state.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12\cdot 1+\frac12 p.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p.
> $$
>
> ## Solve
>
> Using
>
> $$
> p=p_H=\frac12 p_{HH}+\frac12 p_{HT},
> $$
>
> and substituting $p_{HH}=0$ and $p_{HT}=\frac12+\frac12 p$, we get
>
> $$
> p=\frac12\left(0\right)+\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac12 p\right)
> =\frac14+\frac14 p.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \frac34 p=\frac14,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> p=\frac13.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The probability that $\text{HTH}$ occurs before $\text{HHT}$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac13}.
> $$
>
> Thus the odds in favor of $\text{HTH}$ before $\text{HHT}$ are
>
> $$
> \boxed{1:2}.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, the odds against it are
>
> $$
> \boxed{2:1}.
> $$
