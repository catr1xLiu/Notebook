> [!example] Question 1134 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

You have 17 coins and I have 16 coins. We flip all the coins at the same time. If you have more heads, then you win; if we have the same number of heads or you have fewer, then I win. What is your probability of winning?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1135)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained from $17$ fair coins, and let $Y$ be the number of heads obtained from $16$ fair coins. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(17,\tfrac12), 
> \qquad
> Y \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(16,\tfrac12),
> $$
>
> and the two variables are independent.
>
> We want
>
> $$
> P(X>Y).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Split off one coin from the $17$
>
> Write
>
> $$
> X = Z + W,
> $$
>
> where
>
> - $Z \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(16,\tfrac12)$ is the number of heads among $16$ of the $17$ coins,
> - $W \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(1,\tfrac12)$ is the remaining coin,
>
> with $Z$, $W$, and $Y$ independent.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(X>Y)=P(Z+W>Y).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Use symmetry between $Z$ and $Y$
>
> Since $Z$ and $Y$ are independent and identically distributed,
>
> $$
> P(Z>Y)=P(Y>Z).
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> P(Z>Y)=\frac{1-P(Z=Y)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Now consider the two cases for $W$:
>
> - If $W=1$, then $Z+W>Y$ exactly when $Z\ge Y$.
> - If $W=0$, then $Z+W>Y$ exactly when $Z>Y$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(X>Y)
> = \frac12 P(Z\ge Y) + \frac12 P(Z>Y).
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> P(Z\ge Y)=P(Z>Y)+P(Z=Y),
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> P(X>Y)
> = \frac12\bigl(P(Z>Y)+P(Z=Y)\bigr)+\frac12 P(Z>Y)
> = P(Z>Y)+\frac12 P(Z=Y).
> $$
>
> Substitute $P(Z>Y)=\dfrac{1-P(Z=Y)}{2}$:
>
> $$
> P(X>Y)
> =
> \frac{1-P(Z=Y)}{2}+\frac12 P(Z=Y)
> =
> \frac12.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The probability that the player with $17$ coins gets more heads than the player with $16$ coins is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac12}.
> $$
>
> So the probability of winning is exactly $50\%$.
