> [!example] Question 1170 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A can finish a job in 100 minutes, and B can finish the same job in 120 minutes. A and B work together on the job, but after 40 minutes, C comes to help them, and they finish the job in an additional 10 minutes. How long would it take C to finish the job by himself?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1171)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the whole job be $1$ unit.
>
> A’s rate of work is
> $$
> \frac{1}{100}
> $$
> job per minute, and B’s rate is
> $$
> \frac{1}{120}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> So, together their rate is
> $$
> \frac{1}{100}+\frac{1}{120}
> =\frac{6}{600}+\frac{5}{600}
> =\frac{11}{600}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> In the first $40$ minutes, A and B complete
> $$
> 40\cdot \frac{11}{600}
> =\frac{440}{600}
> =\frac{11}{15}
> $$
> of the job.
>
> Hence the remaining work is
> $$
> 1-\frac{11}{15}=\frac{4}{15}.
> $$
>
> This remaining $\frac{4}{15}$ of the job is finished in the next $10$ minutes by A, B, and C together. Therefore their combined rate is
> $$
> \frac{\frac{4}{15}}{10}=\frac{4}{150}=\frac{2}{75}
> $$
> job per minute.
>
> Thus C’s rate is
> $$
> \frac{2}{75}-\frac{11}{600}.
> $$
> Convert to a common denominator:
> $$
> \frac{2}{75}=\frac{16}{600},
> $$
> so
> $$
> \frac{16}{600}-\frac{11}{600}=\frac{5}{600}=\frac{1}{120}.
> $$
>
> Therefore C alone can finish the job in
> $$
> \frac{1}{1/120}=120
> $$
> minutes.
>
> ## Answer
> $$
> \boxed{120\text{ minutes}}
> $$
