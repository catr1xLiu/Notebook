> [!example] Question 1168 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Given a standard shuffled deck of cards, you draw a card from the top. How much would you bet that the next card is higher than the one you drew? Please discuss bet sizing strategies and whether the order of the cards matters.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1169)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Consider a standard deck of $52$ cards, thoroughly shuffled. Draw the top card and reveal its **rank**. You are then offered an even-money bet that the **next** card has higher rank than the one just drawn.
>
> We analyze:
>
> 1. the probability that the next card is higher,
> 2. the fair value of the bet depending on the first card,
> 3. how much to bet,
> 4. whether order matters.
>
> Throughout, let ranks be ordered
> $$
> 2<3<4<\cdots<10<J<Q<K<A.
> $$
>
> Suits do not matter.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Probability that the next card is higher given the first card
>
> Suppose the first card has rank $r$, and let its position among the $13$ ranks be $k$, where
>
> - $k=1$ for a $2$,
> - $k=2$ for a $3$,
> - $\dots$,
> - $k=13$ for an Ace.
>
> After drawing one card of rank $k$, there are $51$ cards left.
>
> ### Higher cards remaining
> There are $13-k$ ranks above rank $k$, and each such rank has $4$ cards remaining, so the number of higher cards left is
> $$
> 4(13-k).
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(\text{next card higher}\mid \text{first rank }k)=\frac{4(13-k)}{51}.
> $$
>
> Similarly, the number of lower cards remaining is
> $$
> 4(k-1),
> $$
> so
> $$
> P(\text{next card lower}\mid \text{first rank }k)=\frac{4(k-1)}{51}.
> $$
>
> There are $3$ cards of the same rank left, so
> $$
> P(\text{tie}\mid \text{first rank }k)=\frac{3}{51}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Is the bet favorable?
>
> If the rule is simply “you win if the next card is higher, otherwise you lose,” then ties count as losses. The bet is favorable exactly when
> $$
> \frac{4(13-k)}{51}>\frac12.
> $$
>
> Solve:
> $$
> 4(13-k)>25.5
> $$
> so
> $$
> 13-k>6.375,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> k<6.625.
> $$
>
> Therefore the favorable first ranks are
> $$
> 2,3,4,5,6,7.
> $$
>
> For these, the chance the next card is higher exceeds $50\%$.
>
> For
> $$
> 8,9,10,J,Q,K,A,
> $$
> the chance is below $50\%$.
>
> In particular:
>
> - **First card $2$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{48}{51}$
> - **First card $3$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{44}{51}$
> - **First card $4$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{40}{51}$
> - **First card $5$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{36}{51}$
> - **First card $6$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{32}{51}$
> - **First card $7$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{28}{51}$
> - **First card $8$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{24}{51}$
> - **First card $9$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{20}{51}$
> - **First card $10$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{16}{51}$
> - **First card $J$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{12}{51}$
> - **First card $Q$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{8}{51}$
> - **First card $K$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{4}{51}$
> - **First card $A$** — $P(\text{higher})$: $\frac{0}{51}$
>
> So if you may choose whether to bet after seeing the first card, you should bet “higher” only when the first card is $7$ or lower.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Fair value of the bet
>
> Suppose you stake $1$ unit at even money:
>
> - win $+1$ if the next card is higher,
> - lose $-1$ otherwise.
>
> Then the expected value given first rank $k$ is
> $$
> \operatorname{EV}(k)
> =
> \frac{4(13-k)}{51}(+1)
> +
> \left(1-\frac{4(13-k)}{51}\right)(-1).
> $$
>
> This simplifies to
> $$
> \operatorname{EV}(k)=2\cdot \frac{4(13-k)}{51}-1
> =\frac{8(13-k)}{51}-1.
> $$
>
> Examples:
>
> - If the first card is $2$:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{EV}=\frac{8\cdot 12}{51}-1=\frac{96}{51}-1=\frac{45}{51}\approx 0.882.
>   $$
> - If the first card is $7$:
>   $$
>   \operatorname{EV}=\frac{8\cdot 6}{51}-1=\frac{48}{51}-1=-\frac{3}{51}<0.
>   $$
>
> This reveals an important point: because ties count as losses, even a $7$ is **not** favorable at even money. Indeed,
> $$
> P(\text{higher}\mid 7)=\frac{24}{51}<\frac12.
> $$
>
> So let us correct the threshold carefully.
>
> The condition is
> $$
> \frac{4(13-k)}{51}>\frac12.
> $$
>
> Checking values:
>
> - For $k=6$ (a $7$): 
>   $$
>   \frac{4(13-6)}{51}=\frac{28}{51}> \frac12
>   $$
>   only if $k=6$ corresponds to rank $7$.
>
> But our indexing was:
> - $k=1$ for $2$,
> - $k=2$ for $3$,
> - $k=3$ for $4$,
> - $k=4$ for $5$,
> - $k=5$ for $6$,
> - $k=6$ for $7$,
> - $k=7$ for $8$.
>
> Thus for a $7$,
> $$
> P(\text{higher})=\frac{4(13-6)}{51}=\frac{28}{51}> \frac12.
> $$
>
> So the favorable cards are indeed
> $$
> 2,3,4,5,6,7.
> $$
>
> And for an $8$,
> $$
> P(\text{higher})=\frac{24}{51}<\frac12.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. What if you can choose “higher” or “lower”?
>
> If after seeing the first card you may choose the better side, then you compare the counts of higher and lower remaining cards.
>
> Given first rank $k$:
>
> - higher cards remaining: $4(13-k)$,
> - lower cards remaining: $4(k-1)$.
>
> You should choose the larger of the two.
>
> Thus:
>
> - If the card is below the middle ranks, bet **higher**.
> - If the card is above the middle ranks, bet **lower**.
> - For the middle card $8$, lower is better:
>   $$
>   P(\text{lower}\mid 8)=\frac{28}{51},\qquad
>   P(\text{higher}\mid 8)=\frac{24}{51}.
>   $$
>
> There is no exact symmetry because ties are possible and count against you.
>
> The best success probability after seeing rank $k$ is
> $$
> \frac{\max\{4(13-k),\,4(k-1)\}}{51}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. If you must decide before seeing the first card
>
> Before any card is revealed, by symmetry among two distinct ranks:
>
> - the probability the second card is higher than the first equals
> - the probability the second card is lower than the first.
>
> The only remaining possibility is a tie in rank.
>
> There are $3$ cards among the remaining $51$ that match the first card’s rank, so
> $$
> P(\text{tie})=\frac{3}{51}=\frac{1}{17}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> P(\text{higher})=P(\text{lower})=\frac{1-\frac{1}{17}}{2}
> =\frac{16/17}{2}
> =\frac{8}{17}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{next card higher})=\frac{8}{17}\approx 0.4706.
> $$
>
> So if you must bet “higher” before seeing the first card, this is a losing even-money bet.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Bet sizing
>
> ### Fair-odds viewpoint
> If the first card has rank $k$, then the probability of winning a “higher” bet is
> $$
> p_k=\frac{4(13-k)}{51}.
> $$
>
> At even money, the expected profit per unit staked is
> $$
> 2p_k-1.
> $$
>
> You should bet only when
> $$
> p_k>\frac12.
> $$
>
> Thus, for an even-money wager, bet only when the first card is
> $$
> 2,3,4,5,6,7.
> $$
>
> ### Kelly criterion
> If you have bankroll $B$ and can repeatedly make such bets at even money, the Kelly-optimal fraction of bankroll to bet is
> $$
> f^*=2p_k-1,
> $$
> provided this is positive; otherwise bet $0$.
>
> So
> $$
> f^*=\max\left(0,\;2\cdot \frac{4(13-k)}{51}-1\right).
> $$
>
> Examples:
>
> - First card $2$:
>   $$
>   p=\frac{48}{51},\qquad
>   f^*=2\cdot \frac{48}{51}-1=\frac{45}{51}\approx 0.882.
>   $$
> - First card $5$:
>   $$
>   p=\frac{36}{51},\qquad
>   f^*=\frac{72}{51}-1=\frac{21}{51}\approx 0.412.
>   $$
> - First card $7$:
>   $$
>   p=\frac{28}{51},\qquad
>   f^*=\frac{56}{51}-1=\frac{5}{51}\approx 0.098.
>   $$
> - First card $8$:
>   $$
>   p=\frac{24}{51},\qquad
>   f^*=0.
>   $$
>
> In practice, one often uses a fractional Kelly strategy, such as half-Kelly, to reduce volatility.
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Does order matter?
>
> Yes, if the question is specifically “is the next card higher than the card you just drew?”
>
> Once the first card is known, the conditional probability depends on its rank. A low first card makes “higher” likely; a high first card makes it unlikely.
>
> However, **before** any cards are revealed, the order is symmetric:
>
> - $P(\text{second higher than first})=\frac{8}{17}$,
> - $P(\text{second lower than first})=\frac{8}{17}$,
> - $P(\text{equal rank})=\frac{1}{17}$.
>
> So ex ante, neither direction has an advantage, but after observing the first card, order matters because it determines the conditional distribution for the second card.
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If the first card has rank position $k$ from low to high, where $k=1$ for $2$ and $k=13$ for Ace, then
> $$
> P(\text{next card higher}\mid k)=\frac{4(13-k)}{51}.
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - Bet **higher** at even money only if the first card is
>   $$
>   2,3,4,5,6,7.
>   $$
> - Do **not** bet higher if the first card is
>   $$
>   8,9,10,J,Q,K,A.
>   $$
>
> If you may choose either direction after seeing the first card, choose the side with more remaining ranks above or below.
>
> If you must decide before seeing any card, then
> $$
> P(\text{next card higher})=\frac{8}{17},
> $$
> so an even-money bet on “higher” is unfavorable.
>
> For bet sizing under repeated play at even money, the Kelly fraction is
> $$
> f^*=\max\left(0,\;2\cdot \frac{4(13-k)}{51}-1\right).
> $$
>
> Thus order matters **after** the first card is revealed, but not **before** it is revealed except through the tie probability.
