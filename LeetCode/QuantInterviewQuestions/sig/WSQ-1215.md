> [!example] Question 1215 · SIG
> ## Summer Intern Interview

You are playing a game where both you and your opponent have each put \$10 in the pot. Your opponent now bets another \$10. What is the minimum probability of winning required for you to call this \$10 bet?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1216)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The pot initially contains
>
> $$
> \$10 + \$10 = \$20.
> $$
>
> Your opponent then bets another $\text{\textdollar}10$, so before you act, the pot is
>
> $$
> \$20 + \$10 = \$30.
> $$
>
> To continue, you must call $\text{\textdollar}10$. If you call, the total pot becomes
>
> $$
> \$30 + \$10 = \$40.
> $$
>
> Let $p$ be your probability of winning.  
> Your expected value from calling is
>
> $$
> p(\$40) - \$10.
> $$
>
> For a call to be at least break-even, this must be nonnegative:
>
> $$
> p(\$40) - \$10 \ge 0.
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> 40p \ge 10
> $$
>
> and hence
>
> $$
> p \ge \frac{10}{40} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the minimum probability of winning required to call is
>
> $$
> \boxed{25\%}.
> $$
