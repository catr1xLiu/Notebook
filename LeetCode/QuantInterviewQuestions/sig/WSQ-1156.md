> [!example] Question 1156 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

How many ways can five people sit in a circle such that you are sitting next to your best friend?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1157)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To count circular seatings, arrangements that differ only by rotation are considered the same.
>
> Let the $5$ people include you and your best friend.
>
> ## Step 1: Count all circular arrangements
> The number of ways to seat $5$ distinct people around a circle is
>
> $$
> (5-1)! = 4! = 24.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Count arrangements where you sit next to your best friend
> Treat you and your best friend as a single block. Then there are effectively $4$ objects to arrange around the circle:
>
> - the block $(\text{you, friend})$,
> - the other $3$ people.
>
> The number of circular arrangements of these $4$ objects is
>
> $$
> (4-1)! = 3! = 6.
> $$
>
> Within the block, you and your friend can switch places in
>
> $$
> 2
> $$
>
> ways.
>
> Thus the total number of favorable arrangements is
>
> $$
> 2 \cdot 3! = 2 \cdot 6 = 12.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{12}
> $$
