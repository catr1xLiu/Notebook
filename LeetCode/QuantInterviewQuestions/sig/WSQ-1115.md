> [!example] Question 1115 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Research Interview

Two points are randomly selected on a circle. What is the expected distance between them?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1116)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the circle have radius $R$. Two points are chosen independently and uniformly on the circumference. We seek the expected length of the chord joining them.
>
> ## 1. Express the distance in terms of the central angle
>
> Fix one point. Let $\Theta$ be the central angle between the two points. Since the second point is uniform on the circle, we may take
> $$
> \Theta \sim \text{Uniform}(0,2\pi).
> $$
>
> The chord length corresponding to angle $\Theta$ is
> $$
> D = 2R \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)
> $$
> for $\Theta \in [0,\pi]$, and more generally
> $$
> D = 2R \left|\sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\right|.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2R \left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right|\, d\theta.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Evaluate the integral
>
> On $[0,2\pi]$, we have $\sin(\theta/2)\ge 0$, so the absolute value is unnecessary:
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{2R}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\, d\theta
> = \frac{R}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\, d\theta.
> $$
>
> Compute the integral:
> $$
> \int_0^{2\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\, d\theta
> = \left[-2\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]_0^{2\pi}
> = -2\cos(\pi) + 2\cos(0)
> = 2 + 2 = 4.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D] = \frac{R}{\pi}\cdot 4 = \frac{4R}{\pi}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final answer
>
> The expected distance between two random points on a circle of radius $R$ is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4R}{\pi}}.
> $$
>
> For the unit circle, this becomes
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{\pi}}.
> $$
