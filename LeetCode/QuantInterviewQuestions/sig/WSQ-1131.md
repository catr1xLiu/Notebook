> [!example] Question 1131 · SIG
> ## Assistant Trader Interview

A submarine is targeted by two torpedoes, each with a 1/3 probability of hitting the target independently. What is the probability that at least one of the torpedoes will hit the submarine?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1132)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For the first question, let each torpedo hit the target with probability $\frac{1}{3}$, and assume the two torpedoes act independently.
>
> The event “at least one torpedo hits” is easiest to compute using the complement:
>
> - Probability a torpedo misses:  
>   $$
>   1-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{3}
>   $$
>
> - Probability both torpedoes miss:  
>   $$
>   \frac{2}{3}\cdot \frac{2}{3}=\frac{4}{9}
>   $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that at least one torpedo hits is
>
> $$
> 1-\frac{4}{9}=\frac{5}{9}
> $$
>
> So the answer is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{9}}
> $$
>
> For the second question, there is not enough information to determine the answer.
