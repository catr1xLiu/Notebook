> [!example] Question 1112 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Nine white cubes are arranged into a 3x3 cube and the outside surfaces are painted green. A cube is randomly selected and rolled. If the side facing up is white, what is the probability that this cube was the middle (center) cube?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1113)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the $3 \times 3 \times 3$ cube made of $27$ small cubes. After the outside is painted green, the small cubes fall into four types:
>
> - **Corner cubes:** $8$ cubes, each with $3$ green faces and $3$ white faces.
> - **Edge-center cubes:** $12$ cubes, each with $2$ green faces and $4$ white faces.
> - **Face-center cubes:** $6$ cubes, each with $1$ green face and $5$ white faces.
> - **Middle cube:** $1$ cube, with $0$ green faces and $6$ white faces.
>
> Let $M$ be the event that the selected cube is the middle cube, and let $W$ be the event that the rolled cube shows a white face on top.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(M \mid W).
> $$
>
> By Bayes’ theorem,
> $$
> P(M \mid W)=\frac{P(W \mid M)P(M)}{P(W)}.
> $$
>
> Now compute each term.
>
> Since the middle cube has all $6$ faces white,
> $$
> P(W \mid M)=1.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> P(M)=\frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Next, compute $P(W)$ by averaging over all cube types:
> $$
> P(W)=\frac{1}{27}\left(8\cdot \frac{3}{6}+12\cdot \frac{4}{6}+6\cdot \frac{5}{6}+1\cdot \frac{6}{6}\right).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
> $$
> P(W)=\frac{1}{27}\left(8\cdot \frac12+12\cdot \frac23+6\cdot \frac56+1\right).
> $$
>
> $$
> = \frac{1}{27}(4+8+5+1)=\frac{18}{27}=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(M \mid W)=\frac{1\cdot \frac{1}{27}}{\frac{2}{3}}
> = \frac{1}{27}\cdot \frac{3}{2}
> = \frac{1}{18}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the required probability is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{18}}.
> $$
