> [!example] Question 1220 · SIG
> ## Junior Trader Interview

Suppose we have a \$10 bet. At any time, I can ask to double the bet. If you accept, we both put in another \$10, so the winner gets \$20. If you lose, you forfeit everything; if you reject the double, you lose the initial \$10. What is the minimum probability of winning that would make you accept the double?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1221)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be your probability of winning the game at the moment the double is offered.
>
> Initially, each player has put in $10$, so the current pot is $20$.
>
> If the double is offered:
>
> - **Rejecting** means you forfeit your initial $10$, so your payoff is
>   $$
>   -10.
>   $$
>
> - **Accepting** means each player adds another $10$, so now you have invested $20$ total.
>   - If you win, you receive the $40$ pot, for a net gain of
>     $$
>     +20.
>     $$
>   - If you lose, you lose your full investment, for a net payoff of
>     $$
>     -20.
>     $$
>
> So the expected value of accepting is
> $$
> E = 20p + (-20)(1-p).
> $$
> Simplify:
> $$
> E = 20p - 20 + 20p = 40p - 20.
> $$
>
> You should accept when this is at least as good as rejecting:
> $$
> 40p - 20 \ge -10.
> $$
> Thus
> $$
> 40p \ge 10,
> $$
> so
> $$
> p \ge \frac{1}{4}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the minimum probability at which you should accept the double is
> $$
> \boxed{25\%}.
> $$
