> [!example] Question 1155 · SIG
> ## Intern Interview

If you are playing a game and your opponent doubles the stakes, what probability of winning should you require to prefer accepting the doubled stakes over resigning from the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1156)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the current stake be $S>0$.
>
> If you **resign**, your payoff is the same as losing the current game, namely
>
> $$
> -S.
> $$
>
> If instead you **accept the doubled stakes**, then the new stake becomes $2S$.
>
> - If you win, your payoff is $+2S$.
> - If you lose, your payoff is $-2S$.
>
> Let $p$ be your probability of winning after accepting the doubled stakes. Then your expected payoff is
>
> $$
> E = p(2S) + (1-p)(-2S).
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> E = 2Sp - 2S + 2Sp = 2S(2p-1).
> $$
>
> You should be willing to accept the double when this expected payoff is at least as good as resigning:
>
> $$
> 2S(2p-1) \ge -S.
> $$
>
> Divide by $S$:
>
> $$
> 2(2p-1) \ge -1.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> 4p - 2 \ge -1,
> $$
>
> $$
> 4p \ge 1,
> $$
>
> $$
> p \ge \frac14.
> $$
>
> Therefore, you should accept the doubled stakes whenever your chance of winning is at least
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac14 \text{ or } 25\%}.
> $$
>
> At exactly $25\%$, you are indifferent; above $25\%$, accepting is better than resigning.
