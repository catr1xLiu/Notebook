> [!example] Question 1199 · SIG
> ## Quant Trader Intern Interview

What is the expected number of fair coin flips required to get two consecutive heads (i.e., 'HH') for the first time?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1200)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of flips needed to obtain the pattern $HH$ for the first time.
>
> A direct way to solve this is to use states based on how much of the target pattern has already been matched.
>
> ## Step 1: Define the states
>
> Let
>
> - $E_0$ = expected number of additional flips starting from scratch,
> - $E_1$ = expected number of additional flips given that the last flip was $H$.
>
> We want $E_0$.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up equations
>
> ### From state $E_0$
>
> From scratch, we flip once.
>
> - With probability $\frac{1}{2}$, we get $H$, moving to state $E_1$.
> - With probability $\frac{1}{2}$, we get $T$, and we are still in state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
>
> ### From state $E_1$
>
> Here the previous flip was $H$, so one more $H$ finishes the pattern.
>
> - With probability $\frac{1}{2}$, the next flip is $H$, and we are done.
> - With probability $\frac{1}{2}$, the next flip is $T$, which breaks the run and returns us to state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 0 + \frac{1}{2}E_0
> = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> From
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0,
> $$
> subtract $\frac{1}{2}E_0$ from both sides:
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}E_0 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_1,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + E_1.
> $$
>
> Now substitute $E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0$:
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + 1 + \frac{1}{2}E_0
> = 3 + \frac{1}{2}E_0.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> \frac{1}{2}E_0 = 3,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E_0 = 6.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The expected number of coin flips before the first occurrence of $HH$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6}.
> $$
