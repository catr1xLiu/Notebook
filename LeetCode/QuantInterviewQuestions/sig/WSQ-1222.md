> [!example] Question 1222 · SIG
> ## Junior Trader Interview

You have two torpedoes to hit a boat. Each torpedo has a 2/3 chance of hitting the boat. If you launch both torpedoes, what is the probability that the boat is hit?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1223)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $A$ be the event that the first torpedo hits, and $B$ the event that the second torpedo hits.  
> Each torpedo hits with probability
>
> $$
> P(A)=P(B)=\frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Assuming the two torpedo outcomes are independent, the boat is hit if **at least one** torpedo hits. Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{boat hit}) = 1 - P(\text{both miss}).
> $$
>
> Since each torpedo misses with probability
>
> $$
> 1-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{1}{3},
> $$
>
> the probability both miss is
>
> $$
> P(\text{both miss})=\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{9}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{boat hit})=1-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{8}{9}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that the boat is hit is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{8}{9}}.
> $$
