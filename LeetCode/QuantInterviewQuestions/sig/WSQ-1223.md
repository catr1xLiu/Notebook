> [!example] Question 1223 · SIG
> ## Junior Trader Interview

You can roll a fair six-sided die a maximum of two times. After each roll, you can choose to accept the current roll's value or roll again (if it is your first roll). You must accept the value on your last roll. What is the expected value of this strategy?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1224)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the strategy be: roll once, observe the result, and then decide whether to keep it or roll a second time. Since the second roll must be accepted, the decision on the first roll should compare the current value to the expected value of a fresh roll.
>
> A fair six-sided die has expected value
>
> $$
> E[\text{die}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> Thus:
>
> - if the first roll is greater than $3.5$, it is better to keep it;
> - if the first roll is less than $3.5$, it is better to reroll;
> - since no face equals $3.5$, this means keep $4,5,6$ and reroll $1,2,3$.
>
> Now compute the expected value under this optimal strategy.
>
> If the first roll is:
>
> - $1,2,3$ (probability $\frac{3}{6}$), reroll and get expected value $3.5$;
> - $4,5,6$ (each with probability $\frac{1}{6}$), keep that value.
>
> So
>
> $$
> E = \frac{1}{6}(3.5+3.5+3.5+4+5+6).
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> E = \frac{10.5+15}{6} = \frac{25.5}{6} = 4.25.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{4.25 = \frac{17}{4}}.
> $$
>
> The optimal rule is: **reroll on $1,2,3$; keep on $4,5,6$**.
