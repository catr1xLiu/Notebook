> [!example] Question 1242 · SIG
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

A fair coin is tossed repeatedly until either the sequence HHT or HTH occurs. What is the expected number of tosses required?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1243)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $E$ denote the expected number of tosses until one of the patterns $HHT$ or $HTH$ first appears.
>
> To compute $E$, use states based on the longest relevant suffix of the current toss sequence.
>
> ## Step 1: Define states
>
> Let
>
> - $E_0$: expected remaining tosses starting from no useful suffix,
> - $E_H$: expected remaining tosses given the current suffix is $H$,
> - $E_{HH}$: expected remaining tosses given the current suffix is $HH$,
> - $E_{HT}$: expected remaining tosses given the current suffix is $HT$.
>
> The process ends as soon as either $HHT$ or $HTH$ appears.
>
> ## Step 2: Set up equations
>
> ### State $E_0$
>
> From the start:
>
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $H$ and move to state $E_H$,
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $T$ and remain in state $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_H + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### State $E_H$
>
> From suffix $H$:
>
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $H$ and move to state $E_{HH}$,
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $T$; the suffix becomes irrelevant, so return to $E_0$.
>
> Hence
> $$
> E_H = 1 + \frac12 E_{HH} + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> ### State $E_{HH}$
>
> From suffix $HH$:
>
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $T$ and obtain $HHT$, so stop,
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $H$ and the suffix remains $HH$.
>
> Therefore
> $$
> E_{HH} = 1 + \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 E_{HH}.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> \frac12 E_{HH} = 1
> \quad\Rightarrow\quad
> E_{HH} = 2.
> $$
>
> ### State $E_{HT}$
>
> From suffix $HT$:
>
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $H$ and obtain $HTH$, so stop,
> - with probability $\frac12$, toss $T$; then the suffix is no longer useful, so return to $E_0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_{HT} = 1 + \frac12 \cdot 0 + \frac12 E_0
> = 1 + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> However, $E_{HT}$ is not actually needed if we model the transition from $E_H$ correctly: after $H$ then $T$, the suffix is $HT$, not $E_0$. So revise the equation for $E_H$:
>
> $$
> E_H = 1 + \frac12 E_{HH} + \frac12 E_{HT}.
> $$
>
> Now substitute the known formulas.
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the system
>
> We have
> $$
> E_{HH} = 2,
> $$
> $$
> E_{HT} = 1 + \frac12 E_0,
> $$
> and
> $$
> E_H = 1 + \frac12 E_{HH} + \frac12 E_{HT}.
> $$
>
> Substitute:
> $$
> E_H = 1 + \frac12(2) + \frac12\left(1 + \frac12 E_0\right)
> = 1 + 1 + \frac12 + \frac14 E_0
> = \frac52 + \frac14 E_0.
> $$
>
> From $E_0$:
> $$
> E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_H + \frac12 E_0.
> $$
>
> So
> $$
> \frac12 E_0 = 1 + \frac12 E_H,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + E_H.
> $$
>
> Substitute $E_H$:
> $$
> E_0 = 2 + \frac52 + \frac14 E_0
> = \frac92 + \frac14 E_0.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> E_0 - \frac14 E_0 = \frac92,
> $$
> $$
> \frac34 E_0 = \frac92,
> $$
> $$
> E_0 = \frac92 \cdot \frac43 = 6.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of tosses until either $HHT$ or $HTH$ occurs is
>
> $$
> \boxed{6}.
> $$
