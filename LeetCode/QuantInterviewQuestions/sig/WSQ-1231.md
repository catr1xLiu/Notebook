> [!example] Question 1231 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Two points are uniformly distributed around the circumference of a unit circle. What is the expected value of the distance between them?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1232)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two random points on the unit circle correspond to angles $\Theta_1$ and $\Theta_2$, each independently uniform on $[0,2\pi)$.
>
> We seek the expected chord length between these two points.
>
> ## 1. Express the distance in terms of the angle difference
>
> By rotational symmetry, the distance depends only on the angular difference
> $$
> \Delta = |\Theta_1-\Theta_2| \pmod{2\pi}.
> $$
> The chord length between two points on the unit circle subtending angle $\Delta$ is
> $$
> D = 2\sin\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right),
> $$
> where we may take $\Delta \in [0,\pi]$ as the smaller central angle.
>
> Since the points are independent and uniform, this smaller angle $\Delta$ is itself uniformly distributed on $[0,\pi]$.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> E[D] = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi 2\sin\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\,d\theta.
> $$
>
> ## 2. Evaluate the integral
>
> Compute:
> $$
> E[D] = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\,d\theta.
> $$
> Let
> $$
> u=\frac{\theta}{2}, \qquad d\theta=2\,du.
> $$
> Then as $\theta$ goes from $0$ to $\pi$, $u$ goes from $0$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}$. Hence
> $$
> E[D]
> = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin(u)\cdot 2\,du
> = \frac{4}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin(u)\,du.
> $$
> Now
> $$
> \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin(u)\,du
> = \left[-\cos(u)\right]_0^{\pi/2}
> = 1.
> $$
> Therefore,
> $$
> E[D] = \frac{4}{\pi}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{\pi}}
> $$
