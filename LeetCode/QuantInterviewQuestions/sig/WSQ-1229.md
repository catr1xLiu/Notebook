> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

1. A painted 3x3 cube consists of 27 small cubes. If one small cube is selected at random and thrown, and upon landing only its unpainted sides are visible, what is the probability that this cube is completely unpainted? 2. Eight people are sitting around a table. If three are selected randomly, what is the probability that at least two of the selected people were sitting next to each other?

> ## 1. Painted $3\times 3\times 3$ cube
>
> A large cube is formed from $27$ small cubes and its outer surface is painted. One small cube is selected at random and tossed. When it lands, only unpainted faces are visible. Find the probability that the chosen small cube is completely unpainted.
>
> ### Step 1: Classify the small cubes
>
> In a painted $3\times 3\times 3$ cube:
>
> - Corner cubes: $8$, each with $3$ painted faces.
> - Edge-center cubes: $12$, each with $2$ painted faces.
> - Face-center cubes: $6$, each with $1$ painted face.
> - Interior cube: $1$, with $0$ painted faces.
>
> Thus the only completely unpainted cube is the unique interior cube.
>
> ### Step 2: Interpret the observation
>
> After tossing the selected cube, the visible faces are exactly the faces not in contact with the table. Since only unpainted faces are seen, all visible faces must be unpainted.
>
> For a cube with $k$ painted faces, this is possible only if every painted face is on the bottom or hidden, but in a landing position only one face is hidden on the bottom, while the other $5$ faces are visible. Therefore all visible faces must be unpainted, so the cube can have at most one painted face, and that painted face must be on the bottom.
>
> So the observation is possible for:
>
> - the interior cube: $0$ painted faces,
> - a face-center cube: $1$ painted face.
>
> It is impossible for cubes with $2$ or $3$ painted faces.
>
> ### Step 3: Compute the conditional probability
>
> Let $A$ be the event that the selected cube is completely unpainted.
>
> Let $B$ be the event that, after tossing, only unpainted faces are visible.
>
> We need $P(A\mid B)$.
>
> #### Probability of $B$ for each type
>
> - Interior cube: always satisfies $B$, so probability $1$.
> - Face-center cube: satisfies $B$ only if its unique painted face lands on the bottom. Since each face is equally likely to be on the bottom, this probability is $\frac{1}{6}$.
> - Edge-center and corner cubes: probability $0$.
>
> Thus
> $$
> P(B)=\frac{1}{27}\cdot 1+\frac{6}{27}\cdot \frac{1}{6}
> =\frac{1}{27}+\frac{1}{27}
> =\frac{2}{27}.
> $$
>
> Also,
> $$
> P(A\cap B)=\frac{1}{27}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{27}.
> $$
>
> Hence
> $$
> P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
> =\frac{\frac{1}{27}}{\frac{2}{27}}
> =\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> ### Answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
> $$
>

> ## 2. Three of $8$ people around a table
>
> Eight people sit around a round table. Three are selected at random. Find the probability that at least two of the three were sitting next to each other.
>
> ### Step 1: Count all possible selections
>
> The number of ways to choose $3$ people from $8$ is
> $$
> \binom{8}{3}=56.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Use the complement
>
> It is easier to count selections in which no two selected people are adjacent.
>
> For $8$ people arranged on a circle, the number of ways to choose $3$ people with no two adjacent is
> $$
> \frac{8}{8-3}\binom{8-3}{3}
> =\frac{8}{5}\binom{5}{3}
> =\frac{8}{5}\cdot 10
> =16.
> $$
>
> So the number of selections with at least one adjacent pair is
> $$
> 56-16=40.
> $$
>
> ### Step 3: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(\text{at least two adjacent})=\frac{40}{56}=\frac{5}{7}.
> $$
>
> ### Answer
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{5}{7}}
> $$
