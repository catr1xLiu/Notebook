> [!example] Question 1225 · SIG
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the average distance between two random points on the circumference of a unit circle?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1226)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let two points be chosen independently and uniformly at random on a circle of radius $R$. The goal is to find the expected straight-line distance between them.
>
> ## 1. Express the distance in terms of the central angle
>
> Fix one point on the circle. Let $\Theta$ be the central angle between the two points. By symmetry, $\Theta$ is uniformly distributed on $[0,2\pi]$.
>
> If $D$ denotes the chord length between the two points, then from elementary geometry,
>
> $$
> D = 2R \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)
> $$
>
> Since $\sin(\Theta/2)\ge 0$ for $\Theta\in[0,2\pi]$, no absolute value is needed.
>
> ## 2. Compute the expected value
>
> Because $\Theta \sim \text{Uniform}(0,2\pi)$,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} 2R\sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\, d\Theta
> $$
>
> Factor out constants:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{R}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\Theta}{2}\right)\, d\Theta
> $$
>
> Let
>
> $$
> u = \frac{\Theta}{2}, \qquad d\Theta = 2\,du
> $$
>
> When $\Theta=0$, $u=0$; when $\Theta=2\pi$, $u=\pi$. Thus,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D]
> = \frac{R}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} 2\sin u\, du
> = \frac{2R}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\sin u\, du
> $$
>
> Now,
>
> $$
> \int_0^{\pi}\sin u\, du = \left[-\cos u\right]_0^{\pi} = 2
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[D] = \frac{2R}{\pi}\cdot 2 = \frac{4R}{\pi}
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The average distance between two random points on a circle of radius $R$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4R}{\pi}}
> $$
>
> For a unit circle, this becomes
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4}{\pi}}
> $$
