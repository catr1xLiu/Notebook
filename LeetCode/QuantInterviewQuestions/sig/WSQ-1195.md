> [!example] Question 1195 · SIG
> ## Trading Interview

What is the probability that a group of people sitting around a round table are seated in strictly ascending or strictly descending order of age?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1196)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let there be $n$ distinct people, all with different ages, seated uniformly at random around a round table.
>
> We seek the probability that, when read around the table in one of the two circular directions, their ages appear in strictly increasing order or strictly decreasing order.
>
> ## Step 1: Count all circular seatings
>
> For $n$ distinct people around a round table, the number of distinct circular arrangements is
>
> $$
> (n-1)!.
> $$
>
> This is because rotations are considered the same.
>
> ## Step 2: Count favorable arrangements
>
> Label the people by age as
>
> $$
> 1,2,3,\dots,n
> $$
>
> where $1$ is the youngest and $n$ is the oldest.
>
> To have ages in ascending order around the table, the circular arrangement must be
>
> $$
> 1,2,3,\dots,n
> $$
>
> up to rotation. This gives exactly one circular arrangement.
>
> To have ages in descending order around the table, the arrangement must be
>
> $$
> 1,n,n-1,\dots,2
> $$
>
> up to rotation. This is one more distinct circular arrangement.
>
> Hence the number of favorable circular arrangements is
>
> $$
> 2.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Compute the probability
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{ascending or descending by age})=\frac{2}{(n-1)!}.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{(n-1)!}}
> $$
>
> for $n \ge 3$.
>
> For completeness:
> - if $n=1$, the probability is $1$;
> - if $n=2$, the probability is also $1$, since the two people are both in increasing and decreasing order around the circle.
