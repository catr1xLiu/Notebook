> [!example] Question 1212 · SIG
> ## Quant Researcher Interview

We have three pancakes. One is burnt on both sides, one is burnt on one side, and one is not burnt at all. The pancakes are stacked on a plate so that all that can be seen is a burnt side on top. What is the probability that the fully burnt pancake is the one on top?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1213)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the three pancakes be:
>
> - $D$: burnt on both sides,
> - $S$: burnt on exactly one side,
> - $N$: not burnt on either side.
>
> They are stacked so that the only visible top face is burnt. We want
>
> $$
> P(\text{top pancake is } D \mid \text{top visible side is burnt}).
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Count possible upward-facing burnt sides
>
> A pancake can appear on top with either of its two sides facing up.
>
> - Pancake $D$ has $2$ burnt sides, so it contributes $2$ possible top appearances with a burnt face up.
> - Pancake $S$ has $1$ burnt side, so it contributes $1$ possible top appearance with a burnt face up.
> - Pancake $N$ has $0$ burnt sides, so it cannot be on top with a burnt face up.
>
> Thus, conditional on seeing a burnt top face, the possible cases are proportional to
>
> $$
> 2 : 1 : 0.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the conditional probability
>
> Among the $3$ equally likely burnt-side-up possibilities, $2$ correspond to pancake $D$ being on top. Therefore,
>
> $$
> P(\text{top pancake is } D \mid \text{burnt side visible on top})
> = \frac{2}{2+1}
> = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
> $$
