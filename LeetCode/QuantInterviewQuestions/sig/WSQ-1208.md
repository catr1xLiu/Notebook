> [!example] Question 1208 · SIG
> ## Equity Research Associate Interview

A property has a 10% chance of containing oil, making it worth \$1 million, a 30% chance of containing coal, making it worth \$500,000, and if no resources are found, it can be sold for \$200,000. What is the expected value of this property?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1209)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> The value of the property is its **expected value**, computed by weighting each possible outcome by its probability.
>
> The outcomes are:
>
> - Oil found: probability $0.10$, value $\text{\textdollar}1{,}000{,}000$
> - Coal found: probability $0.30$, value $\text{\textdollar}500{,}000$
> - No resources found: probability $1 - 0.10 - 0.30 = 0.60$, value $\text{\textdollar}200{,}000$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[\text{Value}] = 0.10(1{,}000{,}000) + 0.30(500{,}000) + 0.60(200{,}000)
> $$
>
> Compute each term:
>
> $$
> 0.10(1{,}000{,}000) = 100{,}000
> $$
>
> $$
> 0.30(500{,}000) = 150{,}000
> $$
>
> $$
> 0.60(200{,}000) = 120{,}000
> $$
>
> Adding them,
>
> $$
> E[\text{Value}] = 100{,}000 + 150{,}000 + 120{,}000 = 370{,}000
> $$
>
> Therefore, the value of the property is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\$370{,}000}
> $$
