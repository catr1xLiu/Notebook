> [!example] Question 989 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

You start a tennis game at a score of 30-30. You have a probability of 0.6 of winning each point. What is the probability that you win the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=990)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p=0.6$ be the probability of winning any given point, and let $q=0.4$ be the probability of losing a point.
>
> Starting from $30$-$30$, the possible ways to win the game are:
>
> 1. **Win the next two points immediately**  
>    This happens with probability
>    $$
>    p^2.
>    $$
>
> 2. **Reach deuce, then eventually win from deuce**  
>    From $30$-$30$, deuce is reached if each player wins one of the next two points. This can occur in two orders:
>    - win then lose,
>    - lose then win.
>
>    So the probability of reaching deuce is
>    $$
>    2pq.
>    $$
>
>    Let $D$ be the probability of eventually winning the game from deuce. From deuce:
>    - you can win two straight points: probability $p^2$,
>    - lose two straight points: probability $q^2$,
>    - split the next two points and return to deuce: probability $2pq$.
>
>    Hence
>    $$
>    D = p^2 + (2pq)D.
>    $$
>    Solving,
>    $$
>    D(1-2pq)=p^2,
>    $$
>    so
>    $$
>    D=\frac{p^2}{1-2pq}.
>    $$
>
> Now substitute $p=0.6$ and $q=0.4$:
>
> $$
> p^2 = 0.36, \qquad 2pq = 2(0.6)(0.4)=0.48.
> $$
>
> Thus
> $$
> D=\frac{0.36}{1-0.48}=\frac{0.36}{0.52}=\frac{9}{13}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of winning the game from $30$-$30$ is
> $$
> p^2 + (2pq)D
> = 0.36 + 0.48\cdot \frac{9}{13}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> 0.36=\frac{9}{25}, \qquad 0.48=\frac{12}{25},
> $$
> so
> $$
> \frac{9}{25}+\frac{12}{25}\cdot\frac{9}{13}
> = \frac{9}{25}+\frac{108}{325}
> = \frac{117}{325}+\frac{108}{325}
> = \frac{225}{325}
> = \frac{9}{13}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of winning the game is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{9}{13}\approx 0.6923}.
> $$
