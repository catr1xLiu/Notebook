> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Can you derive the formula for ordinary linear regression?

> # Ordinary Least Squares for Simple Linear Regression
>
> Consider data points $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$.  
> The simple linear regression model assumes that $y$ is approximated by a line
>
> $$
> \hat y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i,
> $$
>
> where $\beta_0$ is the intercept and $\beta_1$ is the slope.
>
> The ordinary least squares method chooses $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ to minimize the sum of squared residuals:
>
> $$
> S(\beta_0,\beta_1) = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right)^2.
> $$
>
> ## Step 1: Set up the minimization problem
>
> We seek
>
> $$
> \min_{\beta_0,\beta_1} \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right)^2.
> $$
>
> Because $S(\beta_0,\beta_1)$ is a quadratic function of $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$, its minimum occurs where the partial derivatives vanish.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the partial derivatives
>
> Differentiate with respect to $\beta_0$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial S}{\partial \beta_0}
> =
> -2 \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right).
> $$
>
> Set this equal to zero:
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right) = 0.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n y_i - n\beta_0 - \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 0.
> $$
>
> So the first normal equation is
>
> $$
> n\beta_0 + \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = \sum_{i=1}^n y_i.
> $$
>
> Now differentiate with respect to $\beta_1$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial S}{\partial \beta_1}
> =
> -2 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right).
> $$
>
> Set this equal to zero:
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \left(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i\right) = 0.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i - \beta_0 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i - \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 = 0,
> $$
>
> so the second normal equation is
>
> $$
> \beta_0 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i + \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Solve the normal equations
>
> Let
>
> $$
> \bar x = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i,
> \qquad
> \bar y = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n y_i.
> $$
>
> From the first normal equation,
>
> $$
> n\beta_0 + \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = \sum_{i=1}^n y_i,
> $$
>
> divide by $n$:
>
> $$
> \beta_0 + \beta_1 \bar x = \bar y.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \beta_0 = \bar y - \beta_1 \bar x.
> $$
>
> Substitute this into the second normal equation:
>
> $$
> (\bar y - \beta_1 \bar x)\sum_{i=1}^n x_i + \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2
> =
> \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i.
> $$
>
> Since $\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = n\bar x$, this becomes
>
> $$
> n\bar x \bar y - n\beta_1 \bar x^2 + \beta_1 \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2
> =
> \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i.
> $$
>
> Rearranging,
>
> $$
> \beta_1 \left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - n\bar x^2\right)
> =
> \sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i - n\bar x \bar y.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \beta_1
> =
> \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i - n\bar x \bar y}
> {\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - n\bar x^2}.
> $$
>
> Using centered variables, this is more commonly written as
>
> $$
> \beta_1
> =
> \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}
> {\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\bar x)^2}.
> $$
>
> Then the intercept is
>
> $$
> \beta_0 = \bar y - \beta_1 \bar x.
> $$
>
> ## Final formula
>
> The ordinary least squares estimates for simple linear regression are
>
> $$
> \hat\beta_1
> =
> \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}
> {\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\bar x)^2},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1 \bar x.
> $$
>
> Therefore the fitted regression line is
>
> $$
> \hat y = \hat\beta_0 + \hat\beta_1 x.
> $$
>
> ## Matrix form for general linear regression
>
> For the multiple linear regression model,
>
> $$
> y = X\beta + \varepsilon,
> $$
>
> where $X$ is the design matrix, ordinary least squares minimizes
>
> $$
> \|y - X\beta\|^2 = (y-X\beta)^\top (y-X\beta).
> $$
>
> Differentiate with respect to $\beta$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial}{\partial \beta}(y-X\beta)^\top (y-X\beta)
> =
> -2X^\top y + 2X^\top X\beta.
> $$
>
> Setting this equal to zero gives the normal equations:
>
> $$
> X^\top X \beta = X^\top y.
> $$
>
> If $X^\top X$ is invertible, then
>
> $$
> \hat\beta = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y.
> $$
>
> This is the ordinary least squares formula in general form.
