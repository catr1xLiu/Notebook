> [!example] Question 947 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

In a perfectly efficient market, there are two companies: A with a success probability of 2/3 and B with a success probability of 1/2. Both companies are initially worth \$50 each. If both companies either succeed or fail, nothing special happens. However, if one company succeeds and the other does not, then the company that does not succeed goes bankrupt. How would the price of each company change if both succeed?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=948)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the initial values of the two companies be
>
> $$
> V_A = 50, \qquad V_B = 50.
> $$
>
> Their success probabilities are
>
> $$
> p_A = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad p_B = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Assume the companies’ outcomes are independent.
>
> The rule is:
>
> - if both succeed, nothing special happens;
> - if both fail, nothing special happens;
> - if exactly one succeeds, the unsuccessful company becomes worthless.
>
> In a perfectly efficient market, today’s price equals expected future value.
>
> ## 1. Future value states
>
> There are four possible outcomes:
>
> 1. **Both succeed**:
>    $$
>    (A,B) = (50,50)
>    $$
>
> 2. **Both fail**:
>    $$
>    (A,B) = (50,50)
>    $$
>
> 3. **$A$ succeeds, $B$ fails**:
>    then $B$ goes bankrupt:
>    $$
>    (A,B) = (50,0)
>    $$
>
> 4. **$A$ fails, $B$ succeeds**:
>    then $A$ goes bankrupt:
>    $$
>    (A,B) = (0,50)
>    $$
>
> ## 2. Compute current efficient prices
>
> ### Company $A$
>
> $A$ is worth $50$ unless it fails while $B$ succeeds. Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(A \text{ bankrupt}) = \mathbb{P}(A \text{ fails},\, B \text{ succeeds})
> = \left(1-\frac{2}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
> = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}
> = \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(A \text{ survives}) = 1-\frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}.
> $$
>
> So the efficient price of $A$ today is
>
> $$
> P_A = 50 \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{250}{6} = \frac{125}{3}.
> $$
>
> ### Company $B$
>
> $B$ is worth $50$ unless it fails while $A$ succeeds. Thus
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(B \text{ bankrupt}) = \mathbb{P}(B \text{ fails},\, A \text{ succeeds})
> = \left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)
> = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}
> = \frac{1}{3}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(B \text{ survives}) = 1-\frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}.
> $$
>
> So the efficient price of $B$ today is
>
> $$
> P_B = 50 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{100}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Prices conditional on both succeeding
>
> If it is now known that both companies succeed, then neither goes bankrupt and nothing special happens. Therefore each company is worth its full value:
>
> $$
> P_A^{(\text{both succeed})} = 50, \qquad P_B^{(\text{both succeed})} = 50.
> $$
>
> So the price changes are:
>
> ### For company $A$
>
> $$
> \Delta P_A = 50 - \frac{125}{3} = \frac{25}{3}.
> $$
>
> ### For company $B$
>
> $$
> \Delta P_B = 50 - \frac{100}{3} = \frac{50}{3}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> If both companies succeed, their prices become
>
> $$
> A: 50, \qquad B: 50.
> $$
>
> Relative to their efficient prices before the outcome is known, the increases are
>
> $$
> A: \frac{25}{3} \approx 8.33,
> \qquad
> B: \frac{50}{3} \approx 16.67.
> $$
>
> So after both succeed, company $A$ rises from $\frac{125}{3}$ to $50$, and company $B$ rises from $\frac{100}{3}$ to $50$.
