> [!example] Question 898 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

What is the total number of ways to arrange 3 red balls, 7 blue balls, and 9 green balls in a line?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=899)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Treat balls of the same color as identical. Then an arrangement is determined only by the sequence of colors in the $3+7+9=19$ positions.
>
> The number of distinct arrangements of this multiset is the multinomial count
>
> $$
> \frac{19!}{3!\,7!\,9!}.
> $$
>
> This can be computed by first choosing the $3$ positions for the red balls among the $19$ slots, then choosing $7$ of the remaining $16$ positions for the blue balls (the last $9$ positions are then forced to hold the green balls):
>
> $$
> \binom{19}{3}\binom{16}{7}.
> $$
>
> Compute each factor:
>
> $$
> \binom{19}{3} = \frac{19\cdot 18\cdot 17}{3\cdot 2\cdot 1} = 969,
> $$
>
> $$
> \binom{16}{7} = 11440.
> $$
>
> Multiplying,
>
> $$
> \frac{19!}{3!\,7!\,9!}
> = \binom{19}{3}\binom{16}{7}
> = 969 \times 11440
> = 11085360.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the total number of arrangements is
>
> $$
> \boxed{11085360}.
> $$
