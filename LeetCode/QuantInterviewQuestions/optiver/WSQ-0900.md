> [!example] Question 900 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

If you flip 100 fair coins, what is the variance of the number of Heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=901)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained when $100$ fair coins are flipped independently.
>
> Then
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n=100, p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable, the variance is
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p).
> $$
>
> Substituting $n=100$ and $p=\tfrac12$ gives
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X)=100\cdot \frac12 \cdot \frac12 = 25.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the variance of the number of heads is
> $$
> \boxed{25}.
> $$
