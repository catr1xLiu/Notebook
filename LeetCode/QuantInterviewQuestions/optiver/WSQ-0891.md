> [!example] Question 891 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the probability that, given a random number from 1 through 1,000,000, the digit 7 appears at least once in the selected number?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=892)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the integers from $1$ through $1{,}000{,}000$, all equally likely.
>
> The easiest way is to count the complement: numbers that **do not** contain the digit $7$ anywhere.
>
> ## Step 1: Count numbers from $0$ to $999{,}999$ with no digit $7$
>
> Write each number in this range as a $6$-digit string, allowing leading zeros.
>
> Each digit can be any of the $9$ choices
> $$
> \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9\},
> $$
> so the total number with no $7$ is
> $$
> 9^6 = 531{,}441.
> $$
>
> Thus, among the $1{,}000{,}000$ integers from $0$ to $999{,}999$, the number containing at least one $7$ is
> $$
> 1{,}000{,}000 - 531{,}441 = 468{,}559.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Adjust for the range $1$ to $1{,}000{,}000$
>
> The original problem uses $1$ through $1{,}000{,}000$.
>
> - The number $0$ is excluded, and it does **not** contain a $7$.
> - The number $1{,}000{,}000$ is included, and it does **not** contain a $7$.
>
> These two changes cancel each other, so the count of favorable numbers remains
> $$
> 468{,}559.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
> $$
> \frac{468{,}559}{1{,}000{,}000}.
> $$
>
> In decimal form,
> $$
> \frac{468{,}559}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.468559.
> $$
>
> ## Final Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{468{,}559}{1{,}000{,}000} \approx 0.468559}
> $$
>
> So the probability is about $46.8559\%$.
