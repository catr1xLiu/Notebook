> [!example] Question 987 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

Given \$100 to bet and the probabilities of each of 4 teams winning a bracket, how would you allocate your bet to maximize your expected return?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=988)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the four teams be $A,B,C,D$, with win probabilities for the tournament
>
> $$
> p_A,\; p_B,\; p_C,\; p_D,
> $$
>
> where
>
> $$
> p_A+p_B+p_C+p_D=1.
> $$
>
> Suppose you can place bets on which team wins the bracket, and let the betting odds imply decimal payouts
>
> $$
> o_A,\; o_B,\; o_C,\; o_D.
> $$
>
> If you bet amounts
>
> $$
> x_A,\; x_B,\; x_C,\; x_D
> $$
>
> on the four teams, then the budget constraint is
>
> $$
> x_A+x_B+x_C+x_D=100,\qquad x_i\ge 0.
> $$
>
> ## 1. Expected-value formulation
>
> If team $i$ wins, the total return is
>
> $$
> R_i=o_i x_i.
> $$
>
> Hence the expected return is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[R]
> = p_A o_A x_A + p_B o_B x_B + p_C o_C x_C + p_D o_D x_D.
> $$
>
> So the problem is
>
> $$
> \max \; p_A o_A x_A + p_B o_B x_B + p_C o_C x_C + p_D o_D x_D
> $$
>
> subject to
>
> $$
> x_A+x_B+x_C+x_D=100,\qquad x_i\ge 0.
> $$
>
> Because this objective is linear, the maximizing strategy is simple:
>
> - compute each team’s expected-value multiplier
>   $$
>   m_i=p_i o_i;
>   $$
> - bet all $100$ on the team with the largest $m_i$.
>
> Thus, if
>
> $$
> m_k=\max\{p_Ao_A,p_Bo_B,p_Co_C,p_Do_D\},
> $$
>
> then the expected-value optimal allocation is
>
> $$
> x_k=100,\qquad x_i=0\;\; (i\ne k).
> $$
>
> ## 2. Interpretation
>
> - If $p_i o_i>1$, then betting on team $i$ has positive expected profit per dollar.
> - If all $p_i o_i<1$, then every bet has negative expected value, and under pure expected-value reasoning you would either:
>   - still place all $100$ on the least bad option if forced to bet, or
>   - place no bet if abstaining is allowed.
>
> ## 3. If the goal is to guarantee the same payout regardless of winner
>
> Sometimes “how should I bet?” means choosing $x_i$ so that the return is the same no matter which team wins. Then require
>
> $$
> o_Ax_A=o_Bx_B=o_Cx_C=o_Dx_D=C
> $$
>
> for some constant $C$. Hence
>
> $$
> x_i=\frac{C}{o_i}.
> $$
>
> Using the budget constraint,
>
> $$
> \sum_{i}\frac{C}{o_i}=100,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> C=\frac{100}{\frac1{o_A}+\frac1{o_B}+\frac1{o_C}+\frac1{o_D}}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the equal-payout hedge is
>
> $$
> x_i=\frac{100/o_i}{\frac1{o_A}+\frac1{o_B}+\frac1{o_C}+\frac1{o_D}}.
> $$
>
> This does not use the probabilities directly; it is a risk-hedging allocation rather than an expected-value-maximizing one.
>
> ## 4. If only probabilities are given
>
> If only the probabilities
>
> $$
> p_A,p_B,p_C,p_D
> $$
>
> are given, but not the odds, then there is no unique betting solution. The correct bet depends on the payouts. Probabilities alone tell which team is most likely to win, but not which bet has the highest expected return.
>
> - If all bets paid the same odds, then you would bet all $100$ on the team with largest probability.
> - If the odds differ, then you must compare $p_i o_i$.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> With probabilities $p_i$ and decimal odds $o_i$, let
>
> $$
> m_i=p_i o_i.
> $$
>
> To maximize expected return with a $100$ budget, bet
>
> $$
> 100
> $$
>
> on the team with the largest value of $m_i$, and
>
> $$
> 0
> $$
>
> on the others.
>
> If instead you want the same payout regardless of which team wins, bet
>
> $$
> x_i=\frac{100/o_i}{\frac1{o_A}+\frac1{o_B}+\frac1{o_C}+\frac1{o_D}}.
> $$
>
> If you provide the actual team probabilities and the odds offered on each team, the exact dollar amounts can be computed immediately.
