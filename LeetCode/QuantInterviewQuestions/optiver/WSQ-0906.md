> [!example] Question 906 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

A group of people wants to determine their average salary on the condition that no individual can find out anyone else's salary. Can they accomplish this, and if so, how?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=907)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Yes. They can compute the average salary without revealing any individual salary by using a **secure summation protocol** based on random masking.
>
> ### Idea
>
> Suppose there are $n$ people, with salaries
>
> $$
> s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n.
> $$
>
> They want to learn only the average
>
> $$
> \frac{s_1+s_2+\cdots+s_n}{n},
> $$
>
> while preventing any participant from determining another individual’s salary.
>
> ### Protocol
>
> Arrange the people in a circle. Person $1$ begins.
>
> 1. Person $1$ chooses a secret random number $r$ and computes
>
>    $$
>    t_1 = s_1 + r.
>    $$
>
>    Person $1$ sends $t_1$ to Person $2$.
>
> 2. For each $k=2,3,\dots,n$, Person $k$ adds their salary to the received number:
>
>    $$
>    t_k = t_{k-1} + s_k,
>    $$
>
>    and sends $t_k$ to the next person.
>
> 3. After Person $n$ adds their salary, the value
>
>    $$
>    t_n = r + s_1+s_2+\cdots+s_n
>    $$
>
>    is sent back to Person $1$.
>
> 4. Person $1$ subtracts the random number $r$, obtaining
>
>    $$
>    s_1+s_2+\cdots+s_n.
>    $$
>
> 5. Person $1$ announces the total sum, and everyone divides by $n$ to get the average.
>
> ### Why this preserves privacy
>
> - No one except Person $1$ knows $r$.
> - Each intermediate value is masked by the unknown random number $r$.
> - Hence the transmitted partial sums do not reveal the individual salaries.
> - Person $1$ learns only the final total, not the separate salaries of the others.
> - The others do not learn Person $1$’s salary, because it is hidden by the random value $r$.
>
> Thus the group can determine the average salary without exposing any individual salary.
>
> ### Conclusion
>
> They **can** accomplish this. A random offset added by the first participant allows the group to compute the total salary securely; dividing by the number of people gives the average.
