> [!example] Question 968 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the expected number of card draws before encountering an Ace in a standard 52-card deck, assuming cards are drawn one at a time without replacement?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=969)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume a standard deck of $52$ cards, containing $4$ aces, and cards are drawn **without replacement** until the first ace appears.
>
> Let $X$ be the draw on which the first ace occurs. We seek $E[X]$.
>
> ## Method: symmetry of ace positions
>
> In a random ordering of the deck, the $4$ aces occupy $4$ distinct positions among $1,2,\dots,52$, all choices equally likely.
>
> Thus $X$ is the **minimum** of these $4$ positions. A standard symmetry result says that if $k$ positions are chosen uniformly from $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$, then the expected value of the smallest position is
>
> $$
> E[\min] = \frac{n+1}{k+1}.
> $$
>
> Here $n=52$ and $k=4$, so
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{52+1}{4+1} = \frac{53}{5} = 10.6.
> $$
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected number of draws before encountering the first ace is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{53}{5} = 10.6}.
> $$
>
> If “before encountering an ace” is interpreted as the number of non-ace cards drawn **prior** to the ace, then subtract $1$:
>
> $$
> E[X-1] = \frac{53}{5} - 1 = \frac{48}{5} = 9.6.
> $$
>
> So the expected draw on which the first ace appears is $10.6$, and the expected number of non-ace draws before it is $9.6$.
