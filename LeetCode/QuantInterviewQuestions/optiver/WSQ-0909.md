> [!example] Question 909 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Trader Interview

Suppose you flip 100 fair coins. What is the expected number of heads? What is the probability that exactly this many heads appear? What is the standard deviation of the number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=910)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads in $100$ flips of a fair coin. Then
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n=100,p=\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> ## Expected number of heads
>
> For a binomial random variable, the expectation is
>
> $$
> E[X]=np.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> E[X]=100\cdot \frac12=50.
> $$
>
> So the expected number of heads is
>
> $$
> \boxed{50}.
> $$
>
> ## Probability of getting exactly this many heads
>
> The probability of getting exactly $50$ heads is given by the binomial formula:
>
> $$
> P(X=50)=\binom{100}{50}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> This is approximately
>
> $$
> P(X=50)\approx 0.07959.
> $$
>
> So the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{100}{50}2^{-100}\approx 0.0796}.
> $$
>
> ## Standard deviation
>
> For a binomial random variable, the variance is
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p).
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Var}(X)=100\cdot \frac12 \cdot \frac12=25.
> $$
>
> Therefore the standard deviation is
>
> $$
> \sqrt{25}=5.
> $$
>
> So the standard deviation is
>
> $$
> \boxed{5}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[X]=50,\qquad P(X=50)=\binom{100}{50}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}\approx 0.0796,\qquad \sigma=5.}
> $$
