> [!example] Question 980 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Software Developer Interview

What is the use of atomic operations in multiprocessing?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=981)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> In multiprocessing, an **atomic operation** is used to ensure that a read-modify-write action on shared data happens as a single, indivisible step.
>
> ## Why it is needed
>
> When multiple processes access the same shared resource, their operations can interleave unpredictably. If two processes update the same variable at nearly the same time, a **race condition** can occur, producing incorrect results.
>
> For example, suppose two processes both execute:
>
> $$
> x \leftarrow x + 1
> $$
>
> If $x = 5$, both processes may read $5$, both compute $6$, and both write back $6$. The correct result should be $7$, but one update is lost.
>
> ## Use of atomic operations
>
> Atomic operations prevent this problem by guaranteeing that an operation such as:
>
> - increment,
> - decrement,
> - compare-and-swap,
> - exchange,
> - test-and-set
>
> is completed without interruption by another process.
>
> Thus they are used to:
>
> 1. **Protect shared data from race conditions**
> 2. **Implement synchronization primitives** such as locks, mutexes, semaphores, and spinlocks
> 3. **Coordinate concurrent access efficiently** without always requiring heavier locking mechanisms
> 4. **Maintain consistency of counters, flags, and state variables** shared across processes
>
> ## Example
>
> An atomic increment ensures that:
>
> $$
> x \leftarrow x + 1
> $$
>
> is treated as one indivisible action, so concurrent increments from multiple processes are all counted correctly.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The use of atomic operations in multiprocessing is to provide **safe and correct access to shared memory** by making critical updates indivisible, thereby preventing race conditions and enabling synchronization among processes.
