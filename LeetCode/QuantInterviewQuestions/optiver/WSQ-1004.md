> [!example] Question 1004 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

How many levels can you build with 52 cards (a standard deck of cards)? What is the expected value (EV) of throwing a single die, and the EV of the sum of two dice rolled once and rolled 400 times? How would you create a market based on these expected values?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1005)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## 1. How many levels can be built with $52$ cards?
>
> Interpret “levels” as a triangular house of cards in which:
>
> - Level $1$ consists of $1$ inverted-$V$ pair, using $2$ cards.
> - To support higher levels, horizontal cards are placed between adjacent pairs.
> - A structure of $n$ levels uses:
>   - $2(1+2+\cdots+n)=2\cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}=n(n+1)$ cards for the pairs,
>   - $(1+2+\cdots+(n-1))=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ horizontal cards.
>
> So the total number of cards needed for $n$ levels is
>
> $$
> n(n+1)+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
> =\frac{2n(n+1)+n(n-1)}{2}
> =\frac{3n^2+n}{2}.
> $$
>
> We need
>
> $$
> \frac{3n^2+n}{2}\le 52.
> $$
>
> Multiply by $2$:
>
> $$
> 3n^2+n\le 104.
> $$
>
> Test integers:
>
> - For $n=5$:
>
> $$
> \frac{3(25)+5}{2}=\frac{80}{2}=40\le 52.
> $$
>
> - For $n=6$:
>
> $$
> \frac{3(36)+6}{2}=\frac{114}{2}=57>52.
> $$
>
> Hence the maximum is
>
> $$
> \boxed{5\text{ levels}}.
> $$
>

>
> ## 2. Expected value of throwing one die
>
> Let $X$ be the outcome of a fair six-sided die. Then $X\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ with probability $\frac16$ each.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6}=\frac{21}{6}=3.5.
> $$
>
> So the expected value is
>
> $$
> \boxed{3.5}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Expected value of the sum of two dice after one throw
>
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be the results of two independent fair dice, and let
>
> $$
> S=X+Y.
> $$
>
> By linearity of expectation,
>
> $$
> E[S]=E[X]+E[Y]=3.5+3.5=7.
> $$
>
> Therefore, for one throw of two dice, the expected sum is
>
> $$
> \boxed{7}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Expected value of the sum of two dice after $400$ throws
>
> Suppose two dice are thrown $400$ times, and let $S_i$ be the sum on throw $i$, for $i=1,\dots,400$.
>
> Then each $S_i$ has expected value
>
> $$
> E[S_i]=7.
> $$
>
> Let the total sum over $400$ throws be
>
> $$
> T=S_1+S_2+\cdots+S_{400}.
> $$
>
> Again by linearity of expectation,
>
> $$
> E[T]=E[S_1]+\cdots+E[S_{400}]=400\cdot 7=2800.
> $$
>
> So the expected total sum after $400$ throws is
>
> $$
> \boxed{2800}.
> $$
>
> If instead one asks for the expected **average** sum per throw over $400$ throws, it remains
>
> $$
> \boxed{7}.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. How to make a market based on that
>
> A market can be created by offering bets on outcomes and setting prices near expected values, with a margin for profit.
>
> ### (a) Market on one die
>
> If a contract pays the rolled number $X$, then its fair value is
>
> $$
> E[X]=3.5.
> $$
>
> So a fair market price is
>
> $$
> \$3.50.
> $$
>
> To make money as the market maker, quote something like:
>
> - buy at $\text{\textdollar}3.40$,
> - sell at $\text{\textdollar}3.60$.
>
> The spread gives expected profit.
>
> ### (b) Market on the sum of two dice in one throw
>
> If a contract pays the sum $S$, then its fair value is
>
> $$
> E[S]=7.
> $$
>
> So a fair price is
>
> $$
> \$7.00.
> $$
>
> A market maker could quote:
>
> - bid: $\text{\textdollar}6.90$,
> - ask: $\text{\textdollar}7.10$.
>
> ### (c) Market on the total sum over $400$ throws
>
> If a contract pays the total
>
> $$
> T=\sum_{i=1}^{400} S_i,
> $$
>
> then its fair value is
>
> $$
> E[T]=2800.
> $$
>
> A market could be quoted around
>
> $$
> 2800
> $$
>
> for example:
>
> - bid: $2795$,
> - ask: $2805$.
>
> ### (d) Binary markets
>
> Instead of paying the actual sum, one may offer contracts such as:
>
> - “The sum of two dice exceeds $7$.”
> - “The total over $400$ throws exceeds $2800$.”
>
> Then the fair price is the corresponding probability times the payoff.
>
> For example, with two dice:
>
> - $P(S>7)=\frac{15}{36}=\frac{5}{12}$,
>
> so a contract paying $\text{\textdollar}1$ if $S>7$ has fair value
>
> $$
> \frac{5}{12}\approx 0.4167.
> $$
>
> A market maker might quote
>
> $$
> 0.40 \text{ bid},\quad 0.43 \text{ ask}.
> $$
>

>
> ## Final answers
>
> $$
> \boxed{5\text{ levels with }52\text{ cards}}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[\text{one die}]=3.5}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[\text{sum of two dice in one throw}]=7}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[\text{total sum of two dice over }400\text{ throws}]=2800}
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{E[\text{average sum per throw over }400\text{ throws}]=7}
> $$
>
> A fair market would price contracts at these expected values, and a profitable market maker would quote prices slightly above and below them to earn a spread.
