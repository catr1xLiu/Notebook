> [!example] Question 940 · Optiver
> ## Trading Intern Interview

What is the next number in the sequence: 1, 16, 7, 100, 13?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=941)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the sequence in two interleaved parts:
>
> - Odd-position terms (1st, 3rd, 5th, ...): $1,\ 7,\ 13,\ \dots$
> - Even-position terms (2nd, 4th, ...): $16,\ 100,\ \dots$
>
> The odd-position terms form an arithmetic sequence with common difference
>
> $$
> 7-1=6,\qquad 13-7=6,
> $$
>
> so the next odd-position term (which would be the seventh term overall) is
>
> $$
> 13+6=19.
> $$
>
> The even-position terms are perfect squares,
>
> $$
> 16=4^2,\qquad 100=10^2,
> $$
>
> and their bases $4$ and $10$ are exactly the preceding odd-position terms plus $3$:
>
> $$
> 4=1+3,\qquad 10=7+3.
> $$
>
> Since five terms of the sequence are given, the next term to find is the sixth one, which is an even-position term. Applying the same rule to the fifth term gives
>
> $$
> \left(13+3\right)^2 = 16^2 = 256.
> $$
>
> ## Answer
>
> The next number in the sequence is
>
> $$
> \boxed{256}.
> $$
>
> Continuing the sequence one further step would return to the odd-position pattern, giving a seventh term of $13+6=19$, but the term immediately following the given five is $256$.
