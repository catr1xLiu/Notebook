> [!example] Question 917 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

What is the expected value of the sum of the values of three randomly selected cards from a standard deck of 52 playing cards?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=918)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume a standard $52$-card deck, and interpret the “value” of a card by rank:
> $$
> A=1,\; 2=2,\; \dots,\; 10=10,\; J=11,\; Q=12,\; K=13.
> $$
> We want the expected sum of the values of $3$ cards drawn uniformly at random without replacement.
>
> ## Step 1: Expected value of one card
>
> Each rank appears $4$ times, so the expected value of a single card is the average of the integers from $1$ to $13$:
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{1+2+\cdots+13}{13}.
> $$
> Using the formula for an arithmetic series,
> $$
> 1+2+\cdots+13=\frac{13\cdot 14}{2}=91,
> $$
> so
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{91}{13}=7.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Use linearity of expectation
>
> Let the values of the three drawn cards be $X_1$, $X_2$, and $X_3$. Then the total value is
> $$
> S=X_1+X_2+X_3.
> $$
> By linearity of expectation,
> $$
> E[S]=E[X_1]+E[X_2]+E[X_3]=7+7+7=21.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{21}
> $$
>
> If instead “value” means a different convention, the answer changes. For example, if face cards count as $10$ and aces count as $1$, then
> $$
> E[X]=\frac{1+2+\cdots+10+10+10+10}{13}
> =\frac{55+30}{13}
> =\frac{85}{13},
> $$
> so for three cards,
> $$
> E[S]=3\cdot \frac{85}{13}=\frac{255}{13}\approx 19.62.
> $$
