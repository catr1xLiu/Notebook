> [!example] Question 892 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

You throw two dice and then may choose to reroll just one of them, and only once. What is the expected value?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=893)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the two dice show values $x$ and $y$ after the initial throw. You may either keep both dice, or reroll exactly one die once. The goal is to maximize the expected final sum.
>
> ## 1. Best decision after seeing $x$ and $y$
>
> If you keep both dice, your total is
>
> $$
> x+y.
> $$
>
> If you reroll the die showing $x$, then $y$ stays fixed and the rerolled die has expected value
>
> $$
> E[\text{new die}] = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = 3.5.
> $$
>
> So the expected total from rerolling $x$ is
>
> $$
> y+3.5.
> $$
>
> Similarly, rerolling $y$ gives expected total
>
> $$
> x+3.5.
> $$
>
> Therefore, after observing $(x,y)$, the optimal expected value is
>
> $$
> \max\{x+y,\; x+3.5,\; y+3.5\}.
> $$
>
> This can be simplified. Let
>
> $$
> m=\min(x,y), \qquad M=\max(x,y).
> $$
>
> Then the three options are
>
> $$
> m+M,\quad M+3.5,\quad m+3.5.
> $$
>
> Since $M \ge m$, rerolling the smaller die is always at least as good as rerolling the larger die. Hence the optimal value is
>
> $$
> \max\{m+M,\; M+3.5\} = M+\max\{m,3.5\}.
> $$
>
> Thus the strategy is:
>
> - if the smaller die is $1,2,$ or $3$, reroll it;
> - if the smaller die is $4,5,$ or $6$, keep both dice.
>
> ## 2. Compute the overall expected value
>
> We now compute
>
> $$
> E\bigl[M+\max(m,3.5)\bigr].
> $$
>
> A convenient way is to average over all $36$ equally likely ordered outcomes.
>
> ### Case 1: the smaller die is at most $3$
>
> If $m \le 3$, optimal value is
>
> $$
> M+3.5.
> $$
>
> List by the maximum value $M$.
>
> - $M=1$: only $(1,1)$, value $1+3.5=4.5$
> - $M=2$: outcomes $(1,2),(2,1),(2,2)$, each value $2+3.5=5.5$
> - $M=3$: outcomes $(1,3),(3,1),(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)$, each value $3+3.5=6.5$
> - $M=4$: outcomes with at least one $4$ and the other in $\{1,2,3\}$: $6$ outcomes, each value $4+3.5=7.5$
> - $M=5$: similarly $6$ outcomes, each value $5+3.5=8.5$
> - $M=6$: similarly $6$ outcomes, each value $6+3.5=9.5$
>
> The total contribution is
>
> $$
> 1(4.5)+3(5.5)+5(6.5)+6(7.5)+6(8.5)+6(9.5).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> 4.5+16.5+32.5+45+51+57=206.5.
> $$
>
> ### Case 2: the smaller die is at least $4$
>
> Then both dice are in $\{4,5,6\}$, and we keep both dice. The $9$ outcomes are:
>
> - $(4,4)$: value $8$
> - $(4,5),(5,4)$: value $9$
> - $(4,6),(6,4)$: value $10$
> - $(5,5)$: value $10$
> - $(5,6),(6,5)$: value $11$
> - $(6,6)$: value $12$
>
> Total contribution:
>
> $$
> 8+9+9+10+10+10+11+11+12 = 90.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final expectation
>
> Adding both cases, the sum over all $36$ outcomes is
>
> $$
> 206.5+90=296.5.
> $$
>
> Therefore the optimal expected value is
>
> $$
> \frac{296.5}{36}=\frac{593}{72}\approx 8.2361.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The maximum expected final sum is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{593}{72} \approx 8.2361}.
> $$
>
> The optimal strategy is to reroll the lower die if it is $1$, $2$, or $3$, and otherwise keep both dice.
