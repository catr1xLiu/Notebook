> [!example] Question 896 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Roll a die repeatedly until you obtain either a 5 or a 6. If you roll a 5, you cash out the sum of all previous rolls, excluding the 5. If you roll a 6, you receive no payout. You are allowed to cash out mid-game for the sum accrued up to that point. How can you maximize your expected payoff?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=897)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> Let the current accumulated sum of rolls so far be $s$. At any such point, you may either
>
> - **stop now** and take payoff $s$, or
> - **continue** and roll once more.
>
> We seek the strategy that maximizes expected payoff.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Value function
>
> Let $V(s)$ denote the maximum expected payoff when the current accumulated total is $s$.
>
> If you stop, you receive exactly
> $$
> s.
> $$
>
> If you continue, then on the next roll:
>
> - with probability $\frac16$, you roll a $5$ and receive the current sum $s$;
> - with probability $\frac16$, you roll a $6$ and receive $0$;
> - with probability $\frac16$ each, you roll $1,2,3,4$, after which the game continues with total $s+1$, $s+2$, $s+3$, or $s+4$ respectively.
>
> Hence
> $$
> V(s)=\max\left\{\, s,\ \frac16 s+\frac16\cdot 0+\frac16 V(s+1)+\frac16 V(s+2)+\frac16 V(s+3)+\frac16 V(s+4)\right\}.
> $$
>
> So the continuation value is
> $$
> C(s)=\frac16 s+\frac16\bigl(V(s+1)+V(s+2)+V(s+3)+V(s+4)\bigr).
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Show that an optimal strategy is a threshold rule
>
> It is natural that if the current sum is large enough, one should stop. We now determine the threshold.
>
> Suppose that for all larger states $s+1,s+2,s+3,s+4$ it is optimal to stop. Then
> $$
> V(s+i)=s+i \qquad (i=1,2,3,4).
> $$
>
> Under that assumption,
> $$
> C(s)=\frac16 s+\frac16\bigl((s+1)+(s+2)+(s+3)+(s+4)\bigr).
> $$
>
> Compute:
> $$
> (s+1)+(s+2)+(s+3)+(s+4)=4s+10,
> $$
> so
> $$
> C(s)=\frac16 s+\frac16(4s+10)=\frac{5s+10}{6}.
> $$
>
> Continuing is better than stopping exactly when
> $$
> \frac{5s+10}{6}>s.
> $$
> Multiply by $6$:
> $$
> 5s+10>6s,
> $$
> hence
> $$
> 10>s.
> $$
>
> Thus:
>
> - if $s<10$, continuing is better;
> - if $s>10$, stopping is better;
> - if $s=10$, both choices are equally good.
>
> Therefore the optimal strategy is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Continue while the accumulated sum is less than }10,\ \text{and stop when it reaches }10\text{ or more.}}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Compute the maximal expected payoff
>
> It remains to compute the expected payoff from the start, where the current sum is $0$.
>
> Since the optimal strategy stops at $10$ or more, we only need $V(s)$ for
> $$
> s=0,1,\dots,10,
> $$
> with boundary values
> $$
> V(s)=s \qquad \text{for } s\ge 10.
> $$
>
> For $s<10$,
> $$
> V(s)=\frac16 s+\frac16\bigl(V(s+1)+V(s+2)+V(s+3)+V(s+4)\bigr).
> $$
>
> Using $V(10)=10$, $V(11)=11$, $V(12)=12$, $V(13)=13$, work backward.
>
> ### At $s=9$
> $$
> V(9)=\frac16\left(9+10+11+12+13\right)=\frac{55}{6}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=8$
> $$
> V(8)=\frac16\left(8+V(9)+10+11+12\right)
> =\frac16\left(41+\frac{55}{6}\right)
> =\frac{301}{36}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=7$
> $$
> V(7)=\frac16\left(7+V(8)+V(9)+10+11\right)
> =\frac16\left(28+\frac{301}{36}+\frac{55}{6}\right)
> =\frac{1639}{216}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=6$
> $$
> V(6)=\frac16\left(6+V(7)+V(8)+V(9)+10\right)
> =\frac16\left(16+\frac{1639}{216}+\frac{301}{36}+\frac{55}{6}\right)
> =\frac{8881}{1296}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=5$
> $$
> V(5)=\frac16\left(5+V(6)+V(7)+V(8)+V(9)\right)
> =\frac{47911}{7776}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=4$
> $$
> V(4)=\frac16\left(4+V(5)+V(6)+V(7)+V(8)\right)
> =\frac{256321}{46656}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=3$
> $$
> V(3)=\frac16\left(3+V(4)+V(5)+V(6)+V(7)\right)
> =\frac{1357495}{279936}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=2$
> $$
> V(2)=\frac16\left(2+V(3)+V(4)+V(5)+V(6)\right)
> =\frac{7098385}{1679616}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=1$
> $$
> V(1)=\frac16\left(1+V(2)+V(3)+V(4)+V(5)\right)
> =\frac{36499303}{10077696}.
> $$
>
> ### At $s=0$
> $$
> V(0)=\frac16\left(V(1)+V(2)+V(3)+V(4)\right)
> =\frac{183324769}{60466176}.
> $$
>
> Therefore the maximal expected payoff is
> $$
> \boxed{V(0)=\frac{183324769}{60466176}\approx 3.0319.}
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## Final answer
>
> The optimal strategy is the threshold rule
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Keep rolling until your accumulated sum reaches }10\text{ or more, then cash out.}}
> $$
>
> If a $5$ appears before then, you receive the accumulated sum up to that point; if a $6$ appears before then, you receive $0$.
>
> The maximum expected payoff starting from $0$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{183324769}{60466176}\approx 3.0319.}
> $$
