> [!example] Question 902 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

An interviewer will play Padel against me. The interviewer, being better, has a 70% chance to win a point against me. The game is played to 11 points, but a player must win by at least 2 points. The current score is 9 to 9. What is the probability that I win the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=903)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p$ be the probability that the interviewer wins a point, and let $q$ be the probability that you win a point. Then
>
> $$
> p = 0.7, \qquad q = 0.3.
> $$
>
> The score is currently $9$-$9$, and the game is won by the first player to reach $11$ points with a margin of at least $2$.
>
> We compute the probability that you eventually win.
>
> ## Step 1: Ways to win before reaching deuce again
>
> Starting from $9$-$9$, you can win immediately only if you win the next two points:
>
> - win point $1$ to make it $10$-$9$,
> - win point $2$ to make it $11$-$9$.
>
> Thus the probability of winning immediately is
>
> $$
> q^2 = (0.3)^2 = 0.09.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Probability of returning to a tied score
>
> From $9$-$9$, after two points the score can again be tied:
>
> - you win one and lose one, or
> - lose one and win one.
>
> Either way, the score becomes $10$-$10$.
>
> The probability of this is
>
> $$
> 2pq = 2(0.7)(0.3) = 0.42.
> $$
>
> Once the score is tied again, the situation is essentially the same as before: from any tied score such as $10$-$10$, $11$-$11$, etc., one player must win two consecutive points to finish the game.
>
> So let $x$ be the probability that you win starting from any tied score.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> x = q^2 + 2pq \, x.
> $$
>
> The reasoning is:
>
> - with probability $q^2$, you win the next two points and the game;
> - with probability $2pq$, the players split the next two points, returning to a tie, so the probability of eventually winning is again $x$;
> - with probability $p^2$, the interviewer wins immediately, contributing $0$ to your winning probability.
>
> ## Step 3: Solve for $x$
>
> Substitute $p=0.7$ and $q=0.3$:
>
> $$
> x = 0.09 + 0.42x.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> x - 0.42x = 0.09
> $$
>
> $$
> 0.58x = 0.09
> $$
>
> $$
> x = \frac{0.09}{0.58} = \frac{9}{58} \approx 0.15517.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Therefore, the probability that you win from $9$-$9$ is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{9}{58} \approx 15.5\% }.
> $$
