> [!example] Question 942 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

A family has children one by one until the number of daughters exceeds the number of sons by exactly one. What is the expected total number of children they will have?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=943)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let daughters be counted as $+1$ and sons as $-1$. After each child, define
>
> $$
> S_n=\text{(\# daughters among first $n$ children)}-\text{(\# sons among first $n$ children)}.
> $$
>
> Initially, $S_0=0$. The family stops at the first time $S_n=1$, since this is exactly the moment when the number of daughters exceeds the number of sons by one.
>
> Thus the total number of children is the hitting time
>
> $$
> T=\inf\{n\ge 0:S_n=1\},
> $$
>
> where $\{S_n\}$ is a simple symmetric random walk starting from $0$.
>
> ## Step 1: Distribution of the stopping time
>
> To have $T=2k+1$, the walk must first hit $1$ at time $2k+1$. Such a path has:
>
> - exactly $k+1$ daughters,
> - exactly $k$ sons,
> - and never reaches level $1$ before the final step.
>
> The number of such paths is given by the Catalan-number count:
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(T=2k+1)=\frac{1}{2^{2k+1}}\cdot \frac{1}{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{k+1}
> =\frac{1}{2^{2k+1}}\cdot \frac{1}{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T]
> =\sum_{k=0}^\infty (2k+1)\,\mathbb{P}(T=2k+1).
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Asymptotic behavior
>
> Using the standard asymptotic for the central binomial coefficient,
>
> $$
> \binom{2k}{k}\sim \frac{4^k}{\sqrt{\pi k}},
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(T=2k+1)
> =\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^k}\cdot \frac{1}{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}
> \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,k^{-3/2}.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> (2k+1)\mathbb{P}(T=2k+1)\sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,k^{-1/2}.
> $$
>
> But
>
> $$
> \sum_{k=1}^\infty k^{-1/2}
> $$
>
> diverges. Therefore the series for $\mathbb{E}[T]$ diverges.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> The expected total number of children is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\infty}.
> $$
>
> So although the family eventually stops with probability $1$, the expected number of children is infinite.
