> [!example] Question 953 · Optiver
> ## Quant Trading Intern Interview

Out of 100 fair coin flips, what is the probability that you get at least 10 more heads than tails?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=954)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $H$ be the number of heads in $100$ fair coin flips. Then
>
> $$
> H \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(100,\tfrac12).
> $$
>
> If there are $H$ heads, then there are $100-H$ tails. The condition “at least $10$ more heads than tails” means
>
> $$
> H - (100-H) \ge 10.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> 2H - 100 \ge 10
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> 2H \ge 110
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> H \ge 55.
> $$
>
> So the required probability is
>
> $$
> P(H \ge 55)=\sum_{k=55}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least 10 more heads than tails})
> =
> \sum_{k=55}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\,2^{-100}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> P(H \ge 55) \approx 0.1841.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\sum_{k=55}^{100} \binom{100}{k}\,2^{-100} \approx 0.1841.}
> $$
