> [!example] Question 983 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

It is 9-9 in a squash match. If I have a 30% chance to win any given point, what is the probability that I win the game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=984)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $p=0.3$ be the probability that you win any given point, and let $q=1-p=0.7$.
>
> At $9$–$9$, under the usual “win by $2$” rule, the game is in a deuce-type situation: from this point, someone must get $2$ points ahead to win.
>
> ## Method 1: State recursion
>
> Let $P$ be the probability that you eventually win the game from $9$–$9$.
>
> From $9$–$9$, three things can happen over the next two points:
>
> - You win both points: probability $p^2$, and you win the game.
> - You lose both points: probability $q^2$, and you lose the game.
> - You split the two points: probability $2pq$, and the score is effectively deuce again.
>
> Thus,
> $$
> P = p^2 + 2pq\,P.
> $$
>
> Solve for $P$:
> $$
> P(1-2pq)=p^2,
> $$
> so
> $$
> P=\frac{p^2}{1-2pq}.
> $$
>
> Now substitute $p=0.3$ and $q=0.7$:
> $$
> P=\frac{(0.3)^2}{1-2(0.3)(0.7)}
> =\frac{0.09}{1-0.42}
> =\frac{0.09}{0.58}
> \approx 0.15517.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.15517 \approx 15.5\%}
> $$
>
> So the chance you win the game is about $15.5\%$.
