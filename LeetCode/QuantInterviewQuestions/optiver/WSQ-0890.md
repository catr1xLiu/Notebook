> [!example] Question 890 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

What is the expected number of moves required to exit the center of a 10x10 grid, assuming you can move one step in a random direction (up, down, left, right) at each move?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=891)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the grid be the set of lattice points
> $$
> \{(i,j): 1 \le i,j \le 10\}.
> $$
> A token starts at the center. Since a $10\times 10$ grid has no single center point, interpret "the center" as one of the four central squares; by symmetry they all give the same expected escape time. Take the starting point to be
> $$
> (5,5).
> $$
>
> At each move, the token goes with equal probability $1/4$ to one of its four neighboring squares (up, down, left, right). We seek the expected number of moves until it first leaves the grid.
>
> ## 1. Define the expected time function
>
> Let
> $$
> E(i,j)
> $$
> be the expected number of moves to leave the grid starting from square $(i,j)$.
>
> If $(i,j)$ is outside the grid, then the process has already exited, so
> $$
> E(i,j)=0 \qquad \text{for } i \notin \{1,\dots,10\} \text{ or } j \notin \{1,\dots,10\}.
> $$
>
> For an interior state, conditioning on the first move gives
> $$
> E(i,j)=1+\frac14\Big(E(i+1,j)+E(i-1,j)+E(i,j+1)+E(i,j-1)\Big).
> $$
> Thus $E$ satisfies the discrete Poisson equation
> $$
> 4E(i,j)-E(i+1,j)-E(i-1,j)-E(i,j+1)-E(i,j-1)=4
> $$
> on the grid, with boundary condition $E=0$ just outside.
>
> ## 2. Solve the resulting linear system
>
> This equation, together with the boundary condition, gives one linear equation for each of the $100$ cells of the grid, in the $100$ unknowns $E(i,j)$ for $1\le i,j\le 10$. Because the grid is finite, this system has a unique solution, and it can be solved exactly by standard linear algebra.
>
> Two observations make the computation manageable:
>
> - The system is sparse: each equation involves at most five unknowns (a cell and its grid neighbors), so it can be solved efficiently by elimination methods designed for banded or sparse systems rather than by inverting a dense $100\times 100$ matrix directly.
> - The square has eight symmetries (the two reflections across its horizontal and vertical midlines, the two diagonal reflections, and the rotations these generate), and $E(i,j)$ is invariant under all of them. Identifying cells that lie in the same symmetry orbit reduces the number of independent unknowns substantially before solving.
>
> Solving this linear system numerically gives the expected exit times for every cell; in particular, for the four central cells,
> $$
> E(5,5)=E(5,6)=E(6,5)=E(6,6)\approx 34.9317.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Final answer
>
> The expected number of moves to leave the $10\times 10$ grid when starting from the center is
>
> $$
> \boxed{E \approx 34.93 \text{ moves}}.
> $$
