> [!example] Question 949 · Optiver
> ## Trading Interview

How would you price an asset, given a list of bids and asks?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=950)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To price an asset from quoted bids and asks, the standard starting point is the **mid-price**.
>
> ## 1. Mid-price from a single bid–ask quote
>
> If the best bid is $b$ and the best ask is $a$, then the quoted fair price is
>
> $$
> P_{\text{mid}}=\frac{b+a}{2}.
> $$
>
> This is the simplest estimate of the asset’s current value.
>
> ## 2. Bid and ask as executable prices
>
> It is also useful to distinguish between:
>
> - **Bid price** $b$: the highest price someone will pay to buy.
> - **Ask price** $a$: the lowest price someone will accept to sell.
>
> Thus:
>
> - If you want to **sell immediately**, the relevant price is approximately $b$.
> - If you want to **buy immediately**, the relevant price is approximately $a$.
> - If you want a neutral estimate of value, use the midpoint $\frac{b+a}{2}$.
>
> ## 3. Example
>
> Suppose the market shows:
>
> $$
> \text{Bid} = 99, \qquad \text{Ask} = 101.
> $$
>
> Then the mid-price is
>
> $$
> P_{\text{mid}}=\frac{99+101}{2}=100.
> $$
>
> So a reasonable quoted price is
>
> $$
> 100.
> $$
>
> ## 4. If there are multiple bids and asks
>
> If an order book contains several bid and ask levels, the usual approaches are:
>
> ### (a) Best bid and best ask midpoint
> Let $b_1$ be the highest bid and $a_1$ the lowest ask. Then
>
> $$
> P=\frac{b_1+a_1}{2}.
> $$
>
> This is the most common convention.
>
> ### (b) Volume-weighted estimate
> If quantities matter, one may use a weighted average based on available size. For example, if the best bid is $b_1$ with size $q_b$ and the best ask is $a_1$ with size $q_a$, a microprice is
>
> $$
> P_{\text{micro}}=\frac{a_1 q_b + b_1 q_a}{q_b+q_a}.
> $$
>
> This adjusts the estimate toward the side with greater pressure.
>
> ## 5. Spread
>
> The bid–ask spread is
>
> $$
> \text{Spread}=a-b.
> $$
>
> A smaller spread usually indicates a more liquid market and a more precise quoted price.
>
> ## Final answer
>
> Given bids and asks, the standard way to price the asset is to use the midpoint of the best bid and best ask:
>
> $$
> P=\frac{\text{best bid}+\text{best ask}}{2}.
> $$
>
> If instead you need the actual transaction price, use the bid for an immediate sale and the ask for an immediate purchase.
