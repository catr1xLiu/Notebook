What is the difference between UDP and TCP?

> ## Difference Between UDP and TCP
>
> UDP and TCP are both transport-layer protocols, but they differ fundamentally in how they deliver data.
>
> ### 1. Connection Model
> - **TCP** is **connection-oriented**. Before data is sent, a connection is established between sender and receiver.
> - **UDP** is **connectionless**. Data is sent without establishing a prior connection.
>
> ### 2. Reliability
> - **TCP** provides **reliable delivery**:
>   - acknowledgments are used,
>   - lost packets are retransmitted,
>   - duplicate packets are detected,
>   - data is delivered in order.
> - **UDP** provides **no reliability guarantees**:
>   - packets may be lost,
>   - duplicated,
>   - or arrive out of order.
>
> ### 3. Ordering
> - **TCP** guarantees that data reaches the destination in the same order in which it was sent.
> - **UDP** does **not** guarantee packet order.
>
> ### 4. Error Handling
> - **TCP** performs error recovery through retransmission and sequencing.
> - **UDP** only includes basic error checking; it does not recover from errors automatically.
>
> ### 5. Speed and Overhead
> - **UDP** is generally **faster** and has **lower overhead**, since it does not maintain connections or perform retransmissions.
> - **TCP** is **slower** because of connection setup, acknowledgments, flow control, and congestion control.
>
> ### 6. Flow and Congestion Control
> - **TCP** includes **flow control** and **congestion control** to regulate transmission.
> - **UDP** has **no built-in flow or congestion control**.
>
> ### 7. Data Format
> - **TCP** is **stream-oriented**: data is treated as a continuous stream of bytes.
> - **UDP** is **message-oriented**: each packet is sent as an individual datagram.
>
> ### 8. Typical Uses
> - **TCP** is used where accuracy and reliability are important, such as:
>   - web browsing with $HTTP/HTTPS$,
>   - email,
>   - file transfer.
> - **UDP** is used where speed and low latency are more important than perfect reliability, such as:
>   - video streaming,
>   - online gaming,
>   - voice calls,
>   - $DNS$ queries.
>
> ## Summary
> - Use **TCP** when **reliable, ordered delivery** is required.
> - Use **UDP** when **low latency and low overhead** are more important, and occasional packet loss is acceptable.
