> [!example] Question 905 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Six people are sitting around a circle. What is the probability that they are seated in increasing order of age, either clockwise or counterclockwise?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=906)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Arrange the $6$ distinct people uniformly at random around a circle. Since circular arrangements that differ only by rotation are the same, the total number of distinct circular seatings is
>
> $$
> (6-1)! = 5! = 120.
> $$
>
> Now ask: in how many of these arrangements do the ages appear in increasing order as one goes around the circle, either clockwise or counterclockwise?
>
> Fix the people by age as
>
> $$
> A_1 < A_2 < A_3 < A_4 < A_5 < A_6.
> $$
>
> For the ages to be increasing around the circle, once the youngest person’s position is fixed, the others must appear in exactly one order clockwise:
>
> $$
> A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4, A_5, A_6.
> $$
>
> This gives one valid circular arrangement.
>
> Similarly, for increasing order counterclockwise, the clockwise order must be
>
> $$
> A_1, A_6, A_5, A_4, A_3, A_2,
> $$
>
> which gives the other valid circular arrangement.
>
> Hence the number of favorable circular arrangements is
>
> $$
> 2.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the required probability is
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{120}=\frac{1}{60}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{60}}.
> $$
