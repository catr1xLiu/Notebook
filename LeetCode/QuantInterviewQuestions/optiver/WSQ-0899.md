> [!example] Question 899 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

In a family, the parents continue having children until they have more girls than boys. What is the expected number of children in this family?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=900)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $G_n$ and $B_n$ denote the numbers of girls and boys after $n$ children, and consider the difference
>
> $$
> D_n = G_n - B_n.
> $$
>
> Each child is independently a girl or a boy with probability $\tfrac12$, so $D_n$ evolves as a simple symmetric random walk on the integers:
>
> - if the next child is a girl, then $D_n$ increases by $1$;
> - if the next child is a boy, then $D_n$ decreases by $1$.
>
> Initially,
>
> $$
> D_0 = 0.
> $$
>
> The family stops at the first time the number of girls exceeds the number of boys, that is, at the first time
>
> $$
> D_n = 1.
> $$
>
> Thus the total number of children is the hitting time
>
> $$
> T = \inf\{n \ge 0 : D_n = 1\}
> $$
>
> for a simple symmetric random walk starting from $0$.
>
> ## Key fact
>
> For a one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk, the expected hitting time of a nonzero state is infinite. In particular,
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[T] = \infty.
> $$
>
> It remains only to justify this in the present setting.
>
> ## Recurrence relation
>
> Let $m_i$ be the expected time to hit $1$ starting from state $i$:
>
> $$
> m_i = \mathbb{E}_i[T].
> $$
>
> Then $m_1 = 0$, and for $i < 1$ the Markov property gives
>
> $$
> m_i = 1 + \frac12 m_{i+1} + \frac12 m_{i-1}.
> $$
>
> Rewriting,
>
> $$
> m_{i+1} - 2m_i + m_{i-1} = -2.
> $$
>
> A general solution of this second-order difference equation is
>
> $$
> m_i = -i^2 + Ai + B
> $$
>
> for constants $A,B$.
>
> Using $m_1 = 0$ gives
>
> $$
> 0 = -1 + A + B,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> B = 1 - A.
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> m_i = -i^2 + Ai + 1 - A.
> $$
>
> Now $m_i$ must be nonnegative for all $i < 1$. But this expression is a concave quadratic in $i$ with leading coefficient $-1$, so as $i \to -\infty$ it tends to $-\infty$, which is impossible for an expected time, regardless of the value of $A$.
>
> Therefore there is no finite nonnegative solution to the recurrence. The only possibility is that
>
> $$
> m_i = \infty
> $$
>
> for all $i < 1$, in particular
>
> $$
> m_0 = \infty.
> $$
>
> Since the process starts at $0$, the expected number of children is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\infty}.
> $$
>
> So although the family eventually reaches the stopping condition with probability $1$, the expected number of children is infinite.
