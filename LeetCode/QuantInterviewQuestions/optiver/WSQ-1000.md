> [!example] Question 1000 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

What happens to the gamma of your position if you are short a straddle?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1001)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A short straddle consists of:
>
> - short $1$ call at strike $K$
> - short $1$ put at the same strike $K$
> - same expiration $T$
>
> Its payoff at expiration is
>
> $$
> -\max(S_T-K,0)-\max(K-S_T,0)=-|S_T-K|.
> $$
>
> ## Gamma of a short straddle
>
> Gamma measures how quickly delta changes as the underlying price $S$ moves:
>
> $$
> \Gamma=\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}.
> $$
>
> For standard long options:
>
> - a long call has **positive gamma**
> - a long put has **positive gamma**
>
> Therefore, when both are sold, the short straddle has
>
> $$
> \Gamma_{\text{straddle}}=-(\Gamma_{\text{call}}+\Gamma_{\text{put}})<0.
> $$
>
> So a **short straddle has negative gamma**.
>
> ## What that means
>
> Negative gamma implies:
>
> - if the stock price rises, your delta becomes more negative
> - if the stock price falls, your delta becomes more positive
>
> Thus your position becomes increasingly wrong as the market moves away from the strike. You tend to:
>
> - lose money on large upward moves
> - lose money on large downward moves
> - benefit when the underlying stays near the strike and does not move much
>
> ## Where gamma is largest
>
> For a straddle, gamma is largest in magnitude when the options are **at the money**, that is, when
>
> $$
> S \approx K.
> $$
>
> It also increases as expiration approaches, especially if the options remain near the money. Hence a short straddle close to expiry can have **very large negative gamma**.
>
> ## Practical interpretation
>
> A short straddle is therefore:
>
> - **short volatility**
> - **negative gamma**
> - profitable from time decay, since it is usually **positive theta**
> - vulnerable to sharp price moves
>
> If hedged dynamically, negative gamma means you typically must:
>
> - buy after the price rises
> - sell after the price falls
>
> which is unfavorable trading behavior.
>
> ## Conclusion
>
> If you are **short a straddle**, your gamma is **negative**. Its magnitude is greatest near the strike and near expiration, so the position becomes increasingly sensitive to underlying price moves in those conditions.
