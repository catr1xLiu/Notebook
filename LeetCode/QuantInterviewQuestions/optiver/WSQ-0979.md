> [!example] Question 979 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Software Developer Interview

How many bits are required to represent a positive integer with exactly 16 digits and no leading zeros, in binary representation?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=980)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A positive integer with $16$ decimal digits and no leading zeros ranges from
>
> $$
> 10^{15} \le n \le 10^{16}-1.
> $$
>
> The number of bits needed to represent a positive integer $n$ in binary is
>
> $$
> \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1.
> $$
>
> To determine how many bits are required for any such $16$-digit integer, consider the largest possible one:
>
> $$
> n_{\max} = 10^{16}-1.
> $$
>
> Now estimate powers of $2$:
>
> $$
> 2^{53} = 9007199254740992 < 10^{16},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 2^{54} = 18014398509481984 > 10^{16}-1.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> 2^{53} \le 10^{16}-1 < 2^{54},
> $$
>
> so the number $10^{16}-1$ requires
>
> $$
> 54
> $$
>
> bits.
>
> Therefore, a $16$-digit positive integer can require up to
>
> $$
> \boxed{54}
> $$
>
> bits in binary.
