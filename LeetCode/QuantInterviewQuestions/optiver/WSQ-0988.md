> [!example] Question 988 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

What is the expected value of a card drawn at random from a standard deck of cards, where J=11, Q=12, K=13? Then, what would this expected value become if the value of all hearts cards is doubled?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=989)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assign numerical values to the cards as follows:
>
> - Ace $= 1$
> - Number cards $2$ through $10$
> - Jack $= 11$
> - Queen $= 12$
> - King $= 13$
>
> A standard deck has $52$ cards, consisting of $4$ suits, each containing one card of each value from $1$ to $13$.
>
> ## 1. Expected value in a normal deck
>
> Since each value from $1$ to $13$ appears exactly $4$ times, the expected value is the average of the numbers $1$ through $13$:
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{1+2+\cdots+13}{13}
> $$
>
> Using the formula for the sum of the first $13$ integers,
>
> $$
> 1+2+\cdots+13 = \frac{13 \cdot 14}{2} = 91
> $$
>
> So,
>
> $$
> E[X] = \frac{91}{13} = 7
> $$
>
> Thus, the expected value of a card is
>
> $$
> \boxed{7}
> $$
>
> ## 2. Expected value if hearts are doubled
>
> Now suppose every heart card has double its usual value, while all other suits keep their original values.
>
> For each value $v \in \{1,2,\dots,13\}$, the four suits contribute:
>
> - three non-hearts worth $v$ each, for a total of $3v$
> - one heart worth $2v$
>
> So the total contribution for value $v$ is
>
> $$
> 3v + 2v = 5v
> $$
>
> Summing over all $13$ values gives total deck value
>
> $$
> \sum_{v=1}^{13} 5v = 5 \sum_{v=1}^{13} v = 5 \cdot 91 = 455
> $$
>
> Since there are still $52$ cards, the expected value is
>
> $$
> E[Y] = \frac{455}{52}
> $$
>
> This simplifies to
>
> $$
> E[Y] = \frac{35}{4} = 8.75
> $$
>
> Therefore, with hearts doubled, the expected value becomes
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{35}{4} = 8.75}
> $$
