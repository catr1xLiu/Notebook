> [!example] Question 895 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Researcher Interview

Someone has \$100 to gamble. First, he will bet \$1, and if he wins, he will leave. Otherwise, he will double his bet on each subsequent round until he wins. What is his profit from this game?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=896)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let the gambler use the following strategy:
>
> - Bet $\text{\textdollar}1$ first.
> - If he loses, bet $\text{\textdollar}2$ next.
> - If he loses again, bet $\text{\textdollar}4$, then $\text{\textdollar}8$, and so on, doubling each time.
> - Stop as soon as he wins.
>
> Assume each gamble is an even-money bet, so each bet is won with probability $\tfrac12$ and lost with probability $\tfrac12$.
>
> ## 1. Possible stopping points
>
> If the first win occurs on the $k$th bet, then the sequence of bets is
>
> $$
> 1,2,4,\dots,2^{k-1}.
> $$
>
> Before the $k$th bet, the total amount lost is
>
> $$
> 1+2+4+\cdots+2^{k-2}=2^{k-1}-1.
> $$
>
> Then on the $k$th bet he wins $\text{\textdollar}2^{k-1}$, so his net profit is
>
> $$
> 2^{k-1}-(2^{k-1}-1)=1.
> $$
>
> Thus, whenever he succeeds before running out of money, his profit is always $\text{\textdollar}1$.
>
> ## 2. How many losses can he afford?
>
> Starting with $\text{\textdollar}100$, he can make the bets
>
> $$
> 1,2,4,8,16,32
> $$
>
> since their cumulative total is
>
> $$
> 1+2+4+8+16+32=63.
> $$
>
> After losing these six bets, he would have
>
> $$
> 100-63=37
> $$
>
> dollars left, which is not enough to make the next required bet of $\text{\textdollar}64$.
>
> So:
>
> - If he wins on any of the first $6$ bets, his profit is $\text{\textdollar}1$.
> - If he loses the first $6$ bets, he must stop, and his profit is
>
> $$
> -63.
> $$
>
> ## 3. Probabilities
>
> The probability he wins at least once in the first $6$ bets is
>
> $$
> 1-\left(\frac12\right)^6 = 1-\frac1{64}=\frac{63}{64}.
> $$
>
> The probability he loses all first $6$ bets is
>
> $$
> \left(\frac12\right)^6=\frac1{64}.
> $$
>
> ## 4. Expected profit
>
> Therefore the expected profit is
>
> $$
> E[\text{profit}]
> =
> \left(\frac{63}{64}\right)(1)+\left(\frac{1}{64}\right)(-63).
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> E[\text{profit}]
> =
> \frac{63}{64}-\frac{63}{64}=0.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}
> $$
>
> So the expected profit of the game is $\text{\textdollar}0$.
