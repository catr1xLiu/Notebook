> [!example] Question 907 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

Two people each flip a fair coin 4 times. What is the probability that both people end up with the same number of heads?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=908)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X$ be the number of heads obtained by the first person and $Y$ the number of heads obtained by the second person.
>
> Since each person flips a fair coin $4$ times independently,
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12), \qquad Y \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(4,\tfrac12),
> $$
> and $X$ and $Y$ are independent.
>
> We want
> $$
> P(X=Y).
> $$
>
> Using independence,
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\sum_{k=0}^4 P(X=k)P(Y=k).
> $$
>
> For a binomial random variable with parameters $4$ and $\tfrac12$,
> $$
> P(X=k)=\binom{4}{k}\left(\tfrac12\right)^4=\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}.
> $$
> Thus,
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\sum_{k=0}^4 \left(\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{16}\right)^2
> = \frac{1}{256}\sum_{k=0}^4 \binom{4}{k}^2.
> $$
>
> Now compute:
> $$
> \binom{4}{0}^2+\binom{4}{1}^2+\binom{4}{2}^2+\binom{4}{3}^2+\binom{4}{4}^2
> =1^2+4^2+6^2+4^2+1^2
> =1+16+36+16+1
> =70.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
> $$
> P(X=Y)=\frac{70}{256}=\frac{35}{128}.
> $$
>
> So the probability that they end with the same number of heads is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{35}{128}}.
> $$
