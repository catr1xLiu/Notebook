> [!example] Question 929 · Optiver
> ## Software Engineer Interview

Given two dates, calculate the number of days between them.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=930)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the number of days between two dates, use the difference
>
> $$
> \text{Number of days} = \text{Later date} - \text{Earlier date}.
> $$
>
> A standard method is:
>
> 1. Convert each date to a serial day count (for example, the number of days elapsed since a fixed reference date).
> 2. Subtract the smaller count from the larger count.
>
> If the dates are in the same year, one may also use day-of-year values:
>
> $$
> \text{Days between dates} = \text{Day-of-year of later date} - \text{Day-of-year of earlier date}.
> $$
>
> If the dates are in different years, then
>
> $$
> \text{Days between dates}
> =
> \bigl(\text{remaining days in first year}\bigr)
> +
> \bigl(\text{full days in intervening years}\bigr)
> +
> \bigl(\text{elapsed days in last year}\bigr).
> $$
>
> When computing, be sure to account for leap years. A year is a leap year if
>
> - it is divisible by $4$,
> - except years divisible by $100$ are not leap years,
> - except years divisible by $400$ are leap years.
>
> Thus:
>
> - $2024$ is a leap year,
> - $1900$ is not a leap year,
> - $2000$ is a leap year.
>
> To give a numerical answer, the two specific dates are needed.
