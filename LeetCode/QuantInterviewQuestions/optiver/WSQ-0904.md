> [!example] Question 904 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Interview

You have 16 watermelons and a seesaw. What is the minimum number of tests required to determine the two heaviest watermelons?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=905)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Label the watermelons arbitrarily and use the seesaw only to compare equal-sized groups by total weight. The goal is to identify the two heaviest individual watermelons in as few weighings as possible.
>
> ## Key idea
>
> A melon can be one of the two heaviest only if it is never shown to be lighter than at least two other melons.  
> Thus, as in tournament-style comparison problems, we should organize the tests so that:
>
> - one melon emerges as the overall heaviest, and
> - only a small set of melons remain possible candidates for second heaviest.
>
> The natural method is a knockout tournament.
>
> ## Step 1: Find the heaviest watermelon
>
> Pair the $16$ melons and compare each pair on the seesaw.
>
> ### Round 1
> There are $8$ pairwise tests, producing $8$ winners.
>
> ### Round 2
> Compare the $8$ winners in pairs: $4$ tests, producing $4$ winners.
>
> ### Round 3
> Compare the $4$ winners in pairs: $2$ tests, producing $2$ winners.
>
> ### Round 4
> Compare the last two winners: $1$ test, producing the overall heaviest melon.
>
> So finding the heaviest requires
>
> $$
> 8+4+2+1 = 15
> $$
>
> tests.
>
> ## Step 2: Find the second heaviest watermelon
>
> The second heaviest must have lost directly to the heaviest in the tournament.  
> Indeed, if a melon never faced the heaviest, then it lost to some other melon, which in turn lost eventually to the heaviest; hence it cannot be second heaviest unless it lost directly to the heaviest.
>
> Since the tournament has $16=2^4$ melons, the champion faced exactly $4$ opponents, one in each round.
>
> Therefore, the only candidates for second heaviest are these $4$ melons.
>
> To determine the heaviest among these $4$ candidates, we need a mini-tournament:
>
> - compare two pairs: $2$ tests,
> - compare the two winners: $1$ test.
>
> So this takes
>
> $$
> 3
> $$
>
> more tests.
>
> ## Total
>
> Hence the minimum number of tests is
>
> $$
> 15+3=18.
> $$
>
> ## Answer
>
> $$
> \boxed{18}
> $$
