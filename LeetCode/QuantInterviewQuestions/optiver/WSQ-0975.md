> [!example] Question 975 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Software Developer Interview

How many bits are required to represent a positive integer consisting of 16 digits, with no leading zeros, in binary representation?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=976)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> A positive integer with $16$ decimal digits and no leading zeros ranges from
>
> $$
> 10^{15} \le n \le 10^{16}-1.
> $$
>
> The number of bits needed to represent a positive integer $n$ in binary is
>
> $$
> \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1.
> $$
>
> To determine how many bits are required for any such $16$-digit integer, consider the largest possible one:
>
> $$
> n_{\max} = 10^{16}-1.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> 2^{53} = 9007199254740992 < 10^{16}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> 2^{54} = 18014398509481984 > 10^{16}-1,
> $$
>
> we have
>
> $$
> 2^{53} \le 10^{16}-1 < 2^{54}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \lfloor \log_2(10^{16}-1) \rfloor = 53,
> $$
>
> so the number of bits required is
>
> $$
> 53+1=54.
> $$
>
> Thus, a $16$-digit positive integer can require up to **$54$ bits** in binary.
