> [!example] Question 943 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

You roll three dice, remove any pair duplicates, and get paid the final sum. How much would you pay for a call option on this payout with a strike price of \$14?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=944)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Let $X_1,X_2,X_3$ be the outcomes of three fair six-sided dice. The payoff rule is:
>
> - if all three dice are different, the paid sum is $X_1+X_2+X_3$;
> - if exactly two dice are equal, remove that duplicate pair and keep only the remaining distinct die, so the paid sum is the value of the singleton;
> - if all three dice are equal, removing any duplicate pair leaves one die, so the paid sum is that common value.
>
> Thus the final paid amount is the sum of the distinct values appearing among the three dice.
>
> A call option with strike $14$ pays
> $$
> (\,S-14\,)^+ = \max(S-14,0),
> $$
> where $S$ is the final paid sum. Its fair price is the expected payoff.
>
> ## Step 1: Identify when the payoff is positive
>
> Since the maximum possible sum of distinct faces is
> $$
> 4+5+6=15,
> $$
> the only way to exceed $14$ is to have distinct values summing to $15$, namely
> $$
> \{4,5,6\}.
> $$
> Hence the call payoff is positive only when the three rolled dice are exactly $4,5,6$ in some order. In that case,
> $$
> S=15 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (S-14)^+=1.
> $$
>
> In every other outcome, $S\le 14$, so the payoff is $0$.
>
> ## Step 2: Compute the probability of rolling $\{4,5,6\}$
>
> The three dice must show $4,5,6$ in any order. There are
> $$
> 3! = 6
> $$
> such ordered outcomes, out of
> $$
> 6^3 = 216
> $$
> total outcomes. Therefore
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(S=15)=\frac{6}{216}=\frac{1}{36}.
> $$
>
> ## Step 3: Expected payoff
>
> Since the payoff is $1$ on this event and $0$ otherwise,
> $$
> \mathbb{E}\big[(S-14)^+\big]
> = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{36} + 0 \cdot \frac{35}{36}
> = \frac{1}{36}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the fair price of the call option is
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{36}}.
> $$
>
> In decimal form,
> $$
> \boxed{0.02778}.
> $$
