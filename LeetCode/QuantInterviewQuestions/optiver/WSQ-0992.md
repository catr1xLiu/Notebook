> [!example] Question 992 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

If the probability of a truck arriving in 10 minutes is 0.36, what is the probability that it arrives in 5 minutes?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=993)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume truck arrivals in disjoint time intervals are independent and occur at a constant average rate, so the number of arrivals follows a Poisson process.
>
> Let $p_{10}=0.36$ be the probability that at least one truck comes in $10$ minutes. For a Poisson process,
>
> $$
> P(\text{at least one arrival in } t)=1-e^{-\lambda t}.
> $$
>
> So for $10$ minutes,
>
> $$
> 1-e^{-10\lambda}=0.36.
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> e^{-10\lambda}=0.64.
> $$
>
> For $5$ minutes, the probability of at least one truck is
>
> $$
> p_{5}=1-e^{-5\lambda}.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> e^{-5\lambda}=\sqrt{e^{-10\lambda}}=\sqrt{0.64}=0.8,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> p_{5}=1-0.8=0.20.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability of a truck coming in $5$ minutes is
>
> $$
> \boxed{0.20}
> $$
>
> or $\boxed{20\%}$.
