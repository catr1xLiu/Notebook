> ## Software Engineer Interview

Design a class diagram for a mock trading system.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=921)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Class Diagram for a Mock Trading System
>
> A mock trading system typically models users, accounts, orders, trades, market data, and instruments. The class diagram should capture the main entities, their attributes, behaviors, and relationships.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Identify Core Classes
>
> The essential classes are:
>
> - $User$
> - $Account$
> - $Portfolio$
> - $Instrument$
> - $Order$
> - $Trade$
> - $OrderBook$
> - $MarketData$
> - $Exchange$
> - $Position$
>
> These represent the principal components of a trading platform.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Class Responsibilities and Key Attributes
>
> ### $User$
> Represents a person using the system.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $userId : String$
> - $name : String$
> - $email : String$
>
> **Methods**
> - $placeOrder()$
> - $cancelOrder()$
> - $viewPortfolio()$
>
> ---
>
> ### $Account$
> Represents the financial account used for trading.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $accountId : String$
> - $balance : double$
> - $status : AccountStatus$
>
> **Methods**
> - $deposit(amount : double)$
> - $withdraw(amount : double)$
> - $getAvailableFunds()$
>
> A $User$ may own one or more accounts.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Portfolio$
> Tracks holdings associated with an account.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $portfolioId : String$
> - $totalValue : double$
>
> **Methods**
> - $addPosition(position : Position)$
> - $removePosition(position : Position)$
> - $calculateValue()$
>
> Each $Account$ typically has one portfolio.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Instrument$
> Represents a tradable asset.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $symbol : String$
> - $name : String$
> - $type : InstrumentType$
> - $currentPrice : double$
>
> **Methods**
> - $updatePrice(price : double)$
>
> Examples include stocks, bonds, or crypto assets.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Order$
> Represents a buy or sell request.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $orderId : String$
> - $side : OrderSide$
> - $type : OrderType$
> - $quantity : int$
> - $price : double$
> - $status : OrderStatus$
> - $timestamp : DateTime$
>
> **Methods**
> - $validate()$
> - $execute()$
> - $cancel()$
>
> An order is placed by an account for a specific instrument.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Trade$
> Represents an executed transaction.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $tradeId : String$
> - $quantity : int$
> - $executionPrice : double$
> - $executionTime : DateTime$
>
> **Methods**
> - $recordTrade()$
>
> A trade results from matching one or more orders.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Position$
> Represents holdings in a particular instrument.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $positionId : String$
> - $quantity : int$
> - $averagePrice : double$
>
> **Methods**
> - $updatePosition(quantity : int, price : double)$
> - $getMarketValue()$
>
> A portfolio contains multiple positions.
>
> ---
>
> ### $OrderBook$
> Maintains active buy and sell orders for an instrument.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $buyOrders : List<Order>$
> - $sellOrders : List<Order>$
>
> **Methods**
> - $addOrder(order : Order)$
> - $removeOrder(order : Order)$
> - $matchOrders()$
>
> Usually there is one order book per instrument.
>
> ---
>
> ### $MarketData$
> Provides current price and quote information.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $bidPrice : double$
> - $askPrice : double$
> - $lastTradedPrice : double$
> - $volume : int$
>
> **Methods**
> - $publishUpdate()$
> - $getSpread()$
>
> Associated with an instrument.
>
> ---
>
> ### $Exchange$
> Represents the venue where orders are matched.
>
> **Attributes**
> - $exchangeId : String$
> - $name : String$
>
> **Methods**
> - $submitOrder(order : Order)$
> - $cancelOrder(orderId : String)$
> - $matchOrders()$
>
> The exchange manages order books and creates trades.
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Relationships
>
> The main associations are:
>
> 1. A $User$ **owns** one or more $Account$ objects.
>    - Multiplicity: $User \; 1 \rightarrow 0..* \; Account$
>
> 2. An $Account$ **has** one $Portfolio$.
>    - Multiplicity: $Account \; 1 \rightarrow 1 \; Portfolio$
>
> 3. A $Portfolio$ **contains** zero or more $Position$ objects.
>    - Multiplicity: $Portfolio \; 1 \rightarrow 0..* \; Position$
>
> 4. A $Position$ is associated with exactly one $Instrument$.
>    - Multiplicity: $Position \; * \rightarrow 1 \; Instrument$
>
> 5. An $Account$ **places** zero or more $Order$ objects.
>    - Multiplicity: $Account \; 1 \rightarrow 0..* \; Order$
>
> 6. Each $Order$ is for exactly one $Instrument$.
>    - Multiplicity: $Order \; * \rightarrow 1 \; Instrument$
>
> 7. An $Exchange$ manages multiple $OrderBook$ objects.
>    - Multiplicity: $Exchange \; 1 \rightarrow 0..* \; OrderBook$
>
> 8. Each $OrderBook$ corresponds to one $Instrument$.
>    - Multiplicity: $OrderBook \; 1 \rightarrow 1 \; Instrument$
>
> 9. Matching orders on the exchange produces $Trade$ objects.
>    - Multiplicity: $Trade$ is associated with one or more $Order$ objects.
>
> 10. Each $Instrument$ has associated $MarketData$.
>    - Multiplicity: $Instrument \; 1 \rightarrow 1 \; MarketData$
>
> ---
>

> ## 5. Useful Enumerations
>
> To make the model clearer, define supporting enums:
>
> - $AccountStatus = \{ ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, CLOSED \}$
> - $OrderSide = \{ BUY, SELL \}$
> - $OrderType = \{ MARKET, LIMIT \}$
> - $OrderStatus = \{ NEW, PARTIALLY\_FILLED, FILLED, CANCELLED \}$
> - $InstrumentType = \{ STOCK, BOND, ETF, CRYPTO \}$
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Design Notes
>
> - Use **composition** between $Account$ and $Portfolio$, and between $Portfolio$ and $Position$, since these objects strongly belong to their container.
> - Use **association** between $Order$ and $Instrument$, since orders reference instruments but do not own them.
> - The $Exchange$ should be responsible for matching logic, while $OrderBook$ stores active orders.
> - A $Trade$ can reference both a buy order and a sell order if more detailed execution modeling is needed.
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Final Answer
>
> A suitable class diagram for a mock trading system includes the classes $User$, $Account$, $Portfolio$, $Position$, $Instrument$, $Order$, $Trade$, $OrderBook$, $MarketData$, and $Exchange$, with relationships reflecting account ownership, portfolio holdings, order placement, instrument trading, and exchange-based order matching. The diagram above provides a clear object-oriented structure suitable for implementation or further refinement into a full UML model.
