> [!example] Question 945 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader Intern Interview

Given a bracket of teams and their relative strengths or ratings, determine how to allocate bets on the teams to maximize expected profit and loss (PnL).

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=946)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To maximize profit and loss ($\mathrm{PnL}$) when betting on a tournament bracket, one must convert team strengths into win probabilities, compare those probabilities to market odds, and allocate stake only where the expected value is positive, subject to bankroll and risk constraints.
>
> ---
>
> ## 1. Model the tournament outcomes
>
> Suppose each team $i$ has a rating $r_i$.  
> For any matchup between teams $i$ and $j$, convert ratings into a win probability.
>
> A standard choice is a logistic model:
>
> $$
> p_{ij} = \Pr(i \text{ beats } j) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(r_i-r_j)/s}}
> $$
>
> where $s>0$ is a scale parameter.
>
> If Elo-style ratings are used, one may instead write
>
> $$
> p_{ij} = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-(r_i-r_j)/400}}.
> $$
>
> These pairwise probabilities allow computation of each team’s probability of advancing through the bracket.
>
> ---
>
> ## 2. Compute each team’s bracket probabilities
>
> Let $q_{i,k}$ denote the probability that team $i$ reaches round $k$, and let $w_{i,k}$ denote the probability that team $i$ wins in round $k$.
>
> Then:
>
> - $q_{i,1}=1$ for all teams initially in the tournament,
> - for each round, advancement is obtained by summing over possible opponents in that round.
>
> If $S(i,k)$ is the set of teams that could face team $i$ in round $k$, then
>
> $$
> w_{i,k}
> =
> q_{i,k}
> \sum_{j \in S(i,k)} q_{j,k} \, p_{ij},
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> q_{i,k+1} = w_{i,k}.
> $$
>
> Thus, by dynamic programming over the bracket, one obtains:
>
> - probability of reaching each round,
> - probability of winning the tournament,
> - probability of any market-relevant event.
>
> For example, if the betting market is on the team to win the championship, define
>
> $$
> \pi_i = \Pr(i \text{ wins tournament}).
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 3. Translate sportsbook odds into payoffs
>
> Suppose the sportsbook offers decimal odds $O_i$ on team $i$ to win the tournament.
>
> If one stakes $x_i$ on team $i$, then:
>
> - if team $i$ wins, profit is
>
> $$
> x_i (O_i - 1),
> $$
>
> - otherwise, profit is
>
> $$
> -x_i.
> $$
>
> Hence expected profit from betting on team $i$ is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PnL}_i]
> =
> x_i \left[\pi_i (O_i-1) - (1-\pi_i)\right].
> $$
>
> This simplifies to
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PnL}_i]
> =
> x_i (\pi_i O_i - 1).
> $$
>
> Therefore, team $i$ is a positive expected value bet exactly when
>
> $$
> \pi_i O_i > 1.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \pi_i > \frac{1}{O_i}.
> $$
>
> The quantity
>
> $$
> \text{edge}_i = \pi_i - \frac{1}{O_i}
> $$
>
> measures whether the offered odds are better than fair value.
>
> ---
>
> ## 4. Optimization objective
>
> If the goal is to maximize expected $\mathrm{PnL}$ with bankroll $B$, the optimization problem is
>
> $$
> \max_{x_i \ge 0} \sum_i x_i (\pi_i O_i - 1)
> $$
>
> subject to
>
> $$
> \sum_i x_i \le B.
> $$
>
> ### Immediate implication
>
> Since the objective is linear, the unconstrained expected-value optimum is:
>
> - bet nothing on teams with $\pi_i O_i \le 1$,
> - allocate the entire bankroll to the team with largest positive value of $\pi_i O_i - 1$.
>
> Formally, if
>
> $$
> i^* = \arg\max_i (\pi_i O_i - 1),
> $$
>
> then the pure expected-value solution is
>
> $$
> x_{i^*} = B, \qquad x_j = 0 \text{ for } j \ne i^*,
> $$
>
> provided $\pi_{i^*} O_{i^*} > 1$.
>
> If no team satisfies $\pi_i O_i > 1$, then
>
> $$
> x_i = 0 \quad \text{for all } i.
> $$
>
> ---
>
> ## 5. Risk-aware betting: Kelly criterion
>
> Maximizing expected $\mathrm{PnL}$ alone is usually too aggressive. A more practical method is to maximize expected logarithmic wealth.
>
> For a single bet with win probability $\pi_i$ and net odds $b_i = O_i - 1$, the Kelly fraction is
>
> $$
> f_i^* = \frac{\pi_i b_i - (1-\pi_i)}{b_i}
> = \frac{\pi_i O_i - 1}{O_i - 1}.
> $$
>
> Thus stake
>
> $$
> x_i = B f_i^*
> $$
>
> when $f_i^*>0$, and otherwise $x_i=0$.
>
> In practice one often uses fractional Kelly:
>
> $$
> x_i = \lambda B f_i^*, \qquad 0 < \lambda \le 1,
> $$
>
> to reduce variance.
>
> ---
>
> ## 6. Multiple mutually exclusive outright bets
>
> If betting on several teams to win the tournament, these outcomes are mutually exclusive. Let $x_i$ be the stake on team $i$.
>
> If team $k$ wins, the total profit is
>
> $$
> \mathrm{PnL}(k)
> =
> x_k (O_k - 1) - \sum_{i \ne k} x_i.
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \mathrm{PnL}(k)
> =
> x_k O_k - \sum_i x_i.
> $$
>
> Hence expected profit is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PnL}]
> =
> \sum_k \pi_k \left(x_k O_k - \sum_i x_i\right)
> =
> \sum_k x_k \pi_k O_k - \sum_i x_i.
> $$
>
> Since $\sum_k \pi_k = 1$, this becomes
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PnL}]
> =
> \sum_i x_i (\pi_i O_i - 1),
> $$
>
> the same expression as before.
>
> Again, the expected-value optimum is to place all money on the single best positive-edge team.
>
> ---
>
> ## 7. Practical algorithm
>
> Given ratings and odds:
>
> ### Step 1: Compute pairwise probabilities
> For all teams $i,j$,
>
> $$
> p_{ij} = \frac{1}{1+e^{-(r_i-r_j)/s}}.
> $$
>
> ### Step 2: Propagate through the bracket
> Use the bracket structure to compute each team’s title probability $\pi_i$.
>
> ### Step 3: Compute expected edge
> For each team,
>
> $$
> \mathrm{EV}_i = \pi_i O_i - 1.
> $$
>
> ### Step 4: Bet only positive-EV teams
> If maximizing expected $\mathrm{PnL}$ strictly:
>
> $$
> x_i =
> \begin{cases}
> B, & i = \arg\max_j \mathrm{EV}_j \text{ and } \mathrm{EV}_i>0,\\
> 0, & \text{otherwise}.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> If using Kelly sizing:
>
> $$
> x_i = B \max\left(0,\frac{\pi_i O_i - 1}{O_i - 1}\right),
> $$
>
> possibly scaled by a fractional factor $\lambda$.
>
> ---
>
> ## 8. Final answer
>
> Let $\pi_i$ be the probability, derived from the bracket and team ratings, that team $i$ wins the marketed event, and let $O_i$ be the decimal odds.
>
> The expected profit from staking $x_i$ on team $i$ is
>
> $$
> \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{PnL}_i] = x_i(\pi_i O_i - 1).
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> - bet only when
>
> $$
> \pi_i O_i > 1,
> $$
>
> - if the objective is to maximize expected $\mathrm{PnL}$ alone, place the entire bankroll on the team with the largest positive value of
>
> $$
> \pi_i O_i - 1,
> $$
>
> - if risk management matters, use Kelly sizing
>
> $$
> x_i = B \max\left(0,\frac{\pi_i O_i - 1}{O_i - 1}\right),
> $$
>
> or a fractional Kelly variant.
>
> Thus, the optimal betting rule is:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Estimate bracket win probabilities from ratings, compare them to market-implied probabilities, and stake only on positive-edge teams, with full concentration for pure expected-PnL maximization or Kelly fractions for risk-adjusted growth.}}
> $$
