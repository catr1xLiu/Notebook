> [!example] Question 969 · Optiver
> ## Senior Software Engineer Interview

Given a list of exchange rates between different currency pairs and an amount in one currency, convert the amount to another specified currency.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=970)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> ## Solution
>
> To convert an amount from one currency to another when a list of conversion rates is given, model the currencies and rates as a directed weighted graph.
>
> - Each currency is a vertex.
> - A conversion rate from currency $A$ to currency $B$ is a directed edge $A \to B$ with weight equal to the rate.
>
> If an amount $x$ in currency $A$ is converted along a path
>
> $$
> A \to C_1 \to C_2 \to \cdots \to B,
> $$
>
> with rates
>
> $$
> r_1, r_2, \dots, r_k,
> $$
>
> then the final amount in currency $B$ is
>
> $$
> x \cdot r_1 \cdot r_2 \cdots r_k.
> $$
>
> ### Method
>
> Given:
>
> - starting currency $s$,
> - target currency $t$,
> - starting amount $x$,
> - list of rates of the form $(u, v, r)$ meaning
>
> $$
> 1 \text{ unit of } u = r \text{ units of } v,
> $$
>
> proceed as follows:
>
> 1. Build a graph from the conversion list.
> 2. Search for a path from $s$ to $t$.
> 3. Multiply the rates along that path.
> 4. Return
>
> $$
> x \cdot \prod r_i.
> $$
>
> If reverse conversion is allowed but not explicitly given, then from
>
> $$
> u \to v \text{ with rate } r
> $$
>
> one may also add
>
> $$
> v \to u \text{ with rate } \frac{1}{r}.
> $$
>
> ### Example
>
> Suppose the rates are:
>
> - $\text{USD} \to \text{EUR}$ at $0.9$
> - $\text{EUR} \to \text{JPY}$ at $130$
>
> Convert $100$ USD to JPY.
>
> The path is
>
> $$
> \text{USD} \to \text{EUR} \to \text{JPY}.
> $$
>
> So the total conversion factor is
>
> $$
> 0.9 \cdot 130 = 117.
> $$
>
> Hence the converted amount is
>
> $$
> 100 \cdot 117 = 11700.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> 100 \text{ USD} = 11700 \text{ JPY}.
> $$
>
> ### Algorithm
>
> A depth-first search or breadth-first search is sufficient if any valid path is acceptable.
>
> Pseudo-process:
>
> 1. Store adjacency list:
>    $$\text{graph}[u] = \{(v, r)\}.$$
> 2. Start from $(s, 1)$.
> 3. Traverse neighbors while maintaining the accumulated product.
> 4. When $t$ is reached with accumulated factor $p$, answer is
>    $$x \cdot p.$$
>
> ### Final Answer
>
> To convert between two currencies using a list of rates, find a path between the currencies and multiply the rates along that path. If the starting amount is $x$ and the path rates are $r_1, r_2, \dots, r_k$, then the result is
>
> $$
> x \cdot r_1 r_2 \cdots r_k.
> $$
>
> If no path exists, the conversion is impossible.
