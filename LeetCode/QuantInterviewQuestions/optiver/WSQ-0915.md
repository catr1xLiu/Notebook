> [!example] Question 915 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trading Intern Interview

Approximate the probability of getting exactly 50 heads in 100 tosses of a fair coin, not as a combinatorial expression, but as a percentage or a simplified fraction.

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=916)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> For a fair coin tossed $100$ times, the number of heads $X$ has the binomial distribution
>
> $$
> X \sim \operatorname{Bin}(100, 1/2).
> $$
>
> The probability of exactly $50$ heads is
>
> $$
> P(X=50)=\binom{100}{50}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}.
> $$
>
> Since the question asks for an approximation as a percentage or fraction, use the normal approximation to the binomial.
>
> ## Step 1: Mean and standard deviation
>
> For $X \sim \operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$,
>
> $$
> \mu=np, \qquad \sigma=\sqrt{np(1-p)}.
> $$
>
> Here,
>
> $$
> \mu=100\cdot \frac12=50,
> \qquad
> \sigma=\sqrt{100\cdot \frac12\cdot \frac12}=\sqrt{25}=5.
> $$
>
> ## Step 2: Continuity correction
>
> To approximate $P(X=50)$, replace it by
>
> $$
> P(49.5<X<50.5).
> $$
>
> Standardizing,
>
> $$
> P(49.5<X<50.5)
> =
> P\left(\frac{49.5-50}{5}<Z<\frac{50.5-50}{5}\right)
> =
> P(-0.1<Z<0.1),
> $$
>
> where $Z\sim N(0,1)$.
>
> ## Step 3: Evaluate
>
> Using the standard normal distribution,
>
> $$
> P(-0.1<Z<0.1)\approx 0.0796.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(X=50)\approx 0.08.
> $$
>
> Thus the probability is about
>
> $$
> 8\%.
> $$
>
> ## Check against the exact value
>
> The exact probability is
>
> $$
> \binom{100}{50}\left(\frac12\right)^{100}\approx 0.07959,
> $$
>
> which is indeed about
>
> $$
> 7.96\% \approx 8\% \approx \frac{1}{12.5}.
> $$
>
> Hence, the probability of getting exactly $50$ heads in $100$ fair tosses is approximately
>
> $$
> \boxed{8\%}.
> $$
