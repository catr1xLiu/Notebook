> [!example] Question 1001 · Optiver
> ## Trader Interview

What is the next number in the sequence: 1/2, 1/3, 1/6, 1/18, 1/108, ...?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=1002)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Consider the ratios between consecutive terms:
>
> $$
> \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{3}{2}, \quad
> \frac{\frac{1}{3}}{\frac{1}{6}} = 2, \quad
> \frac{\frac{1}{6}}{\frac{1}{18}} = 3, \quad
> \frac{\frac{1}{18}}{\frac{1}{108}} = 6.
> $$
>
> Equivalently, each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{1}{3},\ \frac{1}{6}.
> $$
>
> Now look at the denominators themselves: $2,\ 3,\ 6,\ 18,\ 108$. Each one is the product of the two immediately preceding denominators:
>
> $$
> d_3 = d_2\cdot d_1 = 3\cdot 2 = 6, \quad
> d_4 = d_3\cdot d_2 = 6\cdot 3 = 18, \quad
> d_5 = d_4\cdot d_3 = 18\cdot 6 = 108.
> $$
>
> This multiplicative Fibonacci-type recursion, $d_n=d_{n-1}\cdot d_{n-2}$, also explains the multiplying factors above: from the third term onward, the factor applied is the reciprocal of the denominator two steps back, since
>
> $$
> \frac{d_n}{d_{n+1}}=\frac{d_n}{d_n\cdot d_{n-1}}=\frac{1}{d_{n-1}}.
> $$
>
> Applying the same recursion to find the next denominator,
>
> $$
> d_6 = d_5\cdot d_4 = 108\cdot 18 = 1944.
> $$
>
> Thus the next term is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{108}\cdot \frac{1}{18} = \frac{1}{1944}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the next number is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{1944}}.
> $$
