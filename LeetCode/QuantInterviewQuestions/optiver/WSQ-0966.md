> [!example] Question 966 · Optiver
> ## Graduate Quantitative Researcher Interview

Six dice are rolled simultaneously. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers shown is an even number?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=967)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> To find the probability that the sum of six dice is even, use parity.
>
> Each die shows an even number with probability
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2},
> $$
>
> and an odd number with probability
>
> $$
> \frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> The sum of six numbers is even exactly when the number of odd results is even.
>
> For six independent dice, the number of odd outcomes can be $0,2,4,$ or $6$. Hence
>
> $$
> P(\text{even sum})
> = \sum_{k \in \{0,2,4,6\}} \binom{6}{k}\left(\frac12\right)^6.
> $$
>
> So
>
> $$
> P(\text{even sum})
> = \frac{1}{64}\left[\binom{6}{0}+\binom{6}{2}+\binom{6}{4}+\binom{6}{6}\right].
> $$
>
> Compute the binomial coefficients:
>
> $$
> \binom{6}{0}=1,\quad \binom{6}{2}=15,\quad \binom{6}{4}=15,\quad \binom{6}{6}=1.
> $$
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> P(\text{even sum})
> = \frac{1+15+15+1}{64}
> = \frac{32}{64}
> = \frac{1}{2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability that the sum is even is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.
> $$
