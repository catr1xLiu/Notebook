> [!example] Question 944 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Trader - Intern Interview

What is the probability of getting 10 heads in a row when flipping a fair coin?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=945)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> Assume a fair coin, so each flip has probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of landing heads, and flips are independent.
>
> To get $10$ heads in a row in exactly $10$ flips, all $10$ flips must be heads. Thus,
>
> $$
> P(\text{10 heads in a row}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10}.
> $$
>
> Compute:
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10} = \frac{1}{1024} \approx 0.0009765625.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the probability is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{1}{1024} \approx 0.0977\%}.
> $$
>
> If the coin is not fair and the probability of heads on each flip is $p$, then the probability becomes
>
> $$
> \boxed{p^{10}}.
> $$
