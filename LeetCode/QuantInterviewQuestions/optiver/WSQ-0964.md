> [!example] Question 964 · Optiver
> ## Quantitative Analyst Interview

How many nodes are there in a recombining binomial tree with n steps? How many paths lead to a particular node?

[Source](https://www.thewallstreetquants.com/ebook#p=965)

> [!success]- Solution (Click to expand)
> In a recombining binomial tree, each step is either an up move or a down move, and nodes that correspond to the same number of up moves recombine.
>
> ## Number of nodes
>
> At time step $k$, the possible numbers of up moves are $0,1,2,\dots,k$, so there are
>
> $$
> k+1
> $$
>
> distinct nodes at step $k$.
>
> Therefore, over an $n$-step tree, the total number of nodes is
>
> $$
> \sum_{k=0}^{n} (k+1)
> = 1+2+\cdots+(n+1)
> = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}.
> $$
>
> Thus, the total number of nodes in a recombining binomial tree with $n$ steps is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}}.
> $$
>
> ## Number of paths to a particular node
>
> Consider a node at step $k$ reached by exactly $j$ up moves and $k-j$ down moves. Any path to that node is determined by choosing which $j$ of the $k$ steps are up moves.
>
> Hence, the number of distinct paths to that node is
>
> $$
> \binom{k}{j}.
> $$
>
> In particular, at the final time $n$, the number of paths to the node with $j$ up moves is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{n}{j}}.
> $$
>
> ## Final answer
>
> - Total number of nodes in an $n$-step recombining binomial tree:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}}
> $$
>
> - Number of paths to a particular node at step $k$ with $j$ up moves:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{k}{j}}
> $$
>
> and in particular at maturity $n$:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\binom{n}{j}}.
> $$
